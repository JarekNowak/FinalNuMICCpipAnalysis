\begin{abstract}
This supplement contains the final configuration of the $\mathrm{CC}\pi^{\pm}$ NuMI cross-section
measurement (Part~I), the evidence for its choices (Part~II) and the alternatives that were studied and
not adopted (Part~III).

Charged pions interact hadronically before they stop, so the range-based pion momentum saturates
near $0.34$~GeV/$c$ and carries little information about the true momentum above about
$0.4$~GeV/$c$. Golden-pion tagging, multiple Coulomb scattering, calorimetry, multivariate
regression, recovery of the daughter energy and the proton-tagged selection were all studied, and
none recovers it; a truth-level golden selection performs no better than the classifier, which
places the limitation in the physics of pion interactions rather than in the estimator. The pion
momentum is therefore measured in three regions. No differential $W_{\pi p}$ result is reported. The
topology-level one-bin total cross section of the proton-tagged sample remains measurable, but it is
not described as a $W_{\pi p}$ measurement.

The binnings of both families meet a $0.50$ migration criterion, and the cross-checks of the
extraction (identity closure, the D'Agostini method, ensembles of pseudo-data experiments and the
consistency between configurations) are passed. Numbers shared with the analysis notes are checked
for consistency automatically (\texttt{report/check\_tables.py},
\texttt{xsec\_analyzer/check\_staleness.py}). The corrections that shaped the analysis, with the
values before and after, are recorded in the change log.
\end{abstract}

\section[Final binnings and the binning rule]{Final binnings and the binning rule\scopetag{shared}}
\label{sec:final_binning}

Table~\ref{tab:binning} gives the bin edges of the inclusive observables. The proton-tagged edges are
in \ptref{Table}{tab:pt_binning}, and those of the two two-dimensional results in their curve files
(\S\ref{sec:release_schema}).

\input{tables/binning}

Each one-dimensional binning is selected by the following rule, established by the binning review
(\S\ref{sec:binreview}):
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep1pt
  \item Generate candidate partitions: for every number of bins up to twelve, the maximin partition,
    which has the largest worst-bin migration diagonal over FHC and RHC, found by dynamic programming
    on a fine grid (\texttt{report/tools/maximin\_binning.py}).
  \item Require the event floor: at least $70$ selected FHC signal events per bin at data exposure in
    the inclusive family, and $50$ in the proton-tagged family.
  \item Require every diagonal of the column-normalised migration matrix to be at least $0.50$, in FHC
    and in RHC, on the analysis sample (\S\ref{sec:ppi_samples}); the combined configuration inherits
    the binning.
  \item Extract the candidates that pass in full, in the three configurations: universes, covariance,
    unfolding and closure.
  \item Compute the Wiener filter factors of each extraction and the trace of $A_C$, their sum, which
    is the number of degrees of freedom that the data determine.
  \item Select the fewest bins that reach $90\%$ of the largest trace, averaged over FHC and RHC; ties
    go to the larger worst diagonal.
  \item Check the closure and the per-bin uncertainties of the selected binning.
  \item Freeze the binning.
\end{enumerate}
Four adopted binnings differ from the one the rule selects, for the reasons given in
\S\ref{sec:binreview}: $\cos\theta_\pi$ and $\theta_{\mu\pi}$ keep five bins, $\delta\alpha_T$ keeps its
split at $120^\circ$, and the proton-tagged $p_\pi$ keeps two regions. The $0.68$ criterion of the BNB
CC$1\pi$ measurement is the precedent for the migration criterion and provides a stricter comparison
point; \S\ref{sec:binreview} gives the consequences of both. Everything in Part~III, including the
first binning optimisation, the five-bin $p_\pi$ response and the $\theta_\mu$ parameterisation, is
historical and is not used in the submitted results.

A result can close while it is determined largely by the modelled migrations or by the
regularisation, so the migration criterion and the Wiener filter factors are applied as separate
criteria of the information a binning retains.

\section[Extraction configuration and uncertainty model]{Extraction configuration and uncertainty model\scopetag{shared}}
\label{sec:supp_extraction}

\paragraph{Unfolding.} Every differential result is unfolded with the Wiener-SVD method. The
regularisation matrix is the second derivative of the cross-section density, built with the actual bin
widths: on binnings as non-uniform as these, the uniform stencil reduces $A_C$ to a matrix that passes
the normalisation and discards the shape (\S\ref{sec:reg_widths}). The additional-smearing matrix
$A_C$ of every result is released with it, and every prediction is smeared by it before it is
compared with the result. The one-bin total cross sections are extracted with the Wiener filter off,
$A_C\equiv1$ (\S\ref{sec:supp_total}). The D'Agostini iterative method is a cross-check
(\S\ref{sec:crosschecks}).

\paragraph{Normalisation.} Each run is normalised to its recorded exposure: the simulation of every run
is scaled by the ratio of its beam-on to its simulated POT, and the beam-off samples by the per-run
gate ratios (\noteref{Sec.}{sec:mc}). The flux is the summed $\nu_\mu+\bar\nu_\mu$ flux integrated
above $60$~MeV: FHC $6.81159$, RHC $6.44646$ and combined configuration
$6.59691\times10^{-10}$~cm$^{-2}$~POT$^{-1}$, each configuration normalised with its own value. The
$60$~MeV convention includes the flux below the pion-production threshold, so it lowers the
flux-averaged total relative to a threshold matched to the spectrum (\noteref{Sec.}{sec:flux_status}).
The target count is $N_\mathrm{Ar}=8.710\times10^{29}$ argon nuclei in the fiducial volume.

\paragraph{Covariance.} The official covariance of every result, \texttt{cov\_total\_plusMCS.txt}, is
the framework covariance, \texttt{cov\_total.txt}, plus the MCS muon-momentum-scale term. The framework
covariance sums the flux (PPFX universes), cross-section (GENIE multisim and unisims), hadron
re-interaction, detector-variation, POT and target-count terms and the statistical terms of the
simulation, the beam-off samples and the data, each propagated through the unfolding and released
separately (\S\ref{sec:release_schema}). The MCS term is the half-difference, taken as fully
correlated, of the results obtained when the MCS-measured muon momenta of the pseudo-data are scaled
by $\pm5\%$ on the data side and unfolded with the nominal response. It is computed outside the
framework, is zero for the results that do not use the reconstructed muon momentum, and is
included in every quoted uncertainty and every summary table, among them the systematic breakdowns of
Tables~\ref{tab:systbreak_fhc}--\ref{tab:systbreak_comb} (\noteref{Sec.}{sec:mcs}). The closure
$\chi^2$ and the biases of the robustness tests are quoted with the framework covariance, since they
test the extraction rather than the uncertainty of the data. Three terms lie outside the official
covariance: the beamline-geometry flux term, the uncertainty of the per-run beam-off trigger ratios and that of the use of the Run-1 beam-off
sample for Run~2.

\section[Validation]{Validation\scopetag{shared}}
\label{sec:supp_validation}

The tests of the extraction go from the implementation to the model. The identity closure and the
pseudo-data closure test the implementation (\S\ref{sec:identity}). Every extraction closes: the
inclusive ones with $\chi^2\le2.39/8$ and integral ratios of $0.946$--$1.047$, the proton-tagged ones
with $\chi^2\le6.01/4$ (RHC $\cos\theta_\pi$) and ratios of $0.93$--$1.18$, and the two two-dimensional
results with cell ratios of $0.70$--$1.36$. A result can close while it is determined largely by the
modelled migrations or by the regularisation, so the migration criterion and the trace of $A_C$ are
applied as separate criteria (\S\ref{sec:final_binning}).

Ensembles of $100$ pseudo-experiments test the size of the statistical uncertainties
(\S\ref{sec:supp_ensemble}). Seven inclusive and ten proton-tagged extractions were run, the proton
angles in every configuration and the two two-dimensional results among them; the pull widths are
$0.95$--$1.07$ and $0.94$--$1.08$, and the $68\%$ coverages $63$--$73\%$ and $62$--$72\%$.

Pseudo-data from two reweighted interaction models, GENIE multisim universe~545 and the
$\Delta\to N\pi$ angular unisim, test the robustness to a model that reweighting can reach
(\noteref{Sec.}{sec:altmodel}, \ptref{Sec.}{sec:pt_altmodel}). The largest bias is $1.18\sigma$, in one
sparse inclusive bin (FHC $\cos\theta_\mu$); in the proton-tagged sample the low $\delta p_T$ and $p_n$
bins move by $-1.14\sigma$ and $-1.16\sigma$. The proton angles and the two two-dimensional results stay
within $0.79\sigma$.

No closure test with an independent generator has been made; the NuWro overlay available so far
disagrees with standalone NuWro and is not used (\noteref{Sec.}{sec:limitations}). The control regions,
which test the background model, have been studied with pseudo-data only (\S\ref{sec:request}).

\part{Evidence for the choices}
\section[Pion momentum: the range estimator and its saturation]{Pion momentum: the range estimator and its saturation\scopetag{inclusive}}
\label{sec:supp_ppi}

The inclusive $p_\pi$ is measured in three regions with edges $[0.175, 0.205, 0.26, \infty)$~GeV/$c$,
and the proton-tagged $p_\pi$ in two regions, $[0.175, 0.205, \infty)$~GeV/$c$. The five-bin
configurations are diagnostics, not results. The golden-pion, MCS, calorimetric, regression and
daughter-recovery studies of this section support the coarse range-based measurement.

\subsection{Track sample and analysis sample}
\label{sec:ppi_samples}

Migration diagonals of the pion momentum are quoted on two samples, which are not interchangeable. The
track sample is every truth-matched pion track passing loose quality requirements (purity
$>0.5$, track score $>0.5$, length $>5$~cm), from events of any topology, $126\,518$ tracks in
total; the golden-pion studies use it, and the estimator studies use truth-matched pion tracks of the
same kind. The analysis sample is the events passing the full
CC$1\mu1\pi^\pm$ selection and satisfying the signal definition, about $10\,700$ per beam
mode; the adopted binnings, the binning studies and the hadronic-mass study use it. The analysis
sample has the better response, because the selection removes the events whose pion is poorly
reconstructed.

\subsection{Pion-momentum bias and the conditioning of the $p_\pi$ response}
\label{sec:ppi_bias}

The $p_\pi$ response is the least diagonal of any observable in the analysis. The cause is
physical, and it limits what the measurement can say about the pion momentum spectrum.

\subsubsection{Mass hypothesis and hadronic truncation.}
The reconstructed pion momentum stored in the ntuple,
\texttt{candidate\_pion\_mom\_reco}, is \texttt{trk\_range\_muon\_mom\_v}: a range-based
momentum evaluated under the muon mass hypothesis and applied to a pion track. Two
distinct effects follow, and only the first is corrected.

First, the mass hypothesis biases the estimate low, because for a given range a
pion carries more momentum than a muon. The size is $\sim\!17$--$19\%$ from the CSDA
range scaling, largely independent of momentum. This effect is corrected in the reco
bin definitions rather than in the branch: \texttt{ccpi\_ppi\_bin\_config\_opt.txt} applies
a kinetic-energy-preserving $\mu\to\pi$ mass swap, $E_\mu \to T \to E_\pi \to p_\pi$,
before binning. In the lowest true bin $\langle p^{\rm reco}/p^{\rm true}\rangle$ goes from
$0.888$ with the raw branch to $0.976$ with the correction.

Second, charged pions interact hadronically before coming to rest, and nothing corrects
for this. The reconstructed track then ends at the interaction point rather than the
stopping point, so the visible range, and any momentum inferred from it, is
truncated. The effect grows rapidly with momentum and dominates at high momentum.

\subsubsection{Saturation of the reconstructed momentum.}
Table~\ref{tab:ppi_sat} and Figure~\ref{fig:ppi_sat} show the result: as the
true momentum rises by a factor of four and a half, the reconstructed momentum barely
moves, saturating at $\approx0.34$~GeV/$c$. Above that the estimator carries almost no
information about the energy of the pion.

\subsubsection{Effect on the five-bin response matrix.}
On the analysis sample of Table~\ref{tab:ppi_sat}, migrating the five-bin scheme with the
corrected estimator gives a nearly
lower-triangular matrix: the diagonal fraction falls from $94.3\%$ in the first true bin
to $36.5$, $23.1$, $10.4$ and finally $6.2\%$ in the last. For the highest true bin
($p_\pi>0.55$~GeV/$c$) only $6.2\%$ of events are reconstructed in the corresponding reco
bin while $32.6\%$ fall into the lowest one. Every true bin above the
first therefore deposits most of its events into reco bin 1, the
``pile-up into the first bin'' of Fig.~\ref{fig:resp_ppi5}.

\subsubsection{Treatment in the BNB CC1$\pi^\pm$ analysis.}
The published BNB CC$1\pi^{\pm}$Xp analysis (internal note v0.91) uses an identical
estimator: it too takes ``the momentum of pion candidates from range''. Its treatment is
summarised here and was followed by this analysis.

That analysis does three things this one does not. First, it defines a \emph{golden pion}: one
that comes to rest and leaves a Bragg peak, as opposed to one that scattered or interacted.
Second, it trains a dedicated golden-pion BDT and applies it as an extra cut for
the pion-momentum differential cross section only, leaving the total and angular results on
the generic selection. The BDT separates golden from non-golden pions using track
``wiggliness'' (the standard deviation of the angular difference between directions of
adjacent points along the fitted track, a direct handle on scattering), the number of
reconstructed descendant particles, the track--shower score, and the Bragg likelihood
ratio. The note quotes the cost and benefit: efficiency falls from $20.3\%$ to
$9.5\%$ and purity is largely unchanged ($51.9\to49.5\%$), while the golden fraction of
the selected signal rises from $36.3\%$ to $67.7\%$. The same logic is applied to the muon
momentum, restricting that cross section to contained muons ($8.4\%$ efficiency,
$56.1\%$ purity) so that it uses range only.

Third, and most directly comparable, the binning is chosen against an explicit criterion:
each bin must hold at least 100 predicted MC events and the diagonal element of the
migration (smearing) matrix must exceed $0.68$. The resulting pion-momentum edges are
$0.10, 0.16, 0.19, 0.22, 0.60$~GeV/$c$ with an overflow bin: fine structure only where
the estimator still tracks truth, then a single wide bin from $0.22$ to $0.60$ and
everything above $0.60$ in overflow.

The five-bin scheme fails that criterion: its diagonal fractions are
$94.3, 36.5, 23.1, 10.4$ and $6.2\%$, so one of five bins passes $0.68$
and the top three fail it by a wide margin. Its edges ($0.175$--$0.550$ in five bins, open
above) place four boundaries inside the region where the reconstructed momentum has
saturated.

\subsubsection{Consequences for the unfolding.}
The saturation does not bias the extracted cross section, because the response matrix
contains it and the unfolding inverts it, but it makes the $p_\pi$ result depend more on the
regularisation than any other observable. With $6\%$
diagonal strength in the top bin, the result above $\sim\!0.5$~GeV/$c$ is effectively
inferred from the response model rather than measured.

\subsubsection*{Adopted binning: three $p_\pi$ regions}
\label{sec:ppi2bin_adopted}

The adopted binning has three regions, $[0.175, 0.205]$ and $[0.205, 0.26]$~GeV/$c$ and
everything above. Merging raises the migration diagonals from $96/35/21/10/6\%$ (full
analysis sample; $94.3/36.5/23.1/10.4/6.2\%$ on Run~1 alone, Table~\ref{tab:ppi_sat}) to
$91/51/52\%$ in FHC and $90/53/53\%$ in RHC, which meets the $0.50$ criterion of the analysis
in every region (\S\ref{sec:binreview}). Under the $0.68$ criterion of the BNB measurements
only two regions pass, $[0.175, 0.205]$~GeV/$c$ and everything above, at $91/76\%$. These
diagonals are measured on the analysis sample (\S\ref{sec:ppi_samples}); the golden-pion study of
Sec.~\ref{sec:golden_bdt} uses the looser track sample, whose diagonals are lower, and the two
sets are not interchangeable.

The first boundary follows from a scan of the two-region binning on the analysis sample. The worst diagonal is
always that of the upper region, and it falls monotonically as the boundary rises, so the
binding constraint is the width of the first interval rather than the diagonal itself:

\subsubsection*{Golden-pion classifier}
\label{sec:golden_bdt}

Following the BNB recipe, a golden-pion BDT was trained and evaluated with the macros
\texttt{golden\_pion\_train.C} and \texttt{golden\_pion\_compare.C} of \texttt{macros/}. The truth
label is \texttt{mc\_end\_p} $<0.01$~GeV/$c$ (the pion ranged out), joined to
reconstruction by matching \texttt{backtracked\_p} to \texttt{mc\_p}, which pairs $100\%$ of
candidate tracks with no ambiguity. The inputs are the three deflection variables, the four
Bragg likelihood ratios, the track--shower score and the descendant count. Training used
$126\,518$ labelled tracks of the track sample (\S\ref{sec:ppi_samples}) from Runs 1, 2
and 4; the split is by run, and all numbers
below are on Run 2, which the classifier did not see in training.

\subsubsection{Classifier performance.} The ROC integral is $0.758$. At
$44.8\%$ efficiency the classifier raises the golden fraction from $43.1\%$ to $64.9\%$,
against the BNB $36.3\to67.7\%$ at roughly $47\%$ efficiency, so it reproduces the BNB
benchmark.

gives the migration diagonal in the five bins as the cut is tightened. The first bin
passes and continues to pass; no other bin reaches $0.68$ at any working point, even at
$15\%$ efficiency.

\subsubsection{Summary of the classifier study.} The classifier matches the published
benchmark, but the $p_\pi$ differential measurement above $\sim\!0.23$~GeV/$c$ cannot be made
to meet the BNB binning criterion with a range-based estimator, with or without a
golden-pion cut. The BNB analysis meets that criterion because its resolved bins
($0.10$--$0.16$, $0.16$--$0.19$, $0.19$--$0.22$) all lie below the $0.175$~GeV/$c$
threshold of this analysis, in the region where range still tracks momentum; above $0.22$ it
uses a single wide bin and an overflow. The options for this analysis were therefore to lower
the pion threshold toward $0.10$~GeV/$c$ and resolve the region where the estimator works (tested
and not adopted, \S\ref{sec:supp_lowppi}), to report a coarse $p_\pi$ result with an open upper
region (adopted, \S\ref{sec:ppi2bin_adopted}), or to replace the range-based estimator
(\S\ref{sec:ppi_estimators}). The golden-pion BDT can contribute to any of these but does
not by itself solve the problem.

The limitation described in this subsection concerns attempts to recover a finer multi-bin $p_\pi$
spectrum, and its numbers are for the loose truth-matched track sample. It does not apply to the
adopted three-region result on the analysis sample (\S\ref{sec:ppi2bin_adopted},
Table~\ref{tab:ppi2bin}), whose migration diagonals are $91\%$, $51\%$ and $52\%$.

\subsubsection{Scope of the $p_\pi$ result.} Two routes to a finer $p_\pi$ measurement were
tested. The golden-pion classifier does not repair the response even at perfect truth-level
selection (above), and the proton tag does not change the response appreciably
(below). With merged regions the
inclusive selection satisfies the binning criterion, so $p_\pi$ is retained in the
inclusive measurement; what it cannot support is a five-bin shape measurement.
The other inclusive observables are unaffected: the angular variables and $p_\mu$ retain
their shape information, and the total cross section does not use this estimator.

\subsection{Survey of pion momentum estimators without a magnetic field}
\label{sec:ppi_estimators}

Four approaches were measured on truth-matched pion tracks. All are limited by the same
mechanism: each measures what the pion did before it interacted.

\paragraph{Summary of the estimator survey.} Five approaches were measured
(\S\S\ref{sec:golden_details} and~\ref{sec:estimator_details}):
range, calorimetry, MCS, golden-pion tagging and a multivariate regression, together with
recovery of the interaction products. Each is limited by the same fact, that a charged pion
which interacts before stopping destroys the information a non-magnetised detector uses to
infer its momentum, and that a substantial part of the energy leaves the detector unseen.
The regression is the only one that gives a material improvement, removing the $W_{\pi p}$
bias, at the cost of model dependence and of the low-$W$ region.

One avenue has not been tried and is independent of the pion's own reconstruction: for the
proton-tagged sample, transverse kinematic imbalance constrains the pion transverse momentum
from the muon and proton alone. It is limited by Fermi motion, of order $250$~MeV/$c$, which
is comparable to the present uncertainties rather than smaller, so it would help
but would not change the character of the measurement. Without it,
$p_\pi$ is a coarse measurement in a few wide regions at this exposure and no differential
$W_{\pi p}$ result is reported, both because of physics rather than of analysis choices.


\section[Binning review against the 0.68 and 0.50 migration criteria]{Binning review against the 0.68 and 0.50 migration criteria\scopetag{shared}}
\label{sec:binreview}

The review established the rule and the binnings of \S\ref{sec:final_binning}. It starts from the
binnings produced by the first optimisation and its re-check (\S\ref{sec:binopt}), which are called
the released binnings below and in the tables.

\subsection{Criteria and selection rule}

\paragraph{Admissibility criteria.}
A true bin is admissible when most of its selected signal is reconstructed in the same bin: the
diagonal of the column-normalised migration matrix, measured on the analysis sample
(\S\ref{sec:ppi_samples}), must reach $0.68$, the value used in the BNB
CC$1\pi$ measurements, or $0.50$, the relaxed criterion adopted for this analysis. The criterion bounds how much
of a bin's content the unfolding moves between bins, and therefore how strongly the result leans on
the modelled migrations. It is applied in FHC and in RHC; the combined configuration inherits the
binning. Every true bin must also hold a minimum of selected signal events, counted in FHC at data
exposure: $70$ in the inclusive family, the content of the sparsest released inclusive bin, and $50$
in the proton-tagged family. Neither requirement says how much of the binned shape survives the
regularised unfolding, and that is the third property reviewed.

\paragraph{Retained shape: the Wiener filter factors.}
In the Wiener-SVD unfolding, $A_C$ is similar to the diagonal matrix of the Wiener filter factors
$W_k=S_k^2/(S_k^2+N_k^2)$, one for each component of the pre-whitened response. Its eigenvalues are
these factors, all between zero and one, and its trace is their sum: the number of degrees of
freedom that the data determine. A factor of at least $0.5$ marks a component measured with a
signal-to-noise ratio of at least one. For the released FHC $\cos\theta_\mu$, for example, the
factors are $0.79$, $0.79$, $0.66$, $0.35$ and $0.20$, a trace of $2.79$ in five bins. The first
optimisation and its re-check (\S\ref{sec:binning_recheck}, \noteref{Appendix}{app:altbinning}) used the conditioning
$s_{\min}/s_1$ instead. The conditioning is set by the weakest component alone, so it does not
separate a binning that loses one component from one that retains nothing beyond the normalisation,
and the threshold of about $0.1$ used there to reject finer candidates was not applied to the
released binnings, most of which fall below it: $p_\mu$ ($0.006$ in FHC, $0.0001$ in RHC),
$\theta_{\mu\pi}$ ($0.007$, $0.002$), $\theta_\mu$ ($0.06$, $0.005$) and RHC $\cos\theta_\mu$
($0.0003$). The singular values of $A_C$ are not the filter factors either: $A_C$ is not
symmetric, and its largest singular value exceeds one.

\paragraph{Candidates.}
For each observable and each number of bins up to twelve, the partition with the largest worst-bin
diagonal over FHC and RHC that meets the event floor was found by dynamic programming on a fine
grid, with the edge conventions of the released configurations
(\texttt{report/tools/maximin\_binning.py}). The tool reproduces the unfolder's diagonals of the
released binnings to $0.01$, and to $0.05$ for $W_{\rm had}$, two of whose edges fall between grid
points. The partitions relevant to either criterion, from the coarsest that
could replace the released binning to the finest passing $0.50$, and for the angles also the
partition at the released five bins, which separates the effect of the edges from that of the bin
count, were extracted in full in FHC, RHC and combined: $141$ extractions with universes, covariance,
unfolding and closure , and the proton
momentum $p_p$ added to the proton-tagged family in two to five bins: $153$
extractions in all (\texttt{data\_release/binreview\_eval\_2026-09-26.tsv}). They carry the tune and
pseudo-data predictions only. A partition
has the same diagonals in $\cos\theta_\mu$ and in $\theta_\mu$, and the $\theta_\mu$ grid is finer
at forward angles, so the eight- and ten-bin $\theta_\mu$ partitions, written in $\cos\theta_\mu$,
are $\cos\theta_\mu$ candidates as well.

\paragraph{Selection rule.}
Among the admissible binnings of an observable, the one selected has the fewest bins that reach
$90\%$ of the largest trace of $A_C$, averaged over FHC and RHC; ties go to the larger worst
diagonal. Bins beyond that point add components that the regularisation fills rather than the data.
A choice is marked rule-sensitive when $85\%$ or $95\%$, or an average that includes the combined
configuration, would select a different binning. Figure~\ref{fig:binreview_dfs} shows the trace
against the number of bins for every candidate, Tables~\ref{tab:binreview_incl}
and~\ref{tab:binreview_1p} the selected binnings, and Table~\ref{tab:binreview_edges} their edges
(\texttt{report/tools/binreview\_tables.py},
\texttt{data\_release/binreview\_choices\_2026-09-26.tsv}).

\subsection{Consequences and adopted binnings}

\paragraph{Consequences of the two criteria.}
The released binnings fall into three groups. The seven-bin $p_\mu$ and the six-bin $W_{\rm had}$
fail both criteria, and the $\theta_p$ and $\theta_{\pi p}$ study binnings fall below the event floor:
these change whichever criterion is adopted. The five-bin $\cos\theta_\mu$ and $\theta_\mu$ and the
two-bin $\delta\alpha_T$ fail $0.68$ only; rebinning the muon angles to meet it gains information,
while $\delta\alpha_T$ loses a second component whose filter factor is only $0.2$. The remaining
binnings, $p_\pi$, $\cos\theta_\pi$, $\theta_{\mu\pi}$, $\delta p_T$, $\delta\phi_T$ and $p_n$, pass
both; of these $\cos\theta_\pi$ gains $0.45$ degrees of freedom at seven bins under either criterion,
and the others are at or near their information limit.

Where the two criteria select different binnings, the relaxed one adds $0.2$ to $0.6$ degrees of
freedom per beam: $p_\mu$ (six bins against four), $p_\pi$ (three against two), $\cos\theta_\mu$
(eight against seven), $\theta_\mu$ (ten against eight), $\delta\phi_T$ (three against two) and
$\theta_{\pi p}$ (four against three); for $p_p$ (three against two) the gain is $0.15/0.12$.
$\delta\alpha_T$ and $W_{\pi p}$ gain a second bin whose component the data do not determine. A new component measured at signal-to-noise above one appears
only for $p_\pi$ in RHC, $\theta_\mu$ and $\delta\phi_T$ in the combined configuration (where
$\theta_\mu$ loses one in RHC) and $\theta_{\pi p}$ in FHC and RHC. The price is diagonals down to
$0.50$: bins in which up to half of the selected events come
from neighbouring true bins, with a correspondingly stronger dependence of the result on the
modelled migrations.

\paragraph{Choice of criterion.}
The $0.68$ criterion is that of the BNB measurements. Applied to every observable, it would end the
exemption of $p_\mu$, which rests on the regularisation absorbing migrations that the criterion
exists to limit. Where it differs from $0.50$ it forgoes at most $0.64$ degrees of freedom per beam,
and in all but five observable and beam combinations no component that the data determine at
signal-to-noise above one; its cost falls on $p_\mu$, which would drop from seven bins to four and
from three well-measured components to two. The $0.50$ criterion also ends the exemption. It keeps
six $p_\mu$ bins, with two well-measured components as well, three $p_\pi$ regions and eight
$\cos\theta_\mu$ bins, and it accepts bins in which up to half of the selected events come from
neighbouring true bins. The analysis uses the $0.50$ criterion for both families. The
stronger dependence on the modelled migrations that it admits is tested by the alternative-model
closure and the ensembles, both repeated on the adopted binnings
(\noteref{Sec.}{par:ensemble}; \noteref{Sec.}{sec:altmodel}).

\paragraph{Adopted binnings.}
The adopted binnings are those the rule selects under $0.50$ (Table~\ref{tab:binreview_edges}), with
four exceptions. $\cos\theta_\pi$ keeps its five bins, which pass both criteria ($0.74/0.76$); the
seven bins the rule selects would add $0.44/0.46$ degrees of freedom. $\theta_{\mu\pi}$ keeps its
five bins ($0.79/0.79$), which retain as much as the six selected in FHC and $0.2$ less in RHC.
$\delta\alpha_T$ keeps its split at $120^\circ$: the rule's $122.5^\circ$ raises the worst diagonal
from $0.66$ to $0.67$ and changes the trace by less than $0.02$. The proton-tagged $p_\pi$ keeps two
regions, because the three-region binning fails $0.50$ in the proton-tagged sample. $\theta_\mu$ is
not reported: a partition has the same diagonals in $\theta_\mu$ as in $\cos\theta_\mu$, and the
cosine retains up to one degree of freedom more, because the regularisation acts on the density per
unit of the variable (\S\ref{sec:thetavar}). The binnings are therefore: $p_\mu$ six bins and
$\cos\theta_\mu$ eight in both families; $p_\pi$ three regions in the inclusive family and two in the
proton-tagged one; $\cos\theta_\pi$ five and four; $\theta_{\mu\pi}$ five; $W_{\rm had}$ two regions
split at $1.18$~GeV/$c^2$; $\delta\phi_T$ three bins; $\delta p_T$, $\delta\alpha_T$ and $p_n$ two;
$\theta_p$ and $\theta_{\pi p}$ four at their maximin edges; and $p_p$ three. No differential
$W_{\pi p}$ result is reported. Table~\ref{tab:binning} and \ptref{Table}{tab:pt_binning} list the
edges.

For every replaced binning the generator predictions were computed at the new edges from the
event-level generator files, and the universes, covariances and result files were rebuilt
(\S\ref{sec:pipeline}); the ensembles, the alternative-model closure and the D'Agostini cross-check
were repeated on the adopted binnings. The control-region binnings are frozen separately
(\S\ref{sec:cr_protocol}) and do not change.

\subsection{The $W$ binnings are finer than the resolution}
\label{sec:w_binning}

In the six-bin scheme, $W_{\pi p}$ shows the least model tension of the proton-tagged
observables. The origin is the binning, not physics.

The six bin edges were chosen for equal population, without reference to the resolution.
The resolution and the bin widths, measured on selected signal in FHC Runs 1/2/4 (analysis sample), are:

\noindent The second and third $W_{\pi p}$ bins are $40$~MeV/$c^2$ wide against a resolution
of $101$--$142$~MeV/$c^2$: they are two to three times narrower than the detector can
resolve. The bias alone, $-111$~MeV/$c^2$, is larger than three of the six bin widths. The
migration diagonals are correspondingly low, $87.6/22.3/11.7/12.0/10.0/2.3\%$: only the
first bin is measured, and the top bin, at $2.3\%$, is largely unmeasured.

The five-bin $p_\pi$ scheme fails in the same way and for the same reason. $W_{\pi p}$ is
reconstructed from the pion and proton momenta, so it inherits the saturation of the
range-based pion momentum described in \S\ref{sec:ppi_bias}; its resolution cannot be
better than that of its inputs.

\noindent Raising the boundary increases the first-bin population at the expense of the
second-bin diagonal: the two diagonals trade against each other steeply, and the window in
which both exceed $0.68$ is only about $\pm0.01$~GeV/$c^2$ wide. The window is this narrow
because the resolution is comparable to the full width of the peak region.

The first bin is thin, $137$ selected signal events against a floor of $100$, so its
uncertainty is large once systematics are included; the $p_\pi$ first bin, with $469$
events, carries $83\%$ (Table~\ref{tab:ppi2bin_1p}). A boundary at $1.17$ doubles

The scheme is implemented in \texttt{configs/ccpi1p\_Wpipr\_bin\_config\_2bin.txt}, with the

(\S\ref{sec:wpipr_limits}) meets the migration criterion, but the first bin, with about

rank one for statistical rather than algebraic reasons. Widening the first bin restores its
population at the cost of the upper-bin diagonal, and no scanned edge satisfies both
requirements at once.

The apparent $W_{\pi p}$ agreement at six bins therefore does not persist in a binning the
detector can resolve. No differential $W_{\pi p}$ result is reported. The topology-level one-bin
total cross section of the proton-tagged sample remains measurable, but it is not described as a
$W_{\pi p}$ measurement. $W_\mathrm{had}$ carries the same
limitation, since it depends on the same pion momentum.




\subsection{Limits of the $W_{\pi p}$ extraction}
\label{sec:wpipr_limits}

The two-bin $W_{\pi p}$ scheme of \S\ref{sec:w_binning} was run through the full
extraction. It fails for a reason distinct from the six-bin failure, and the follow-up
studies establish what the pion momentum can and cannot deliver.

\paragraph{The two-bin extraction fails on statistics.} The response behaves as designed:
the framework returns migration diagonals of $0.802$ and $0.773$, matching the truth-level
scan, and the closure is acceptable at $\chi^2=1.17/2$ ($p=0.56$). The extraction
nonetheless carries almost no shape information, because $A_C$ collapses. The matrices

\subsection{Reconstruction of the proton-tagged observables from input quantities}
\label{sec:wtki_reco}
All six observables are built from the three candidate tracks (muon, charged pion,
proton) using only reconstructed quantities stored in the input ntuples: the track
direction cosines $\hat n$, the muon-hypothesis range and multiple-scattering momenta and
the proton-hypothesis range kinetic energy.

\emph{Candidate three-momenta.} The muon momentum magnitude is range-based when the muon
track ends inside the containment volume (\noteref{Sec.}{sec:fv_where}), and
multiple-Coulomb-scattering based otherwise.
The charged pion, being minimum-ionising, starts from the muon-hypothesis range momentum, converted to the pion hypothesis at fixed kinetic
energy in the reconstructed-bin definition exactly as in the inclusive analysis
(\noteref{Sec.}{sec:selection}); the same configuration formula is used. The proton momentum follows from its
kinetic energy under the proton hypothesis,
$p_p=\sqrt{T_p^2+2m_pT_p}$, with $T_p$ taken from the track range (\texttt{trk\_energy\_proton\_v}, an
exact function of the track length). Each
three-momentum is $\vec p_i=p_i\,\hat n_i$, and the energies are
$E_\mu=\sqrt{p_\mu^2+m_\mu^2}$, $E_\pi=\sqrt{p_\pi^2+m_\pi^2}$, $E_p=T_p+m_p$
(masses $m_\mu=105.7$, $m_\pi=139.6$, $m_p=938.3$~MeV).

\emph{Directly measured mass $W_{\pi p}$.} The invariant mass of the pion--proton
four-vector sum, using no neutrino-direction assumption:
\[
  W_{\pi p}=\sqrt{(E_\pi+E_p)^2-|\vec p_\pi+\vec p_p|^2}.
\]

\emph{Transverse kinematic imbalance.} The TKI observables treat the muon against the
visible hadronic system $\vec p_\mathrm{had}=\vec p_\pi+\vec p_p$,
$E_\mathrm{had}=E_\pi+E_p$. With the components transverse to the neutrino direction (the $\hat z$ axis of the beam frame) written
$\vec p_T^{\,\mu}$ and $\vec p_T^{\,\mathrm{had}}$,
\[
  \delta p_T=|\vec p_T^{\,\mu}+\vec p_T^{\,\mathrm{had}}|,\quad
  \delta\alpha_T=\arccos\frac{-\vec p_T^{\,\mu}\!\cdot(\vec p_T^{\,\mu}+\vec p_T^{\,\mathrm{had}})}{|\vec p_T^{\,\mu}|\,\delta p_T},\quad
  \delta\phi_T=\arccos\frac{-\vec p_T^{\,\mu}\!\cdot\vec p_T^{\,\mathrm{had}}}{|\vec p_T^{\,\mu}|\,|\vec p_T^{\,\mathrm{had}}|},
\]
both angles in degrees folded onto $[0,180]$. For a stationary nucleon with no
final-state interactions $\delta p_T\to0$; Fermi motion broadens it and FSI drive
$\delta\alpha_T\to180^\circ$.

\emph{Calorimetric mass $W_\mathrm{had}$ and struck-nucleon momentum $p_n$.} The
calorimetric available energy is $E_\mathrm{cal}=E_\mu+(E_\mathrm{had}-m_p)+E_b$ with the
argon removal energy $E_b=24.78$~MeV, giving the momentum transfer
$Q^2=-\big[(0,0,E_\mathrm{cal},E_\mathrm{cal})-p^\mu\big]^2$ and
\[
  W_\mathrm{had}=\sqrt{m_p^2+2m_p(E_\mathrm{cal}-E_\mu)-Q^2}
\]
(returned as $0$ where reconstruction fluctuations make the argument negative). The
nucleon-momentum proxy $p_n$ combines the transverse imbalance with a longitudinal
component $p_L$ from energy--momentum balance, $p_n=\sqrt{\delta p_T^2+p_L^2}$, using the
MINERvA prescription~\cite{minerva_tki} as implemented in the framework's transverse-kinematics tool (option~1):
\[
  p_L=\frac{R}{2}-\frac{M_{A'}^2+\delta p_T^2}{2R},\qquad
  R=M_A+p^{\mu}_{z}+p^{\mathrm{had}}_{z}-E_\mu-E_\mathrm{had},
\]
with the target mass $M_A=22\,m_n+18\,m_p-0.34381$~GeV (argon-40), the residual mass
$M_{A'}=M_A-m_n+E_b$ and $E_b=24.78$~MeV; $z$ is the beam axis (\noteref{Sec.}{sec:beamdir}).
The tool was written for a single-proton hadronic system: it receives the summed
three-momentum $\vec p_\pi+\vec p_p$ and energy $E_\mathrm{had}=E_\pi+E_p$ in the proton
slot and forms the hadronic kinetic energy as $E_\mathrm{had}-m_p$, which is why $m_p$
appears in $E_\mathrm{cal}$ and $W_\mathrm{had}$ above. $\delta p_T$, $\delta\alpha_T$ and
$\delta\phi_T$ depend only on the transverse momenta and are exact for the
$\mu+(\pi p)$ system; $p_L$, $p_n$ and $W_\mathrm{had}$ inherit the single-nucleon
residual-mass and removal-energy assumptions of a quasi-elastic-oriented prescription and
are therefore proxies, not the initial-state nucleon momentum. The generator
predictions apply the identical formulas to the generator final states
(Sec.~\ref{sec:acwtki}), so the comparison is like for like; a truth-level closure of
this prescription against a pion-production-specific definition, and a quantification of
the bias from the residual-mass assumption, have not been performed
(\ptref{Sec.}{sec:pt_limitations}).



\paragraph{Two exposure conventions in the processed files.}
One normalisation point is specific to the proton-tagged sample. The processed
ntuples use two POT conventions: the files behind the inclusive selection store the
total run MC POT duplicated in every file of a multi-file run, whereas the
proton-tagged files store each file's own POT. Normalising by the individual file
POT would be correct for the first set but would scale every split of a multi-file run to
the full run exposure in the second, inflating the simulated background by the split
multiplicity ($\sim\!3\times$ for RHC) and driving the background-subtracted spectrum
negative; normalising by the summed per-file POT has the mirror problem. The analysis
therefore normalises each run by the sum of the distinct per-file POT values
within that run, which reduces to the correct run total under either convention; FHC,
with one MC file per run, is unaffected by the choice.

\subsection{Additional smearing in the proton-tagged observables}
\label{sec:acwtki}

Because the Wiener--SVD additional-smearing matrix $A_C$ is not norm-preserving, each
observable's predictions are rescaled by a different factor when they are brought into the
measurement space. Table~\ref{tab:acwtki} gives that factor, measured as the ratio of the
$A_C$-smeared to the truth-level generator integral, for the proton-tagged observables.
Its dominant behaviour is set by the response and the binning; the exact factor is
prediction-dependent, and for the tested generators GENIE and GiBUU agree to a few per
cent throughout.

In the released extractions the proton-tagged observables are suppressed by $0.57$--$0.85$
(Table~\ref{tab:acwtki}): the two-region $W_\mathrm{had}$ is the least affected ($0.85$), the
two-bin TKI variables, $W_{\pi p}$, $p_p$ and the proton angles sit at $0.68$--$0.79$, and the
three-bin $\delta\phi_T$ is the most affected ($0.57$). The suppression is not driven by sharpness alone: $W_{\pi p}$ is the sharpest
distribution measured here (the ratio of its largest to smallest bin is $\sim\!36$) yet is
suppressed no more than the TKI variables, because $A_C$ removes structure that the
response cannot resolve and $W_{\pi p}$ is built from directly measured pion and
proton momenta with equal-population bins that track the $\Delta(1232)$ peak.

No observable is enhanced by $A_C$: in the released configurations every
prediction-dependent integral factor is below unity. This is an empirical property of these
extractions, not a mathematical requirement on $A_C$, which is a regularisation operator and
can suppress or enhance; an amplification would signal a mis-specified regularisation
(\S\ref{sec:thetavar}). The suppression is applied identically to data, tune and generators, so model
comparisons read off any panel are made on equal footing.

\section[Two-dimensional pairs]{Two-dimensional pairs\scopetag{shared}}
\label{sec:supp_2d}

Eight two-dimensional pairs were screened and extracted in the three configurations. A
two-dimensional binning is a flat list of analysis bins ordered slice by slice, so the extraction
uses the same machinery, and the integrated normalisation of the two-dimensional chain agrees with
the one-bin total to a median of $1.5\%$ once each is compared with its own $A_C$-smeared truth.
Two pairs are usable, both in the combined configuration only: $\theta_p\times\delta p_T$
($s_{\min}/s_1=0.611$) and $\cos\theta_\pi\times\cos\theta_\mu$ ($0.564$) (Table~\ref{tab:2dextract}).
Combining the beam modes roughly doubles their conditioning ($0.43\to0.61$ and $0.25\to0.56$), through
the larger sample and the shared flux rather than through any separation of $\nu$ and $\bar\nu$. The
pairs built on $W_{\pi p}$ are not usable, as the $39\%$ leakage between its outer bins implies.

The two usable pairs are each a \statSecondary{} in the combined configuration only:
$d^2\sigma/d\theta_p\,d\delta p_T$ on the proton-tagged sample, presented in the proton-tagged note,
and $d^2\sigma/d\cos\theta_\pi\,d\cos\theta_\mu$ on the inclusive sample, presented in
\noteref{Sec.}{sec:incl_2d}. Their FHC and RHC extractions and the other six pairs are

\section[Bin widths in the regularisation operator]{Bin widths in the regularisation operator\scopetag{shared}}
\label{sec:reg_widths}

Wiener--SVD regularises with a matrix $C$; this analysis uses the second-derivative form,
built on the density with the actual bin widths, because of what the plain tridiagonal
stencil $(1,-2,1)$ does on these binnings. That stencil is a second
derivative only on a uniform grid; these binnings are strongly non-uniform, with
width ratios up to $14{:}1$ (Table~\ref{tab:reg_widths}), so on them it is not a second
derivative at all. It is near-singular, its row sums are the $10^{-6}$ regularisation
floor, and its near-null direction is the constant vector rather than anything physical.
The Wiener filter is then free to retain that direction alone, which is a rank-1 $A_C$
that passes normalisation and discards shape.

The FHC $\theta_\mu$ extraction at its pre-review binning (\S\ref{sec:thetavar}) is the clearest
case, because its bin widths span a factor of $10.4$. Unfolded with the uniform stencil, the unfolded data, the $A_C$-smeared truth,
the MicroBooNE tune and all four generators share the same normalised shape to four
decimal places (Table~\ref{tab:thetamu_rank1}): writing the four generator predictions as
the columns of an input matrix and their $A_C$-smeared counterparts as the columns of the
output matrix, the inputs are full rank ($s_2/s_1=5.0\times10^{-2}$) while the outputs
give $s_2/s_1=2.8\times10^{-6}$. Every shape degree of freedom is annihilated, each model
is mapped onto one common curve and only its normalisation survives; any agreement with the
data is then true by construction, and a model comparison made on such an extraction is
meaningless. $\cos\theta_\mu$, the next most non-uniform binning at $14.5$, is the other
observable strongly affected; the remaining observables sit at width ratios of $3$--$6$
and are affected far less.

\section[Identity closure: unfolding the prediction with itself]{Identity closure: unfolding the prediction with itself\scopetag{shared}}
\label{sec:identity}

The pseudo-data closure tests reported in the analysis notes compare an unfolded Poisson throw against
the truth it was thrown from. They therefore test the whole chain at once, and a modest
$\chi^2$ can hide a genuine inconsistency inside the unfolding itself, because the
statistical fluctuation of the throw is the dominant term.

The identity test removes that fluctuation. Wiener--SVD returns
$\hat{x} = U r$ for a measured reco vector $r$, and defines the additional-smearing matrix
as $A_C = U R$, where $R$ is the response matrix. Feeding an exact
prediction $r = R\,t$ back through the chain must therefore return
\begin{equation}
  \hat{x} = U R\, t = A_C\, t
\end{equation}
identically, for any true vector $t$. Rather than pick one $t$, we test the matrix
identity directly:
\begin{equation}
  \bigl\| U R - A_C \bigr\|_\infty \;\overset{!}{=}\; 0 .
\end{equation}
This is stronger than a single-vector test and independent of the data. It is sensitive to
the failure mode a pseudo-data test cannot see: the unfolding matrix and the additional-smearing
matrix
being built from different responses, or from responses evaluated in different universes,
which would bias every extracted cross section by a fixed factor while leaving the closure
$\chi^2$ untouched.

The check is implemented inside \texttt{UnfolderNuMI} and runs on every extraction, printing
a \texttt{[IDENTITY]} line. All $59$ released extractions pass it: five inclusive and twelve
proton-tagged observables, each in three beam configurations, the two proton angles in three
configurations and the two combined two-dimensional results. The worst deviation over the $59$
extractions is
$6.1\times10^{-5}$ relative, on FHC $\cos\theta_\mu$; the $\cos\theta_\mu$ extractions of the two
families are the only ones above $4\times10^{-6}$. These are the
rounding errors of the singular-value decomposition and the matrix products that follow it,
not physical effects: they remain more than two orders of magnitude below the smallest systematic
uncertainty in the analysis.

The deviations are larger than the $10^{-10}$ level obtained with regularisation matrices that
are not width-aware, and the reason is arithmetic rather than physical. The width-aware
operator carries factors of $1/w_j$ that span decades across a binning with a width ratio of up
to $56{:}1$, so the matrix products accumulate more floating-point error. $\cos\theta_\mu$, whose
eight bins have the largest width ratio in the analysis, is the worst case. The identity itself
holds exactly; only the arithmetic that verifies it is noisier. The unfolding construction is therefore exactly self-consistent,
and every deviation seen in the pseudo-data closure tests is attributable to the statistical
throw and to the response model, not to the unfolding implementation.

\subsection{The three-region \texorpdfstring{$p_\pi$}{p pi} result}
Charged pions interact hadronically before ranging out, so the reconstructed momentum
saturates near $0.34$~GeV/$c$: a pion of true momentum $0.95$~GeV/$c$ reconstructs at
almost the same value as one of $0.55$~GeV/$c$. The three regions have migration diagonals
of $91\%$, $51\%$ and $52\%$ (column-normalised $M$, \noteref{Sec.}{sec:unfolding_resp}) and
meet the $0.50$ criterion, but the upper region integrates over a very wide physical interval
and is not a differential point in the usual sense. The true upper region is open-ended,
$p_\pi>0.26\GeVc$ (the true-bin definition places no upper edge on it); the $1\GeVc$ that
appears in the data release and in \noteref{Appendix}{app:binning} is only the finite boundary
adopted to assign the region a width for plotting, not a kinematic limit. The released
$d\sigma/dp_\pi$ values are therefore bin-averaged densities over very different intervals
($0.030$, $0.055$ and $0.74\GeVc$), and the panel draws the three at equal visual width,
flagged on the axis, so the plotted area does not represent cross section. The result is
stated as the three bin-integrated partial cross sections
(Table~\ref{tab:ppi_partial}); the density representation derives from them and depends on
the conventional plotting width of the open region. These are the partial cross sections,
$\sigma_i=(d\sigma/dp_\pi)_i\,\Delta p_{\pi,i}$, which do not depend on the choice of plotting
boundary for the two lower regions and depend on it for the upper one only through the
physical content above $1\GeVc$, which the open true bin already includes. For the open upper
region the released density and the conventional width of $0.74\GeVc$ form a matched
representation of the extracted bin integral: a user should compare bin-integrated predictions
after applying $A_C$, not reinterpret the density over a different finite upper boundary, since

\subsection{Interpretation of the closure \texorpdfstring{$\chi^2$}{chi-squared} values}
The closure $\chi^2$ quoted for each extraction compares the unfolded result with the
pseudo-data truth using the complete configured systematic covariance, and the resulting $p$-values
cluster near unity (median $0.98$ across all fifty-one extractions; eleven lie below $0.9$
and the lowest is $0.20$). This is
not a sign of excellent agreement with the model.  For a self-closure with
correctly-sized uncertainties the $p$-values would be roughly uniform on $[0,1]$, and
they are not.

The reason is that the measurement and the truth it is being compared against share their
systematics: both come from the same generator, the same flux and the same exposure, so
the flux, cross-section and exposure terms are common-mode and largely cancel in the
difference while still inflating the covariance used to judge it.  Recomputing the same
comparison with the statistical covariance alone raises $\chi^2/\mathrm{ndf}$ from
$0.02$--$0.2$ to $0.5$--$0.8$, much nearer the expected value. The table below was evaluated
on a different pseudo-data throw from the released one and illustrates the effect; the released
closure values are those of \noteref{Tables}{tab:fhc}--\ref{N-tab:comb}.

The closure $\chi^2$ is therefore a check that the extraction does not disagree
with its input beyond the quoted band, which it passes, and not a measure of how well the
band is sized.  A test of the latter needs either a covariance restricted to the terms that differ
between measurement and reference, or an ensemble of throws. The restricted covariance is
not used for the released closure tables, whose $p$-values should be read with that
limitation in mind; the sizing is tested instead with the ensembles of
\noteref{Sec.}{par:ensemble} and \S\ref{sec:supp_ensemble}.

Three features bear on the interpretation of the results.

The suppression is large: inclusive $\cos\theta_\mu$ has a row sum as low as $0.26$ in
RHC, and the proton-tagged $\delta\phi_T$ one of $0.19$, a strong suppression of that output mode
for a flat input.  It also differs strongly between observables, which is
why the integrated cross section obtained by summing a differential result
varies from observable to observable while the underlying physical total cannot.  The
D'Agostini cross-check, whose integral is flat across observables, establishes
that this spread is the smearing rather than a physical or acceptance effect.

One extraction has a negative row sum, and the pattern across configurations identifies its
cause. In $W_{\pi p}$ the top row carries negative off-diagonal terms in all three
configurations (summing to $-0.15$ FHC, $-0.30$ combined, $-0.61$ RHC against diagonals of
$0.73$, $0.62$ and $0.47$); only RHC tips negative overall (row sum $-0.14$), because its
diagonal element is the smallest of the three. The first $\cos\theta_\mu$ bin of the
proton-tagged RHC sample has a positive row sum with the eight adopted bins.

This is therefore not an RHC-specific defect but the regularisation pushing back against the
least-determined bin of the observable: for $W_{\pi p}$, the same top bin that is only
$2\%$ diagonal in the six-bin response and is the reason no differential $W_{\pi p}$ result is
reported.  A negative row is not forbidden, since $A_C$ is a regularisation
operator rather than a probability matrix, but a prediction smeared through one is being
told that raising the truth in that bin lowers the measured content, which is a
statement about the regularisation and not about the detector. That row should be used with
corresponding caution.


\section[Ensembles: reference construction and full-covariance pulls]{Ensembles: reference construction and full-covariance pulls\scopetag{shared}}
\label{sec:supp_ensemble}

\paragraph{Ensemble coverage and the size of the uncertainties.}
\label{par:ensemble}
The closure test above uses a single throw.  It can show that the extraction reproduces
its input, but a single residual has no width, so it cannot say whether the quoted
uncertainty is correctly sized.  To test that, one hundred statistically independent
Poisson realisations of the data of each configuration (six extractions: $p_\mu$ and
$\cos\theta_\mu$ in FHC, $\cos\theta_\mu$ in RHC, the three-region $p_\pi$ in the combined
configuration, the FHC and RHC one-bin totals; $100$ members each) were each put through the
whole chain (thrown, universes rebuilt from that throw, unfolded), and the per-bin pull
\[
  p_i \;=\; \frac{\hat{x}_i - \left(A_C\,x_{\rm CV}\right)_i}{\sigma_i}
\]
formed against the fixed central-value truth smeared by that member's own $A_C$.  (The
per-throw pseudo-data truth is not a valid reference: it follows the Poisson draw, so
numerator and reference fluctuate together and the width comes out too small.)

The throws follow the weight rule of \noteref{Eq.}{eq:safeweight}, and the extractions are
chosen to bracket the conditioning and the configurations: the well-behaved $p_\mu$;
$\cos\theta_\mu$, whose $A_C$ row sums fall as low as $0.49$ in FHC ($0.26$ in RHC) so that the
regularisation removes up to $51\%$ ($74\%$) of a bin's normalisation; the three-region $p_\pi$ of
the combined configuration; and the one-bin totals, which have no regularisation at all. The four
differential ensembles use the released binnings (members built from staged configurations
identical to the released ones). If the uncertainties were mis-sized anywhere, it would show in
$\cos\theta_\mu$; if the extraction carried a bias independent of the regularisation, it would
show in the one-bin totals.

Against the statistical covariance all six widths are consistent with unity ($0.95$--$1.07$,
each determined to about $\pm7\%$, $1/\sqrt{2\times100}$, more for the multi-bin extractions,
whose bins are correlated), and the interval coverage is correct within the same statistics. The
statistical uncertainties are therefore correctly sized, and the strongly suppressed observable
behaves no differently from the well-conditioned one. Of the two, the $\cos\theta_\mu$ result
is the more informative: $p_\mu$ was expected to pass, whereas it was not known in advance
whether the severe $A_C$ suppression in $\cos\theta_\mu$ would distort its uncertainties.

Against the full framework covariance, without the MCS term, the pull width
is $0.22$--$0.46$ for the differential results and $0.11$ for the totals, and coverage is
nearly $100\%$: the quoted
uncertainty is two to five times the throw-to-throw spread for the differential results and about
nine times for the totals, because the ensemble varies
only the data statistics while the denominator carries flux, detector and cross-section
terms that are identical in every member.  This is the same effect as the closure
$p$-values clustered near unity, measured here directly.

The ensemble means sit below the reference in all six cases, by $0.4$--$1.1\%$
($0.6$--$2.0\sigma$ on the mean; largest in significance for the RHC total, $-0.81\%$ at $2.0\sigma$,
and in size for FHC $\cos\theta_\mu$, $-1.14\%$ at $1.3\sigma$), with pull means of $-0.05$
to $-0.11$ and $-0.21$ for the RHC total (Table above; \texttt{data\_release/ensemble\_2026-09-26.tsv}). The
extractions of one configuration share the same throws and are correlated, so this is a few
measurements of a small negative offset, each consistent with zero at the present ensemble
size. The throw itself is unbiased: the mean selected count over the 32
members of an FHC ensemble built with the same throw rule is $1578.7$ against the neutrino-MC expectation of $1580.6$ (ratio $0.999$), so
whatever offset remains is in the extraction, not in the pseudo-data. The offset is at most two standard
deviations of the ensemble mean and is largest where the regularisation is strongest,
but the one-bin totals, which have no regularisation, also sit low, so the excess in
$\cos\theta_\mu$ over the common $\approx1\%$ is the only part that can be attributed
to $A_C$. No correction is applied and no uncertainty is assigned; a larger ensemble
would settle whether the common offset is real.

A truncated read of the pseudo-data occurs when two ensembles sharing a throw number write the
same throw file at the same time and a member opens it mid-write, seeing part of the tree. Ten of the $600$ members were first built from such a
read. Four RHC-total members (Run~2 read at $47$--$72\%$) and one FHC
$\cos\theta_\mu$ member ($43\%$) were affected materially; they widened the RHC-total pull width
to $1.28$. Every member was checked for this (the number of events univmake read against the entries on disk;
$2791$ of $2800$ inputs agree exactly, and the five remaining one-entry differences are one
out-of-range truth event in the one-bin binning, which does not enter the reco-level data), and
the affected members were rebuilt. Each throw is created once and never replaced
(\texttt{macros/throw\_guard.h}; change log). The four differential ensembles of the table were built
this way, and the entry-count scan of their $2000$ inputs and of the proton-tagged $W_{\rm had}$
ensemble finds no truncated read; the members short by one entry are the boundary case of an
event with $\cos\theta_\mu=1.0$ exactly (change log).

The study covers the inclusive sample in all three configurations. The two-dimensional results and
the proton angles have their own $100$-member ensembles: the inclusive
$\cos\theta_\pi\times\cos\theta_\mu$ (\noteref{Sec.}{sec:incl_2d}), and on the proton-tagged sample
$\theta_p$ and $\theta_{\pi p}$ in each configuration and $\theta_p\times\delta p_T$, with statistical
pull widths of $0.94$--$1.04$ (\texttt{data\_release/ensemble\_v1\_2026-09-28.tsv}). The pulls of a
two-dimensional result are read from its all-slice closure file, which holds every analysis bin in the
order of the covariance. The proton-tagged family, where the statistics are sparser, has in addition

\section[Consistency between configurations and with the sibling $\nu_e$ measurement]{Consistency between configurations and with the sibling $\nu_e$ measurement\scopetag{inclusive}}
\label{sec:supp_closure}

\subsection{Consistency with the sibling $\nu_e\,\mathrm{CC}\,1\pi$ measurement}
\label{sec:nueconsistency}

This analysis is one of a pair of MicroBooNE NuMI CC single-charged-pion
measurements; the companion $\nu_e\,\mathrm{CC}\,1\pi$ analysis shares the same
cross-section-extraction framework, beam and reconstruction. A cross-check against
its internal note confirms consistency on the essential conventions and identifies
the differences that a combined NuMI CC$\pi$ programme should reconcile.

\emph{Consistent by construction.} Both use the Wiener--SVD extraction framework;
the updated NuMI flux with the fixed geometry bug and Geant~v4.10.4; the same
signal topology (exactly one $\pi^\pm$, zero $\pi^0$ or heavier mesons, any number
of protons, charged current), and the same systematic suite, PPFX flux
hadronic universes (the 12 beamline-geometry unisims the $\nu_e$ analysis carries are
evaluated but not in this analysis's official covariance, \noteref{Sec.}{sec:notincov}), the
600-universe GENIE cross-section multisim with unisims, the Geant4 reinteraction
reweighting, the standard detector-variation set, and $2\%$ / $1\%$ / $100\%$
POT / target / dirt normalisation uncertainties. The FHC beam-on data POT per run
agrees exactly (Run~2 $1.268$, Run~4 $2.075\times10^{20}$).

\emph{Differences reconciled or flagged.} (i)~\textbf{POT accounting:} the
$\nu_e$ note quotes Run~1 FHC $=3.283\times10^{20}$~POT ($9\,846\,635$ triggers), the
figure that includes the open-trigger stream. The beam-on sample processed here is the
non-open-trigger dataset, $2.192\times10^{20}$~POT with $5\,748\,692$ triggers, confirmed
against the beam bookkeeping; the total FHC exposure is $7.766\times10^{20}$ over Runs~1, 2, 4
and 5. (ii)~\textbf{Run~4:} the \texttt{Run4} overlay
is identical to \texttt{Run4c}$+$\texttt{Run4d} combined (same $859{,}368$ events
and $2.834\times10^{21}$ POT); only one may be used to avoid double counting.
(iii)~\textbf{Pion threshold:} $\nu_e$ uses $\mathrm{KE}_\pi>40$~MeV
($p_\pi>0.113\GeVc$) versus our $p_\pi>0.175\GeVc$
(\noteref{Table}{tab:pithresh}). (iv)~\textbf{Opening-angle cut:} $\nu_e$ excludes
$\theta_{e\pi}>170^\circ$ (a shower-specific back-to-back misreconstruction),
whereas our muon-track topology uses $\theta_{\mu\pi}<2.6$~rad ($149^\circ$).
(v)~\textbf{Regularisation:} the $\nu_e$ analysis smooths on the first derivative,
this analysis on the second (\S\ref{sec:reg_widths}). (vi)~\textbf{Scope:}
both analyses cover Runs~1--5 in FHC and RHC; the present measurement reports FHC,
RHC and the combined configuration.

\subsection{The combined configuration and its inputs}
\label{sec:combtruth}

The integrated cross section extracted from a given configuration is not a
configuration-independent number: it depends on the additional-smearing matrix $A_C$,
which differs per configuration and per observable, so comparisons across configurations
are made observable by observable and against the pseudo-data truth rather than between
measurements. Two tests compare the combined configuration with its inputs.

\paragraph{The combined truth against the exposure-weighted average of its inputs.}
A configuration spanning both horn currents should predict a cross section between those
of its two inputs, equal to their average weighted by the fluence of each mode ($0.425$ FHC,
$0.575$ RHC). The realised truths of the one-bin totals (\noteref{Table}{tab:cutcount}) give a
ratio of the combined truth to that average of $1.000$ for the inclusive family and $1.001$ for the
proton-tagged family. Observable by observable, the $A_C$-smeared truth integrals give
$1.006$ ($0.981$--$1.030$) and $1.046$ ($0.984$--$1.187$), the spread being the configuration
dependence of $A_C$ rather than of the truth. The test is sensitive to the treatment of the
detector variations: the two per-mode detector-variation sets of the combined configuration
must be pooled as one sample scaled once to the data exposure, since scaling each set to the full
exposure and summing would double the detector covariance, over-regularise the combined
extraction and push its truth ratio to $0.91$ with a worst observable at $0.77$. With the
pooled treatment the combined detector term lies between or close to those of its inputs
(analysis note, Tables~\ref{N-tab:fhc}--\ref{N-tab:comb}: $23.7\%$ against $21.2\%$ in FHC and
$23.8\%$ in RHC for $p_\mu$).

\paragraph{The RHC deficit is in the input, not the extraction.}
The one-bin totals give an RHC/FHC ratio of the injected tune of $0.901$
(inclusive) and $0.878$ (proton-tagged), and the extracted one-bin ratios are $0.901$ and
$0.873$ (\noteref{Table}{tab:cutcount}). Normalisation is not implicated: the exposures
sum exactly ($7.766\times10^{20}+1.1082\times10^{21}=1.8848\times10^{21}$~POT) and the
per-configuration flux values reproduce the POT-weighted combination to $0.01\%$. The
deficit is therefore a property of the $\bar\nu_\mu$-dominant sample, and the number
to be compared with data at unblinding is the tune ratio.

\paragraph{External reproduction of the released curves.}
The transformation $\left(d\sigma/dx\right)_i = w_i^{-1}\sum_j (A_C)_{ij}\,p_j$
applied to the released truth-level predictions reproduces every released generator curve
to a worst relative per-bin deviation of $4\times10^{-8}$, the precision of the text files
($96$ curves). This check is part of the release
procedure because an internal closure cannot make it: the unfolded
data and the truth can both be right while the model comparison is wrong, and only an
external user's arithmetic exposes it.

\section[Control regions: definitions and roles]{Control regions: definitions and roles\scopetag{inclusive}}
\label{sec:request}
% =============================================================================

The four reconstruction-level control regions of the inclusive $\mathrm{CC}\,1\mu1\pi Xp$ selection
are CC$0\pi$, multi-$\pi$, $\pi^0$ and large muon--pion opening angle (called ``cosmic'' below for
brevity: it is cosmic-enriched, $34$--$41\%$ beam-off, not cosmic-pure); \noteref{Sec.}{sec:sidebands}
gives their definitions. Throughout, ``beam-off'', ``EXT'' and ``cosmic data'' name the same data-driven
component, the beam-off trigger sample scaled by trigger counts; ``signal contamination'' means the
inclusive signal unless the proton-tagged selection is named. Each region is anchored by the inversion
of one nominal signal requirement; requirements that become undefined once that inversion is made, or
that would sculpt the targeted control sample, are dropped as listed in Table~\ref{tab:cr_defs}. Each
region is disjoint from the signal region at reconstruction level (demonstrated event by event,
\S\ref{sec:cr_overlap}); their expected signal contaminations are $8$--$24\%$. The selections, the
run-dependent normalisation, the beam-off scaling, the displayed observables and binning, the covariance
construction, the goodness-of-fit tests and the responses to a discrepancy are fixed in
\S\ref{sec:cr_protocol} before beam-on data in the regions are examined. The data are compared with the
complete prediction (neutrino MC $+$ beam-off $+$ dirt) separately in FHC and RHC, with the combined
configuration as a cross-check. The proton-tagged control regions are discussed in
\S\ref{sec:cr_1p_scope}.

\paragraph{Role of each region.} The transfer factors of \S\ref{sec:cr_transfer} set
the physics role of each region.
\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
  \item \textbf{CC$0\pi$} is the high-statistics validation of the dominant neutrino
    background class: CC$0\pi$ events with a proton or other track taken as the pion are
    $215$ of the $560$ FHC neutrino-background events ($38\%$). The region has $72\%$
    target purity ($65\%$ of the full stack) and $0.6\%$ raw statistical precision. It
    validates the normalisation and kinematics of the underlying CC$0\pi$ population,
    not the proton-to-pion misidentification probability that transfers that
    population into the signal region: the transfer carries a $31$--$34\%$ uncertainty
    dominated by the detector variations. It is therefore a validation test rather than
    a per-cent-level constraint, and it is conditionally eligible for a fitted
    normalisation correction under \S\ref{sec:cr_protocol}. The direct test of the
    misidentification probability would be an inverted pion-PID region, which has not been
    defined.
  \item \textbf{$\pi^0$} tests the shower veto and the CC$+$NC~$\pi^0$ background. Its
    target purity is $50.1\%$ (FHC) and $51.0\%$ (RHC) of the neutrino MC, its transfer
    factor $0.02$ with a $17$--$19\%$ uncertainty set by the Monte-Carlo-statistical
    floor of the detector-variation samples. The region is diagnostic only; a fit of the
    CC$+$NC~$\pi^0$ normalisation is considered only after the pre-fit result is recorded,
    together with the fit model, the leverage of the fixed components, the transfer
    uncertainty and its floor, and the effect on the signal-region prediction.
  \item \textbf{Multi-$\pi$} is diagnostic only. Its transfer factor is $\simeq2.5$
    (the signal region holds more of the target class than the region does) and its
    target purity $47\%$; it is not used to fit or constrain the signal-region
    normalisation, but it exposes gross modelling failures and shape discrepancies.
  \item \textbf{Cosmic} validates the beam-off normalisation and shape to $\sim\!50\%$
    once the prediction uncertainties of its neutrino component are included ($17\%$
    from data statistics alone). This precision is sufficient because beam-off is $1.9\%$ of the
    selected signal-region prediction: a $50\%$ uncertainty on it is an effect of order
    $1\%$ on the selected sample. It is not eligible for a fitted correction.
\end{itemize}
About $30\%$ of the FHC neutrino background has no region of its own: NC and
$\nu_e$CC without a $\pi^0$, out-of-fiducial-volume events and CC$1\pi^\pm$ outside the
measured phase space (\S\ref{sec:cr_transfer}). These components keep their model
uncertainties unchanged; the CC$0\pi$ and $\pi^0$ distributions carry indirect
sensitivity to the first two through the shared reconstruction, and each is small
enough individually that no dedicated region is justified at this exposure.

\paragraph{Sensitivity of the test and its blind direction.} The pass/fail statistic is
a global normalisation $\chi^2$ over the eight (region, mode) totals with the full
pre-fit covariance (\S\ref{sec:cr_protocol}). It detects a discrepancy confined to one
region beyond $12\%$ of the CC$0\pi$ or $\pi^0$ total and beyond $50$--$62\%$ of the
multi-$\pi$ or cosmic total, an FHC/RHC inconsistency, and, through the
normalisation-marginalised shape tests, a shape difference. A common
normalisation shift of all regions fails it only beyond $58\%$, because the correlated
flux, cross-section and detector terms absorb it. That blind direction is acceptable
because it is the direction the signal-region measurement already carries as its flux
and cross-section uncertainty: the control regions test the background model region by
region, not the overall normalisation.

\paragraph{Scope of the pseudo-data studies.}
The control-region machinery has been exercised on Poisson-thrown central-value pseudo-data.
Those studies validate the selection logic, the per-run exposure scaling, the
central-value weighting and, in the full-stack variant of \S\ref{sec:cr_fullstack}, the
exact bookkeeping path that beam-on data will follow. They do not validate the
background model: the pseudo-data are drawn from the same simulation that supplies the
prediction, so agreement is guaranteed up to statistics. The background model is the
largest untested assumption in the analysis, and the control regions exist to test it. The
control-region comparison is made at reconstruction level, so it does not depend on the unfolding or
on a closure test with an independent generator.

\noindent The $\pi^0$ region is the furthest from a single inversion: besides the pion

sculpting (photon showers reconstructed as additional track-like primaries). The
comparison of \S\ref{sec:cr_pi0_final} finds no material change in target purity, transfer uncertainty or the frozen
leading-shower-energy shape. The no-cut definition is retained because it provides
$11$--$14\%$ more events, not because the study demonstrated a material bias from the
cut.

\paragraph{Expected yields and precision.}
\noteref{Table}{tab:sideband_yields} gives the expected composition per region and beam. The raw
statistical precision of the data samples is $0.6\%$ (CC$0\pi$), $0.8\%$ ($\pi^0$) and $6\%$ (multi-$\pi$) in
the combined configuration, and the prediction in each region carries $21$--$35\%$ flux, cross-section,
re-interaction and detector uncertainties (\S\ref{sec:cr_transfer}). The regions are blind to the signal
at this precision (\S\ref{sec:cr_protocol}, item~4).

\subsection{Region overlaps}
\label{sec:cr_overlap}
The four regions are disjoint from the signal region by construction: CC$0\pi$ and multi-$\pi$ require
$n_\pi=0$ and $n_\pi\ge2$ against the signal region's $n_\pi=1$, the $\pi^0$ region requires the shower
veto to fail and the cosmic region the opening-angle cut to fail. On the files carrying the region
flags, the number of events with both the signal-region flag and any control-region flag is exactly zero
in the simulation and in the pseudo-data of every configuration. Among themselves the regions overlap in
one place: multi-$\pi$ does not apply the shower veto, so $38\%$ (FHC) and $43\%$ (RHC) of its
simulated events also belong to the $\pi^0$ region. A simultaneous fit of the two would have to account
for that; the per-region comparisons of \S\ref{sec:cr_protocol} do not.

\subsection{Full-stack pseudo-data test}
\label{sec:cr_fullstack}
To exercise the path that beam-on data will take, the regions were compared with pseudo-data made of the
neutrino-simulation throw plus an independent Poisson throw of the beam-off and dirt expectations,
against the complete prediction. The data-to-prediction ratios are $0.99$--$1.05$ in FHC, $0.99$--$1.12$
in RHC and $0.99$--$1.08$ combined, each within its statistical uncertainty ($0.6$--$10\%$). This tests
the bookkeeping, not the physics. Figure~\ref{fig:sb_plots_fhc} shows the frozen FHC distributions
with these pseudo-data in place of the beam-on points; the RHC distributions are drawn from the same
list.

\subsection{The $\pi^0$ region with and without the final multiplicity cut}
\label{sec:cr_pi0_final}
The frozen $\pi^0$ definition drops the final multiplicity cut (Sec.~\ref{sec:request}). A reprocess of the
simulation, dirt and beam-off samples with the alternative flag, beam-on files untouched, compares the two.
The cut removes $11\%$ (FHC) and $14\%$ (RHC) of the region and every component in nearly the same
proportion, so the target purity changes by less than half a percent, the overlap with multi-$\pi$ is
unchanged and the transfer-factor uncertainties are unchanged within their precision ($T$ itself rises by
$12$--$15\%$). The leading-shower-energy shape changes by at most $0.7\%$ of unit area in any bin. The
frozen definition is kept as the larger sample. Its $11$--$14\%$ of events that fail the signal-region
multiplicity cut cannot populate the signal region, so a discrepancy confined to them would be attributed
to the target class; the stored multiplicities allow item~4(i) of the procedure to locate it.

\subsection{Frozen procedure for the comparison with beam-on data}
\label{sec:cr_protocol}
The procedure below is fixed before beam-on data in the control regions are examined.

\begin{enumerate}\itemsep2pt
  \item \textbf{What is examined.} For each region the event total and the reconstructed $p_\mu$,
    $\cos\theta_\mu$ and muon--pion opening angle, and for the $\pi^0$ region the leading shower energy,
    at the binning of the released observables where one exists and otherwise at the frozen binning
    (Fig.~\ref{fig:sb_plots_fhc}). FHC and RHC are examined separately, the combined configuration as a
    cross-check, and the totals also per run period. Data are compared with the complete prediction,
    neutrino simulation $+$ beam-off $+$ dirt.
  \item \textbf{Statistics.} For each region and beam mode, against the pre-fit prediction: (a)~the
    normalisation pull, with the summed covariance of the complete prediction, reported together with
    the pull against the statistical and detector terms alone, whose resolution ($9$--$24\%$) is set by
    the detector term; (b)~a shape $\chi^2$ of the pre-declared distributions with the normalisation
    marginalised, so that the common-mode flux and cross-section terms do not make the test powerless
    (\noteref{Sec.}{sec:chi2}); (c)~a global normalisation $\chi^2$ over the eight (region, mode) totals
    with the full pre-fit covariance, the single pass/fail statistic. On the full-stack pseudo-data the
    global $\chi^2$ is $0.66/8$, the sixteen pulls lie within $\pm0.5$ and all twenty-two shape tests
    have $p>0.47$. A discrepancy confined to one region fails the set at $95\%$ confidence beyond $12\%$
    of the CC$0\pi$ or $\pi^0$ total and beyond $50$--$62\%$ of the multi-$\pi$ or cosmic total; a common
    shift of all eight totals, which the correlated flux, cross-section and detector terms absorb, fails
    it only beyond $58\%$. The regions test the background model region by region, not the overall
    normalisation, which the measurement carries as its flux and cross-section uncertainty.
  \item \textbf{Acceptable agreement.} The set passes if the global $\chi^2$ has $p>0.05$. Under the null
    ($2\times10^5$ pseudo-experiments, Fig.~\ref{fig:cr_null_calibration}) the test fails at $p<0.05$ in
    $5.1\%$ and at $p<0.01$ in $1.1\%$ of cases; the sampling noise of the covariance is not re-thrown.
    A region with $|{\rm pull}|>2$ or shape $p<0.05$ is flagged for item~4 even when the set passes; with
    sixteen pulls and twenty-two shape tests about two false flags are expected. A single-bin excursion
    beyond $3\sigma$ with a passing total is examined for a reconstruction pathology but does not flag the
    region.
  \item \textbf{Responses.} Entered when the global test fails or a region is flagged; a flagged region
    within a passing set receives at most response (b). Regions are treated in the order CC$0\pi$,
    $\pi^0$, then the two diagnostic regions, since the CC$0\pi$ class is $15\%$ of the $\pi^0$ region.
    (i)~\emph{Localised reconstruction failure.} A run period or a single bin beyond $3\sigma$ while the
    rest passes triggers a reconstruction-quality investigation, not a model correction. The period is
    excluded only if an independent detector or processing defect is established, which removes it from
    the comparison and from the measurement and makes a new frozen release; otherwise it stays in. No cut
    or binning is changed and no single bin is removed.
    (ii)~\emph{Fitted normalisation correction (c).} Only for CC$0\pi$ and $\pi^0$ (target purity above
    $50\%$ of the neutrino simulation) and only if the region's shape test passes: a one-parameter fit of
    the target class to FHC and RHC together, applied if it improves the fit at $p<0.01$ in a
    likelihood-ratio test. The $\pi^0$ region meets the purity rule narrowly ($50.1$/$51.0\%$), with a
    class and denominator settled after the purities were known, so a $\pi^0$ correction is considered only
    after the pre-fit result is documented. The two fitted regions are disjoint. The fit rescales the
    target class in every systematic universe, so that its correlation with the signal-region prediction
    is kept; its uncertainty is the transfer-factor uncertainty or its floor, whichever is larger, added in
    quadrature to the leverage of the components held fixed (the non-target background, beam-off and signal
    contamination, with shares $0.80$, $0.23$, $0.20$ of the target in the $\pi^0$ region and $0.26$,
    $0.15$, $0.13$ in CC$0\pi$).
    (iii)~\emph{Added uncertainty (b).} Otherwise the prediction is left unchanged and the full observed
    discrepancy, as a fraction of the target class, is assigned as a normalisation uncertainty on that
    class in the signal region through its transfer factor.
    (iv)~\emph{Model revision (d).} If the post-treatment global $\chi^2$ still has $p<0.05$, or a region
    remains flagged, the background model is revised before the signal region is opened. The pass/fail
    test is always the pre-fit one.
    FHC and RHC share one background model: a class that passes in one mode and fails in the other is
    treated under (b). The signal contamination of each region is held at the tune in any fit, which keeps
    the regions blind to the signal: a $30\%$ signal excess would move the CC$0\pi$ total by $2.8\%$, inside
    the $9$--$24\%$ resolution of every pull. Multi-$\pi$ and $\pi^0$ share events and are treated jointly.
  \item \textbf{Prohibited responses.} Changing a selection cut or the binning of a released observable,
    choosing a generator or tune after inspecting the regions, bin-by-bin reweighting, and any use of the
    beam-on signal-region sample.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Validation of the background fit}
\label{sec:bkgfit_validation}

The background fit, the normalisation fit of the control-region target classes, is validated on
simulation. Sets of $300$ toys give pull means of $0.02$--$0.07$, widths of $0.89$--$1.03$ and coverage of
$67$--$75\%$. On Asimov data, injected shifts of a class are recovered exactly, a $30\%$ signal excess
moves the normalisations by $0.13\sigma$ and a detector variation by at most $0.14\sigma$. A
likelihood-ratio test of common against beam-specific normalisations detects a $+15\%$ shift of the RHC
simulation alone ($\Delta\chi^2=12.9/2$ on the totals). An adaptive Metropolis sampler reproduces the
MIGRAD/MINOS intervals within $0.02$ in the mean and $2\%$ in the width. Ensembles of $10^5$ toys show
that with the combined Neyman--Pearson data variance the pull mean of the two-dimensional fits does not
converge to zero ($+0.02$ to $+0.03$, six to ten standard errors), because that variance depends on the
fluctuating data; the offset vanishes when the prediction is the data variance, and the fit uses that
Pearson form.

\paragraph{Choice of phase space and opening-angle cut.} The

  \item Total background: $243.6$ ($\approx199.7~\nu\text{-bkg} + 40.1~\mathrm{EXT} + 3.8~\mathrm{dirt}$)
  \item True signal generated (efficiency denominator): 2577--2684 events (observable-dependent)
  \item Signal purity: 57.1\%
  \item Signal efficiency: 12.1--12.6\%

\noindent\emph{Per-run scaling.} The yields above and in \noteref{Table}{tab:cutflow} use
the per-run POT factors of \noteref{Table}{tab:pot} (each run scaled by its own
$D_{\text{run}}/\text{MC}_{\text{run}}$), and the EXT by the per-run trigger ratios
(per-run trigger ratios, \noteref{Sec.}{sec:mc}). A single per-mode factor would differ from the
per-run scheme by about $2\%$ in the FHC final-cut signal, a small and nearly uniform
re-weighting of the run mixture that moves efficiency and purity by $<0.5\%$. The per-run
scheme is used because real beam-on data require it. The pseudo-data are thrown
from the same per-run-scaled prediction, so the closure tests the bookkeeping of the
scheme, not its correctness against data.

\paragraph{Measured phase space and efficiency turn-on.}
The reference selection has almost no efficiency at low momentum and at large
opening angle. In the fine truth distributions the per-bin
efficiency is only $\sim$2--5\% for $p_\mu,p_\pi<0.15\GeVc$ (the 10/20~cm
track-length cuts), and the region above the reco $\theta_{\mu\pi}=2.6$~rad cut is
dominated by cosmics ($\sim$7\% purity). The measured phase space
$p_\mu>0.15$, $p_\pi>0.175\GeVc$, $\theta_{\mu\pi}<2.6$~rad
(\noteref{Sec.}{sec:signal}) raises and flattens the efficiency to $\sim$13--20\%
(\noteref{Fig.}{fig:efficiency}). Table~\ref{tab:phasespace} compares the selection
across the iterations, and it fixes the two opening-angle choices.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),noitemsep]
  \item The $2.6$~rad upper cut is kept. On the full exposure the events it removes are
    exactly those of the cosmic control region (\noteref{Table}{tab:sideband_yields}): in
    FHC, $5.1$ signal, $60$ neutrino background, $40$ beam-off and $1.9$ dirt, a signal
    purity of $4.7\%$. Dropping the cut would lower the signal-region purity from $58.5\%$
    to $54.8\%$, raise its beam-off content from $23$ to $64$ events and recover only
    $2.7\%$ of the generated signal ($2055\to1999$ in the table). The removed region
    serves instead as the beam-off control region.
  \item No lower opening-angle cut is used. The small-angle region has high purity
    ($\sim$58\%), so a lower cut reduces the selected signal without improving purity.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Momentum-threshold validation}
The two signal-definition momentum thresholds sit at the selection efficiency
turn-on, measured against a threshold-free true signal (the CC $\nu_\mu$
$1\mu1\pi^\pm$ topology with the momentum/angle cuts removed):

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
Threshold & below & above & turn-on \\
\midrule
$p_\mu > 0.15$~GeV/c  & $\sim$0--1.5\% & $\sim$9--12\%  & 0.145$\to$0.155 \\
$p_\pi > 0.175$~GeV/c & $\sim$0--3\%   & $\sim$12--16\% & 0.165$\to$0.185 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent Both are correctly placed: lowering them would add near-zero-efficiency
phase space, raising them would discard measurable signal. The reconstructed pion
momentum (muon-hypothesis range) is biased low and increasingly so with momentum
(mean reco$-$true $\approx0$ at threshold, $-0.15$~GeV/c at 0.40) from the
$\mu$-vs-$\pi$ mass hypothesis plus pion re-interaction; a pion-hypothesis mass
correction is applied to the $p_\pi$ reco bins.

\section[Analysis pipeline and automated guards]{Analysis pipeline and automated guards\scopetag{shared}}
\label{sec:pipeline}
% =============================================================================

Every released number is produced by the \textsc{XSecAnalyzer} chain in four stages that
are re-runnable from the raw PeLEE ntuples:
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep1pt
  \item the event processing applies the selection(s) to each PeLEE ntuple and writes the
    processed event files, for the neutrino overlays, the dirt overlay and the per-run
    beam-off samples;
  \item the universe builder constructs, per observable and configuration, the true and
    reconstructed histograms of every systematic universe from the files listed in that
    configuration;
  \item the unfolder sums the universes with the per-run exposure scaling, builds the
    covariance, unfolds, and writes the closure sidecar, the systematics dump and the
    matrix tables;
  \item the export step writes the data release (curves, $A_C$ matrices, covariance
    decompositions); the figure macros draw from the closure sidecars; the one-bin total
    uses the same chain with the single-bin configurations; the count-level tables (cut
    flow, cut-and-count check, control-region yields) are produced with the same weights
    and scalings.
\end{enumerate}

Five automated guards protect the normalisation, the currency of released artefacts,
the result tables and the blinding:
\begin{itemize}\itemsep1pt
  \item A normalisation manifest reads the authoritative configuration files and asserts
    that exposure, flux, target count and beam-off scaling agree across all
    configurations, and that every declared systematic is backed by a file.
  \item A coupling assertion in the unfolder refuses a configuration whose declared
    detector variations are not present in its file list.
  \item A staleness check refuses to ship any released artefact or result figure older
    than the extraction it depicts, and compares the integrals printed on the $p_\pi$
    figures with their sidecars.
  \item A table check parses every result table of the analysis notes and this supplement and fails if a value
    disagrees with the released results table, if a table does not sit under its own
    label, or if a row's column count differs from its header.
  \item A blinding guard treats a beam-on file without MC truth as real data and refuses it
    unless an explicit unblinding switch is set; the same detection selects the beam-off
    treatment (added to pseudo-data, subtracted from real data), so no manual switch
    exists to forget.
\end{itemize}
The release was built from the processed ntuples in a single pass with every cached
universe deleted, so no intermediate result is carried over. A clean-cache rebuild of the
same kind reproduced a previous release bin by bin to $10^{-9}$ (recorded in the change
log); that comparison has not been repeated on the current release.




\part{Rejected alternatives and development history}
The alternatives below were studied and not adopted; none is used in the results. The change log gives the
chronology.

\section[The first binning optimisation]{The first binning optimisation\scopetag{inclusive}}
\label{sec:binopt}
\label{sec:binning_recheck}

The first optimisation required bins at least as wide as the local detector resolution and holding at
least $45$ selected signal events. It gave $p_\mu$ seven bins, $p_\pi$, $\cos\theta_\mu$ and
$\cos\theta_\pi$ five and $\theta_{\mu\pi}$ seven, and lowered the total uncertainty of every observable
($p_\mu$ from $33$ to $25\%$, $\cos\theta_\pi$ from $49$ to $34\%$), chiefly through the statistical and
unfolding-amplified terms. Where $A_C$ left no resolved information, $\cos\theta_\pi$ and $\theta_{\mu\pi}$
were then coarsened to four and five bins. A re-check against the $0.68$ criterion, with pre-screened
candidates extracted in full, found that $p_\mu$, $\cos\theta_\mu$, $\theta_\mu$ and $p_\pi$ could not go
finer, that further $\theta_{\mu\pi}$ bins carried no resolved information (the smallest singular value of
$A_C$ went to zero), and that $\cos\theta_\pi$ admitted a fifth bin in the forward hemisphere, which
improved the conditioning of $A_C$ from $0.27$ to $0.46$ (FHC) and was adopted. The binning review of
\S\ref{sec:binreview}, with edges optimised at every bin count on the corrected exposure, revised these
conclusions and replaced the binnings of this section (\S\ref{sec:final_binning}).

\section[The muon angle: cos theta versus theta]{The muon angle: \texorpdfstring{$\cos\theta_\mu$}{cos(theta mu)} versus \texorpdfstring{$\theta_\mu$}{theta mu}\scopetag{inclusive}}
\label{sec:thetavar}

The muon angle is reported only as $\cos\theta_\mu$. The same partition of the angle retains up to one
degree of freedom more as $\cos\theta_\mu$ than as $\theta_\mu$, because the regulariser acts on the density
(\S\ref{sec:binreview}), so a $\theta_\mu$ result adds no information. In the five-bin binnings that preceded
the review, $A_C$ suppressed the integral of the forward-peaked $\cos\theta_\mu$ strongly (row sums
$0.17$--$0.39$ in FHC, against $0.66$--$0.91$ for $p_\mu$). The suppression follows the conditioning of the
response, not the shape of the truth: across the observables the integral factor of $A_C$ is uncorrelated
with peakedness, and the flattest generators are smeared no differently from the others. With the
width-aware operator the tune, from which the pseudo-data are thrown, fits $\theta_\mu$ best ($\chi^2=2.14$
on five bins, against $5.55$--$9.10$ for the generators); under the uniform stencil every model gave
$2.8$--$4.7$, because every model had been mapped onto the same curve (\S\ref{sec:reg_widths}).

\section[Extending the pion threshold below 0.175 GeV/c]{Extending the pion threshold below 0.175\,GeV/\textit{c}\scopetag{inclusive}}
\label{sec:supp_lowppi}

A $[0.113,0.175]\GeVc$ bin was added to the pion-momentum binning as a test, lowering the phase space to
the $\nu_e$ threshold. Its efficiency is $2.2\%$, an order of magnitude below the $15$--$17\%$ of the
other bins, as the reconstruction turn-on implies. The extraction stayed well behaved (closure
$\chi^2=1.41/6$, flux term unchanged) at the price of a large uncertainty in the new bin, and GiBUU could
not be re-binned because its generator-level event record was not retained. The threshold remains
$p_\pi>0.175$~GeV/$c$.

\section[Golden-pion classifier and other pion-momentum estimators]{Golden-pion classifier and other pion-momentum estimators\scopetag{inclusive}}
\label{sec:golden_details}
\label{sec:estimator_details}

All four inputs of the BNB golden-pion classifier are available here, the track wiggliness as
\texttt{trk\_avg\_deflection\_stdev\_v}. Golden pions are the wigglier ones, by a factor of two in the mean,
because a pion that stops scatters strongly in its last centimetres while one that interacts is cut short
while still fast; a cut at $0.001$ keeps $87\%$ of golden pions and raises the golden fraction from $40$ to
$53\%$. In the trained classifier (\S\ref{sec:golden_bdt}) the deflection variables dominate and the Bragg
ratios contribute almost nothing. Tight working points appear better than perfect truth selection because
a hard cut keeps long tracks and so cuts on the reconstructed momentum itself; they are not used. Golden
pions still reconstruct at $0.73$ of the true momentum, a momentum-dependent bias that no global rescaling
repairs.

The other estimators reach the same limit. The MCS momentum is unbiased on average in the top bin but has
an RMS of $210\%$ there, because these contained, interacting pions are $25$--$84$~cm long and MCS needs of
order a metre; as a classifier input the range--MCS difference ranks high but leaves the ROC integral
unchanged, the signature of a redundant input. Calorimetry is truncated at the interaction point like range,
with three to five times its RMS. Above $0.8$~GeV/$c$ golden and non-golden pions reconstruct identically,
since a pion that stops inside the TPC at that momentum has already lost most of its energy. A BDTG
regression removes most of the bias ($W_{\pi p}$ bias from $-0.127$ to $-0.005$~GeV/$c^2$) but recovers only
a quarter of the attainable resolution ($0.119$ against a ceiling of $0.046$) and moves migration from the
top bins into the first; trained on simulation and upstream of every observable, it is not adopted. Adding
the energy of the pion daughters recovers of order $10\%$ of the missing energy at high momentum, because
absorption on argon puts much of it into neutrons and into tracks too short to reconstruct; it raises the
best upper diagonal only from $5.5$ to $15.7\%$ and is not adopted.

\section[Cosmic-direction cuts]{Cosmic-direction cuts\scopetag{inclusive}}
\label{sec:supp_cosmiccut}

Beam-off events are enriched at backward reconstructed muon angles ($32\%$ at $\cos\theta_\mu<0$ against
$4\%$ of the signal), but a cut does not pay. The beam-off background, $23$ of the $1\,423$ selected FHC
events, is subtracted without bias, so removing all of it would reduce the statistical uncertainty of the
subtraction by about $4$ events; any cut that costs more than about $0.5\%$ of the signal is a net loss. A
$\cos\theta_\mu<0$ cut would remove about $7$ beam-off and $33$ signal events. The calorimetric direction flag
of the CC$0\pi$ analysis is bounded by the same ceiling.
