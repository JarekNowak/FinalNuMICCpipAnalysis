\begin{abstract}
This supplement contains the final configuration of the $\mathrm{CC}\pi^{\pm}$ NuMI cross-section
measurement (Part~I), the evidence for its choices (Part~II), the alternatives that were studied and
not adopted (Part~III) and an extended account of the physics background (Part~IV).

Charged pions interact hadronically before they stop, so the range-based pion momentum saturates
near $0.34$~GeV/$c$ and carries little information about the true momentum above about
$0.4$~GeV/$c$. Golden-pion tagging, multiple Coulomb scattering, calorimetry, multivariate
regression, recovery of the daughter energy and the proton-tagged selection were all studied, and
none recovers it; a truth-level golden selection performs no better than the classifier, which
places the limitation in the physics of pion interactions rather than in the estimator. The pion
momentum is therefore measured in three regions. No differential $W_{\pi p}$ result is reported. The
topology-level one-bin total cross section of the proton-tagged sample remains measurable, but it is
not described as a $W_{\pi p}$ measurement.

The binnings of both families meet a $0.50$ migration criterion, and the cross-checks of the
extraction (identity closure, the D'Agostini method, ensembles of pseudo-data experiments and the
consistency between configurations) are passed. Numbers shared with the analysis notes are checked
for consistency automatically (\texttt{report/check\_tables.py},
\texttt{xsec\_analyzer/check\_staleness.py}). The corrections that shaped the analysis, with the
values before and after, are recorded in the change log.
\end{abstract}

\section[Final binnings and the binning rule]{Final binnings and the binning rule\scopetag{shared}}
\label{sec:final_binning}

Table~\ref{tab:binning} gives the bin edges of the inclusive observables. The proton-tagged edges are
in \ptref{Table}{tab:pt_binning}, and those of the two two-dimensional results in their curve files
(\S\ref{sec:release_schema}).

\input{tables/binning}

Each one-dimensional binning is selected by the following rule, established by the binning review
(\S\ref{sec:binreview}):
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep1pt
  \item Generate candidate partitions: for every number of bins up to twelve, the maximin partition,
    which has the largest worst-bin migration diagonal over FHC and RHC, found by dynamic programming
    on a fine grid (\texttt{report/tools/maximin\_binning.py}).
  \item Require the event floor: at least $70$ selected FHC signal events per bin at data exposure in
    the inclusive family, and $50$ in the proton-tagged family.
  \item Require every diagonal of the column-normalised migration matrix to be at least $0.50$, in FHC
    and in RHC, on the analysis sample (\S\ref{sec:ppi_samples}); the combined configuration inherits
    the binning.
  \item Extract the candidates that pass in full, in the three configurations: universes, covariance,
    unfolding and closure.
  \item Compute the Wiener filter factors of each extraction and the trace of $A_C$, their sum, which
    is the number of degrees of freedom that the data determine.
  \item Select the fewest bins that reach $90\%$ of the largest trace, averaged over FHC and RHC; ties
    go to the larger worst diagonal.
  \item Check the closure and the per-bin uncertainties of the selected binning.
  \item Freeze the binning.
\end{enumerate}
Four adopted binnings differ from the one the rule selects, for the reasons given in
\S\ref{sec:binreview}: $\cos\theta_\pi$ and $\theta_{\mu\pi}$ keep five bins, $\delta\alpha_T$ keeps its
split at $120^\circ$, and the proton-tagged $p_\pi$ keeps two regions. The $0.68$ criterion of the BNB
CC$1\pi$ measurement is the precedent for the migration criterion and serves as a stricter comparison
point; \S\ref{sec:binreview} gives the consequences of both. Everything in Part~III, including the
first binning optimisation, the five-bin $p_\pi$ response and the $\theta_\mu$ parameterisation, is
historical and is not used in the submitted results.

\medskip\noindent\fbox{\parbox{0.97\linewidth}{Closure is necessary but not sufficient. A result may
close while remaining dominated by modelled migrations or by the regularisation; the migration
criterion and the Wiener filter factors are therefore imposed as independent information-content
criteria.}}
\medskip

\section[Extraction configuration and uncertainty model]{Extraction configuration and uncertainty model\scopetag{shared}}
\label{sec:supp_extraction}

\paragraph{Unfolding.} Every differential result is unfolded with the Wiener-SVD method. The
regularisation matrix is the second derivative of the cross-section density, built with the actual bin
widths: on binnings as non-uniform as these, the uniform stencil reduces $A_C$ to a matrix that passes
the normalisation and discards the shape (\S\ref{sec:reg_widths}). The additional-smearing matrix
$A_C$ of every result is released with it, and every prediction is smeared by it before it is
compared with the result. The one-bin total cross sections are extracted with the Wiener filter off,
$A_C\equiv1$ (\S\ref{sec:supp_total}). The D'Agostini iterative method is a cross-check
(\S\ref{sec:crosschecks}).

\paragraph{Normalisation.} Each run is normalised to its recorded exposure: the simulation of every run
is scaled by the ratio of its beam-on to its simulated POT, and the beam-off samples by the per-run
gate ratios (\noteref{Sec.}{sec:mc}). The flux is the summed $\nu_\mu+\bar\nu_\mu$ flux integrated
above $60$~MeV: FHC $6.81159$, RHC $6.44646$ and combined configuration
$6.59691\times10^{-10}$~cm$^{-2}$~POT$^{-1}$, each configuration normalised with its own value. The
$60$~MeV convention includes the flux below the pion-production threshold, so it lowers the
flux-averaged total relative to a threshold matched to the spectrum (\noteref{Sec.}{sec:flux_status}).
The target count is $N_\mathrm{Ar}=8.710\times10^{29}$ argon nuclei in the fiducial volume.

\paragraph{Covariance.} The official covariance of every result, \texttt{cov\_total\_plusMCS.txt}, is
the framework covariance, \texttt{cov\_total.txt}, plus the MCS muon-momentum-scale term. The framework
covariance sums the flux (PPFX universes), cross-section (GENIE multisim and unisims), hadron
re-interaction, detector-variation, POT and target-count terms and the statistical terms of the
simulation, the beam-off samples and the data, each propagated through the unfolding and released
separately (\S\ref{sec:release_schema}). The MCS term is the half-difference, taken as fully
correlated, of the results obtained when the MCS-measured muon momenta of the pseudo-data are scaled
by $\pm5\%$ on the data side and unfolded with the nominal response. It is computed outside the
framework, is zero for the results that do not use the reconstructed muon momentum, and is
included in every quoted uncertainty and every summary table, among them the systematic breakdowns of
Tables~\ref{tab:systbreak_fhc}--\ref{tab:systbreak_comb} (\noteref{Sec.}{sec:mcs}). The closure
$\chi^2$ and the biases of the robustness tests are quoted with the framework covariance, since they
test the extraction rather than the uncertainty of the data. Three terms lie outside the official
covariance and are to be included before publication (prerequisite U7): the beamline-geometry flux
term, the uncertainty of the per-run beam-off gate ratios and that of the use of the Run-1 beam-off
sample for Run~2.

\subsection{Track sample and analysis sample}
\label{sec:ppi_samples}

Migration diagonals of the pion momentum are quoted on two samples, which are not interchangeable. The
track sample is every truth-matched pion track passing loose quality requirements (purity
$>0.5$, track score $>0.5$, length $>5$~cm), from events of any topology, $126\,518$ tracks in
total; the golden-pion studies use it, and the estimator studies use truth-matched pion tracks of the
same kind. The analysis sample is the events passing the full
CC$1\mu1\pi^\pm$ selection and satisfying the signal definition, about $10\,700$ per beam
mode; the adopted binnings, the binning studies and the hadronic-mass study use it. The analysis
sample has the better response, because the selection removes the events whose pion is poorly
reconstructed.

\subsection{Pion-momentum bias and the conditioning of the $p_\pi$ response}
\label{sec:ppi_bias}

The $p_\pi$ response is the least diagonal of any observable in the analysis. The cause is
physical, and it limits what the measurement can say about the pion momentum spectrum.

\subsubsection{Mass hypothesis and hadronic truncation.}
The reconstructed pion momentum stored in the ntuple,
\texttt{candidate\_pion\_mom\_reco}, is \texttt{trk\_range\_muon\_mom\_v}: a range-based
momentum evaluated under the muon mass hypothesis and applied to a pion track. Two
distinct effects follow, and only the first is corrected.

First, the mass hypothesis biases the estimate low, because for a given range a
pion carries more momentum than a muon. The size is $\sim\!17$--$19\%$ from the CSDA
range scaling, essentially independent of momentum. This effect is corrected in the reco
bin definitions rather than in the branch: \texttt{ccpi\_ppi\_bin\_config\_opt.txt} applies
a kinetic-energy-preserving $\mu\to\pi$ mass swap, $E_\mu \to T \to E_\pi \to p_\pi$,
before binning. In the lowest true bin $\langle p^{\rm reco}/p^{\rm true}\rangle$ goes from
$0.888$ with the raw branch to $0.976$ with the correction.

Second, charged pions interact hadronically before coming to rest, and nothing corrects
for this. The reconstructed track then ends at the interaction point rather than the
stopping point, so the visible range, and any momentum inferred from it, is
truncated. The effect grows rapidly with momentum and dominates at high momentum.

\subsubsection{Saturation of the reconstructed momentum.}
Table~\ref{tab:ppi_sat} and Figure~\ref{fig:ppi_sat} show the result: as the
true momentum rises by a factor of four and a half, the reconstructed momentum barely
moves, saturating at $\approx0.34$~GeV/$c$. Above that the estimator carries almost no
information about the energy of the pion.

\subsubsection{Effect on the five-bin response matrix.}
On the analysis sample of Table~\ref{tab:ppi_sat}, migrating the five-bin scheme with the
corrected estimator gives a nearly
lower-triangular matrix: the diagonal fraction falls from $94.3\%$ in the first true bin
to $36.5$, $23.1$, $10.4$ and finally $6.2\%$ in the last. For the highest true bin
($p_\pi>0.55$~GeV/$c$) only $6.2\%$ of events are reconstructed in the corresponding reco
bin while $32.6\%$ fall into the lowest one. Every true bin above the
first therefore deposits most of its events into reco bin 1, the
``pile-up into the first bin'' of Fig.~\ref{fig:resp_ppi5}.

\subsubsection{Treatment in the BNB CC1$\pi^\pm$ analysis.}
The published BNB CC$1\pi^{\pm}$Xp analysis (internal note v0.91) uses an identical
estimator: it too takes ``the momentum of pion candidates from range''. Its treatment is
summarised here and was followed by this analysis.

That analysis does three things this one does not. First, it defines a \emph{golden pion}: one
that comes to rest and leaves a Bragg peak, as opposed to one that scattered or interacted.
Second, it trains a dedicated golden-pion BDT and applies it as an extra cut for
the pion-momentum differential cross section only, leaving the total and angular results on
the generic selection. The BDT separates golden from non-golden pions using track
``wiggliness'' (the standard deviation of the angular difference between directions of
adjacent points along the fitted track, a direct handle on scattering), the number of
reconstructed descendant particles, the track--shower score, and the Bragg likelihood
ratio. The note quotes the cost and benefit: efficiency falls from $20.3\%$ to
$9.5\%$ and purity is essentially unchanged ($51.9\to49.5\%$), while the golden fraction of
the selected signal rises from $36.3\%$ to $67.7\%$. The same logic is applied to the muon
momentum, restricting that cross section to contained muons ($8.4\%$ efficiency,
$56.1\%$ purity) so that it uses range only.

Third, and most directly comparable, the binning is chosen against an explicit criterion:
each bin must hold at least 100 predicted MC events and the diagonal element of the
migration (smearing) matrix must exceed $0.68$. The resulting pion-momentum edges are
$0.10, 0.16, 0.19, 0.22, 0.60$~GeV/$c$ with an overflow bin: fine structure only where
the estimator still tracks truth, then a single wide bin from $0.22$ to $0.60$ and
everything above $0.60$ in overflow.

The five-bin scheme fails that criterion: its diagonal fractions are
$94.3, 36.5, 23.1, 10.4$ and $6.2\%$, so one of five bins passes $0.68$
and the top three fail it by a wide margin. Its edges ($0.175$--$0.550$ in five bins, open
above) place four boundaries inside the region where the reconstructed momentum has
saturated.

\subsubsection{Consequences for the unfolding.}
The saturation does not bias the extracted cross section, because the response matrix
contains it and the unfolding inverts it, but it makes the $p_\pi$ result depend more on the
regularisation than any other observable. With $6\%$
diagonal strength in the top bin, the result above $\sim\!0.5$~GeV/$c$ is effectively
inferred from the response model rather than measured.

\subsubsection*{Adopted binning: three $p_\pi$ regions}
\label{sec:ppi2bin_adopted}

The adopted binning has three regions, $[0.175, 0.205]$ and $[0.205, 0.26]$~GeV/$c$ and
everything above. Merging raises the migration diagonals from $96/35/21/10/6\%$ (full
analysis sample; $94.3/36.5/23.1/10.4/6.2\%$ on Run~1 alone, Table~\ref{tab:ppi_sat}) to
$91/51/52\%$ in FHC and $90/53/53\%$ in RHC, which meets the $0.50$ criterion of the analysis
in every region (\S\ref{sec:binreview}). Under the $0.68$ criterion of the BNB measurements
only two regions pass, $[0.175, 0.205]$~GeV/$c$ and everything above, at $91/76\%$. These
diagonals are measured on the analysis sample (\S\ref{sec:ppi_samples}); the golden-pion study of
Sec.~\ref{sec:golden_bdt} uses the looser track sample, whose diagonals are lower, and the two
sets are not interchangeable.

The first boundary follows from a scan of the two-region binning on the analysis sample. The worst diagonal is
always that of the upper region, and it falls monotonically as the boundary rises, so the
binding constraint is the width of the first interval rather than the diagonal itself:

\noindent Both regions meet $0.68$ anywhere between roughly $0.19$ and $0.22$, so the choice
within that window is set by width rather than by the criterion. $0.205$ is the narrowest
first interval with precedent: $30$~MeV/$c$ matches the narrowest bin used in the BNB CC$1\pi$
analysis. Going narrower trades first-bin diagonal for upper-bin diagonal, and at $10$~MeV/$c$
the first bin itself falls to $80\%$ on only $325$ events. An exhaustive scan over all edge
sets (\texttt{macros/ppi\_binning\_scan.C}) returns $0.205$ as the best first boundary for
every number of bins tried, and in the proton-tagged sample as well.

The second boundary costs $25$ points on the worst diagonal. The best three-region binning,
with edges $0.205$ and $0.26$~GeV/$c$ from the maximin scan of \S\ref{sec:binreview}, reaches
$91/51/52\%$, against $91/76\%$ for two regions: it meets $0.50$ but not $0.68$. Two
$30$~MeV/$c$ intervals followed by an open region ($0.205$, $0.235$) are worse at $91/39/62\%$,
and degrade the upper region too, from $76\%$ to $62\%$: narrowing its reconstructed range
pushes events of high true momentum out of their own bin. Four regions reach only
$91/39/39/42\%$. Above $\sim\!0.25$~GeV/$c$ the reconstructed momentum barely varies, so a
further boundary there separates almost nothing. The same ordering holds under a golden-pion
cut ($67/52/46\%$), so it is not an artefact of the selection.

\subsubsection{Closure.} The three-region scheme is carried through the full chain
(universe construction, Wiener--SVD unfolding and systematics) and closes in all three
configurations (Table~\ref{tab:ppi2bin}). A five-bin extraction on the same sample also
closes, at $\chi^2\approx2/5$: closure cannot expose a response that carries no shape

\subsubsection*{Golden-pion classifier}
\label{sec:golden_bdt}

Following the BNB recipe, a golden-pion BDT was trained and evaluated with the macros
\texttt{golden\_pion\_train.C} and \texttt{golden\_pion\_compare.C} of \texttt{macros/}. The truth
label is \texttt{mc\_end\_p} $<0.01$~GeV/$c$ (the pion ranged out), joined to
reconstruction by matching \texttt{backtracked\_p} to \texttt{mc\_p}, which pairs $100\%$ of
candidate tracks with no ambiguity. The inputs are the three deflection variables, the four
Bragg likelihood ratios, the track--shower score and the descendant count. Training used
$126\,518$ labelled tracks of the track sample (\S\ref{sec:ppi_samples}) from Runs 1, 2
and 4; the split is by run, and all numbers
below are on Run 2, which the classifier did not see in training.

\subsubsection{Classifier performance.} The ROC integral is $0.758$. At
$44.8\%$ efficiency the classifier raises the golden fraction from $43.1\%$ to $64.9\%$,
against the BNB $36.3\to67.7\%$ at roughly $47\%$ efficiency, so it reproduces the BNB
benchmark.

the pion threshold toward $0.10$~GeV/$c$ and resolve the region where the estimator works (tested
and not adopted, \S\ref{sec:supp_lowppi}), to report a coarse $p_\pi$ result with an open upper
region (adopted, \S\ref{sec:ppi2bin_adopted}), or to replace the range-based estimator
(\S\ref{sec:ppi_estimators}). The golden-pion BDT can contribute to any of these but does
not by itself solve the problem.

\paragraph{Summary of the estimator survey.} Five approaches were measured
(\S\S\ref{sec:golden_details} and~\ref{sec:estimator_details}):
range, calorimetry, MCS, golden-pion tagging and a multivariate regression, together with
recovery of the interaction products. Each is limited by the same fact, that a charged pion
which interacts before stopping destroys the information a non-magnetised detector uses to
infer its momentum, and that a substantial part of the energy leaves the detector unseen.
The regression is the only one that gives a material improvement, removing the $W_{\pi p}$
bias, at the cost of model dependence and of the low-$W$ region.

One avenue has not been tried and is independent of the pion's own reconstruction: for the
proton-tagged sample, transverse kinematic imbalance constrains the pion transverse momentum
from the muon and proton alone. It is limited by Fermi motion, of order $250$~MeV/$c$, which
is comparable to the present uncertainties rather than smaller, so it would help
but would not change the character of the measurement. Without it,
$p_\pi$ is a coarse measurement in a few wide regions at this exposure and no differential
$W_{\pi p}$ result is reported, both because of physics rather than of analysis choices.


\section[Binning review against the 0.68 and 0.50 migration criteria]{Binning review against the 0.68 and 0.50 migration criteria\scopetag{shared}}
\label{sec:binreview}

The review established the rule and the binnings of \S\ref{sec:final_binning}. It starts from the
binnings produced by the first optimisation and its re-check (\S\ref{sec:binopt}), which are called
the released binnings below and in the tables.

\subsection{Criteria and selection rule}

\paragraph{Admissibility criteria.}
A true bin is admissible when most of its selected signal is reconstructed in the same bin: the
diagonal of the column-normalised migration matrix, measured on the analysis sample
(\S\ref{sec:ppi_samples}), must reach $0.68$, the value used in the BNB
CC$1\pi$ measurements, or $0.50$, the relaxed criterion adopted for this analysis. The criterion bounds how much
of a bin's content the unfolding moves between bins, and therefore how strongly the result leans on
the modelled migrations. It is applied in FHC and in RHC; the combined configuration inherits the
binning. Every true bin must also hold a minimum of selected signal events, counted in FHC at data
exposure: $70$ in the inclusive family, the content of the sparsest released inclusive bin, and $50$
in the proton-tagged family. Neither requirement says how much of the binned shape survives the
regularised unfolding, and that is the third property reviewed.

\paragraph{Retained shape: the Wiener filter factors.}
In the Wiener-SVD unfolding, $A_C$ is similar to the diagonal matrix of the Wiener filter factors
$W_k=S_k^2/(S_k^2+N_k^2)$, one for each component of the pre-whitened response. Its eigenvalues are
these factors, all between zero and one, and its trace is their sum: the number of degrees of
freedom that the data determine. A factor of at least $0.5$ marks a component measured with a
signal-to-noise ratio of at least one. For the released FHC $\cos\theta_\mu$, for example, the
factors are $0.79$, $0.79$, $0.66$, $0.35$ and $0.20$, a trace of $2.79$ in five bins. The first
optimisation and its re-check (\S\ref{sec:binning_recheck}, \noteref{Appendix}{app:altbinning}) used the conditioning
$s_{\min}/s_1$ instead. The conditioning is set by the weakest component alone, so it does not
separate a binning that loses one component from one that retains nothing beyond the normalisation,
and the threshold of about $0.1$ used there to reject finer candidates was not applied to the
released binnings, most of which fall below it: $p_\mu$ ($0.006$ in FHC, $0.0001$ in RHC),
$\theta_{\mu\pi}$ ($0.007$, $0.002$), $\theta_\mu$ ($0.06$, $0.005$) and RHC $\cos\theta_\mu$
($0.0003$). The singular values of $A_C$ are not the filter factors either: $A_C$ is not
symmetric, and its largest singular value exceeds one.

\paragraph{Candidates.}
For each observable and each number of bins up to twelve, the partition with the largest worst-bin
diagonal over FHC and RHC that meets the event floor was found by dynamic programming on a fine
grid, with the edge conventions of the released configurations
(\texttt{report/tools/maximin\_binning.py}). The tool reproduces the unfolder's diagonals of the
released binnings to $0.01$, and to $0.05$ for $W_{\rm had}$, two of whose edges fall between grid
points. The partitions relevant to either criterion, from the coarsest that
could replace the released binning to the finest passing $0.50$, and for the angles also the
partition at the released five bins, which separates the effect of the edges from that of the bin
count, were extracted in full in FHC, RHC and combined: $141$ extractions with universes, covariance,
unfolding and closure (SLURM 3428213, 3428222, 3428223, 3433027 and 3433033), and the proton
momentum $p_p$ added to the proton-tagged family in two to five bins (SLURM 3433088): $153$
extractions in all (\texttt{data\_release/binreview\_eval\_2026-09-26.tsv}). They carry the tune and
pseudo-data predictions only. A partition
has the same diagonals in $\cos\theta_\mu$ and in $\theta_\mu$, and the $\theta_\mu$ grid is finer
at forward angles, so the eight- and ten-bin $\theta_\mu$ partitions, written in $\cos\theta_\mu$,
are $\cos\theta_\mu$ candidates as well.

\paragraph{Selection rule.}
Among the admissible binnings of an observable, the one selected has the fewest bins that reach
$90\%$ of the largest trace of $A_C$, averaged over FHC and RHC; ties go to the larger worst
diagonal. Bins beyond that point add components that the regularisation fills rather than the data.
A choice is marked rule-sensitive when $85\%$ or $95\%$, or an average that includes the combined
configuration, would select a different binning. Figure~\ref{fig:binreview_dfs} shows the trace
against the number of bins for every candidate, Tables~\ref{tab:binreview_incl}
and~\ref{tab:binreview_1p} the selected binnings, and Table~\ref{tab:binreview_edges} their edges
(\texttt{report/tools/binreview\_tables.py},
\texttt{data\_release/binreview\_choices\_2026-09-26.tsv}).

\subsection{Consequences and adopted binnings}

\paragraph{Consequences of the two criteria.}
The released binnings fall into three groups. The seven-bin $p_\mu$ and the six-bin $W_{\rm had}$
fail both criteria, and the $\theta_p$ and $\theta_{\pi p}$ study binnings fall below the event floor:
these change whichever criterion is adopted. The five-bin $\cos\theta_\mu$ and $\theta_\mu$ and the
two-bin $\delta\alpha_T$ fail $0.68$ only; rebinning the muon angles to meet it gains information,
while $\delta\alpha_T$ loses a second component whose filter factor is only $0.2$. The remaining
binnings, $p_\pi$, $\cos\theta_\pi$, $\theta_{\mu\pi}$, $\delta p_T$, $\delta\phi_T$ and $p_n$, pass
both; of these $\cos\theta_\pi$ gains $0.45$ degrees of freedom at seven bins under either criterion,
and the others are at or near their information limit.

Where the two criteria select different binnings, the relaxed one adds $0.2$ to $0.6$ degrees of
freedom per beam: $p_\mu$ (six bins against four), $p_\pi$ (three against two), $\cos\theta_\mu$
(eight against seven), $\theta_\mu$ (ten against eight), $\delta\phi_T$ (three against two) and
$\theta_{\pi p}$ (four against three); for $p_p$ (three against two) the gain is $0.15/0.12$.
$\delta\alpha_T$ and $W_{\pi p}$ gain a second bin whose component the data do not determine. A new component measured at signal-to-noise above one appears
only for $p_\pi$ in RHC, $\theta_\mu$ and $\delta\phi_T$ in the combined configuration (where
$\theta_\mu$ loses one in RHC) and $\theta_{\pi p}$ in FHC and RHC. The price is diagonals down to
$0.50$: bins in which up to half of the selected events come
from neighbouring true bins, with a correspondingly stronger dependence of the result on the
modelled migrations.

\paragraph{Choice of criterion.}
The $0.68$ criterion is that of the BNB measurements. Applied to every observable, it would end the
exemption of $p_\mu$, which rests on the regularisation absorbing migrations that the criterion
exists to limit. Where it differs from $0.50$ it forgoes at most $0.64$ degrees of freedom per beam,
and in all but five observable and beam combinations no component that the data determine at
signal-to-noise above one; its cost falls on $p_\mu$, which would drop from seven bins to four and
from three well-measured components to two. The $0.50$ criterion also ends the exemption. It keeps
six $p_\mu$ bins, with two well-measured components as well, three $p_\pi$ regions and eight
$\cos\theta_\mu$ bins, and it accepts bins in which up to half of the selected events come from
neighbouring true bins. The analysis uses the $0.50$ criterion for both families. The
stronger dependence on the modelled migrations that it admits is tested by the alternative-model
closure and the ensembles, both repeated on the adopted binnings
(\noteref{Sec.}{par:ensemble}; \noteref{Sec.}{sec:altmodel}).

\paragraph{Adopted binnings.}
The adopted binnings are those the rule selects under $0.50$ (Table~\ref{tab:binreview_edges}), with
four exceptions. $\cos\theta_\pi$ keeps its five bins, which pass both criteria ($0.74/0.76$); the
seven bins the rule selects would add $0.44/0.46$ degrees of freedom. $\theta_{\mu\pi}$ keeps its
five bins ($0.79/0.79$), which retain as much as the six selected in FHC and $0.2$ less in RHC.
$\delta\alpha_T$ keeps its split at $120^\circ$: the rule's $122.5^\circ$ raises the worst diagonal
from $0.66$ to $0.67$ and changes the trace by less than $0.02$. The proton-tagged $p_\pi$ keeps two
regions, because the three-region binning fails $0.50$ in the proton-tagged sample. $\theta_\mu$ is
not reported: a partition has the same diagonals in $\theta_\mu$ as in $\cos\theta_\mu$, and the
cosine retains up to one degree of freedom more, because the regularisation acts on the density per
unit of the variable (\S\ref{sec:thetavar}). The binnings are therefore: $p_\mu$ six bins and
$\cos\theta_\mu$ eight in both families; $p_\pi$ three regions in the inclusive family and two in the
proton-tagged one; $\cos\theta_\pi$ five and four; $\theta_{\mu\pi}$ five; $W_{\rm had}$ two regions
split at $1.18$~GeV/$c^2$; $\delta\phi_T$ three bins; $\delta p_T$, $\delta\alpha_T$ and $p_n$ two;
$\theta_p$ and $\theta_{\pi p}$ four at their maximin edges; and $p_p$ three. No differential
$W_{\pi p}$ result is reported. Table~\ref{tab:binning} and \ptref{Table}{tab:pt_binning} list the
edges.

For every replaced binning the generator predictions were computed at the new edges from the
event-level generator files, and the universes, covariances and result files were rebuilt
(\S\ref{sec:pipeline}); the ensembles, the alternative-model closure and the D'Agostini cross-check
were repeated on the adopted binnings. The control-region binnings are frozen separately
(\S\ref{sec:cr_protocol}) and do not change.

\subsection{The $W$ binnings are finer than the resolution}
\label{sec:w_binning}

In the six-bin scheme, $W_{\pi p}$ shows the least model tension of the proton-tagged
observables. The origin is the binning, not physics.

The six bin edges were chosen for equal population, without reference to the resolution.
The resolution and the bin widths, measured on selected signal in FHC Runs 1/2/4 (analysis sample), are:

\noindent The second and third $W_{\pi p}$ bins are $40$~MeV/$c^2$ wide against a resolution
of $101$--$142$~MeV/$c^2$: they are two to three times narrower than the detector can
resolve. The bias alone, $-111$~MeV/$c^2$, is larger than three of the six bin widths. The
migration diagonals are correspondingly low, $87.6/22.3/11.7/12.0/10.0/2.3\%$: only the
first bin is measured, and the top bin, at $2.3\%$, is essentially unmeasured.

The five-bin $p_\pi$ scheme fails in the same way and for the same reason. $W_{\pi p}$ is
reconstructed from the pion and proton momenta, so it inherits the saturation of the
range-based pion momentum described in \S\ref{sec:ppi_bias}; its resolution cannot be
better than that of its inputs.

The first bin is thin, $137$ selected signal events against a floor of $100$, so its
uncertainty is large once systematics are included; the $p_\pi$ first bin, with $469$

total cross section of the proton-tagged sample remains measurable, but it is not described as a
$W_{\pi p}$ measurement. $W_\mathrm{had}$ carries the same
limitation, since it depends on the same pion momentum.




\subsection{Limits of the $W_{\pi p}$ extraction}
\label{sec:wpipr_limits}

The two-bin $W_{\pi p}$ scheme of \S\ref{sec:w_binning} was run through the full
extraction. It fails for a reason distinct from the six-bin failure, and the follow-up
studies establish what the pion momentum can and cannot deliver.

\paragraph{The two-bin extraction fails on statistics.} The response behaves as designed:
the framework returns migration diagonals of $0.802$ and $0.773$, matching the truth-level
scan, and the closure is acceptable at $\chi^2=1.17/2$ ($p=0.56$). The extraction
nonetheless carries almost no shape information, because $A_C$ collapses. The matrices
below come from the extraction pass in which the two-bin $W_{\pi p}$ scheme was run and are
written with true bins as rows; the two $p_\pi$ rows are the same-pass comparison, and the
released inclusive $p_\pi$ matrix is that of Fig.~\ref{fig:ppi2bin_AC}.
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\toprule
two-bin scheme & $A_C$ & $s_2/s_1$ \\\midrule
$W_{\pi p}$, first bin $N=137$      & $\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix} 0.018 & -0.410 \\ 0.034 & 0.892\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$ & $\mathbf{0.031}$ \\
$p_\pi$ proton-tagged, $N=469$      & $\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix} 0.498 & -0.089 \\ 0.013 & 0.854\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$ & $0.577$ \\
$p_\pi$ inclusive, $N=1056$         & $\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix} 0.594 & -0.382 \\ 0.011 & 0.891\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$ & $0.528$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent The top-left entry is $0.018$: $A_C$ passes under two per cent of the first bin and
takes $-0.41$ from the second. This is a rank-one collapse of the kind described in
\S\ref{sec:thetavar}, but its cause is statistical rather than a defective regulariser. With
$137$ events the first bin has such poor signal-to-noise that the Wiener filter suppresses
that mode almost entirely, and the ordering across the three schemes, $137\to0.031$,
$469\to0.577$, $1056\to0.528$, follows population rather than anything geometric.

$W_{\pi p}$ is therefore subject to two incompatible requirements. Bins narrow enough to
satisfy the response criterion leave the first bin too sparse to unfold; bins wide enough
for a stable unfolding ($1.19$ gives $468$ events, matching the working $p_\pi$ case) fail
the criterion at $56\%$.

\paragraph{No two- or three-bin scheme satisfies both requirements.} A scan of every two-
and three-bin scheme with edges between $1.12$ and $1.70$~GeV/$c^2$ (FHC Run1$+$2$+$4,
$3505$ selected signal events) finds none that keeps both a migration diagonal above $0.68$
and adequate statistics in every bin:

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
two-bin edge & $N_1 / N_2$ & diag$_1$ / diag$_2$ & \\\midrule
$1.15$ & $137 / 3367$  & $0.832 / 0.779$ & first bin too sparse \\
$1.18$ & $351 / 3153$  & $0.866 / 0.611$ & \\
$1.24$ & $1298 / 2206$ & $0.930 / 0.347$ & \\
$1.27$ (median) & $1744 / 1760$ & $0.951 / \mathbf{0.261}$ & upper bin unusable \\
$1.45$ & $2966 / 538$  & $0.993 / 0.030$ & \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent The trend follows from the reconstruction bias. Reconstructed $W_{\pi p}$ is biased about
$111$~MeV low, inheriting the saturation of the range-based pion momentum
(\S\ref{sec:ppi_bias}), so raising the edge far enough to balance the statistics drains the
upper bin by downward migration: diag$_1$ climbs from $0.786$ to $0.999$ across the scan
while diag$_2$ collapses from $0.927$ to $0.004$. There is no edge at which both bins are
simultaneously populated and diagonal, and no three-bin combination does better: the
third-bin diagonal never exceeds $0.347$.

At this exposure $W_{\pi p}$ does not support a shape measurement unless the pion momentum
improves. No differential $W_{\pi p}$ result is reported. The topology-level one-bin total cross
section of the proton-tagged sample remains measurable, but it is not described as a $W_{\pi p}$
measurement. It is a one-bin extraction rather than a regularised unfolding: with a single bin there
is nothing to unfold, since the response is $1\times1$, $A_C$ is trivially unity and the
regularisation has no effect. It is reported through the total flux-averaged cross-section
path of \noteref{Sec.}{sec:total_xsec}. Generator predictions integrated over the $W_{\pi p}$ range
are built by \texttt{generator\_predictions/newg4/make\_wpipr\_1bin.C}, summing the per-bin
cross sections of the six-bin flux-averaged predictions.

\paragraph{Six-bin $W_{\pi p}$ closure, diagnostic only.} The six-bin extraction was
carried through the full chain in all three configurations, detector variations included.
It closes against the $A_C$-smeared truth, but that closure cannot restore shape
information the response does not contain (the top bin is $2\%$ diagonal), so these numbers
are a check of the machinery and not a result, and they do not appear in the result tables of the
proton-tagged note. The six-bin generator predictions (GENIE $0.501$, NuWro $0.646$,
GiBUU $0.735$, NEUT $0.783\times10^{-38}$~cm$^2$/Ar, FHC) are in the release for the same
diagnostic purpose.

\subsection{Reconstruction of the proton-tagged observables from input quantities}
\label{sec:wtki_reco}
All six observables are built from the three candidate tracks (muon, charged pion,
proton) using only reconstructed quantities stored in the input ntuples: the track
direction cosines $\hat n$, the muon-hypothesis range and multiple-scattering momenta and
the proton-hypothesis range kinetic energy.

\emph{Candidate three-momenta.} The muon momentum magnitude is range-based when the muon
track ends inside the containment volume (\noteref{Sec.}{sec:fv_where}), and
multiple-Coulomb-scattering based otherwise.
The charged pion, being minimum-ionising, starts from the muon-hypothesis range momentum, converted to the pion hypothesis at fixed kinetic
energy in the reconstructed-bin definition exactly as in the inclusive analysis
(\noteref{Sec.}{sec:selection}); the same configuration formula is used. The proton momentum follows from its
kinetic energy under the proton hypothesis,
$p_p=\sqrt{T_p^2+2m_pT_p}$, with $T_p$ taken from the track range (\texttt{trk\_energy\_proton\_v}, an
exact function of the track length). Each
three-momentum is $\vec p_i=p_i\,\hat n_i$, and the energies are
$E_\mu=\sqrt{p_\mu^2+m_\mu^2}$, $E_\pi=\sqrt{p_\pi^2+m_\pi^2}$, $E_p=T_p+m_p$
(masses $m_\mu=105.7$, $m_\pi=139.6$, $m_p=938.3$~MeV).

\emph{Directly measured mass $W_{\pi p}$.} The invariant mass of the pion--proton
four-vector sum, using no neutrino-direction assumption:
\[
  W_{\pi p}=\sqrt{(E_\pi+E_p)^2-|\vec p_\pi+\vec p_p|^2}.
\]

\emph{Transverse kinematic imbalance.} The TKI observables treat the muon against the
visible hadronic system $\vec p_\mathrm{had}=\vec p_\pi+\vec p_p$,
$E_\mathrm{had}=E_\pi+E_p$. With the components transverse to the neutrino direction (the $\hat z$ axis of the beam frame) written
$\vec p_T^{\,\mu}$ and $\vec p_T^{\,\mathrm{had}}$,
\[
  \delta p_T=|\vec p_T^{\,\mu}+\vec p_T^{\,\mathrm{had}}|,\quad
  \delta\alpha_T=\arccos\frac{-\vec p_T^{\,\mu}\!\cdot(\vec p_T^{\,\mu}+\vec p_T^{\,\mathrm{had}})}{|\vec p_T^{\,\mu}|\,\delta p_T},\quad
  \delta\phi_T=\arccos\frac{-\vec p_T^{\,\mu}\!\cdot\vec p_T^{\,\mathrm{had}}}{|\vec p_T^{\,\mu}|\,|\vec p_T^{\,\mathrm{had}}|},
\]
both angles in degrees folded onto $[0,180]$. For a stationary nucleon with no
final-state interactions $\delta p_T\to0$; Fermi motion broadens it and FSI drive
$\delta\alpha_T\to180^\circ$.

\emph{Calorimetric mass $W_\mathrm{had}$ and struck-nucleon momentum $p_n$.} The
calorimetric available energy is $E_\mathrm{cal}=E_\mu+(E_\mathrm{had}-m_p)+E_b$ with the
argon removal energy $E_b=24.78$~MeV, giving the momentum transfer
$Q^2=-\big[(0,0,E_\mathrm{cal},E_\mathrm{cal})-p^\mu\big]^2$ and
\[
  W_\mathrm{had}=\sqrt{m_p^2+2m_p(E_\mathrm{cal}-E_\mu)-Q^2}
\]
(returned as $0$ where reconstruction fluctuations make the argument negative). The
nucleon-momentum proxy $p_n$ combines the transverse imbalance with a longitudinal
component $p_L$ from energy--momentum balance, $p_n=\sqrt{\delta p_T^2+p_L^2}$, using the
MINERvA prescription~\cite{minerva_tki} as implemented in the framework's transverse-kinematics tool (option~1):
\[
  p_L=\frac{R}{2}-\frac{M_{A'}^2+\delta p_T^2}{2R},\qquad
  R=M_A+p^{\mu}_{z}+p^{\mathrm{had}}_{z}-E_\mu-E_\mathrm{had},
\]
with the target mass $M_A=22\,m_n+18\,m_p-0.34381$~GeV (argon-40), the residual mass
$M_{A'}=M_A-m_n+E_b$ and $E_b=24.78$~MeV; $z$ is the beam axis (\noteref{Sec.}{sec:beamdir}).
The tool was written for a single-proton hadronic system: it receives the summed
three-momentum $\vec p_\pi+\vec p_p$ and energy $E_\mathrm{had}=E_\pi+E_p$ in the proton
slot and forms the hadronic kinetic energy as $E_\mathrm{had}-m_p$, which is why $m_p$
appears in $E_\mathrm{cal}$ and $W_\mathrm{had}$ above. $\delta p_T$, $\delta\alpha_T$ and
$\delta\phi_T$ depend only on the transverse momenta and are exact for the
$\mu+(\pi p)$ system; $p_L$, $p_n$ and $W_\mathrm{had}$ inherit the single-nucleon
residual-mass and removal-energy assumptions of a quasi-elastic-oriented prescription and
are therefore proxies, not the initial-state nucleon momentum. The generator
predictions apply the identical formulas to the generator final states
(Sec.~\ref{sec:acwtki}), so the comparison is like for like; a truth-level closure of
this prescription against a pion-production-specific definition, and a quantification of
the bias from the residual-mass assumption, have not been performed (prerequisite P4) and are listed in
Sec.~\ref{N-sec:limitations}.



\paragraph{Two exposure conventions in the processed files.}
One normalisation point is specific to the proton-tagged sample. The processed
ntuples use two POT conventions: the files behind the inclusive selection store the
total run MC POT duplicated in every file of a multi-file run, whereas the
proton-tagged files store each file's own POT. Normalising by the individual file
POT would be correct for the first set but would scale every split of a multi-file run to
the full run exposure in the second, inflating the simulated background by the split
multiplicity ($\sim\!3\times$ for RHC) and driving the background-subtracted spectrum
negative; normalising by the summed per-file POT has the mirror problem. The analysis
therefore normalises each run by the sum of the distinct per-file POT values
within that run, which reduces to the correct run total under either convention; FHC,
with one MC file per run, is unaffected by the choice.

\subsection{Additional smearing in the proton-tagged observables}
\label{sec:acwtki}

Because the Wiener--SVD additional-smearing matrix $A_C$ is not norm-preserving, each
observable's predictions are rescaled by a different factor when they are brought into the
measurement space. Table~\ref{tab:acwtki} gives that factor, measured as the ratio of the
$A_C$-smeared to the truth-level generator integral, for the proton-tagged observables.
Its dominant behaviour is set by the response and the binning; the exact factor is
prediction-dependent, and for the tested generators GENIE and GiBUU agree to a few per
cent throughout.

In the released extractions the proton-tagged observables are suppressed by $0.57$--$0.85$
(Table~\ref{tab:acwtki}): the two-region $W_\mathrm{had}$ is the least affected ($0.85$), the
two-bin TKI variables, $W_{\pi p}$, $p_p$ and the proton angles sit at $0.68$--$0.79$, and the
three-bin $\delta\phi_T$ is the most affected ($0.57$). The suppression is not driven by sharpness alone: $W_{\pi p}$ is the sharpest
distribution measured here (the ratio of its largest to smallest bin is $\sim\!36$) yet is
suppressed no more than the TKI variables, because $A_C$ removes structure that the
response cannot resolve and $W_{\pi p}$ is built from directly measured pion and
proton momenta with equal-population bins that track the $\Delta(1232)$ peak.

No observable is enhanced by $A_C$: in the released configurations every
prediction-dependent integral factor is below unity. This is an empirical property of these
extractions, not a mathematical requirement on $A_C$, which is a regularisation operator and
can suppress or enhance; an amplification would signal a mis-specified regularisation
(\S\ref{sec:thetavar}). The suppression is applied identically to data, tune and generators, so model
comparisons read off any panel are made on equal footing.

\section[Two-dimensional pairs]{Two-dimensional pairs\scopetag{shared}}
\label{sec:supp_2d}

Eight two-dimensional pairs were screened and extracted in the three configurations. A
two-dimensional binning is a flat list of analysis bins ordered slice by slice, so the extraction
uses the same machinery, and the integrated normalisation of the two-dimensional chain agrees with
the one-bin total to a median of $1.5\%$ once each is compared with its own $A_C$-smeared truth.
Two pairs are usable, both in the combined configuration only: $\theta_p\times\delta p_T$
($s_{\min}/s_1=0.611$) and $\cos\theta_\pi\times\cos\theta_\mu$ ($0.564$) (Table~\ref{tab:2dextract}).
Combining the beam modes roughly doubles their conditioning ($0.43\to0.61$ and $0.25\to0.56$), through
the larger sample and the shared flux rather than through any separation of $\nu$ and $\bar\nu$. The
pairs built on $W_{\pi p}$ are not usable, as the $39\%$ leakage between its outer bins implies.

The two usable pairs are each a \statSecondary{} in the combined configuration only:
$d^2\sigma/d\theta_p\,d\delta p_T$ on the proton-tagged sample, presented in the proton-tagged note,
and $d^2\sigma/d\cos\theta_\pi\,d\cos\theta_\mu$ on the inclusive sample, presented in
\noteref{Sec.}{sec:incl_2d}. Their FHC and RHC extractions and the other six pairs are
\statNotReported{} (Tables~\ref{tab:inventory} and~\ref{tab:pt_status}). The single-mode extractions
of the two pairs are not shape measurements, because their responses cannot be inverted. Every
one-dimensional result is quoted per beam mode as well as combined; whether a result available only in
the combined configuration is acceptable requires a collaboration decision (prerequisite R1).

The pair $\theta_{\pi p}\times W_{\pi p}$ conditions at $0.537$, as well as the two usable pairs, yet
its extraction carries no usable information: a $78\%$ data-statistical term, per-bin uncertainties up
to $276\%$ and closure ratios down to $-0.80$. Conditioning bounds how much of the response can be
inverted and says nothing about how much data survives in the inverted directions, so the two must be
read together. Conversely, $\cos\theta_\mu\times p_\mu$ screened best of the eight on the migration
diagonal ($0.70/0.68$, analysis sample) and is rank-deficient in FHC ($0.002$), while
$\theta_p\times\delta p_T$ screened worst of the proton-tagged pairs and conditions best of all. The
migration diagonal is a necessary condition, not a sufficient one; the one-dimensional binning review
measures what the regularised response retains by the Wiener filter factors of $A_C$
(\S\ref{sec:binreview}).

\section[Bin widths in the regularisation operator]{Bin widths in the regularisation operator\scopetag{shared}}
\label{sec:reg_widths}

Wiener--SVD regularises with a matrix $C$; this analysis uses the second-derivative form,
built on the density with the actual bin widths, because of what the plain tridiagonal
stencil $(1,-2,1)$ does on these binnings. That stencil is a second
derivative only on a uniform grid; these binnings are strongly non-uniform, with
width ratios up to $14{:}1$ (Table~\ref{tab:reg_widths}), so on them it is not a second
derivative at all. It is near-singular, its row sums are the $10^{-6}$ regularisation
floor, and its near-null direction is the constant vector rather than anything physical.
The Wiener filter is then free to retain that direction alone, which is a rank-1 $A_C$
that passes normalisation and discards shape.

The FHC $\theta_\mu$ extraction at its pre-review binning (\S\ref{sec:thetavar}) is the clearest
case, because its bin widths span a factor of $10.4$. Unfolded with the uniform stencil, the unfolded data, the $A_C$-smeared truth,
the MicroBooNE tune and all four generators share the same normalised shape to four
decimal places (Table~\ref{tab:thetamu_rank1}): writing the four generator predictions as
the columns of an input matrix and their $A_C$-smeared counterparts as the columns of the
output matrix, the inputs are full rank ($s_2/s_1=5.0\times10^{-2}$) while the outputs
give $s_2/s_1=2.8\times10^{-6}$. Every shape degree of freedom is annihilated, each model
is mapped onto one common curve and only its normalisation survives; any agreement with the
data is then true by construction, and a model comparison made on such an extraction is
meaningless. $\cos\theta_\mu$, the next most non-uniform binning at $14.5$, is the other
observable strongly affected; the remaining observables sit at width ratios of $3$--$6$
and are affected far less.

\section[Identity closure: unfolding the prediction with itself]{Identity closure: unfolding the prediction with itself\scopetag{shared}}
\label{sec:identity}

The pseudo-data closure tests reported in the analysis notes compare an unfolded Poisson throw against
the truth it was thrown from. They therefore test the whole chain at once, and a modest
$\chi^2$ can hide a genuine inconsistency inside the unfolding itself, because the
statistical fluctuation of the throw is the dominant term.

The identity test removes that fluctuation. Wiener--SVD returns
$\hat{x} = U r$ for a measured reco vector $r$, and defines the additional-smearing matrix
as $A_C = U R$, where $R$ is the response (smearceptance) matrix. Feeding an exact
prediction $r = R\,t$ back through the chain must therefore return
\begin{equation}
  \hat{x} = U R\, t = A_C\, t
\end{equation}
identically, for any true vector $t$. Rather than pick one $t$, we test the matrix
identity directly:
\begin{equation}
  \bigl\| U R - A_C \bigr\|_\infty \;\overset{!}{=}\; 0 .
\end{equation}
This is stronger than a single-vector test and independent of the data. It is sensitive to
the failure mode a pseudo-data test cannot see: the unfolding matrix and the additional-smearing
matrix
being built from different responses, or from responses evaluated in different universes,
which would bias every extracted cross section by a fixed factor while leaving the closure
$\chi^2$ untouched.

The check is implemented inside \texttt{UnfolderNuMI} and runs on every extraction, printing
a \texttt{[IDENTITY]} line. All $59$ released extractions pass it: five inclusive and twelve
proton-tagged observables, each in three beam configurations, the two proton angles in three
configurations and the two combined two-dimensional results. The worst deviation over the $59$
extractions is
$6.1\times10^{-5}$ relative, on FHC $\cos\theta_\mu$; the $\cos\theta_\mu$ extractions of the two
families are the only ones above $4\times10^{-6}$. These are the
rounding errors of the singular-value decomposition and the matrix products that follow it,
not physical effects: they remain more than two orders of magnitude below the smallest systematic
uncertainty in the analysis.

The deviations are larger than the $10^{-10}$ level obtained with regularisation matrices that
are not width-aware, and the reason is arithmetic rather than physical. The width-aware
operator carries factors of $1/w_j$ that span decades across a binning with a width ratio of up
to $56{:}1$, so the matrix products accumulate more floating-point error. $\cos\theta_\mu$, whose
eight bins have the largest width ratio in the analysis, is the worst case. The identity itself
holds exactly; only the arithmetic that verifies it is noisier. The unfolding construction is therefore exactly self-consistent,
and every deviation seen in the pseudo-data closure tests is attributable to the statistical
throw and to the response model, not to the unfolding implementation.

\subsection{The three-region \texorpdfstring{$p_\pi$}{p pi} result}
Charged pions interact hadronically before ranging out, so the reconstructed momentum
saturates near $0.34$~GeV/$c$: a pion of true momentum $0.95$~GeV/$c$ reconstructs at
almost the same value as one of $0.55$~GeV/$c$. The three regions have migration diagonals
of $91\%$, $51\%$ and $52\%$ (column-normalised $M$, \noteref{Sec.}{sec:unfolding_resp}) and
meet the $0.50$ criterion, but the upper region integrates over a very wide physical interval
and is not a differential point in the usual sense. The true upper region is open-ended,
$p_\pi>0.26\GeVc$ (the true-bin definition places no upper edge on it); the $1\GeVc$ that
appears in the data release and in \noteref{Appendix}{app:binning} is only the finite boundary
adopted to assign the region a width for plotting, not a kinematic limit. The released
$d\sigma/dp_\pi$ values are therefore bin-averaged densities over very different intervals
($0.030$, $0.055$ and $0.74\GeVc$), and the panel draws the three at equal visual width,
flagged on the axis, so the plotted area does not represent cross section. The result is
stated as the three bin-integrated partial cross sections
(Table~\ref{tab:ppi_partial}); the density representation derives from them and depends on
the conventional plotting width of the open region. These are the partial cross sections,

boundary for the two lower regions and depend on it for the upper one only through the
physical content above $1\GeVc$, which the open true bin already includes. For the open upper

representation of the extracted bin integral: a user should compare bin-integrated predictions

\subsection{Interpretation of the closure \texorpdfstring{$\chi^2$}{chi-squared} values}
The closure $\chi^2$ quoted for each extraction compares the unfolded result with the
pseudo-data truth using the complete configured systematic covariance, and the resulting $p$-values
cluster near unity (median $0.98$ across all fifty-one extractions; eleven lie below $0.9$
and the lowest is $0.20$). This is
not a sign of excellent agreement with the model.  For a self-closure with
correctly-sized uncertainties the $p$-values would be roughly uniform on $[0,1]$, and
they are not.

The reason is that the measurement and the truth it is being compared against share their
systematics: both come from the same generator, the same flux and the same exposure, so
the flux, cross-section and exposure terms are common-mode and largely cancel in the
difference while still inflating the covariance used to judge it.  Recomputing the same
comparison with the statistical covariance alone raises $\chi^2/\mathrm{ndf}$ from
$0.02$--$0.2$ to $0.5$--$0.8$, much nearer the expected value. The table below was evaluated
on a different pseudo-data throw from the released one and illustrates the effect; the released
closure values are those of \noteref{Tables}{tab:fhc}--\ref{N-tab:comb}.

with its input beyond the quoted band, which it passes, and not a measure of how well the
band is sized.  A test of the latter needs either a covariance restricted to the terms that differ
between measurement and reference, or an ensemble of throws. The restricted covariance is
not used for the released closure tables, whose $p$-values should be read with that
limitation in mind; the sizing is tested instead with the ensembles of
\noteref{Sec.}{par:ensemble} and \S\ref{sec:supp_ensemble}.

\label{sec:supp_ac}

Table~\ref{tab:ac_rowsums_all} gives the row sums of the released additional-smearing
matrices of every inclusive and proton-tagged extraction. One matrix has a negative row.

\input{tables/ac_rowsums_all}

Three features bear on the interpretation of the results.

The suppression is large: inclusive $\cos\theta_\mu$ has a row sum as low as $0.26$ in
RHC, and the proton-tagged $\delta\phi_T$ one of $0.19$, a strong suppression of that output mode
for a flat input.  It also differs strongly between observables, which is
why the integrated cross section obtained by summing a differential result
varies from observable to observable while the underlying physical total cannot.  The
D'Agostini cross-check, whose integral is flat across observables, establishes
that this spread is the smearing rather than a physical or acceptance effect.

One extraction has a negative row sum, and the pattern across configurations identifies its
cause. In $W_{\pi p}$ the top row carries negative off-diagonal terms in all three
configurations (summing to $-0.15$ FHC, $-0.30$ combined, $-0.61$ RHC against diagonals of
$0.73$, $0.62$ and $0.47$); only RHC tips negative overall (row sum $-0.14$), because its
diagonal element is the smallest of the three. The first $\cos\theta_\mu$ bin of the
proton-tagged RHC sample has a positive row sum with the eight adopted bins.

This is therefore not an RHC-specific defect but the regularisation pushing back against the
least-determined bin of the observable: for $W_{\pi p}$, the same top bin that is only
$2\%$ diagonal in the six-bin response and is the reason no differential $W_{\pi p}$ result is
reported.  A negative row is not forbidden, since $A_C$ is a regularisation
operator rather than a probability matrix, but a prediction smeared through one is being
told that raising the truth in that bin lowers the measured content, which is a
statement about the regularisation and not about the detector. That row should be used with
corresponding caution.


\section[Ensembles: reference construction and full-covariance pulls]{Ensembles: reference construction and full-covariance pulls\scopetag{shared}}
\label{sec:supp_ensemble}

\paragraph{Ensemble coverage and the size of the uncertainties.}
\label{par:ensemble}
The closure test above uses a single throw.  It can show that the extraction reproduces
its input, but a single residual has no width, so it cannot say whether the quoted
uncertainty is correctly sized.  To test that, one hundred statistically independent
Poisson realisations of the data of each configuration (six extractions: $p_\mu$ and
$\cos\theta_\mu$ in FHC, $\cos\theta_\mu$ in RHC, the three-region $p_\pi$ in the combined
configuration, the FHC and RHC one-bin totals; $100$ members each) were each put through the
whole chain (thrown, universes rebuilt from that throw, unfolded), and the per-bin pull
\[
  p_i \;=\; \frac{\hat{x}_i - \left(A_C\,x_{\rm CV}\right)_i}{\sigma_i}
\]
formed against the fixed central-value truth smeared by that member's own $A_C$.  (The
per-throw pseudo-data truth is not a valid reference: it follows the Poisson draw, so
numerator and reference fluctuate together and the width comes out too small.)

The throws follow the weight rule of \noteref{Eq.}{eq:safeweight}, and the extractions are
chosen to bracket the conditioning and the configurations: the well-behaved $p_\mu$;
$\cos\theta_\mu$, whose $A_C$ row sums fall as low as $0.49$ in FHC ($0.26$ in RHC) so that the
regularisation removes up to $51\%$ ($74\%$) of a bin's normalisation; the three-region $p_\pi$ of
the combined configuration; and the one-bin totals, which have no regularisation at all. The four
differential ensembles use the released binnings (members built from staged configurations
identical to the released ones). If the uncertainties were mis-sized anywhere, it would show in
$\cos\theta_\mu$; if the extraction carried a bias independent of the regularisation, it would
show in the one-bin totals.

Against the statistical covariance all six widths are consistent with unity ($0.95$--$1.07$,
each determined to about $\pm7\%$, $1/\sqrt{2\times100}$, more for the multi-bin extractions,
whose bins are correlated), and the interval coverage is correct within the same statistics. The
statistical uncertainties are therefore correctly sized, and the strongly suppressed observable
behaves no differently from the well-conditioned one. Of the two, the $\cos\theta_\mu$ result
is the more informative: $p_\mu$ was expected to pass, whereas it was not known in advance
whether the severe $A_C$ suppression in $\cos\theta_\mu$ would distort its uncertainties.

Against the full covariance the pull width
is $0.22$--$0.46$ for the differential results and $0.11$ for the totals, and coverage is
essentially $100\%$: the quoted
uncertainty is two to five times the throw-to-throw spread for the differential results and about
nine times for the totals, because the ensemble varies
only the data statistics while the denominator carries flux, detector and cross-section
terms that are identical in every member.  This is the same effect as the closure
$p$-values clustered near unity, measured here directly.

The ensemble means sit below the reference in all six cases, by $0.4$--$1.1\%$
($0.6$--$2.0\sigma$ on the mean; largest in significance for the RHC total, $-0.81\%$ at $2.0\sigma$,
and in size for FHC $\cos\theta_\mu$, $-1.14\%$ at $1.3\sigma$), with pull means of $-0.05$
to $-0.11$ and $-0.21$ for the RHC total (Table above; \texttt{data\_release/ensemble\_2026-09-26.tsv}). The
extractions of one configuration share the same throws and are correlated, so this is a few
measurements of a small negative offset, each consistent with zero at the present ensemble
size. The throw itself is unbiased: the mean selected count over the 32
members of an FHC ensemble built with the same throw rule is $1578.7$ against the neutrino-MC expectation of $1580.6$ (ratio $0.999$), so
whatever offset remains is in the extraction, not in the pseudo-data. The offset is at most two standard
deviations of the ensemble mean and is largest where the regularisation is strongest,
but the one-bin totals, which have no regularisation, also sit low, so the excess in
$\cos\theta_\mu$ over the common $\approx1\%$ is the only part that can be attributed
to $A_C$. No correction is applied and no uncertainty is assigned; a larger ensemble
would settle whether the common offset is real.

A truncated read of the pseudo-data occurs when two ensembles sharing a throw number write the
same throw file at the same time and a member opens it mid-write, seeing part of the tree. Ten of the $600$ members were first built from such a
read. Four RHC-total members (Run~2 read at $47$--$72\%$) and one FHC
$\cos\theta_\mu$ member ($43\%$) were affected materially; they widened the RHC-total pull width
to $1.28$. Every member was checked for this (the number of events univmake read against the entries on disk;
$2791$ of $2800$ inputs agree exactly, and the five remaining one-entry differences are one
out-of-range truth event in the one-bin binning, which does not enter the reco-level data), and
the affected members were rebuilt. Each throw is created once and never replaced
(\texttt{macros/throw\_guard.h}; change log). The four differential ensembles of the table were built
this way, and the entry-count scan of their $2000$ inputs and of the proton-tagged $W_{\rm had}$
ensemble finds no truncated read; the members short by one entry are the boundary case of an
event with $\cos\theta_\mu=1.0$ exactly (change log).

The study covers the inclusive sample in all three configurations. The proton-tagged family,
where the statistics are sparser, has three further $100$-member ensembles ($W_{\rm had}$ in its two
regions and the two-bin $\delta p_T$ in FHC, the two-bin $p_n$ combined): statistical pull widths
$0.94$--$1.08$, $68\%$ coverage $62$--$72\%$ and integral offsets within $0.7\%$
(\ptref{Sec.}{sec:pt_ensemble}, \texttt{data\_release/ensemble\_1p\_2026-09-26.tsv}).

\section[Consistency between configurations and with the sibling $\nu_e$ measurement]{Consistency between configurations and with the sibling $\nu_e$ measurement\scopetag{inclusive}}
\label{sec:supp_closure}

\subsection{Consistency with the sibling $\nu_e\,\mathrm{CC}\,1\pi$ measurement}
\label{sec:nueconsistency}

This analysis is one of a pair of MicroBooNE NuMI CC single-charged-pion
measurements; the companion $\nu_e\,\mathrm{CC}\,1\pi$ analysis shares the same
cross-section-extraction framework, beam and reconstruction. A cross-check against
its internal note confirms consistency on the essential conventions and identifies
the differences that a combined NuMI CC$\pi$ programme should reconcile.

\emph{Consistent by construction.} Both use the Wiener--SVD extraction framework;
the updated NuMI flux with the fixed geometry bug and Geant~v4.10.4; the same
signal topology (exactly one $\pi^\pm$, zero $\pi^0$ or heavier mesons, any number
of protons, charged current), and the same systematic suite, PPFX flux
hadronic universes (the 12 beamline-geometry unisims the $\nu_e$ analysis carries are
evaluated but not in this analysis's official covariance, prerequisite U7, \noteref{Sec.}{sec:limitations}), the
600-universe GENIE cross-section multisim with unisims, the Geant4 reinteraction
reweighting, the standard detector-variation set, and $2\%$ / $1\%$ / $100\%$
POT / target / dirt normalisation uncertainties. The FHC beam-on data POT per run
agrees exactly (Run~2 $1.268$, Run~4 $2.075\times10^{20}$).

\emph{Differences reconciled or flagged.} (i)~\textbf{POT accounting:} the
$\nu_e$ note quotes Run~1 FHC $=3.283\times10^{20}$~POT ($9\,846\,635$ triggers), the
figure that includes the open-trigger stream. The beam-on sample processed here is the
non-open-trigger dataset, $2.192\times10^{20}$~POT with $5\,748\,692$ triggers, confirmed
against the beam bookkeeping; the total FHC exposure is $7.766\times10^{20}$ over Runs~1, 2, 4
and 5. (ii)~\textbf{Run~4:} the \texttt{Run4} overlay
is identical to \texttt{Run4c}$+$\texttt{Run4d} combined (same $859{,}368$ events
and $2.834\times10^{21}$ POT); only one may be used to avoid double counting.
(iii)~\textbf{Pion threshold:} $\nu_e$ uses $\mathrm{KE}_\pi>40$~MeV
($p_\pi>0.113\GeVc$) versus our $p_\pi>0.175\GeVc$
(\noteref{Table}{tab:pithresh}). (iv)~\textbf{Opening-angle cut:} $\nu_e$ excludes
$\theta_{e\pi}>170^\circ$ (a shower-specific back-to-back misreconstruction),
whereas our muon-track topology uses $\theta_{\mu\pi}<2.6$~rad ($149^\circ$).
(v)~\textbf{Regularisation:} the $\nu_e$ analysis smooths on the first derivative,
this analysis on the second (\S\ref{sec:reg_widths}). (vi)~\textbf{Scope:}
both analyses cover Runs~1--5 in FHC and RHC; the present measurement reports FHC,
RHC and the combined configuration.

\subsection{The combined configuration and its inputs}
\label{sec:combtruth}

The integrated cross section extracted from a given configuration is not a
configuration-independent number: it depends on the additional-smearing matrix $A_C$,
which differs per configuration and per observable, so comparisons across configurations
are made observable by observable and against the pseudo-data truth rather than between
measurements. Two tests compare the combined configuration with its inputs.

\paragraph{The combined truth against the exposure-weighted average of its inputs.}
A configuration spanning both horn currents should predict a cross section between those
of its two inputs, equal to their average weighted by the fluence of each mode ($0.425$ FHC,
$0.575$ RHC). The realised truths of the one-bin totals (\noteref{Table}{tab:cutcount}) give a
ratio of the combined truth to that average of $1.000$ for the inclusive family and $1.001$ for the
proton-tagged family. Observable by observable, the $A_C$-smeared truth integrals give
$1.006$ ($0.981$--$1.030$) and $1.046$ ($0.984$--$1.187$), the spread being the configuration
dependence of $A_C$ rather than of the truth. The test is sensitive to the treatment of the
detector variations: the two per-mode detector-variation sets of the combined configuration
must be pooled as one sample scaled once to the data exposure, since scaling each set to the full
exposure and summing would double the detector covariance, over-regularise the combined
extraction and push its truth ratio to $0.91$ with a worst observable at $0.77$. With the
pooled treatment the combined detector term lies between or close to those of its inputs
(analysis note, Tables~\ref{N-tab:fhc}--\ref{N-tab:comb}: $23.7\%$ against $21.2\%$ in FHC and
$23.8\%$ in RHC for $p_\mu$).

\paragraph{The RHC deficit is in the input, not the extraction.}
The one-bin totals give an RHC/FHC ratio of the injected tune of $0.901$
(inclusive) and $0.878$ (proton-tagged), and the extracted one-bin ratios are $0.901$ and
$0.873$ (\noteref{Table}{tab:cutcount}). Normalisation is not implicated: the exposures
sum exactly ($7.766\times10^{20}+1.1082\times10^{21}=1.8848\times10^{21}$~POT) and the
per-configuration flux values reproduce the POT-weighted combination to $0.01\%$. The
deficit is therefore a property of the $\bar\nu_\mu$-dominant sample, and the number
to be compared with data at unblinding is the tune ratio.

\paragraph{External reproduction of the released curves.}
The transformation $\left(d\sigma/dx\right)_i = w_i^{-1}\sum_j (A_C)_{ij}\,p_j$
applied to the released truth-level predictions reproduces every released generator curve
to a worst relative per-bin deviation of $4\times10^{-8}$, the precision of the text files
($96$ curves). This check is part of the release
procedure because an internal closure cannot make it: the unfolded
data and the truth can both be right while the model comparison is wrong, and only an
external user's arithmetic exposes it.

\medskip

\paragraph{Request.}
We request approval to inspect beam-on data in four control regions: CC$0\pi$,
multi-$\pi$, $\pi^0$ and large muon--pion opening angle (called ``cosmic'' below for
brevity: it is cosmic-enriched, $34$--$41\%$ beam-off, not cosmic-pure).
Throughout, ``beam-off'', ``EXT'' and ``cosmic data'' name the same data-driven
component, the beam-off trigger sample scaled by gate counts; ``signal contamination''
means the inclusive signal unless the proton-tagged selection is named. Each region is
anchored by the inversion of one nominal signal requirement; requirements that become
undefined once that inversion is made, or that would sculpt the targeted control sample,
are dropped as listed in Table~\ref{tab:cr_defs}. Each region is disjoint from the
signal region at reconstruction level (demonstrated event by event,
\S\ref{sec:cr_overlap}); their expected signal contaminations are $8$--$24\%$. The
signal region remains blind. Before the regions are opened, the selections, the
run-dependent normalisation, the beam-off scaling, the displayed observables and binning,
the covariance construction, the goodness-of-fit tests and the permitted responses to a
discrepancy are frozen as written in \S\ref{sec:cr_protocol}. Control-region data will be
compared with the complete prediction (neutrino MC $+$ beam-off $+$ dirt) separately in
FHC and RHC, with the combined configuration as a cross-check. Any discrepancy beyond
the pre-declared criteria requires a documented treatment and renewed approval before
the signal region is opened. The proton-tagged control regions are not part of
this request (\S\ref{sec:cr_1p_scope}).

\paragraph{Role of each region.} The transfer factors of \S\ref{sec:cr_transfer} set
the physics role of each region.
\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
  \item \textbf{CC$0\pi$} is the high-statistics validation of the dominant neutrino
    background class: CC$0\pi$ events with a proton or other track taken as the pion are
    $215$ of the $560$ FHC neutrino-background events ($38\%$). The region has $72\%$
    target purity ($65\%$ of the full stack) and $0.6\%$ raw statistical precision. It
    validates the normalisation and kinematics of the underlying CC$0\pi$ population,
    not the proton-to-pion misidentification probability that transfers that
    population into the signal region: the transfer carries a $31$--$34\%$ uncertainty
    dominated by the detector variations. It is therefore a validation test rather than
    a per-cent-level constraint, and it is conditionally eligible for a fitted
    normalisation correction under \S\ref{sec:cr_protocol}. The direct test of the
    misidentification probability is an inverted pion-PID region, which does not exist
    yet and is listed among the prerequisites for opening the signal region.
  \item \textbf{$\pi^0$} tests the shower veto and the CC$+$NC~$\pi^0$ background. Its
    target purity is $50.1\%$ (FHC) and $51.0\%$ (RHC) of the neutrino MC, its transfer
    factor $0.02$ with a $17$--$19\%$ uncertainty set by the Monte-Carlo-statistical
    floor of the detector-variation samples. For this opening the region is
    diagnostic only. Approval to inspect it does not authorise a fitted correction; a
    proposal to fit the CC$+$NC~$\pi^0$ normalisation would require a separate
    documented review after the pre-fit result has been recorded, with the fit model,
    the leverage of the fixed components, the transfer uncertainty and its floor, and
    the expected effect on the signal-region prediction.
  \item \textbf{Multi-$\pi$} is diagnostic only. Its transfer factor is $\simeq2.5$
    (the signal region holds more of the target class than the region does) and its
    target purity $47\%$; it is not used to fit or constrain the signal-region
    normalisation, but it exposes gross modelling failures and shape discrepancies.
  \item \textbf{Cosmic} validates the beam-off normalisation and shape to $\sim\!50\%$
    once the prediction uncertainties of its neutrino component are included ($17\%$
    from data statistics alone). This precision is sufficient because beam-off is $1.9\%$ of the
    selected signal-region prediction: a $50\%$ uncertainty on it is an effect of order
    $1\%$ on the selected sample. It is not eligible for a fitted correction.
\end{itemize}
About $30\%$ of the FHC neutrino background has no region of its own: NC and
$\nu_e$CC without a $\pi^0$, out-of-fiducial-volume events and CC$1\pi^\pm$ outside the
measured phase space (\S\ref{sec:cr_transfer}). These components keep their model
uncertainties unchanged; the CC$0\pi$ and $\pi^0$ distributions carry indirect
sensitivity to the first two through the shared reconstruction, and each is small
enough individually that no dedicated region is justified at this exposure.

\paragraph{Sensitivity of the test and its blind direction.} The pass/fail statistic is
a global normalisation $\chi^2$ over the eight (region, mode) totals with the full
pre-fit covariance (\S\ref{sec:cr_protocol}). It detects a discrepancy confined to one
region beyond $12\%$ of the CC$0\pi$ or $\pi^0$ total and beyond $50$--$62\%$ of the
multi-$\pi$ or cosmic total, an FHC/RHC inconsistency, and, through the
normalisation-marginalised shape tests, a shape difference. A common
normalisation shift of all regions fails it only beyond $58\%$, because the correlated
flux, cross-section and detector terms absorb it. That blind direction is acceptable
because it is the direction the signal-region measurement already carries as its flux
and cross-section uncertainty: the control regions test the background model region by
region, not the overall normalisation.

\paragraph{Scope of the pseudo-data studies.}
The control-region machinery has been exercised on Poisson-thrown central-value pseudo-data.
Those studies validate the selection logic, the per-run exposure scaling, the
central-value weighting and, in the full-stack variant of \S\ref{sec:cr_fullstack}, the
exact bookkeeping path that beam-on data will follow. They do not validate the
background model: the pseudo-data are drawn from the same simulation that supplies the
prediction, so agreement is guaranteed up to statistics. The background model is the
largest untested assumption in the analysis, and the control regions exist to test it. The control-region comparison is made at reconstruction level, so it does not
depend on the unfolding or on the alternative-generator closure that is required before
the signal region is opened (prerequisite U3, \noteref{Sec.}{sec:limitations}).

\paragraph{Expected yields and precision.}
\noteref{Table}{tab:sideband_yields} gives the expected composition per region and beam. The
raw statistical precision of the data samples is $0.6\%$ (CC$0\pi$), $0.8\%$ ($\pi^0$)
and $6\%$ (multi-$\pi$) for the combined configuration. The neutrino-MC prediction in each
region carries the flux, cross-section and re-interaction uncertainties of
Table~\ref{tab:cr_syst}, including the detector variations. The regions carry a
few-per-cent sensitivity to the signal normalisation in principle (the CC$0\pi$ region
holds $987$ signal events, $9\%$ of its neutrino MC, so a $30\%$ signal excess would
appear as a $2.8\%$ excess), which is inside the $9$--$24\%$ detector-variation
uncertainty on the region prediction and so invisible to every pull of
\S\ref{sec:cr_protocol}. This keeps the regions blind to the signal, and the response
tree holds the signal at its central value in any case.

\subsection{Region overlaps}
\label{sec:cr_overlap}
The four regions are disjoint from the signal region by construction: CC$0\pi$ and
multi-$\pi$ require $n_\pi=0$ and $n_\pi\ge2$ against the signal region's $n_\pi=1$; the
$\pi^0$ region requires the shower veto to fail; the cosmic region requires the
opening-angle cut to fail. An event-by-event check on the files instrumented with
the control-region flags confirms this: the number of events carrying both the signal-region
flag and any control-region flag is exactly zero in the Monte Carlo and in the pseudo-data
of every configuration. The regions are not mutually exclusive: multi-$\pi$ does not
apply the shower veto, so events with two pion candidates and a failed shower veto belong to
both multi-$\pi$ and $\pi^0$ ($47$ of the $124$ FHC multi-$\pi$ MC events, $68$ of $159$
RHC); all other pairs are disjoint. Any simultaneous fit of the regions must account for
that overlap; the per-region comparisons of \S\ref{sec:cr_protocol} do not need to.

\paragraph{Monte-Carlo-statistical floor of the detector term.}
The detector entries of Tables~\ref{tab:cr_syst} and~\ref{tab:cr_transfer_unc} are
quadrature sums of eight differences between finite samples, so part of each is the
statistics of the variation samples rather than a detector effect. The floor is
estimated by evaluating every shift on two disjoint halves of the generated events (split
on the true neutrino energy, which is identical across the central-value and variation
samples): half the difference of the two half-sample shifts estimates the statistical
noise of the full-sample shift, and its quadrature over the eight knobs is the floor.
For the region yields the floors are small: $1.4\%$ (FHC) and $1.7\%$ (RHC) against
signal-region detector terms of $20.7\%$ and $14.2\%$, and $1$--$2\%$ for the
CC$0\pi$ and $\pi^0$ regions. For the transfer factors they are not: the CC$0\pi$
target-class term ($27\%$ FHC, $31\%$ RHC) sits well above its floor ($9\%$, $6\%$)
and is a genuine detector sensitivity, but the $\pi^0$ target-class term ($15\%$ FHC,
$11\%$ RHC) is comparable to or smaller than its floor ($13\%$, $17\%$), so it is an
upper limit set by the variation-sample statistics rather than a measurement (the same
holds for the adopted CC$+$NC~$\pi^0$ class: $15\%$ against a $12\%$ floor in FHC,
$12\%$ against $18\%$ in RHC), and the multi-$\pi$ target-class term ($18$--$21\%$)
sits below its floor ($25\%$ FHC, $23\%$ RHC); the half-sample estimator itself becomes
unstable for the smallest classes, where floors of several hundred per cent appear. Any fitted correction under
item~4(ii) of the procedure must therefore carry the larger of the quoted
transfer uncertainty and its floor; for the $\pi^0$ class that is the floor.

\paragraph{Two limits of the control-region set.} First, about $30\%$ of the FHC neutrino
background has no region: NC and $\nu_e$CC without a $\pi^0$ ($79$ events),
out-of-fiducial-volume ($41$) and CC$1\pi^\pm$ outside the measured phase space ($47$),
the last subtracted from the same generator with a reconstruction bias of $+30$--$66\%$
for below-threshold pions (\noteref{Table}{tab:pithresh}). Second, the CC$0\pi$ region constrains
the CC$0\pi$ rate, not the proton-as-pion misidentification that carries it into
the signal region ($T=0.03$, detector-dominated); an inverted pion-PID, proton-enriched
region would test the misidentification without touching the signal region and is not
part of this request.

item~4 of \S\ref{sec:cr_protocol}. That is accepted for the larger sample; the stored
multiplicities allow the reconstruction-failure investigation of item~4(i) to locate such
a discrepancy if one appears.

\subsection{Frozen procedure for opening the control regions}
\label{sec:cr_protocol}
The analysers proposed the procedure below for ratification with the approval. It is
frozen, and any departure from it is itself a change requiring approval.

\begin{enumerate}\itemsep2pt
  \item \textbf{What is examined.} For each of the four regions: the event total, and the
    reconstructed distributions of $p_\mu$, $\cos\theta_\mu$ and the muon--pion opening
    angle, and for the $\pi^0$ region the leading shower energy (the primary-track and
    non-proton multiplicities are stored by the frozen skim,
    \S\ref{sec:cr_pi0_final}, but are not in the pre-declared list and are not tested;
    they are available to the reconstruction-failure investigation of item~4(i) only), at the binning of the released
    observables where one exists and otherwise at the binning fixed in
    the frozen plotting list and shown, with full-stack pseudo-data, in
    Figs.~\ref{fig:sb_plots_fhc} and~\ref{fig:sb_plots_rhc}. FHC and RHC are examined separately; the
    combined configuration is a cross-check only, and within each mode the event total is
    also compared per run period (FHC Runs~1, 2, 4, 5; RHC Runs~1, 2, 3, 4), so that a
    single-run excursion is detectable. Data are compared with the complete
    prediction, neutrino MC $+$ beam-off $+$ dirt, with the run-period beam-off scaling of
    \noteref{Sec.}{sec:mc}.
  \item \textbf{Statistics.} Three quantities are computed for each region and beam
    mode, all against the pre-fit prediction. (a)~The normalisation pull,
    $(N_{\rm data}-N_{\rm pred})/\sigma$, with $\sigma^2 = N_{\rm data} +
    \mathbf{1}^{\mathsf T}V_{\rm pred}\mathbf{1}$ and $V_{\rm pred}$ the summed
    covariance of the complete prediction (neutrino MC, beam-off and dirt: their finite
    statistics, flux, cross-section, re-interaction and detector terms, with all
    correlations among the prediction components). Because $V_{\rm pred}$ carries the
    $21$--$35\%$ prediction systematics of Table~\ref{tab:cr_syst}, this pull cannot
    fail a discrepancy smaller than about $40$--$60\%$; it is therefore reported
    together with the pull against the statistical and detector terms alone, whose
    resolution ($9$--$24\%$) is set by the detector term. (b)~A shape $\chi^2$ in
    the pre-declared distributions with the normalisation marginalised: the prediction is
    scaled to the data total and the covariance is projected onto shape (the
    normalisation mode removed), so that the common-mode flux and cross-section terms do
    not make the test powerless, as the closure $\chi^2$ of \noteref{Sec.}{sec:chi2} shows
    they otherwise would. (c)~A global normalisation $\chi^2$ over the eight
    (region, mode) totals with the full $8\times8$ pre-fit covariance, which is the
    single pass/fail statistic. All three are implemented in
    the protocol code (flux, cross-section and re-interaction multisims
    index-matched across the two modes, the eight detector knobs as correlated unisims,
    MC, beam-off, dirt and data statistics) and were exercised on the full-stack
    pseudo-data of \S\ref{sec:cr_fullstack}: the global $\chi^2$ is $0.66/8$ ($p=1.00$),
    the sixteen pulls lie within $\pm0.5$ and all twenty-two shape tests have $p>0.47$. The same run gives the power of the
    test: a discrepancy confined to one region fails the set at $95\%$ confidence beyond
    $12\%$ of the CC$0\pi$ or $\pi^0$ total and beyond $50$--$62\%$ of the multi-$\pi$
    or cosmic total, while a common normalisation shift of all eight totals, which
    the $40$--$91\%$ correlated flux, cross-section and detector terms absorb, fails it
    only beyond $58\%$. This is the blind spot of the test: it tests the background
    model region by region, not the overall normalisation, which the signal-region
    measurement carries as its flux and cross-section uncertainty.
  \item \textbf{Acceptable agreement.} The control regions pass as a set if the global $\chi^2$ of item~2(c) has $p>0.05$; the
    expected false-failure rate is then $5\%$ by construction. In the calibration,
    $2\times10^5$ pseudo-experiments under the null (prediction drawn from its pre-fit
    covariance, data Poisson-fluctuated, $\chi^2$ with the nominal covariance) fail the
    nominal $p<0.05$ threshold in $5.1\%$ of cases and $p<0.01$ in $1.1\%$
    (Fig.~\ref{fig:cr_null_calibration}).
    The calibration treats the covariance as known; the finite-universe and
    detector-unisim sampling noise is not re-thrown, which is the residual approximation
    in the declared threshold. The per-region pulls and shape tests of items~2(a) and 2(b) are
    diagnostic: a region with $|{\rm pull}|>2$ (complete prediction covariance) or shape $p<0.05$ is
    flagged for the response tree of item~4 even when the set passes, but the eight
    independent flags are not themselves pass/fail criteria (with sixteen pulls and
    twenty-two shape tests at these thresholds about two false flags are expected, and at
    least one with $85\%$ probability). A single-bin excursion
    beyond $3\sigma$ with a passing total is recorded and examined for a reconstruction
    pathology but does not by itself flag the region.
  \item \textbf{Permitted responses and the rule for choosing between them.} The response
    tree is entered when the global test of item~3 fails or a region is flagged; a
    flagged region within a passing set may receive at most response (b) below, never a
    fitted correction. Regions are treated in the order CC$0\pi$, $\pi^0$, then the two
    diagnostic regions, because the CC$0\pi$ class is $15\%$ of the $\pi^0$ region and a
    CC$0\pi$ treatment changes the $\pi^0$ prediction before it is judged.
    (i)~\emph{Localised reconstruction failure.} If one run period's total in the region
    pulls beyond $3\sigma$ (statistical$+$detector) while the other periods pass, or one
    bin of a pre-declared distribution exceeds $3\sigma$ while the rest of the distribution
    passes, the excursion triggers a reconstruction-quality investigation, not a model
    correction and not an automatic exclusion: a $3\sigma$ excursion is expected to occur
    by chance somewhere among the periods, regions and bins examined, and a
    control-region discrepancy alone does not show that the signal region of that
    period is unusable. The period is excluded only if an independent detector or
    processing defect is established (detector-quality monitors, an independent
    inclusive sample, reproduction in more than one pre-declared variable, or a known
    production or calibration boundary); the exclusion then removes the period from
    both the control-region comparison and the measurement with its exposure
    removed from the normalisation, changes the flux weighting, the detector covariance
    and the response, and therefore requires a new frozen release and explicit approval.
    If no defect is established the period stays in the analysis and the discrepancy is
    treated through the model responses below. No cut or binning may be changed and a
    single bin is never removed.
    (ii)~\emph{Fitted normalisation correction (c).} Permitted only for CC$0\pi$ and
    $\pi^0$ (target-class purity above $50\%$ of the neutrino-MC prediction; target
    classes $\nu_\mu$CC~$0\pi$ and CC$+$NC~$\pi^0$, adopted, ratification
    requested in Sec.~\ref{sec:request}) and only if the
    region's shape test 2(b) passes, so that the discrepancy is consistent with a pure
    normalisation shift: a one-parameter fit of the target class, to FHC and RHC
    simultaneously, applied if it improves the fit by a likelihood-ratio test at
    $p<0.01$. The $\pi^0$ region meets the purity rule narrowly ($50.1$/$51.0\%$) and
    with a class and denominator settled after the purities were known
    (Sec.~\ref{sec:request}), so its eligibility is not pre-authorised: a fitted
    $\pi^0$ correction may be proposed only after the pre-fit
    control-region result is documented, and the fit model, the leverage of the fixed
    components, the transfer-factor uncertainty and its Monte-Carlo-statistical floor
    are reviewed by the collaboration before the correction is propagated to the signal
    region. CC$0\pi$ ($72\%$) is pre-authorised under the same rule. The two fitted
    regions are disjoint by construction (CC$0\pi$ requires the shower veto to pass,
    $\pi^0$ requires it to fail), so a simultaneous fit counts no event twice; the
    multi-$\pi$/$\pi^0$ overlap ($47/124$ FHC, $68/159$ RHC events) concerns a
    diagnostic region that is never fitted, and the global test of item~2(c) carries it
    as a covariance between totals. The fit updates the normalisation of
    the target class only, re-evaluated in every systematic universe so that its
    correlation with the flux and cross-section terms of the signal-region prediction is
    unchanged (the $1+B/S$ coherence of \noteref{Sec.}{sec:fluxanatomy} is retained, with $B$
    rescaled); the fitted uncertainty below replaces the class's prior normalisation
    uncertainty and enters as an independent term; the signal/background joint
    covariance is not otherwise modified. Its uncertainty is the transfer-factor uncertainty of
    Table~\ref{tab:cr_transfer_unc} or its Monte-Carlo-statistical floor, whichever is
    larger (\S\ref{sec:cr_transfer}), added in quadrature to the leverage of the components
    held fixed in the fit: the prediction uncertainties of the non-target neutrino
    background, the beam-off and the signal contamination, each multiplied by its share
    of the target class ($0.80$, $0.23$ and $0.20$ in the $\pi^0$ region; $0.26$, $0.15$
    and $0.13$ in CC$0\pi$; Sec.~\ref{sec:request}).
    (iii)~\emph{Added uncertainty (b).} Otherwise the central prediction is left
    unchanged and the full observed discrepancy, as a fraction of the target class in the
    region, is assigned as a one-standard-deviation normalisation uncertainty on that
    class in the signal region through its transfer factor.
    (iv)~\emph{Model revision (d).} If the post-treatment global $\chi^2$, computed
    with the fitted normalisation or the added uncertainty in place and reported
    separately from the pre-fit value of item~3, still has $p<0.05$, or a region remains
    flagged, the background model is revised and renewed collaboration approval is
    required before the signal region is opened. The pass/fail test of item~3 is always
    the pre-fit one; the post-treatment value is labelled as such wherever it is quoted.
    The response letters used here and in Sec.~\ref{sec:request} are (a)~no action,
    (b)~added uncertainty, (c)~fitted normalisation correction, (d)~model revision. FHC and RHC share one background
    model: a class that passes in one mode and fails in the other is treated under (b),
    never corrected in one mode alone. The signal contamination of each region
    ($987$ events in CC$0\pi$ and $582$ in $\pi^0$ for FHC, Table~\ref{tab:cr_transfer}, more than
    the $833$ signal events of the signal region) is held at the central-value tune in any fit,
    which keeps the regions blind to the signal: a $30\%$ signal excess would
    move the CC$0\pi$ total by $2.8\%$, well inside the $9$--$24\%$ detector-limited
    resolution of every pull. If more than one region is flagged, no independent
    corrections are combined without accounting for shared events and correlated
    systematic variations: in particular, multi-$\pi$ and $\pi^0$ share events
    (\S\ref{sec:cr_overlap}) and are treated jointly, or multi-$\pi$ is designated
    diagnostic only, as its transfer factor indicates.
  \item \textbf{Prohibited responses.} Changing any signal-selection cut; changing the
    binning of any released observable; choosing a generator or tune after inspecting the
    regions; bin-by-bin reweighting; and any use of the beam-on signal-region sample.
  \item \textbf{Sign-off.} The analysis conveners, the flux and detector-systematics
    contacts, and the collaboration review committee, on the basis of a short report
    containing the comparisons of item~1 and the response chosen from item~4.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Scope: inclusive regions only}
\label{sec:cr_1p_scope}
The request of Sec.~\ref{sec:request} covers the four inclusive regions. The proton-tagged
regions of \S\ref{sec:sidebands_1p} apply the proton tag rather than inverting it and
therefore do not constrain the $\approx\!13\%$ proton mis-tag background that most
affects the hadronic observables; the inverted-proton-PID region that would do so has not yet
been implemented. The proton-tagged control-region programme is consequently not
ready for equivalent approval, and opening the inherited proton-tagged regions, while
useful diagnostically, would not validate the proton-tagged background model.

\subsection[Control regions for the proton-tagged sample]{Control regions for the proton-tagged sample\scopetag{proton-tagged}}
\label{sec:sidebands_1p}

The proton-tagged selection inherits its cut logic from the inclusive selection, and with it
the four control-region definitions of \noteref{Sec.}{sec:sidebands}: the reprocessed proton-tagged
files already carry the four region flags. No new definitions or reprocessing are
required to exercise the background model of the proton-tagged extractions; the same four regions
can be evaluated either as inherited (the inclusive phase space) or with a reconstructed
tagged proton additionally required, which places them in the same phase space as the
measurement itself. Table~\ref{tab:sidebands_1p} gives both.

All four regions are more
signal-depleted for the proton-tagged signal than they were for the inclusive one:
$5.8$--$7.2\%$ signal contamination in CC$0\pi$ and $\pi^0$, against $9.2$--$10.0\%$ for the
same regions in the inclusive phase space and a $52\%$ purity in the signal region, because
requiring a proton does not make a pion-less or $\pi^0$ event look like signal. The
proton requirement costs about a third of the statistics
(CC$0\pi$: $12\,181\to8\,386$ pseudo-data events for FHC) but suppresses the beam-off contribution by roughly
a factor six ($1\,358\to225$, FHC CC$0\pi$, Table~\ref{tab:sidebands_1p}): cosmic-induced events rarely contain a reconstructed
proton above threshold, so the proton tag is itself a strong cosmic rejector, and the EXT
normalisation matters far less in the proton-tagged extractions than in the inclusive ones. The pseudo-data to
neutrino-MC ratios of the two large regions stay at $0.987$--$1.007$ with the tag, so the
per-run bookkeeping holds in the proton-tagged phase space as well; the small regions
scatter more widely ($0.85$--$1.52$), which on $37$--$62$ pseudo-data events is Poisson
scatter of one to two standard deviations, not a normalisation effect.

\paragraph{Limits of the proton-tagged regions.}
All four apply the proton tag rather than invert it, so none of them constrains the
proton mis-tag background: the $\sim\!13\%$ of tagged protons that are really a
pion or another track, which smears $W_{\pi p}$ and the TKI variables directly. Testing
that requires a region defined by inverting the proton PID (selecting candidates that pass
the momentum and containment requirements but fail the LLR cut), which does need a new
flag in the proton-tagged selection and a reprocess. Slicing a measured W/TKI observable is
not an alternative: because the signal definition is inclusive in those variables,
cutting on them splits the signal region rather than depleting it. On the
Run-1 FHC overlay the signal region has $53.7\%$ purity and the slices $W_{\pi p}>1.5$~GeV,
$W_{\pi p}<1.15$~GeV, $p_n>0.8$~GeV/$c$ and $\delta p_T>0.7$~GeV/$c$ give $50.0\%$,
$48.9\%$, $47.3\%$ and $52.0\%$ respectively, so none of the slices is depleted. Even the
kinematically forbidden region $W_\mathrm{had}<m_\pi+m_p$ retains $53.4\%$ signal, because
the calorimetric mass is systematically under-reconstructed and true signal populates it
through resolution.

\subsection{Validation of the background fit}
\label{sec:bkgfit_validation}

The background fit, the normalisation fit of the control-region target classes to the
control-region totals and distributions, is validated on simulation
(Fig.~\ref{fig:validation}).
\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
  \item \textbf{Toys.} Sets of $300$ toys give pull means of $0.02$--$0.07$, widths of
    $0.89$--$1.03$ and coverage of $67$--$75\%$; ensembles of up to $10^5$ toys per model are
    described in \S\ref{sec:bkgfit_ensembles}.
  \item \textbf{Injections} (Asimov data, two-parameter fit of the totals). Shifts of a class are
    recovered exactly; a $30\%$ signal excess moves the normalisations by $0.13\sigma$, an unfitted
    class at $+30\%$ by $-0.07\sigma$, a detector variation by at most $0.14\sigma$, and flux and GENIE
    universes by $-0.97$ to $+0.84\sigma$, within the uncertainty they are part of.
  \item \textbf{FHC against RHC.} A likelihood-ratio test of common against beam-specific
    normalisations inside the joint covariance (a parameter goodness-of-fit test is not valid, since
    the modes share universes): a $+15\%$ shift of the RHC neutrino simulation alone gives
    $\Delta\chi^2=12.9/2$ ($p=0.002$, totals) and $20.3/2$ ($p=4\times10^{-5}$, two-dimensional).
  \item \textbf{Markov chain.} An adaptive Metropolis sampler with four chains and flat priors gives
    posterior means within $0.02$ and widths within $2\%$ of MIGRAD/MINOS on the Asimov dataset and
    on a CC$0\pi\times1.25$ injection, with $\hat R\le1.004$. With a few parameters and a nearly
    quadratic likelihood the profile likelihood suffices, and the sampler is kept as a cross-check.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Convergence of the pull means.}
\label{sec:bkgfit_ensembles}
With $300$ toys the uncertainty of a pull mean is $0.058$, so a mean of $+0.07$ cannot be
distinguished from zero. Ensembles of $10^5$ toys per model (uncertainty $0.003$) and three
diagnostic variants of $5\times10^4$ toys each resolve this (Table~\ref{tab:ensembles},
Fig.~\ref{fig:ensembles}). With the combined Neyman--Pearson data variance, the pull mean of the
two-dimensional fits does not converge to zero: $+0.019$ remains for CC$0\pi$ and $+0.034$ and
$+0.031$ for CC$0\pi$+$\pi^0$, six to ten standard deviations of the ensemble. The offset persists
with Gaussian instead of Poisson fluctuations ($+0.031$), so it does not come from Poisson counting
in sparse bins. It disappears when the data variance is the prediction at the nominal parameters
($+0.000$, $-0.004$, $-0.001$): the combined Neyman--Pearson variance depends on the fluctuating
data, which correlates the weight of a bin with its own fluctuation. The bias is small, $0.3$--$0.7\%$
on the normalisation against a $16$--$21\%$ uncertainty, but avoidable, so the fit uses the Pearson
form, which every data result uses. The pull widths are $1.00$ and the coverage $68\%$ in every
variant.

\subsection{Selection-optimisation study}
\label{sec:supp_optim}

\paragraph{Choice of phase space and opening-angle cut.} The
measured phase space and the $\theta_{\mu\pi}<2.6$~rad cut were chosen on the Run-1 FHC

\section[Analysis pipeline and automated guards]{Analysis pipeline and automated guards\scopetag{shared}}
\label{sec:pipeline}
% =============================================================================

Every released number is produced by the \textsc{XSecAnalyzer} chain in four stages that
are re-runnable from the raw PeLEE ntuples:
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep1pt
  \item the event processing applies the selection(s) to each PeLEE ntuple and writes the
    processed event files, for the neutrino overlays, the dirt overlay and the per-run
    beam-off samples;
  \item the universe builder constructs, per observable and configuration, the true and
    reconstructed histograms of every systematic universe from the files listed in that
    configuration;
  \item the unfolder sums the universes with the per-run exposure scaling, builds the
    covariance, unfolds, and writes the closure sidecar, the systematics dump and the
    matrix tables;
  \item the export step writes the data release (curves, $A_C$ matrices, covariance
    decompositions); the figure macros draw from the closure sidecars; the one-bin total
    uses the same chain with the single-bin configurations; the count-level tables (cut
    flow, cut-and-count check, control-region yields) are produced with the same weights
    and scalings.
\end{enumerate}

Five automated guards protect the normalisation, the currency of released artefacts,
the result tables and the blinding:
\begin{itemize}\itemsep1pt
  \item A normalisation manifest reads the authoritative configuration files and asserts
    that exposure, flux, target count and beam-off scaling agree across all
    configurations, and that every declared systematic is backed by a file.
  \item A coupling assertion in the unfolder refuses a configuration whose declared
    detector variations are not present in its file list.
  \item A staleness check refuses to ship any released artefact or result figure older
    than the extraction it depicts, and compares the integrals printed on the $p_\pi$
    figures with their sidecars.
  \item A table check parses every result table of the analysis notes and this supplement and fails if a value
    disagrees with the released results table, if a table does not sit under its own
    label, or if a row's column count differs from its header.
  \item A blinding guard treats a beam-on file without MC truth as real data and refuses it
    unless an explicit unblinding switch is set; the same detection selects the beam-off
    treatment (added to pseudo-data, subtracted from real data), so no manual switch
    exists to forget.
\end{itemize}
The release was built from the processed ntuples in a single pass with every cached
universe deleted, so no intermediate result is carried over. A clean-cache rebuild of the
same kind reproduced a previous release bin by bin to $10^{-9}$ (recorded in the change
log); repeating that comparison on the release to be unblinded is on the pre-unblinding
list (prerequisite U6, \noteref{Sec.}{sec:limitations}).

\section[The muon angle: \texorpdfstring{$\cos\theta_\mu$}{cos(theta mu)} versus \texorpdfstring{$\theta_\mu$}{theta mu}]{The muon angle: \texorpdfstring{$\cos\theta_\mu$}{cos(theta mu)} versus \texorpdfstring{$\theta_\mu$}{theta mu}\scopetag{inclusive}}
\label{sec:supp_ang}

\noindent\emph{Historical configuration, not used in the submitted results. The change log gives the chronology.}

\subsection{Angular variable: $\cos\theta_\mu$ versus $\theta_\mu$}
\label{sec:thetavar}

The muon angle is reported only as $\cos\theta_\mu$. The binning review
(\S\ref{sec:binreview}) shows that the same partition of the angle retains up to one degree of
freedom more as $\cos\theta_\mu$ than as $\theta_\mu$, because the regulariser acts on the density,
so a $\theta_\mu$ result adds no information to a $\cos\theta_\mu$ result with the same bins.
The comparison below uses the five-bin $\cos\theta_\mu$ and $\theta_\mu$ binnings that
preceded the review.

In those binnings the two variables spanned the same full range but were different
partitions of it: the $\theta_\mu$ edges ($0.43$, $0.62$, $0.84$, $1.17$~rad) are not the
$\arccos$ images of the $\cos\theta_\mu$ edges ($0.45$, $0.64$, $0.86$, $1.10$~rad). The two
behave very differently under the additional-smearing matrix $A_C$ of the Wiener--SVD unfolding,
which affects how
the measurement compares to models.

In $\cos\theta_\mu$ the signal is sharply forward-peaked: the last two bins
($\cos\theta_\mu>0.8$) hold most of the rate, and $A_C$ suppresses the integral of any
truth-level prediction strongly (row sums $0.17$--$0.39$ in FHC at that binning,
against $0.66$--$0.91$ for $p_\mu$). Because the same $A_C$ is applied to the data, the tune
and every generator, they are compared on equal footing.

The suppression is not a property of the shape of the truth distribution. Across the
observables the $A_C$ integral factor shows no correlation with peakedness: $W_{\pi p}$ is
the sharpest distribution in the analysis (peak-to-minimum ratio $36$) yet its factor is
$0.75$, $\delta p_T$ is comparatively flat (ratio $10$) at $0.70$, and the six-bin
$W_\mathrm{had}$ then under study, neither sharp nor flat, was the most suppressed at $0.61$; the
inclusive $\cos\theta_\mu$ factors sat far below all of
these ($0.17$--$0.39$ against $0.66$--$0.91$ for $p_\mu$) because its response is the
worst conditioned, not because its peak is the sharpest. Within $\cos\theta_\mu$ itself
NEUT and NuWro are the flattest of the five truth-level curves (peak-to-minimum
$3.8$ and $4.3$, against $16.3$ for the tune) and are smeared no differently from the
others: no smeared generator bin is negative and both sit above the tune in
every bin. $A_C$ is the Wiener
filter acting on the SVD modes of the response: it removes whatever the data cannot
statistically constrain, which is set by the conditioning of the response for that
observable and binning, not by the shape of the truth distribution, and it is not
norm-preserving, so it can suppress or enhance.

\paragraph{$\theta_\mu$ with the width-aware operator.} The pseudo-data are Poisson throws from the
MicroBooNE tune, so the tune is the model that should fit best, and it does
(FHC $\theta_\mu$, $\chi^2$ on five bins: tune $2.14$, $p=0.83$; GENIE $5.55$; GiBUU
$6.07$; NEUT $8.46$; NuWro $9.10$; closure $1.52$, $p=0.91$). Under the uniform stencil (\S\ref{sec:reg_widths})
every model gave $2.8$--$4.7$, indistinguishable, because every model had been mapped onto
the same curve. At those binnings the $\theta_\mu$ $\chi^2$ were systematically larger than the
$\cos\theta_\mu$ ones for every model, the truth included.

\section[Golden-pion classifier: details]{Golden-pion classifier: details\scopetag{inclusive}}
\label{sec:golden_details}

\noindent\emph{Rejected alternative, not used in the submitted results. The change log gives the chronology.}

\subsection{Available inputs}
The BNB note supplies a tested recipe, and all four of its BDT inputs are
available in this analysis. The Bragg likelihood ratios (\texttt{trk\_bragg\_pion},
\texttt{\_mip}, \texttt{\_mu}, \texttt{\_p}) and the track--shower score are in the
ntuples; the multi-pion pion-ID BDT in this analysis trains on exactly those,
and the descendant counts are \texttt{pfp\_trk\_daughters\_v} /
\texttt{pfp\_shr\_daughters\_v}. Wiggliness is available under a different name: the PeLEE
ntuples carry \texttt{trk\_avg\_deflection\_stdev\_v} (with companions
\texttt{\_mean\_v} and \texttt{\_separation\_mean\_v}), which is the standard
deviation of the angular deflection between adjacent track points. It is fully populated
and is carried forward by \texttt{ProcessNTuples}.

\subsection{Track wiggliness as a golden-pion tag}
The variable was tested on truth-matched charged pions in the Run-1 FHC overlay, calling a
pion ``golden-like'' when the mass-corrected range momentum recovers the true momentum to
within $20\%$, and ``interacting'' when it recovers less than $60\%$. The two populations
separate clearly, but golden pions are the wigglier ones, by about a factor two in the
mean ($0.0048$ against $0.0027$), the opposite of the naive expectation. This is not a
momentum artefact: it persists when binned in track length, with the ratio staying at
$0.51$--$0.63$ from $5$~cm to $400$~cm.

The sign has a physical explanation. A pion that stops travels its full range and
scatters strongly through its final slow centimetres, so the deflection spread averaged
along the track is large. A pion that interacts hadronically is cut short while still fast
and never reaches that wiggly end, so its track is straighter. High wiggliness is thus a
signature of having stopped, which is the population for which a range-based
momentum is meaningful.

As a single cut it is useful: on this sample the golden fraction is $39.9\%$ before
any cut, and requiring \texttt{trk\_avg\_deflection\_stdev\_v}$\,>0.001$ keeps $86.8\%$ of
golden pions while rejecting $48.8\%$ of interacting ones, raising the golden fraction to
$53.0\%$. A tighter cut at $0.002$ gives $57.8\%$ efficiency and $55.7\%$ golden. For
comparison, the BNB four-variable BDT reaches $67.7\%$ golden at roughly half the
generic-selection efficiency, so one variable recovers a good part of the gain, and the
remaining three variables, all available here, could recover more.

Three options follow, in increasing order of effort: rebin $p_\pi$ against
a migration criterion, which on the numbers above means one or two resolved intervals at
threshold plus a wide open region; add a golden-pion cut for the $p_\pi$ cross section
built from the available variables, accepting the factor-two efficiency loss the
BNB analysis accepts; or reproduce the BNB BDT in full. None changes the total or angular
results, which do not use this estimator. The first option, applied with the $0.50$
criterion of the analysis (\S\ref{sec:binreview}), gives the adopted binning
(\S\ref{sec:ppi2bin_adopted}); the classifier was trained and tested and does not
change this conclusion (\S\ref{sec:golden_bdt}).

\subsection{Variable ranking}
The variable ranking of the classifier of \S\ref{sec:golden_bdt} is dominated by the deflection
variables (\texttt{wmean}, then \texttt{wstd}); the Bragg ratios contribute essentially nothing,
although Bragg was expected to carry the stopping signature.

\subsection{Track-sample and analysis-sample diagonals}
Table~\ref{tab:golden_compare} is built on the track sample (\S\ref{sec:ppi_samples}). On it the
five-bin scheme reads $99/24/13/6/6\%$ and the two-bin scheme $98/62\%$, against $96/35/21/10/6\%$ and
$91/76\%$ on the analysis sample. The track-sample figures are therefore not the analysis response,
and the $62\%$ does not mean that the inclusive $p_\pi$ measurement fails the usable-bin criterion: on
the analysis sample the upper bin of the two-bin scheme is at $76\%$. The comparisons made within the
table, between rows of the same sample, are valid as they stand.

\subsection{Multiple Coulomb scattering as estimator and classifier input}
Range
saturates because the pion interacts before stopping, which
truncates the track. Multiple-Coulomb-scattering (MCS) momentum is inferred from the
scattering along whatever track exists and is not truncated by an interaction, so it is the
natural candidate to replace range. The CC0$\pi$ analysis uses this pairing, range
for contained tracks and MCS for exiting ones. Measured on $122\,878$ truth-matched pion
tracks (\texttt{trk\_mcs\_muon\_mom\_v}, filled for $99.9\%$ of them):

\noindent In the top bin range is biased to $0.319$ (the saturation), while MCS is
essentially unbiased at $1.011$; but MCS carries a $210\%$ RMS
there and $1400\%$ in the lowest bin, which makes it unusable as a per-event estimator. The
reason is structural and explains why the CC0$\pi$ recipe does not transfer: that analysis
applies MCS to exiting tracks, which are long by construction, whereas the problematic pions
here are contained but interacting, $25$--$84$~cm long, and MCS needs of order a metre.

The CC0$\pi$ note also uses the disagreement between the two estimators as a track-quality
BDT input rather than as an estimator. The motivation applies here: a pion that interacts has a
truncated range but an untruncated MCS, so the fractional difference
$(p_\mathrm{range}-p_\mathrm{MCS})/p_\mathrm{range}$ should flag the events that
degrade the response. Added to the golden-pion classifier, it ranks second of ten

\noindent The new variable is highly informative but not additionally informative: it is
strongly correlated with the deflection variables already present, since both measure whether
the track scattered heavily and ended abruptly. A high importance rank with an unchanged ROC
integral is the signature of a redundant input. This independently supports the ceiling
argument above: the classifier already extracts what the golden label can offer, and the limit
is the physics of the label rather than the quality of the classifier.

A further variant was not pursued. Retraining on the label ``range is
accurate'', $|p_\mathrm{corr}/p_\mathrm{true}-1|<0.2$, would target the quantity of interest
directly and would raise the diagonals, but it would select on the answer:
keeping events where the estimator happens to be right sculpts the response by construction
and inflates the diagonal without measuring anything, the same effect described for the tight
BDT working points below. The golden label is used because it is a physical property
of the pion, independent of how well it was reconstructed.


\subsection{Tight working points}
The $>0.15$ row of Table~\ref{tab:golden_compare} appears better than
perfect truth selection ($46.9, 42.2, 36.1, 19.5\%$), which is impossible for an unbiased
classifier. This arises because a hard cut on
the classifier preferentially keeps long tracks: inside the top true bin, the surviving
tracks have a mean length of $120.8$~cm against $78.2$~cm for all pions, and a mean
reconstructed momentum of $0.406$ against $0.307$~GeV/$c$. The cut is thus partly a cut on
the reconstructed momentum itself, which raises the diagonal by construction while sculpting
the spectrum. The apparent gain is circular, and the tight working points are not used.
This is the failure mode anticipated for track length as a direct input; it enters here
through the deflection variables, which correlate with length, even though length itself was
excluded from training.

\subsection{Residual bias of golden pions}
Golden pions still reconstruct at
$\langle p^{\rm corr}/p^{\rm true}\rangle = 0.73$ on the track sample. Interaction is therefore
not the only problem: the momentum conversion is itself biased. A single flat rescaling does
not repair it: applying the best global factor drops the first bin from $98.9$ to
$49.1\%$ and leaves $0/5$ bins passing, because the bias is momentum-dependent, as
the saturation curve of Figure~\ref{fig:ppi_sat} shows.

Coarser binning helps but is not sufficient. Collapsing to two bins ($0.175$,
$0.230$, $\infty$) gives $98.7$ and $58.3\%$ with perfect golden selection, an improvement
but still short of $0.68$. Only a single bin, i.e.\ no differential measurement,
passes trivially.

Its effect on the binning is a redistribution of the migration rather than an overall
improvement. The six-bin diagonals move
from $98/30/19/19/13/6\%$ to $63/38/33/43/52/56\%$: the top bin improves nearly tenfold, but
the first bin degrades from $98\%$ to $63\%$, and in the two-bin scheme the worst bin falls

toward the sample centre, and it damages the low-$W$ region where the resonance physics sits.
The same pattern appears in $p_\pi$ itself, where the first bin falls from $99.2\%$ to
$60.6\%$ while the top bin rises from $0.5\%$ to $33.2\%$.

Two considerations argue against adopting it. The estimator would be MC-trained and would
sit upstream of every observable, so its model dependence would become a systematic on every
result rather than on one cut. And the trade it offers moves measurability out of the region
of interest. It is not adopted.

\paragraph{Remaining handles.} The ceiling measurements above bound what is available:
a perfect pion momentum would give $W_{\pi p}$ a resolution of $0.046$, a factor of three
better than the regression achieves and enough to support several bins. The information is
carried away by the interaction products, so the approach that addresses the cause is to
recover their energy: the pion and its daughters treated as a mini-calorimeter, using the
per-PFP calorimetric energies and daughter links already present in the input files. For the
proton-tagged sample there is a second, independent handle in transverse kinematic imbalance,
where the muon and proton constrain the pion transverse momentum without using the pion's own
reconstruction at all, at the cost of Fermi-motion smearing of order $250$~MeV/$c$. The
transverse-imbalance route has not been tried; the daughter-recovery route was tested next.



\paragraph{Recovery of the interaction products.} The ceiling

interacts, so the natural remedy is to treat the pion and its products as a single
calorimeter, and this was tested. PeLEE carries no parent index, so association is geometric,

\paragraph{Directional cut against residual cosmics.} Part of the
surviving beam-off sample is thought to consist of broken cosmic muon tracks, in which the
break is reconstructed as a vertex and the track direction bears no relation to it. Such
events carry a directional signature, which is seen in the data: among selected events the
beam-off sample is enriched at backward reconstructed muon angles by nearly an order of magnitude
relative to signal.

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
reco $\cos\theta_\mu$ & EXT (beam-off) & MC signal & enrichment \\\midrule
$<0$    & $31.8\%$ & $4.1\%$ & $7.8\times$ \\
$<-0.5$ & $9.1\%$  & $1.4\%$ & $6.4\times$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent Despite this enrichment, a cut does not pay off. At the final cut the FHC sample is $833$
signal, $23$ beam-off and $1\,418$ predicted events in total (\noteref{Table}{tab:cutflow}). A
$\cos\theta_\mu<0$ requirement would remove about $32\%$ of the beam-off background, about $7$ events,
and about $4\%$ of the signal, about $33$ events. The beam-off background is subtracted from the data
directly, and that subtraction is unbiased by construction, so removing part of it only reduces the
statistical uncertainty of the subtraction. That uncertainty is $4.2$ events for the $23$, dominated by

The same bound applies to any cosmic cut. The beam-off background is $1.6\%$ of the selected sample
and is subtracted without bias, so removing all of it with no signal loss would gain about $4$ events
of statistical uncertainty on a sample of $1\,418$. Any cut costing more than about $0.5\%$ of the
signal is a net loss regardless of how efficiently it tags cosmic rays. The residual beam-off
contribution is therefore left in place and handled by subtraction.

A more selective variant has not been tested. The CC0$\pi$ analysis tags mis-directed tracks with
a calorimetric direction-preference flag in coincidence with a poor
forward-muon fit, $\chi^2_\mu>6$, which fires on tracks whose $dE/dx$ profile disagrees with
the assigned direction rather than on backward-going tracks as such. That would not cut
genuine backward muons, which outnumber mis-reconstructed ones here by roughly $8{:}1$. Both
quantities exist in the input ntuples but are not carried into the processed files. Such a
cut is bounded by the same $\approx6$-event ceiling on the EXT statistical uncertainty, so it
could not bring a significant improvement.

\subsection{Production mechanisms}
\label{sec:supp_prodmech}

At neutrino energies of 0.5--5~GeV, charged-current single charged-pion
production proceeds through three mechanisms.

\textbf{Baryon resonance production} dominates at $E_\nu \lesssim 2$~GeV.
The neutrino excites the struck nucleon to a baryon resonance, most prominently
the $\Delta(1232)$ isobar, which then decays to a nucleon and pion:
\begin{equation}
  \nu_\mu n \;\to\; \mu^- \Delta^{++} \;\to\; \mu^- \pi^+ p, \qquad
  \nu_\mu p \;\to\; \mu^- \Delta^{+} \;\to\; \mu^- \pi^0 p
    \;\text{(or }\pi^+ n\text{)}.
\end{equation}
The standard model is that of Rein and Sehgal~\cite{rs1981}, which uses the
Feynman--Kislinger--Ravndal (FKR) quark model to compute form factors for all
baryon resonances up to $W \lesssim 2$~GeV.  The modern implementation used in
GENIE for resonance (RES) production is the
Kuzmin--Lyubushkin--Naumov Berger--Sehgal (KLN-BS)
model~\cite{kuzmin2004,graczyk2008,berger2007,nowak2009}, which combines
the lepton-mass-corrected vector and axial form factors of
Kuzmin \textit{et al.}~\cite{kuzmin2004} with the updated $\Delta(1232)$
electromagnetic form factors of Graczyk and Sobczyk~\cite{graczyk2008} and
the improved treatment of the $\Delta$ line shape and the lepton-mass
effects of Berger and Sehgal~\cite{berger2007}. Berger and Sehgal also discuss the
interference between the resonant and non-resonant amplitudes; that interference term is
not implemented in the GENIE~v3 configuration underlying the \uboone{} tune~\cite{uboone_tune}, where the
non-resonant contribution is added incoherently. Higher resonances
($N(1440)P_{11}$, $N(1520)D_{13}$, etc.) are included up to
$W \lesssim 2$~GeV and contribute at $E_\nu \gtrsim 1.5$~GeV.

\textbf{Non-resonant and DIS backgrounds.}
A non-resonant continuum contributes at all $W$; in a full amplitude-level
treatment it interferes (constructively or destructively by isospin) with the resonant
amplitude, although the generators used here add it incoherently.  Deep-inelastic
scattering (DIS) becomes significant above $W \approx 1.6$~GeV.  Together
these processes represent $\sim$20\% of the CC$\pi^{\pm}$ rate in the energy range
of this analysis and are modelled in GENIE via the Bodek--Yang formalism.

\textbf{Coherent pion production.}
The neutrino can scatter coherently off the entire nucleus~\cite{rs_coherent,berger_sehgal_coherent},
$\nu_\mu A \to \mu^- \pi^+ A^*$, leaving the nucleus in its ground state.
The cross section is small ($\lesssim 10\%$ of the total) below 5~GeV. The signal
definition of \noteref{Sec.}{sec:signal} is a final-state topology (one muon, one charged pion,
no other mesons) with no truth-level veto on the interaction mode, so coherent
$\mathrm{CC}\pi^{\pm}$ events that satisfy it are included in the signal as a small
component, not treated as background.

\subsection{Nuclear effects and final-state interactions}
\label{sec:supp_fsi}

On a heavy nucleus such as argon ($A = 40$), two classes of nuclear correction
modify the CC$\pi^{\pm}$ rate and kinematics significantly.

\textbf{Initial-state effects.} Fermi motion of the struck nucleon broadens
kinematic distributions, and Pauli blocking suppresses transitions to low-momentum
nucleon final states near threshold. Nuclear binding also shifts the effective
nucleon mass relative to free-nucleon predictions.

\textbf{Final-state interactions (FSI).}  The produced pion must traverse the
nuclear medium before being detected. Intranuclear rescattering occurs via:
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
  \item \textit{Pion absorption} ($\pi A \to NN\ldots$): probability $\sim$25--30\%
    on Ar; converts CC$\pi^{\pm}$ events into CC0$\pi$ topology.
  \item \textit{Charge exchange} ($\pi^+ n \to \pi^0 p$): produces a CC$\pi^0$ event.
  \item \textit{Quasi-elastic scattering}: pion survives but changes direction;
    modifies $p_\pi$ and $\theta_\pi$ distributions.
  \item \textit{Inelastic production} ($\pi N \to \pi\pi N$): rare below
    $p_\pi \approx 400$~MeV/$c$.
\end{itemize}
FSI are modelled in GENIE via the INTRANUKE (hA) cascade. Because absorption and
charge exchange mix CC$\pi^{\pm}$, CC$\pi^0$, and CC0$\pi$ topologies at the
$\sim$30\% level, the signal in any CC$\pi$ analysis is necessarily defined by
the final-state particle content observed in the detector, not by the underlying
production process (\noteref{Sec.}{sec:signal}).

\subsection{Generator predictions and known tensions}
\label{sec:supp_generators}

Major neutrino event generators reproduce the inclusive CC cross section to
10--15\% but show systematic spread in the CC$\pi^{\pm}$ rate, owing to differences
in the resonance model, the treatment of the transition region between resonance
and DIS, and the FSI cascade.

\paragraph{GENIE v3~\cite{genie,genie3}.}
For resonance production GENIE implements the KLN-BS
model~\cite{kuzmin2004,graczyk2008,berger2007,nowak2009} for the full tower of
18 baryon resonances up to $W \lesssim 2$~GeV, including the
$\Delta(1232)P_{33}$, $N(1440)P_{11}$ (Roper), $N(1520)D_{13}$,
$N(1535)S_{11}$, $\Delta(1620)S_{31}$, $\Delta(1700)D_{33}$,
$N(1680)F_{15}$, $\Delta(1950)F_{37}$, and their isospin partners.
The hadronic invariant mass range $1.0 < W < 1.7$~GeV is bridged by a
resonance--DIS transition using the AGKY (Andreopoulos--Gallagher--Kehayias--Yang)
model, which applies KNO scaling in the transition region and the Bodek--Yang
DIS model above $W \approx 1.7$~GeV.
Non-resonant single-pion production is included through the low-$W$ extension of
the Bodek--Yang DIS model and is added incoherently to the resonant contribution; no
resonant/non-resonant interference is implemented.
Coherent $\pi^+$ production uses the Rein--Sehgal coherent model.
FSI are handled by the INTRANUKE \textit{hA} empirical model, in which each hadron
undergoes at most one effective interaction whose probability and outcome are tuned to
hadron--nucleus data. The alternative \textit{hN} microscopic cascade, also
available in GENIE, propagates hadrons step by step using free-nucleon $\pi N$ and
$NN$ cross sections.
GENIE has been reported to over-predict the CC$\pi^{\pm}$ rate by 20--35\% relative to
bubble-chamber and MiniBooNE data in several comparisons when normalised to the BNL
dataset; the tension is reduced but not eliminated in the AR23 tune.

\paragraph{NuWro~\cite{nuwro}.}
NuWro models resonance production via the $\Delta(1232)$ only;
no higher baryon resonances are included.
The $\Delta$ is treated with the Paschos--Yu formalism for the
$\Delta$ excitation amplitude, retaining the full angular structure of the
pion decay.
A smooth analytic interpolation connects the single-$\Delta$ resonance
region to the DIS regime at higher hadronic invariant mass
($W \gtrsim 1.4$~GeV), using Bodek--Yang structure functions for the deep
inelastic contribution; the transition avoids a hard boundary between
resonance and continuum.
Non-resonant single-pion production is included as a separate amplitude.
FSI are implemented using the microscopic Salcedo--Oset model, which treats
pion propagation in nuclear matter through the pion self-energy in the
random-phase approximation, naturally accounting for medium modifications
such as $\Delta$ broadening and pion absorption via two-body processes
($\pi N N \to N N$) that are absent in purely cascade-based approaches.
NuWro has historically shown better agreement with MiniBooNE and bubble-chamber
data than GENIE, partly attributable to the absence of overestimated higher-resonance
contributions.

\paragraph{NEUT~\cite{neut,neut2021}.}
NEUT, the generator used by T2K and Super-Kamiokande, implements resonance
production via a modified Rein--Sehgal model including 18 resonances up to
$W \lesssim 2$~GeV, with Graczyk--Sobczyk~\cite{graczyk2008} electromagnetic
form factors for the $\Delta(1232)$.
The axial mass $M_A^{\rm RES}$ is a tunable parameter; T2K applies a data-driven
tune that reduces the overall CC$\pi^{\pm}$ normalisation by $\sim$10--20\% relative
to the default.
The transition to DIS is handled similarly to GENIE, using GRV98 LO parton
distribution functions with Bodek--Yang corrections above $W \approx 2$~GeV.
Coherent pion production follows the Rein--Sehgal coherent model with a
Berger--Sehgal-style correction for finite lepton mass.
FSI are modelled by a custom NEUT intranuclear cascade that tracks each produced
hadron through the nucleus using step-by-step interactions sampled from $\pi N$
cross sections, with nuclear density and mean-free-path corrections derived from
the optical potential.

\paragraph{GiBUU~\cite{gibuu}.}
The Giessen Boltzmann--Uehling--Uhlenbeck (GiBUU) generator takes a different
approach: instead of a probabilistic step-by-step cascade, it solves a
coupled set of kinetic transport equations for the full hadronic system inside the
nuclear medium.
Resonance production includes the $\Delta(1232)$ and higher resonances whose
in-medium spectral functions are broadened by collisional damping; the $\Delta$
width in the nuclear medium is substantially larger than its free-space value of
$\Gamma_0 \approx 118$~MeV, suppressing threshold production.
Pion propagation after production is governed by a mean-field potential (the pion
optical potential), which shifts pion momenta as they traverse the nucleus. This
effect is absent in cascade models and is particularly important for low-momentum pions
($p_\pi \lesssim 200$~MeV/$c$) near the pion production threshold.
The transition to DIS uses PYTHIA for string fragmentation above
$W \approx 1.6$~GeV.
GiBUU predictions for pion angular distributions and for the backward-hemisphere
$d\sigma/d\cos\theta_\pi$ can differ substantially from those of cascade-based
generators, which makes GiBUU a useful cross-check of FSI systematics.

Because the present results are extracted on central-value pseudo-data, no data/MC
deficit is measured here. The analysis does provide the spread of the standalone
generators in the measured phase space, a factor of $\sim\!1.6$ between GENIE and NEUT
(Table~\ref{tab:total_xsec}), which brackets the injected \uboone{} tune. The over-prediction
seen for several generator configurations in parts of the relevant phase space in the
measurements surveyed below is the context for that spread, not a result of this analysis.

\subsubsection*{Deuterium bubble chambers: ANL and BNL (1979--1986)}

The ANL 12-foot bubble chamber~\cite{anl_radecky1982} and BNL 7-foot bubble
chamber~\cite{bnl_kitagaki1986} provided the first high-statistics measurements of
$\nu_\mu d \to \mu^- \pi^+ pp$ in the 1--3~GeV range. Because the deuterium target
is close to a free nucleon, these measurements need smaller nuclear corrections than
those on heavier targets. The ANL and BNL total cross
sections disagree by $\sim$15\% in absolute normalisation, a discrepancy long
attributed to flux uncertainties; the BNL flux is believed to be the better
constrained. This ANL--BNL tension propagated directly into the spread of
generator predictions: models tuned to ANL tend to predict lower rates than
those tuned to BNL. The MINERvA experiment~\cite{minerva_lownu} subsequently
resolved much of this tension by simultaneously constraining the flux and the
cross section.

\subsubsection*{K2K near detector (2008)}

The K2K experiment~\cite{k2k_rodriguez2008} made the first accelerator-based
total CC$\pi^+$ cross-section measurement on a carbon target at
$\langle E_\nu \rangle = 1.3$~GeV using its fine-grained near detector. The
result was consistent with NEUT predictions and established the nuclear-target
correction from deuterium to carbon at intermediate energies.

\subsubsection*{MiniBooNE ($\nu_\mu$ on CH$_2$, BNB beam, 2011)}

The MiniBooNE detector (818-tonne mineral-oil Cherenkov detector) published
differential CC$\pi^+$ cross sections on CH$_2$ in the Booster Neutrino Beam
($E_\nu^{\rm peak} \approx 0.7$~GeV)~\cite{miniboone_ccpi}. MiniBooNE was the
first experiment to provide high-statistics differential distributions as a
function of $Q^2$, the invariant hadronic mass $W$, and the outgoing muon and
pion kinematics. The flux-integrated total cross section was measured to be
$\sigma \approx 3.4\times10^{-38}$~cm$^2$ per CH$_2$ molecule, or equivalently
$\sim2.0\times10^{-38}$~cm$^2$ per carbon nucleus at $\langle E_\nu \rangle
\approx 1$~GeV~\cite{uboone_bnb2026_ccpi}. The measured rate exceeded the NUANCE
prediction by $\sim$20\%, so that model under-predicted the data. The
``generator over-prediction'' discussed in the later literature is the opposite
tension, in which GENIE-based predictions lie above subsequent measurements in parts of
the phase space; it depends on the generator and the phase space and is not universal. The hydrogen content of
CH$_2$ complicates the extraction of the carbon-only cross section, and the
spherical Cherenkov geometry makes it difficult to measure low-energy pions
or reconstruct proton recoils. These limitations led to the subsequent adoption of
finer-grained tracking detectors and liquid-argon targets.

\subsubsection*{MINERvA ($\nu_\mu$ on CH, NuMI medium energy, 2015--)}

The MINERvA experiment~\cite{minerva_ccpi} uses a finely-segmented
scintillator-strip tracking calorimeter in the NuMI medium-energy beam
($\langle E_\nu \rangle \approx 3.6$~GeV). It has published the most
kinematically complete CC$\pi^+$ measurements to date, reporting differential
cross sections as a function of: pion momentum $p_\pi$, pion angle $\theta_\pi$,
muon momentum $p_\mu$, muon angle $\theta_\mu$, and the full set of Adler
angles describing the pion in the hadronic centre-of-mass frame. MINERvA also
measured CC$\pi^+$ on iron and lead targets~\cite{minerva_fe}, providing direct
constraints on nuclear $A$-dependence at the same beam energy. GENIE
consistently over-predicts MINERvA by 20--35\% across all kinematic variables
in the $\Delta(1232)$ peak, the same qualitative tension seen at lower energies.
The higher beam energy at MINERvA means that the $p_\pi > 0.5$~GeV/$c$ region
is well populated, which complements the MicroBooNE NuMI result, statistically
limited at high pion momentum.

\subsubsection*{T2K ND280 ($\nu_\mu$ on CH, off-axis beam, 2020)}

T2K measured CC$\pi^+$ at its near detector ND280 in the off-axis T2K beam
(OAB), which peaks at $\langle E_\nu \rangle \approx 0.85$~GeV~\cite{t2k_ccpip}.
The T2K OAB spectrum closely overlaps with the MicroBooNE NuMI off-axis spectrum
in the kinematically sensitive 0.3--1.5~GeV range, so this is the prior measurement
most directly comparable to the present work. T2K reported differential
cross sections in $p_\mu$ and $\cos\theta_\mu$, finding a data/MC deficit of
$\sim$30\% relative to NEUT. The carbon target ($A = 12$) differs from argon
($A = 40$), but the overlapping beam spectrum enables direct comparison of
nuclear-mass dependence at fixed neutrino energy.

\subsubsection*{ArgoNEUT ($\nu_\mu + \bar{\nu}_\mu$ on Ar, NuMI, 2018)}

ArgoNEUT~\cite{argoneut_ccpi} was a 175-litre LArTPC placed upstream of the
MINOS near detector in the NuMI low-energy beam
($\langle E_\nu \rangle \approx 9.6$~GeV).  Using $1.25\times10^{20}$~POT,
ArgoNEUT selected 115~$\nu_\mu$ and 158~$\bar{\nu}_\mu$ CC$\pi^+$ candidate
events after background subtraction~\cite{argoneut_ccpi}, publishing the
first CC$\pi^+$ total cross section on argon as a function of neutrino energy.
The result was consistent with GENIE predictions at these high energies, where
sensitivity to the $\Delta(1232)$ resonance is suppressed. The LArTPC technology
and argon target make ArgoNEUT the direct predecessor to MicroBooNE, but the
factor $\sim$10 higher beam energy means that extrapolation to the
$E_\nu < 2$~GeV regime relevant for oscillation experiments requires generator
assumptions and limits the constraining power on FSI models in the resonance
region.

\subsubsection*{MicroBooNE BNB channel: inclusive precedent (2022)}

The same MicroBooNE detector used in this analysis published the first
measurement of energy-dependent inclusive $\nu_\mu$ charged-current cross
sections on argon in the Booster Neutrino Beam~\cite{genieccpi}
($E_\nu^{\rm peak} \approx 0.7$~GeV), establishing the detector's absolute
cross-section capability on argon together with the BNB flux modelling and
reconstruction performance relevant to the present work.  The dedicated
exclusive CC$\pi^+$ measurement in the same beam followed on the full BNB
dataset (below), and it is that exclusive result to which the present NuMI
CC$\pi^+$ measurement is most directly compared.

\subsubsection*{MicroBooNE BNB channel: full dataset (2026)}

Reference~\cite{uboone_bnb2026_ccpi} reports CC$\pi^+$ differential cross
sections using the complete MicroBooNE BNB dataset of
$1.11\times10^{21}$~POT~\cite{uboone_bnb2026_ccpi}, selecting 6816 events after
background subtraction and measuring differential cross sections in muon momentum,
muon angle, pion momentum and pion angle, four of the five observables of the
present analysis.  The flux-integrated total cross section is
\begin{equation*}
  \sigma = (3.75 \pm 0.07_{\rm stat} \pm 0.80_{\rm syst})\times10^{-38}~\text{cm}^2/\text{Ar}
\end{equation*}
at mean neutrino energy $\langle E_\nu \rangle \approx 0.8$~GeV. Most generators
reproduce the data within uncertainties; a notable exception is the muon angle with
respect to the beam, where all tested generators overestimate the cross section at
very forward angles ($\cos\theta_\mu \to 1$)~\cite{uboone_bnb2026_ccpi}. That
pion momentum differential cross section was the first such measurement on an argon
target.

The BNB and NuMI analyses probe complementary phase space: BNB is on-axis with a
narrow $\nu_\mu$ spectrum peaked at $\sim$700~MeV and negligible $\bar{\nu}_\mu$
content, while NuMI FHC at 135~mrad off-axis provides a softer, broader spectrum
with a $\sim$35\% $\bar{\nu}_\mu$ admixture and extends sensitivity to
$E_\nu \sim 0.1$--$0.5$~GeV.

\subsubsection*{MicroBooNE NuMI $\nu_e + \bar{\nu}_e$ CC$\pi^+$ (2025)}

Reference~\cite{uboone_nue_ccpi} presents the first measurement of
$\nu_e + \bar{\nu}_e$ CC single charged-pion differential cross sections on argon,
using the full MicroBooNE NuMI dataset ($2.0\times10^{21}$~POT, FHC and
RHC combined). The observable set (electron energy $E_e$, electron angle
$\theta_e$, pion angle $\theta_\pi$, and electron--pion opening angle
$\theta_{e\pi}$) mirrors the muonic observables of the present analysis.
The flux-integrated total cross section is measured to be
\begin{equation*}
  \sigma = (0.93 \pm 0.13_{\rm stat} \pm 0.27_{\rm syst})
  \times10^{-39}~\text{cm}^2/\text{nucleon}
\end{equation*}
at a mean $\nu_e$ energy of 730~MeV, in good agreement with all tested generator
predictions (GENIE, NuWro, NEUT, GiBUU) within the combined uncertainties.
Prior to this measurement, CC$\pi^+$ production by electron neutrinos on argon
had not been measured; the result is an input to the DUNE $\nu_e$-appearance
analysis, where CC$\pi^+$ events are a background to the $\nu_e$ quasi-elastic
signal.
