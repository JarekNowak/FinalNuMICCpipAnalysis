  {\LARGE\bfseries Measurement of $\nu_\mu + \bar{\nu}_\mu$ Charged-Current
  Single Pion Production Differential Cross Sections on Argon
  with the MicroBooNE Detector in the NuMI Beam\par}
  \vspace{0.8cm}
  {\large Analysis note for the full FHC, RHC and combined exposures\par}


  \vspace{1.5cm}
  {\large Ishanee Pophale and Jarek Nowak\par}
  \vspace{0.5cm}
  {\normalsize\textit{Lancaster University}\par}
  \vspace{2cm}
  \begin{abstract}
    We measure flux-averaged $\nu_\mu+\bar\nu_\mu$ charged-current single-charged-pion
    (CC$\pi^{\pm}$) cross sections on argon with the full \uboone{} NuMI exposure, in three
    configurations: forward horn current (FHC, $7.766\times10^{20}$~POT), reverse horn current
    (RHC, $11.082\times10^{20}$~POT) and their combination ($18.848\times10^{20}$~POT).

    The selection requires a muon candidate and exactly one contained charged-pion candidate
    attached to a vertex in the fiducial volume, vetoes electromagnetic showers and limits the
    muon--pion opening angle to $2.6$~rad. It reaches $17.5\%$ efficiency at $58$--$59\%$ purity.
    Differential cross sections in the muon momentum, the muon polar angle (as $\cos\theta_\mu$ and
    as $\theta_\mu$), the pion polar angle, the muon--pion opening angle and the pion momentum (two
    regions) are extracted by Wiener-SVD unfolding. The total flux-averaged cross section is
    extracted with the same machinery as a single bin. Every result comes with its
    additional-smearing matrix $A_C$, through which a prediction must be passed before comparison,
    and with a covariance that contains the flux, interaction-model, hadron re-interaction,
    detector, normalisation and statistical terms. The total cross section carries a $38\%$
    uncertainty, dominated by the flux ($28$--$30\%$) and detector ($20$--$22\%$) terms.

    The signal region is blind, and all results are extracted from Poisson
    fluctuations of the central-value simulation. The four background control regions have been
    compared with beam-on data: they require no correction of the background model, and the data lie
    $12$--$20\%$ above the prediction, within its flux and cross-section uncertainties, in every run
    period except FHC Run~1, whose $13\%$ offset from the other periods is not yet explained. The
    extraction is validated by closure, by ensembles of $100$ independent pseudo-experiments in six
    extractions (pull widths $0.92$--$1.07$) and by pseudo-data from reweighted interaction models
    (largest bias $0.93\sigma$). A closure test with an independent generator remains to be done.
  \end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
% =============================================================================

Charged-current single charged-pion (CC$\pi^{\pm}$) production,
\begin{equation}
  \nu_\mu(\bar{\nu}_\mu) + \mathrm{Ar} \;\longrightarrow\;
  \mu^{\mp} + \pi^{\pm} + X,
\end{equation}
is among the most frequent neutrino interactions at energies of $1$--$5$~GeV and a significant
background to oscillation measurements at the same energies, in particular to $\nu_e$ appearance
at NOvA~\cite{nova} and T2K~\cite{t2k} and at the future DUNE~\cite{dune}. The cross section on
nuclear targets is poorly constrained: the GENIE~v3, NuWro and NEUT generators differ by
$20$--$30\%$ in absolute rate and show shape differences in the individual kinematic variables.

\uboone{} is a liquid argon time-projection chamber (LArTPC)~\cite{uboone_nim} at Fermilab that
recorded data from 2015 to 2021, on axis in the Booster Neutrino Beam and off axis in the NuMI
beam. The NuMI exposure contains neutrino- and antineutrino-enhanced samples with a softer and
broader spectrum than the Booster beam. We measure CC$\pi^{\pm}$ cross sections on argon with the
full NuMI exposure, using Pandora pattern recognition~\cite{pandora,uboone_pandora} and Wiener-SVD
unfolding~\cite{wienervsd}.

\subsection{Measurement overview}
\label{sec:status}

The two horn polarities are analysed as independent measurements, FHC ($7.766\times10^{20}$~POT)
and RHC ($11.082\times10^{20}$~POT), each with its own exposure and integrated flux, and as their
combination ($18.848\times10^{20}$~POT). The signal is defined by the particles that leave the
nucleus: one muon, one charged pion, no neutral pion and any number of nucleons, with the vertex
in the fiducial volume and $p_\mu>0.15\GeVc$, $p_\pi>0.175\GeVc$ and
$\theta_{\mu\pi}<2.6$~rad (\S\ref{sec:signal}). Coherent pion production satisfies this definition
and is part of the signal. All polar angles are measured about the neutrino direction, which at
the \uboone{} position is $28.6^\circ$ from the detector axis (\S\ref{sec:beamdir}).

Figure~\ref{fig:chain} shows the extraction. The same selection is applied to the beam data, to
the beam-off data that measure the cosmic-ray background, and to the simulation. The simulated
neutrino background, the beam-off data and the simulated interactions outside the cryostat are
subtracted, and the remaining spectrum is unfolded with the simulated response. The result of each
extraction is a cross section, the additional-smearing matrix $A_C$ that describes the bias of the
regularised unfolding, and the covariance matrix of the uncertainties.

Six differential distributions and the total cross section are measured in each configuration
(Table~\ref{tab:inventory}): $d\sigma/dp_\mu$, $d\sigma/d\cos\theta_\mu$ and
$d\sigma/d\theta_\mu$, $d\sigma/d\cos\theta_\pi$, $d\sigma/d\theta_{\mu\pi}$, and the cross section
in two regions of pion momentum. The two-region form of $p_\pi$ follows from the saturation of the
range-based pion momentum near $0.34\GeVc$ (\S\ref{sec:ppi_two_region}). The differential results
estimate $A_C$ times the true spectrum, so a prediction is compared after multiplication by $A_C$
(\S\ref{sec:ac_rowsums}); $A_C$ does not preserve the normalisation, and the total cross section is
taken from the one-bin extraction, for which $A_C=1$ (\S\ref{sec:total_xsec}).

The signal region is blind. All results are extracted from fake data, Poisson fluctuations of the
central-value simulation at the recorded exposure, and every figure of an unfolded result carries
the label ``BLINDED: CV PSEUDO-DATA''. The four background control regions are disjoint from the
signal region and have been compared with beam-on data (\S\ref{sec:cr_outcome}).

The inclusive analysis, that is the signal definition, event selection, background treatment,
unfolding, uncertainty model and the result set of Table~\ref{tab:inventory}, is presented for
approval. Opening the signal region further requires the items listed in
\S\ref{sec:unblinding}, among them the confirmation of the absolute flux normalisation and an
understanding of the FHC Run-1 offset seen in the control regions.

The proton-tagged subsample, which adds hadronic-mass and transverse-kinematic-imbalance
observables, is analysed in a companion note~\cite{pt_note}. The optimisation studies and
cross-checks behind the choices described here are collected in the technical supplement, and the
comparison of the control regions with beam-on data in the control-sample note. The
additional-smearing and covariance matrices of every extraction are provided as text files in
\texttt{report/data\_release/}.

\section{Physics context}
\label{sec:theory}
% =============================================================================

\subsection{Production mechanisms and nuclear effects}
\label{sec:prod_mechanisms}
\label{sec:fsi}

Between $0.5$ and $5$~GeV, charged-current single charged-pion production proceeds mainly through
baryon resonances, dominated by the $\Delta(1232)$ and modelled with Rein--Sehgal-type form
factors~\cite{rs1981,kuzmin2004,graczyk2008,berger2007,nowak2009}. A non-resonant continuum and
deep-inelastic scattering together contribute about $20\%$ of the rate in the present phase space
and are added incoherently by every generator used here. Coherent production off the whole nucleus
contributes at most $10\%$; it satisfies the topological signal definition of \S\ref{sec:signal}
and is part of the signal. In argon, Fermi motion, Pauli blocking and binding modify the
kinematics, and final-state interactions (pion absorption at $25$--$30\%$, charge exchange and
quasi-elastic scattering) move events between the CC$\pi^{\pm}$, CC$\pi^0$ and CC$0\pi$ topologies
at the $30\%$ level. For this reason the signal is defined by the final-state particle content
rather than by the production process. The same mixing makes CC$0\pi$ the largest background:
pion absorption, and a proton reconstructed as the pion, bring such events into the selection
(\suppref{Sec.}{sec:request}).

\subsection{Generators}
\label{sec:generators}

Four generators are compared with the results; the model choices relevant to this measurement are
listed in Table~\ref{tab:gen_config}. The response and the fake data are built from the \uboone{}
tune of GENIE~v3~\cite{uboone_tune}. In the measured phase space the standalone generators span a
factor of $1.6$ in the total cross section, from GENIE (lowest) to NEUT (highest)
(\suppref{Table}{tab:total_xsec}), and bracket the tune. Since the present results are extracted
from fake data, the model comparisons test the procedure; the spread indicates the size of the
model differences the measurement is expected to resolve.

\subsection{Previous measurements}
\label{sec:prev_meas}

Table~\ref{tab:prev_meas} lists published CC$\pi^+$ measurements on nuclear targets. The two most
relevant are from \uboone{}. The Booster-beam measurement~\cite{uboone_bnb2026_ccpi}, on the same
detector with an on-axis spectrum peaked at $0.7$~GeV and a negligible $\bar\nu_\mu$ component,
gives $\sigma=(3.75\pm0.07_{\rm stat}\pm0.80_{\rm syst})\times10^{-38}$~cm$^2$/Ar and finds all
tested generators above the data at very forward muon angles. The NuMI $\nu_e+\bar\nu_e$ CC$\pi^+$
measurement~\cite{uboone_nue_ccpi} uses the same beam, reconstruction and extraction framework and
conventions as this analysis (\suppref{Sec.}{sec:nueconsistency}). The NuMI flux at \uboone{} is
softer and broader than the Booster flux and has a $35\%$ $\bar\nu_\mu$ component in FHC, and the
flux-averaged cross section divides by the whole flux above $60$~MeV while pion production starts
near $0.3$~GeV; the value of about $1.0\times10^{-38}$~cm$^2$/Ar found here is therefore not
directly comparable with the Booster result (\S\ref{sec:flux_status}). Among other experiments, the
T2K off-axis measurement on carbon~\cite{t2k_ccpip} has the most similar spectrum. A longer survey
is given in \suppref{App.}{sec:supp_physics}.

\subsection{NuMI beam and flux composition}

The NuMI beam is produced by $120$~GeV protons from the Fermilab Main Injector on a graphite
target. Two magnetic horns focus charged secondary hadrons of one sign. In forward horn current
(FHC) the horns focus positive pions and kaons and the beam is predominantly $\nu_\mu$; in reverse
horn current (RHC) they focus negative mesons and the beam is predominantly $\bar{\nu}_\mu$.
\uboone{} is located $134.6$~mrad ($7.71^\circ$) off the NuMI beam axis\footnote{From the detector
position in beam coordinates, $(5502,\,7259,\,67270)$~cm: $91.1$~m transverse at $672.7$~m
downstream (\S\ref{sec:beamdir}).}, which places the flux peak between $0.5$ and $2.5$~GeV and
narrows the spectrum. At this off-axis position and energy the sign selection of the horns is
incomplete, and both modes contain a large wrong-sign component, largest in RHC.

The flux composition at the detector is taken from the new-G4 central-value flux histograms,
integrated above $E_\nu=60$~MeV. The threshold removes an unphysical accumulation near
$E_\nu=0$, below the reaction threshold, which the flux group removes in the same way. The
muon-flavour fractions of these histograms reproduce the authoritative integrated fluxes of
\S\ref{sec:flux_status} exactly.

\subsection{Beam direction in detector coordinates}
\label{sec:beamdir}

Two different angles describe the geometry of \uboone{} in the NuMI beam
(Fig.~\ref{fig:numigeom}). The off-axis angle is a property of the beamline: the detector lies
$91.1$~m from the NuMI axis at $672.7$~m downstream of the target, a baseline of $678.8$~m and an
off-axis angle of $7.71^\circ$ ($134.6$~mrad).\footnote{Computed from the detector position in beam
coordinates, $(5502,\,7259,\,67270)$~cm, with the rotation matrix used for the NuMI beamline
geometry weights.} This angle does not describe the orientation of the TPC.

The angle that enters the measurement is the direction of the neutrinos relative to the detector
$z$ axis. Averaged over interactions in the active volume, the true neutrino direction in detector
coordinates is $(0.4624,\,0.0489,\,0.8853)$, $28.6^\circ$ from $z$. The detector $z$ axis is
therefore neither the direction from which the neutrinos arrive nor the NuMI beamline axis, which
is itself $23.2^\circ$ from $z$.

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{numi_geometry}
  \caption{The NuMI geometry seen by \uboone{}. (a) Beamline geometry, to scale: the
    detector sits $91.1$~m off the NuMI axis at a baseline of $678.8$~m, an off-axis
    angle of $7.7^\circ$. (b) The same neutrinos in TPC coordinates, to scale (the
    $x$--$z$ projection; the quoted $28.6^\circ$ is the full three-dimensional angle):
    the beam arrives $28.6^\circ$ away from the detector $z$ axis, so one and the same
    muon is assigned $\theta_\mu=12^\circ$ about $z$ but $\theta_\mu=40.6^\circ$ about
    the neutrino. The generators mean the second. (c) The resulting shift in the true
    $\cos\theta_\mu$ distribution; dotted lines mark the analysis bin edges. Under the
    two conventions $64.6\%$ of signal events fall in a different bin ($44.6\%$ for
    $\cos\theta_\pi$).}
  \label{fig:numigeom}
\end{figure}

Every polar angle and every transverse-kinematic-imbalance variable is defined with respect to the
neutrino direction. In the generator predictions the neutrino defines the $+z$ axis (the GENIE
event records used here have $p_x^\nu=p_y^\nu=0$ and $p_z^\nu=E_\nu$ exactly), so angles measured
about the detector $z$ axis would not correspond to the predictions they are compared with. The
difference is large: $64.6\%$ of signal events change $\cos\theta_\mu$ bin between the two
conventions, and $44.6\%$ change $\cos\theta_\pi$ bin. For the transverse-kinematic-imbalance
variables the error exceeds the size of the observable, with a mean $\delta p_T$ of
$0.742$~GeV/$c$ about $z$ against $0.264$~GeV/$c$ about the beam.

All angular and transverse observables are therefore measured about the neutrino direction,
following the $\beta$ convention of the NuMI $\nu_e$ CC$1\pi$ analysis~\cite{numi_nue_ccpi} and of
the earlier NuMI $\nu_e$ measurement it builds on~\cite{mistry_thesis}, which keeps the two NuMI
measurements directly comparable. At truth level the exact neutrino direction of each event is
used, which reproduces the generator convention. At reconstruction level the neutrino direction is
approximated by the vector from the NuMI target, at $(-31388,\,-3316,\,-60100)$~cm in detector
coordinates ($684$~m from the detector centre and $678.8$~m from the TPC origin), to the
reconstructed interaction vertex.

The result is insensitive to this choice of reconstruction-level axis. A single fixed axis and the
per-event $\beta$ differ by a median of $2.4$~mrad (at most $7.3$~mrad), which moves $0.42\%$ of
muons across a $\cos\theta_\mu$ bin edge, compared with $64.6\%$ for the detector-$z$ convention.
Against the true direction the two perform almost identically, with mean opening angles of
$1.95^\circ$ ($\beta$) and $2.00^\circ$ (fixed axis). Neither recovers the divergence of the beam:
the neutrinos come from decays along the whole decay pipe rather than from the target, and over a
$684$~m baseline a $2.5$~m detector subtends about $4$~mrad, so the vertex position carries little
information on the direction. This residual is a resolution effect and is absorbed by the response
matrix.

The muon and pion momenta, the muon--pion opening angle $\theta_{\mu\pi}$, the invariant mass
$W_{\pi p}$ and all integrated cross sections do not depend on the choice of axis. Neither does the
event selection, since no selection cut and no BDT input uses a polar angle.

written confirmation from the NuMI flux contact that the constants of Table~\ref{tab:flux} are
PPFX-corrected central values, consistent with the per-event weighting, is required before the
signal region is opened\todo{ask flux expert}.

$V_{\mathrm{FV}}=4.1761\times10^{7}$~cm$^3$; without the subtraction the target count would be
$9.705\times10^{29}$. The extraction computes $N_{\mathrm{Ar}}$ from the same box and subtraction,
and the value enters the conversion factor of \S\ref{sec:unfolding} and the total of
\S\ref{sec:total_xsec}. The normalisation of each configuration is $N_{\mathrm{norm}} =
\Phi_\mathrm{mode}\cdot N_{\mathrm{POT,mode}}\cdot N_{\mathrm{Ar}}$, with the flux and exposure of
that mode (Tables~\ref{tab:pot} and~\ref{tab:flux}).

\paragraph{Event weights.}
Each simulated event is weighted by
\begin{equation}
  w = \text{Scale}[\text{run}] \times w_{\mathrm{PPFX}} \times w_{\mathrm{tune}},
\end{equation}
where $\text{Scale}[\text{run}] = (\text{run Data POT})/(\text{run MC POT})$ is the per-run factor
of Table~\ref{tab:pot}, $w_{\mathrm{PPFX}}$ the PPFX central-value flux correction and
$w_{\mathrm{tune}}$ the GENIE tune weight. In the framework each overlay file receives the exposure
of its run from the matching beam-on (here fake-data) entry, and the simulated exposure of a run is
the sum of the distinct exposure values stored in its files, which is the run total under both
file conventions in use. One rule is applied to every event weight,
\begin{equation}
  w_{\mathrm{safe}} = \begin{cases} 1, & w\ \text{non-finite},\ w<0,\ \text{or}\ w>30,\\
  w, & \text{otherwise (a weight of exactly zero is kept)},\end{cases}
  \label{eq:safeweight}
\end{equation}
to the product $w=\text{Scale}\times w_{\mathrm{PPFX}}\times w_{\mathrm{tune}}$, including the
$0.65$ normalisation weight of the dirt overlay that scales its generated exposure to the rate of
interactions outside the cryostat expected at the NuMI site. The universes, the covariance, the
unfolding inputs, the cut-flow histograms, the count-level tables, the control-region yields and
transfer factors, and the beam-on comparison at unblinding all use Eq.~\eqref{eq:safeweight}, so
that count-level and covariance-level numbers agree at the per-cent level.

\paragraph{Beam-off normalisation.}
Beam-off data contain cosmic rays only, so a beam-off sample is matched to a beam-on run by run
period, independently of the horn polarity in which it was recorded. Each run period uses the
beam-off samples of that period, scaled by the ratio of beam-on to beam-off gates times the NuMI
beam-occupancy factor $0.98$. Several beam-off files of one run are pooled:
\begin{equation}
  \text{EXT}_{\text{run}} = 0.98\;\frac{\text{bnb\_gates}[\text{run}]}
  {\sum_{\text{files}\in\text{run}}\text{ext\_gates}}\;\sum_{\text{files}\in\text{run}} N^{\text{sel}}_{\text{EXT}} .
\end{equation}
No beam-off sample exists for Run~2; the Run-1 sample, which like Run~2 predates the cosmic-ray
tagger, is used in its place and scaled by its own gate count. The same beam-off
files serve the FHC and RHC runs of a period, and the combined configuration lists each (file, run)
pair once. The selected beam-off rate per $10^{6}$ gates is $1.75$ (Run~1), $0.43$ (Run~3b),
$1.15$ (Run~4) and $0.52$ (Run~5), from $8$, $14$, $40$ and $10$ selected beam-off events, so the
rates carry Poisson uncertainties of $16$--$35\%$. The Run-1 rate is $1.5$ to $4$ times the rates
of the later periods, which is why the samples are matched by period instead of pooled. At the
final cut the beam-off background is $23$ events in FHC, $23$ in RHC and $46$ in the combination,
$1.6\%$, $1.3\%$ and $1.5\%$ of the selected sample. Figure~\ref{fig:ext_perrun} shows the rates,
scale factors and yields per period; the numbers are in \texttt{data\_release/ext\_gates.tsv}, and
the normalisation manifest (\suppref{Sec.}{sec:pipeline}) checks that every (beam-off file, run)
pair enters each configuration once and that the gate counts are consistent.

\paragraph{Cosmic-ray rejection.} No cosmic-ray rejection beyond the selection of
\S\ref{sec:selection} is applied. The beam-off contribution, $1.6\%$ of the selected FHC
prediction, is removed by a data-driven subtraction, so an additional cut could only reduce the
statistical uncertainty of that subtraction, about five events in a sample of $1\,418$, and every
variant studied removes more signal than that. The directional and calorimetric variants are
described in \suppref{Sec.}{sec:supp_cosmiccut}.

\paragraph{Software trigger.}
Beam-on and beam-off files are filtered on the software trigger. The beam-off files of Runs~4 and~5
carry a trigger flag that is zero throughout, and for them the \texttt{swtrig\_pre} flag is used
instead. Simulated events that fail the software trigger are removed at the
NeutrinoSlice cut; every simulated event that passes the topological cut also passes the trigger,
so the requirement has no effect on the selected sample.

\section{Signal definition}
\label{sec:signal}
% =============================================================================

The signal is defined at generator level and is used to build the efficiency and the response;
it is never applied as a reconstruction cut. It does not distinguish the charge of the lepton:
$\nu_\mu \to \mu^- \pi^+$ and $\bar{\nu}_\mu \to \mu^+ \pi^-$ are both signal.

\begin{description}[style=nextline]
  \item[Neutrino flavour]
    $|\nu~\mathrm{PDG}| = 14$ ($\nu_\mu$ or $\bar{\nu}_\mu$).
  \item[Interaction type]
    Charged current.
  \item[True vertex]
    Inside the fiducial volume (\S\ref{sec:fv}).
  \item[Primary multiplicity]
    Among the primary final-state particles, exactly one muon ($|\mathrm{PDG}| = 13$), exactly one
    charged pion ($|\mathrm{PDG}| = 211$) of any momentum, no neutral pion ($|\mathrm{PDG}| = 111$),
    no kaon ($K^\pm$, $K^0$, $K_S$, $K_L$) and no other meson. A second charged pion below the
    momentum threshold makes the event background. Pions from the decay of primary hyperons are not
    primary particles and are not counted. Any number of protons and neutrons is allowed.
  \item[Kinematic thresholds]
    $p_\mu > 0.15\GeVc$, $p_\pi > 0.175\GeVc$ and $\theta_{\mu\pi} < 2.6$~rad.
\end{description}

The thresholds define the measured phase space. The momentum efficiency rises sharply between
$0.15$ and $0.20\GeVc$, set by the $10$~cm muon and $20$~cm pion track-length requirements; below
it the efficiency is $1$--$3\%$ and the correction strongly model dependent. The upper bound on
the opening angle removes the region above $2.6$~rad, which is dominated by cosmic-ray and dirt
background (about $7\%$ purity in the $\theta_{\mu\pi}>2.513$~rad bin). No lower bound is applied:
the small-angle region has a purity of about $58\%$, so a cut there would remove signal without
improving the purity. The same opening-angle requirement is applied at reconstruction level so that
the signal and selection acceptances match. The first $p_\mu$ and $p_\pi$ bins start, and the last
$\theta_{\mu\pi}$ bin ends, at these thresholds.

\paragraph{Pion threshold.}
\label{sec:ppi_regimes}
The NuMI $\nu_e$ CC$1\pi$ analysis defines its pion signal by kinetic energy,
$\mathrm{KE}_\pi>40$~MeV, equivalent to $p_\pi>0.113\GeVc$. The threshold used here,
$p_\pi>0.175\GeVc$ ($\mathrm{KE}_\pi>84$~MeV), is higher because the pion momentum, reconstructed
from the track range under the muon hypothesis and converted to the pion mass, degrades rapidly
below it (Table~\ref{tab:pithresh}). The pion-momentum bias is the signed
quantity $b(p_\pi)=\langle(p_\pi^{\rm reco}-p_\pi^{\rm true})/p_\pi^{\rm true}\rangle$, which has
two regimes. Below the threshold the few pions that are reconstructed at all are biased upwards
($+30$ to $+131\%$): they are the tracks long enough to pass the selection. At the threshold the
mass conversion removes most of the hypothesis bias ($-8\%$, with $31\%$ resolution). Above about
$0.4\GeVc$ the bias becomes increasingly negative, because pions interact before they stop and the
visible range is truncated; the mean reconstructed momentum saturates near $0.34\GeVc$, and the
bias reaches $-65\%$ above $0.8\GeVc$. Between $0.113$ and $0.175\GeVc$ the resolution is
$51$--$56\%$ and the selection efficiency $2.2\%$. A lower threshold would therefore add a bin with
large, strongly model-dependent unfolding corrections, even as a single coarse bin, and it is not
adopted; no cross section is reported below $p_\pi=0.175\GeVc$.

\section{Fiducial volume}
\label{sec:fv}
% =============================================================================

The fiducial volume (FV) is
\begin{equation}
  x \in [10.0,\;246.0]\cm, \quad
  y \in [-101.0,\;+101.0]\cm, \quad
  z \in [10.0,\;986.0]\cm,
\end{equation}
with the region of unresponsive wires, $z \in [675.1,\;775.1]\cm$, excluded. Its volume is
\begin{align}
  V_{\mathrm{FV}} &= (246-10)\times 202 \times (976-100)\cm^3 \nonumber\\
                  &= 236 \times 202 \times 876\cm^3
                   \approx 4.17\times10^7\cm^3.
\end{align}

A larger containment volume, inset by $2$~cm from the faces of the active volume and by $10$~cm
from the top face, is used to require that the pion-candidate track ends inside the detector:
\begin{equation}
  x \in [2.0,\;254.35]\cm,\quad
  y \in [-113.53,\;+107.47]\cm,\quad
  z \in [2.1,\;1034.9]\cm.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Use of the volumes}
\label{sec:fv_where}

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
volume & extent & used for \\\midrule

The true and reconstructed fiducial volumes are the same box. The signal definition tests the true
neutrino vertex and the selection the reconstructed vertex, through the same function, so both
apply the exclusion of the unresponsive region. Vertex resolution and space-charge distortions can
move a reconstructed vertex across a boundary; such events are part of the simulated response,
true-in and reconstructed-out as inefficiency and true-out and reconstructed-in as the
out-of-fiducial-volume background of Table~\ref{tab:cutflow}.

The exclusion of the unresponsive region applies to the vertex only. An event with its vertex
outside the region is kept even if one of its tracks crosses it; such tracks have degraded
calorimetry and range, and the resulting inefficiency is part of the response. The region is also
removed from the target count (Eq.~\eqref{eq:nar}); keeping it would raise the target number by
$100/876 \approx 11\%$ and lower every cross section by the same factor.

The fiducial volume is inset from the active TPC by about $10\cm$ in $x$, $15\cm$ in $y$, $10\cm$
at the upstream $z$ face and $51\cm$ at the downstream face. The containment volume is inset by
only $2\cm$ ($10\cm$ at the top), since the range-based momentum and the pion-containment cut need
the track to end inside the detector, not inside the fiducial region.


% =============================================================================
\section{Event selection}
\label{sec:selection}
% =============================================================================

The selection is implemented as a selection class of the extraction framework, which produces the
response matrices and spectra of every result; an independent standalone implementation of the
same cut sequence is kept as a cross-check. It consists of ten sequential cuts, with Cut~0 the
unselected reference sample.

\subsection{BDT-based particle identification}
\label{sec:bdt}

Three gradient-boosted decision trees (TMVA~\cite{tmva}), trained on the \uboone{} overlay
simulation, provide the calorimetric particle identification of the muon and pion candidates. All
three use the same per-track inputs: the Bragg-peak $dE/dx$ likelihoods under the proton, muon and
minimum-ionising hypotheses, the log-likelihood-ratio (LLR) PID score, the Pandora track-shower
score, and the space-charge-corrected track end points.
\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
  \item \textbf{MIP BDT}: separates minimum-ionising tracks (muons, pions) from stopping protons.
    Both candidates must have a MIP score above $-0.1$ (Cuts~4 and~5).
  \item \textbf{Pion BDT}: a pion-versus-proton discriminant for the pion candidate, with a
    required score above $-0.1$ (Cut~5).
  \item \textbf{Muon BDT}: the same inputs plus the track length. It applies no threshold. The
    tracks that qualify as muon candidates are defined by the cuts of Table~\ref{tab:mupid}, and the
    qualifying track with the highest muon-BDT score is taken as the muon.
\end{itemize}
The two candidate-PID BDTs do not use the track length, so that their scores do not bias the
momentum spectra. A fourth, cosmic-rejection BDT is available but not used: it brought no
improvement over the Pandora topological score, which provides the cosmic rejection at Cut~3.

\subsection{Cut definitions}
\label{sec:cutdefs}

\begin{description}[style=nextline, leftmargin=1.5cm, labelwidth=1.3cm]
  \item[\textbf{Cut 0.}] \textbf{EventsInTrueFV.}
    All events; the reference for truth-level studies.
  \item[\textbf{Cut 1.}] \textbf{NeutrinoSlice.}
    At least one reconstructed track and the software trigger. The cosmic-BDT requirement at this
    stage is disabled.
  \item[\textbf{Cut 2.}] \textbf{InFiducialVol.}
    The space-charge-corrected reconstructed neutrino vertex lies inside the FV.
  \item[\textbf{Cut 3.}] \textbf{Topological.}
    Topological score $> 0.67$. This Pandora multivariate score separates cosmic-ray from neutrino
    topologies and provides the main cosmic rejection: the beam-off yield falls by a factor of
    $28$--$29$ between Cut~2 and Cut~3 (Table~\ref{tab:cutflow}).
  \item[\textbf{Cut 4.}] \textbf{MuonCandidate.}
    At least one primary track satisfies the muon criteria of Table~\ref{tab:mupid}. Of the
    qualifying tracks, the one with the highest muon-BDT score (\S\ref{sec:bdt}) is the muon
    candidate; the track length enters only through the $10$~cm requirement and as a BDT input.
  \item[\textbf{Cut 5.}] \textbf{ContainedPion.}
    Exactly one primary track other than the muon candidate satisfies the pion criteria of
    Table~\ref{tab:pipid} and ends inside the containment volume. The track with the highest LLR
    PID score is the pion candidate.
  \item[\textbf{Cut 6.}] \textbf{MuonIn3Planes.}
    The muon candidate has at least one hit on each of the U, V and Y planes.
  \item[\textbf{Cut 7.}] \textbf{PionIn3Planes.}
    The pion candidate has at least one hit on each of the U, V and Y planes.
  \item[\textbf{Cut 8.}] \textbf{ShowerCut.}
    No primary electromagnetic shower, unless there is exactly one shower with fewer than $50$
    Y-plane hits more than $10$~cm from the vertex, as expected for a delta ray or a low-energy
    nuclear photon.
  \item[\textbf{Cut 9.}] \textbf{OpeningAngle.}
    The reconstructed muon--pion opening angle satisfies $\theta_{\mu\pi} < 2.6$~rad
    ($\approx149^\circ$), which removes the large-angle cosmic-ray and dirt background. The same
    requirement is part of the signal definition (\S\ref{sec:signal}).
  \item[\textbf{Cut 10.}] \textbf{Nonprotons} (final cut).
    Fewer than four primary tracks with LLR PID $> 0.1$ (non-proton-like), and fewer than five
    primary tracks in total.
\end{description}

\subsection{Kinematic reconstruction}

\paragraph{Muon momentum.}
The muon momentum uses the range when the muon-candidate track ends inside the detector and
multiple Coulomb scattering (MCS)~\cite{uboone_mcs} when it exits, since the range of an exiting
track measures only the distance to the detector boundary. In the selected FHC sample $37\%$ of the
muons are contained and $63\%$ exit, so the muon momentum is mostly measured by MCS: NuMI muons are
energetic and the TPC is small. This matters for the resolution and for the uncertainty, since the
two estimators have different resolutions and systematic effects. The MCS estimator has a $-10\%$
bias and $30\%$ resolution (Fig.~\ref{fig:resolution}), which enter through the response matrix.
The detector variations do not change the MCS momentum scale directly; a dedicated momentum-scale
term is evaluated in \S\ref{sec:mcs}.

\paragraph{Pion momentum.}
The pion momentum starts from the range momentum under the muon hypothesis. Before a
reconstructed $p_\pi$ bin is assigned, it is converted to the pion hypothesis at fixed kinetic
energy, $T=\sqrt{p_{\mu\text{-hyp}}^2+m_\mu^2}-m_\mu$ and $p_\pi=\sqrt{(T+m_\pi)^2-m_\pi^2}$. The
stored branch is unchanged; the conversion is part of the reconstructed-bin definition in the bin
configuration, and it raises the reconstructed-to-true ratio of the lowest bin from $0.888$ to
$0.976$ (\suppref{Sec.}{sec:ppi_bias}). Because the muon mass ($105.7$~MeV) is close to the pion
mass ($139.6$~MeV), the range-to-momentum conversion is a good estimate for a pion that stops, and
it yields a momentum that is binned against the true momentum on the same axis. For a pion that
interacts before stopping, the visible range is truncated and the estimate is biased low; this
truncation, more than the mass hypothesis, limits the observable (\S\ref{sec:ppi_regimes}). The kinetic energy
from the proton-hypothesis range is not used: binned with the momentum edges it would shift the
migration matrix systematically (a true $0.2$~GeV/$c$ pion has $KE\approx0.104$~GeV, two bins
lower, and a true $0.175$~GeV/$c$ pion would fall below the first reconstructed bin). The range
momentum is only meaningful for contained tracks, which the containment requirement ensures.

\paragraph{Angles.}
Polar angles are measured with respect to the neutrino direction $\hat{u}_\nu$:
$\cos\theta_\mu = \hat{u}_\mu \cdot \hat{u}_\nu$ and $\cos\theta_\pi = \hat{u}_\pi \cdot
\hat{u}_\nu$. For reconstructed quantities $\hat{u}_\nu$ is the $\beta$ direction of
Refs.~\cite{numi_nue_ccpi,mistry_thesis}, from the NuMI target to the reconstructed vertex; at truth
level it is the neutrino direction of the event (\S\ref{sec:beamdir}). The opening angle
$\theta_{\mu\pi} = \arccos(\hat{u}_\mu \cdot \hat{u}_\pi)$ is computed from the reconstructed track
directions and does not depend on $\hat{u}_\nu$.

\subsection{Proton-tagged subsample}
\label{sec:selection_1p}
Requiring in addition a contained proton candidate with $p_p>0.3\GeVc$ selects a proton-tagged
subsample with one further cut. It gives access to hadronic-mass and transverse-kinematic-imbalance
observables but carries its own proton-assignment and model-dependence requirements; it is
described in the companion note (\ptref{Sec.}{sec:pt_selection}) and is not part of the approval
request.

\paragraph{Phase space and opening-angle requirement.}
The thresholds were chosen from the efficiency turn-on and purity studies of the Run-1 FHC sample
(\suppref{Sec.}{sec:supp_optim}); the measured phase space excludes the momentum regions of
near-zero efficiency and the low-purity region of large opening angle. The events removed by the
requirement $\theta_{\mu\pi}<2.6$~rad form the cosmic control region
(Table~\ref{tab:sideband_yields}): in FHC $5.1$ signal, $60.1$ neutrino-background, $40.3$
beam-off and $1.9$ dirt events, a signal purity of $4.7\%$. Without the requirement the purity of
the selected sample would fall from $58.8\%$ to $55.0\%$ and its beam-off content would rise from
$23$ to $64$ events. No lower bound on the opening angle is applied, since the small-angle region
has a purity of $58\%$ and a cut there would remove signal without improving the purity.

The regions differ in size and in the background they probe:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{CC$0\pi$} is the largest region, with about $10.6$k events in FHC, $12.6$k in RHC and
    $23$k combined. Its neutrino component is dominated by quasi-elastic and $2p2h$ interactions,
    which reach the signal region through pion absorption or a proton reconstructed as the pion. It
    probes the final-state interactions that move events between CC$\pi^{\pm}$ and CC$0\pi$, the
    largest migration path into and out of the signal definition (\S\ref{sec:signal}).
  \item \textbf{$\pi^0$} is the second largest, $5.8$--$8.2$k events per mode and $14$k combined. It is
    enriched in charged- and neutral-current $\pi^0$ events, whose photon showers the veto (Cut~8)
    removes, and it probes the veto efficiency together with the $\pi^0$ production rate: these are
    the events that enter the signal region when a photon is missed.
  \item \textbf{multi-$\pi$} is small, $102$--$175$ events per mode ($277$ combined), and has the
    largest signal content, $22$--$24\%$, because a multi-pion event with one pion lost is the
    background the selection is most exposed to. Its size limits its constraining power.
  \item \textbf{cosmic} has the largest beam-off share: in FHC $40$ beam-off against $65$ neutrino
    events, a ratio of $0.6$, against $0.1$ in the CC$0\pi$ region. It probes the beam-off
    normalisation and shape. Its beam-off purity is $38\%$ in FHC and $34\%$ in RHC, below the
    target purity of the frozen procedure (\suppref{Sec.}{sec:cr_protocol}), so it can add an
    uncertainty to the beam-off normalisation but not correct it.
\end{itemize}

The combined CC$0\pi$ and $\pi^0$ regions have raw statistical precisions of $0.7\%$ and $0.8\%$.
Their constraint on the signal-region background is weaker, because it passes through transfer
factors that depend on the model (\suppref{Sec.}{sec:cr_transfer}): the factors are small ($0.03$
and $0.02$), but the transfer from a CC$0\pi$ event to a selected CC$1\pi$ topology carries its own
cross-section and detector uncertainties, which limit the constraint more than the sample size
does. The cosmic region would constrain the beam-off normalisation only to about $60\%$: the
combined region contains $82$ beam-off events among about $233$, with $15$ events of statistical
uncertainty and a $32\%$ systematic uncertainty on its $147$ neutrino events ($\pm47$ events); the
statistics alone would give $19\%$. With the beam-off background at $1.6\%$ of the signal region this
is sufficient. The multi-$\pi$ region, $277$ combined events, gives about $6\%$, useful as a
cross-check.

multi-$\pi$ at $1.10$ on $175$ events ($8\%$). A variant with the beam-off and dirt components added
to the pseudo-data tests the complete bookkeeping (\suppref{Sec.}{sec:cr_fullstack}).

\subsection{Comparison with beam-on data}
\label{sec:cr_outcome}

The four regions have been compared with beam-on data at reconstruction level
(Fig.~\ref{fig:cr_data}), under a procedure fixed beforehand: frozen region definitions, a
pre-agreed test statistic and the conditions under which a correction may be applied
(\suppref{Sec.}{sec:request}). No beam-on sample of Run~2 exists, and Run~2 is removed from both data
and prediction in this comparison.

\begin{itemize}\itemsep3pt
  \item \textbf{Global test.} The frozen global test passes, $\chi^2=8.1/8$ ($p=0.42$).
  \item \textbf{No class-specific correction.} A joint FHC$+$RHC fit of the background-class
    normalisations gives the dominant CC$0\pi$ class at $0.949\pm0.190$ over all run periods
    ($0.984\pm0.191$ without FHC Run~1), consistent with unity (\crref{Sec.}{sec:jointfit}). The
    nominal background prediction is kept. The fit uses the CC$0\pi$, $\pi^0$ and cosmic regions; the
    multi-$\pi$ region, which shares $47$ FHC and $68$ RHC events with the $\pi^0$ region, is
    diagnostic and not in the fit, so the overlap does not enter the fit covariance.
  \item \textbf{Common excess.} The neutrino-dominated regions lie $12$--$20\%$ above the prediction,
    within the correlated flux and cross-section uncertainties. The excess does not follow the
    signal content of a region: tightening each region to reduce its signal (the CC$0\pi$ region from
    $8.6\%$ to $2.2\%$ signal while keeping $58\%$ of its events) leaves the data-to-prediction ratio
    unchanged, $1.12$ to $1.13$ (\crref{Sec.}{sec:farsb}), and the cosmic region approaches unity as
    its beam-off share increases. The excess therefore lies in the neutrino simulation and is not
    signal in the control regions.
  \item \textbf{FHC Run~1.} Period by period, the data lie $12$--$25\%$ above the prediction in every
    run period except FHC Run~1, which is consistent with unity (\crref{Table}{tab:perperiod_corrected}).
    A free normalisation factor on FHC Run~1 fits $0.869\pm0.022$ and improves the per-period fit by
    $\Delta\chi^2=37$, while a free factor on any other period gains at most $15$. The common excess
    of the other periods is covered by the correlated uncertainties; a $13\%$ offset of one period
    relative to the others is not. The cosmic region, dominated by beam-off events, is at
    $0.95\pm0.17$ in Run~1, so the Run-1 trigger count is not obviously wrong; the POT bookkeeping,
    the completeness of the Run-1 beam-on sample with respect to its run list and the normalisation of
    the Run-1 overlay remain to be checked (\S\ref{sec:unblinding}). The same comparison fixes the
    Run-1 exposure assignment: normalised to the open-trigger bookkeeping ($3.283\times10^{20}$~POT and
    $9\,846\,635$ triggers), FHC Run~1 lies at $0.60$--$0.72$ in the CC$0\pi$, $\pi^0$ and cosmic
    regions, with a fitted exposure factor of $0.520\pm0.020$, whereas the non-open-trigger values used
    here ($2.192\times10^{20}$~POT and $5\,748\,692$ triggers) give $0.95$--$1.10$ in the same regions.
  \item \textbf{Muon-momentum shape.} In two dimensions ($\cos\theta_\mu\times p_\mu$) the residual is
    organised in $p_\mu$: the data over the prediction are $1.16$--$1.82$ in the $0.35$--$0.75$~GeV/$c$
    bins, $1.05$--$1.40$ below and mostly $0.8$--$1.1$ above, in every angular bin, in both modes and in
    regions of different composition. Normalisation parameters do not remove it. It points to the
    muon momentum rather than to a background class, remains open, and two-dimensional
    normalisation fits are not quoted while it stands (\crref{Sec.}{sec:jointfit}).
\end{itemize}

A normalisation fit propagated under the frozen rules cannot reduce the uncertainty of the
measurement. Conditioning the prediction on the control-region totals could, through the flux and
detector correlations: on an Asimov dataset it reduces the uncertainty of the one-bin total from
$35.8\%$ to $11.3\%$. Adopting such a constraint would change the role of the control regions and
requires a collaboration decision.

\subsection{Validation of the background fit}

The background fit was validated on simulation before it was applied to data
(Fig.~\ref{fig:validation}).
\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
  \item \textbf{Toys.} Sets of $300$ toys give pull means of $0.02$--$0.07$, widths of
    $0.89$--$1.03$ and coverage of $67$--$75\%$; ensembles of up to $10^5$ toys per model are
    described below.
  \item \textbf{Injections} (Asimov data, two-parameter fit of the totals). Shifts of a class are
    recovered exactly; a $30\%$ signal excess moves the normalisations by $0.13\sigma$, an unfitted
    class at $+30\%$ by $-0.07\sigma$, a detector variation by at most $0.14\sigma$, and flux and GENIE
    universes by $-0.97$ to $+0.84\sigma$, within the uncertainty they are part of.
  \item \textbf{FHC against RHC.} A likelihood-ratio test of common against beam-specific
    normalisations inside the joint covariance (a parameter goodness-of-fit test is not valid, since
    the modes share universes): a $+15\%$ shift of the RHC neutrino simulation alone gives
    $\Delta\chi^2=12.9/2$ ($p=0.002$, totals) and $20.3/2$ ($p=4\times10^{-5}$, two-dimensional).
  \item \textbf{Markov chain.} An adaptive Metropolis sampler with four chains and flat priors gives
    posterior means within $0.02$ and widths within $2\%$ of MIGRAD/MINOS on the Asimov dataset and
    on a CC$0\pi\times1.25$ injection, with $\hat R\le1.004$. With a few parameters and a nearly
    quadratic likelihood the profile likelihood suffices, and the sampler is kept as a cross-check.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Extraction chain}
\label{sec:chain}

The extraction proceeds in three stages, shown for each configuration in the reconstructed-bin
space in which the unfolding operates, with all analysis slices concatenated.

\paragraph{Selected events.} The selected reconstructed spectrum is scaled to the exposure of each
configuration with the per-run factors of \S\ref{sec:mc}, and the simulation is split into signal
and background (Fig.~\ref{fig:chain_reco}). At the final cut the purity is $58$--$59\%$
(Table~\ref{tab:cutflow}), so about $40\%$ of the selected events are background. The points are
the fake data.

\paragraph{Background subtraction.} The estimated background is subtracted bin by bin
(Fig.~\ref{fig:chain_bsub}). The neutrino background is taken from the simulation, the cosmic-ray
background from the beam-off data and the background from interactions outside the cryostat from
the dirt overlay; the control regions of \S\ref{sec:cr} test this prediction. For observable $x$
the background-subtracted spectrum in reconstructed bin $i$ is
\begin{equation}
  d_i^{\,\mathrm{sig}} = d_i^{\,\mathrm{data}} - b_i^{\,\nu\text{-bkg}}
                          - b_i^{\,\mathrm{EXT}} - b_i^{\,\mathrm{dirt}} .
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Unfolding.} The subtracted spectrum still contains the detector response and the
selection efficiency, described by the response matrix of \S\ref{sec:unfolding_resp}. With
$50$--$99\%$ of the events migrating between bins in some observables, a direct inversion is
unstable (\S\ref{sec:unfmethods}), and the spectrum is unfolded with the Wiener-SVD method. The
regularisation introduces the additional-smearing matrix $A_C$: the unfolded result estimates
$A_C$ times the true signal, and every prediction is multiplied by $A_C$ before it is compared with
the result (\S\ref{sec:ac_rowsums}).

\subsection{Response matrix}
\label{sec:unfolding_resp}

Two matrices are used, and they differ by the efficiency. The migration matrix
\begin{equation}
  M_{ij} = P(\text{reco in bin } i \,|\, \text{true in bin } j,\ \text{selected}),
  \qquad \textstyle\sum_i M_{ij} = 1,
\end{equation}
describes how selected events move between bins. The smearceptance matrix also contains the
probability to be selected,
\begin{equation}
  S_{ij} = \varepsilon_j\, M_{ij},
  \qquad \textstyle\sum_i S_{ij} = \varepsilon_j,
  \label{eq:smearcept}
\end{equation}
where $\varepsilon_j$ is the ratio of selected to generated signal events in true bin $j$. The
unfolding uses $S$, built from selected signal events with the same binning on both axes, and the
efficiency of Fig.~\ref{fig:efficiency} is its column sum. The folding relation that is inverted is
\begin{equation}
  n_i^{\mathrm{reco}} = \sum_j S_{ij}\,N_j^{\mathrm{true}} + b_i,
  \qquad S_{ij}=\varepsilon_j M_{ij},
  \label{eq:folding}
\end{equation}
so the unfolded $\hat N_j^{\mathrm{true}}$ is already corrected for the efficiency, and no separate
division by $\varepsilon_j$ follows (Eq.~\eqref{eq:xsec_def}). Figure~\ref{fig:response} shows $S$
as passed to the unfolding, with each column summing to the efficiency of its true bin. The
migration diagonals quoted in the text, for example $91\%$ and $76\%$ for the two $p_\pi$
bins, are elements of the column-normalised $M$, obtained by dividing each column of $S$ by its
sum.

\subsection{Regularised unfolding}
\label{sec:unfmethods}

Unfolding estimates the true signal spectrum $\vec{s}$ from the measured $\vec{m}$ through the
folding relation $\vec{m} = A\,\vec{s} + \vec{b}$. The direct inverse $A^{-1}(\vec{m}-\vec{b})$ is
unbiased, but bin migration makes $A$ nearly singular, and the inverse turns statistical
fluctuations into large anticorrelated oscillations between bins. Regularisation reduces this
variance at the cost of a controlled bias. Two methods are used.

\paragraph{Wiener-SVD (primary).} After a singular-value decomposition of the response, each mode
$k$ is weighted by the Wiener filter $W_k=\sigma_k^2/(\sigma_k^2+N_k)$, the weighting that minimises
the mean squared error: it is close to one for well-measured modes and tends to zero for modes
dominated by noise, and its strength is not tuned by hand. The resulting linear bias is known: the
unfolded data estimate the smeared spectrum $A_C\vec{s}$, and the additional-smearing matrix $A_C$
is provided with every result so that each prediction is smeared in the same way before
comparison.

\paragraph{D'Agostini iterative Bayesian~\cite{dagostini} (cross-check).} Bayes' theorem inverts
the migration, $P(C_j|E_i)=R_{ij}\pi_j/\sum_l R_{il}\pi_l$, and the unfolded estimate is
$\hat{s}_j=\varepsilon_j^{-1}\sum_i P(C_j|E_i)(m_i-b_i)$. The prior $\pi_j$, initially the
simulation, is replaced by the result and the procedure iterated; the number of iterations is the
regularisation parameter, with few iterations biased towards the prior and many iterations noisy.
There is no $A_C$, and predictions are compared directly. The residual bias is towards the prior and
can only be evaluated with fake data that differ from the prior. The uncertainty is the data
covariance propagated through the iterations at fixed prior.

Wiener-SVD provides the results, for its objective regularisation and its explicit bias $A_C$.
D'Agostini serves as a cross-check (\suppref{Sec.}{sec:crosschecks}). Its integral is independent
of the iteration count and of the observable, and lies $23$--$31\%$ above the Wiener-SVD integrals,
depending on the configuration; this is consistent with the spread of the Wiener-SVD integrals
between observables (Table~\ref{tab:sigint_all}) being caused by the $A_C$ smearing, which the row
sums of \S\ref{sec:ac_rowsums} show directly. With fake data equal in expectation to the
central-value simulation that also serves as its prior, D'Agostini returns its prior, so the
stability of its integral does not measure its bias for a different truth; that requires fake
data from a different model (\S\ref{sec:altmodel}).

\subsection{Wiener-SVD implementation and normalisation}

The Wiener-SVD unfolding~\cite{wienervsd} of the XSecAnalyzer framework~\cite{xsec_analyzer,uboone_tki}
produces every result: the data covariance is whitened by a Cholesky decomposition, the
regularisation is applied inside the SVD of $R\,C^{-1}$, and the Wiener filter of Eq.~3.24 of
Ref.~\cite{wienervsd} is set by the data covariance. The regularisation matrix $C$ is the
second-derivative operator on the density, corrected for the non-uniform bin widths, with one-sided
first-derivative rows at the ends. The width correction matters because the bin widths vary by up
to a factor of $14$ ($\cos\theta_\mu$; $10$ for $\theta_\mu$, $6$ for $p_\mu$): a stencil for a
uniform grid weights the curvature of the density wrongly and drives $A_C$ towards rank one, so that
every model is smeared onto one shape and the result carries no shape information
(\suppref{Sec.}{sec:thetavar}). A standalone replica of the unfolding is used for cross-checks
between pipelines; it takes the covariance from the framework because it generates no universes,
and with a statistical covariance alone it under-regularises and over-corrects the integral by
about $26\%$, so it is not used for any result.

The differential cross section in true bin $j$ is
\begin{equation}
  \left.\frac{d\sigma}{dx}\right|_j
  = \frac{\hat{N}_j^{\,\mathrm{true}}}
         {\Delta x_j \cdot \Phi_{\mathrm{total}} \cdot N_{\mathrm{Ar}}},
  \label{eq:xsec_def}
\end{equation}
where $\hat{N}_j^{\,\mathrm{true}}$ is the Wiener-SVD estimate of the true signal count obtained by
inverting Eq.~\eqref{eq:folding}, $\Phi_{\mathrm{total}} = \Phi_{\mathrm{per\,POT}} \times
T_{\mathrm{POT}}$ the integrated flux and $N_{\mathrm{Ar}}$ the number of argon nuclei in the FV.
Since the unfolding uses $S=\varepsilon M$, the efficiency correction is part of the inversion and
no factor $1/\varepsilon_j$ appears. The implementation forms each bin as
$\hat N_j^{\,\mathrm{true}}/(\mathrm{conv}\cdot\Delta x_j)$ with the conversion
$\mathrm{conv} = N_{\mathrm{Ar}}\,\Phi_{\mathrm{total}}/10^{-38}$, whose inputs are:

\noindent The integrated cross section of a differential result is the width-weighted sum
\begin{equation}
  \sigma_{\mathrm{int}} = \sum_j \left.\frac{d\sigma}{dx}\right|_j \Delta x_j .
\end{equation}
Since the Wiener-SVD result estimates $A_C\,x_{\mathrm{true}}$ and $A_C$ does not preserve the
normalisation, $\sigma_{\mathrm{int}}$ differs between observables (\S\ref{sec:ac_rowsums}). The
total cross section, independent of the observable, is the one-bin extraction of
\S\ref{sec:total_xsec}.

$24$--$29\%$ (Tables~\ref{tab:fhc}--\ref{tab:comb}; $28$--$42\%$ for the two-bin $p_\pi$). The
difference comes from the background subtraction, not from the unfolding: the one-bin total has
no regularisation and carries the same factor. A flux universe scales the whole reconstructed
prediction, signal and neutrino background together, and the covariance is that of the measured
quantity $(N_{\rm pred}-B)/(\varepsilon\Phi N_{\rm Ar})$, with $B$ and $\Phi$ moving together in
every universe. Relative to the signal, the flux variation is therefore
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\delta\sigma}{\sigma} = \frac{\delta\Phi}{\Phi}\left(1+\frac{B}{S}\right),
\end{equation}
with $B$ the neutrino background; the beam-off and dirt contributions carry no flux weight. For
FHC, $B/S=560/833$ (Table~\ref{tab:cutflow}) and a reconstructed-level flux term of $17.7\%$ give
$29.6\%$, against $29.9\%$ for the one-bin extraction. For RHC,
$15\%\times(1+720/1015)=25.6\%$ against $27.7\%$, and for the proton-tagged FHC total, a sample of
different purity, $17.7\%\times(1+298/331)=33.6\%$ against $35.5\%$. The differential flux terms
show the same effect bin by bin, with the conditioning of the response adding a few per cent in the
least well-conditioned observables ($p_\pi$, $\cos\theta_\mu$) and nothing in the best
($\theta_{\mu\pi}$).

The flux term is thus fully correlated between the normalisation and the subtracted background,
which is correct when both come from the same flux prediction: $17\%$ is the normalisation part,
and the remainder is the flux dependence of the background model. It can be reduced by a higher
purity or by a background constraint from the control regions (\S\ref{sec:cr}), not by the
regularisation. The reconstructed-level flux term is $16.4$--$17.7\%$ bin-averaged and flat across
observables, as expected of a normalisation term; the $600$ PPFX universes reweight the same
events, so the term carries no simulation-statistics component.

\subsection{Muon momentum scale}
\label{sec:mcs}

The momentum of $63\%$ of the selected muons is measured by multiple Coulomb scattering, whose scale
and resolution are not varied by the detector-variation samples. The momentum of a selected muon is
exactly the range value when the track is contained and exactly the MCS value when it exits, so
the MCS component can be scaled on its own. A momentum-scale error is a difference between data and
simulation, so the variation is applied to the data side: the fake data are thrown again with every
MCS-measured muon momentum multiplied by $1\pm0.05$, while the simulation, the response and the
selection stay nominal, and each variation is unfolded with the nominal response. The
half-difference of the two results is taken as a fully correlated term. The size of the variation,
$\pm5\%$, is to be confirmed by a data-driven comparison of the MCS and range momenta of contained
muons (\S\ref{sec:unblinding}).

For FHC $d\sigma/dp_\mu$ the half-difference is $8$--$13\%$ of the bin value below $0.95$~GeV/$c$,
where the regularised spectrum is steep, $2\%$ in the fifth bin and $8$--$10\%$, with opposite sign,
in the two highest bins; this is $0.05$--$0.34\sigma$ of the per-bin uncertainty. The integral moves
by $-6.8\%$ and $+2.1\%$. The combined configuration, in which FHC and RHC are scaled together as
one common MCS scale, behaves like FHC: $5$--$10\%$ in the five lowest bins and $14\%$ in the
highest, at most $0.35\sigma$, with the integral moving by $-2.5\%$ and $+2.4\%$. In RHC the six
lowest bins move by $6$--$15\%$ ($0.15$--$0.32\sigma$), but the open highest bin moves by $+21\%$
and $-44\%$ ($0.85\sigma$), because a downward scale moves exiting muons out of it on a steeply
falling spectrum; the RHC integral moves by $-0.8\%$ and $-6.8\%$. Adding the term raises the
per-bin total uncertainty from $38$--$58\%$ to $39$--$58\%$ (FHC), from $35$--$48\%$ to
$36$--$48\%$ (combined) and from $38$--$49\%$ to $40$--$51\%$ (RHC, with $38\%$ to $50\%$ in the
highest bin). The term is provided for the three $p_\mu$ extractions as \texttt{cov\_MCSscale.txt},
together with \texttt{cov\_total\_plusMCS.txt}, and is not yet included in the covariance computed
by the framework.

The selection applies no requirement on the reconstructed muon momentum, and no other inclusive
observable depends on it. The scaled fake data therefore give results identical to the nominal ones
for $\cos\theta_\mu$, $\theta_\mu$, $\cos\theta_\pi$, $\theta_{\mu\pi}$, $p_\pi$ and the total cross
section, as verified for FHC $\cos\theta_\mu$, $\cos\theta_\pi$, $p_\pi$ and the total, and the
term is zero for these results. The comparison of the control regions with data is also unaffected,
since it is made at reconstruction level with the same estimator in data and simulation.

\subsection{Effects not included in the covariance}
\label{sec:notincov}

Besides the MCS scale, three effects are evaluated or bounded but not included in the covariance.
The beamline-geometry flux term ($1.6$--$3.3\%$) is discussed above. The covariance contains the
statistical uncertainty of the beam-off samples but no uncertainty on the per-run gate ratios, nor
on the use of the Run-1 beam-off sample for Run~2, which is $16\%$ of the FHC and $23\%$ of the RHC
exposure. With the beam-off background at $1.6\%$ of the selected sample, a $20\%$ uncertainty on
either would add $0.3\%$ to the total uncertainty.

\section{Validation}
\label{sec:validation}
% =============================================================================

The validation uses simulation, where the true answer is known, and has three parts.
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep3pt
  \item \textbf{Closure under the nominal model.} Fake data thrown from the simulation that built
    the response are unfolded, and the truth must be recovered. This tests the implementation: the
    matrices, the binning, the normalisation and the bookkeeping. It is reported with every
    extraction as the closure $\chi^2$ and is passed.
  \item \textbf{Statistical coverage.} Many independent Poisson realisations are carried through
    the whole chain, and the spread of the results is compared with the uncertainty each reports.
    This is passed for six extractions covering the three configurations.
  \item \textbf{Robustness to a different model.} Fake data whose truth differs from the model of
    the response are unfolded, which measures the bias from the regularisation and the nominal
    response. With reweighted GENIE fake data the largest bias is $0.93\sigma$
    (\S\ref{sec:altmodel}); a test with an independent generator has not yet been possible.
\end{enumerate}
The first two parts test the procedure; the third measures how much of a result comes from the data
and how much from the model used to unfold it.

\subsection{Closure under the nominal model}

Unfolding a spectrum thrown from the simulation that built the response detects an inverted
matrix, a misaligned binning or a normalisation applied twice. It is reported with every
extraction as the $\chi^2$ against the $A_C$-smeared truth of the throw, and the ratio of unfolded
to true integral averages $1.011$ over the eighteen inclusive extractions ($0.944$--$1.135$). The
test cannot detect a wrong model, since the model is the same on both sides, nor a wrongly sized
uncertainty, since a single throw does not sample the distribution of throws. The closure $\chi^2$
uses the full covariance, whose systematic terms are common to the fake data and the reference and
cancel in the residual while still entering the covariance; its $p$-values therefore cluster near
unity and are not a sensitive test. The statistical coverage is tested with ensembles.

\subsection{Ensemble coverage}
\label{par:ensemble}

One hundred independent Poisson realisations of the data of each configuration were each carried
through the whole chain, with the universes rebuilt from that realisation, and the pull in each bin,
$p_i=(\hat{x}_i-(A_C x_{\rm CV})_i)/\sigma_i$, was formed against the central-value truth smeared by
the $A_C$ of that realisation. Six extractions were chosen to span the conditioning and the
configurations: $p_\mu$ and $\cos\theta_\mu$ in FHC (the $A_C$ row sums of the latter fall to
$0.22$), $\cos\theta_\mu$ in RHC ($0.05$), the two-region $p_\pi$ in the combined configuration,
and the one-bin totals of FHC and RHC, which have no regularisation.

With $100$ members each width is determined to about $\pm13\%$. The widths are consistent with one
and the coverage with its nominal value, so the statistical uncertainties are correctly sized in the

ones. Against the full covariance the pull widths are $0.22$--$0.35$ ($0.11$ for the totals) with
essentially complete coverage, because the ensemble varies only the data statistics while the full
uncertainty also contains the flux, detector and cross-section terms, which are the same in every
member; this is the origin of the closure $p$-values near unity. The ensemble means lie below the
reference by $0.4$--$1.1\%$, at $0.5$--$1.4$ standard deviations of the mean ($0.8\%$ at $2.0$ for
the RHC total), and the per-bin pull means are $-0.07$ to $-0.10$ ($-0.21$ for the RHC total). This
common negative offset of about $1\%$ is well inside the statistical uncertainty of a single
measurement and is not corrected. The proton-tagged extractions have not yet been tested this way.
The construction of the reference and the full-covariance pulls are described in
\suppref{Sec.}{sec:supp_ensemble}.

\subsection{Robustness to a different interaction model}
\label{sec:altmodel}

Here the fake data are thrown from a reweighted interaction model, while the response, the
efficiency and the background prediction remain nominal, so the bias that appears is the combined
effect of the regularisation and of a response built from the wrong model. The fake-data universe
of the framework is filled from the thrown files at truth and reconstruction level, so the truth
used for comparison is that of the alternative model, and the reweighting applies to signal and
background alike. The fake data are reconstructed GENIE events reweighted event by event to an
alternative parameter set, with the same detector simulation, unfolded with the nominal response and
compared using the nominal covariance. This is a model-distortion test; a test with a different
generator and its own detector simulation would be stronger and is pending.

Two variations are used, in FHC and RHC. The first is GENIE multisim universe~545, a simultaneous
variation of all interaction parameters, chosen because it changes the shapes more than the rate:
the signal rate changes by $4\%$ while the true distributions change by up to $29\%$ in
$\cos\theta_\pi$ and $24\%$ in $p_\pi$. The second is the $\Delta\to N\pi$ decay angular unisim, a
targeted change of the pion angular distribution in resonance events. Both changes are larger than
the differences between the tune and the standalone generators.

Three sparse bins do not follow it. The near-threshold $p_\pi$ interval, $0.175$--$0.205$~GeV/$c$,
returns $0.40$ of the alternative truth under the multisim variation; the highest $p_\mu$ bin returns

truth under the multisim variation, $-1.1$ to $-1.2\sigma$ (\ptref{Sec.}{sec:pt_altmodel}).

\paragraph{Independent-generator closure.} The one existing NuWro overlay, for Run-1 FHC, is not used
for this test. Its normalisation disagrees with the standalone NuWro prediction for the same flux:
its true signal rate per POT is $1.76$ times that of GENIE, against $1.6$ expected from the
standalone NuWro cross section and the flux. Its production could not be traced, so a closure test
with it would test the sample rather than the extraction. A validated NuWro or GiBUU overlay
is required (\S\ref{sec:unblinding}).

\subsection{Comparing a prediction with the results}
\label{sec:ac_rowsums}

The Wiener-SVD result estimates $A_C x_{\rm true}$, and $A_C$ does not preserve the normalisation, so
the sum of a differential result over its bins is not the total cross section. The row sums of $A_C$
(Table~\ref{tab:ac_rowsums}) describe the effect: a row summing to one passes the normalisation of a
flat bin-integrated truth unchanged, and a row summing to $0.2$ passes one fifth of it. For a
non-flat prediction, bin $i$ receives $\sum_j (A_C)_{ij}\,t_j$, which depends on the individual
elements and on the shape of $t$. For FHC $p_\mu$ the ratio of the smeared integral to the true
total is $0.78$, $0.78$, $0.76$ and $0.79$ for GENIE, GiBUU, NEUT and NuWro. The true totals of the
same generators span $0.82$ to $1.32\times10^{-38}$~cm$^2$/Ar (\suppref{Table}{tab:total_xsec}), a
factor $1.6$, so a common factor near $0.78$ does not produce the differences between the
generators; they are model differences.

\paragraph{Numerical results.}
All fifty-one differential extractions, inclusive and proton-tagged, are provided in
\texttt{report/data\_release/}, indexed by \texttt{index\_extractions.tsv}: the additional-smearing
matrix, the covariance of every component in cross-section units, and \texttt{curves\_*.tsv} with the
unfolded data and every model curve shown here. The six one-bin totals, with their uncertainty
breakdown and the realised truth, are in \texttt{total\_xsec.tsv}. A prediction given as a cross
section $p_j$ in true bin $j$ is compared with the results through
\[
  \left(\frac{d\sigma}{dx}\right)^{\rm smeared}_i
  \;=\; \frac{1}{w_i}\sum_j \left(A_C\right)_{ij}\, p_j ,
\]
with $w_i$ the width of bin $i$. Applied to the true generator predictions, this reproduces the
generator curves of the result files to a largest difference of $1.2\times10^{-10}$ per bin, so a
user can check an implementation against any column of \texttt{curves\_*.tsv} before applying it to
a new model. The check detects a prediction smeared twice, which would miss the curves by up to
$55\%$ and which a closure test cannot see. The physical total cross section is the one-bin
extraction, which is not subject to $A_C$; the sum of a Wiener-SVD differential result is a quantity
in smeared space.

\subsection{Total cross section}
\label{sec:total_xsec}

The total flux-averaged cross section in the measured phase space is extracted with the same
machinery as a one-bin distribution: a single true bin that contains every signal event, defined on
$\cos\theta_\mu$ over its full range $[-1,1]$ so that no overflow exists, and a single reconstructed
bin over the same range. With one bin the response $S=\varepsilon M$ reduces to the efficiency,
nothing is regularised, the Wiener filter is switched off, and the unfolding is the inversion
\begin{equation}
  \sigma = \frac{N_\mathrm{sel} - N_\mathrm{bkg}}{\epsilon \, \Phi \, N_\mathrm{Ar}},
  \label{eq:countxsec}
\end{equation}
with $A_C\equiv1$ exactly. Here $N_\mathrm{sel}$ is the selected count, $N_\mathrm{bkg}$ the predicted
background (neutrino background, beam-off and dirt), $\epsilon =
N^{\mathrm{sig}}_\mathrm{sel}/N^{\mathrm{sig}}_\mathrm{gen}$ the selection efficiency in the measured
phase space ($17.5\%$ in every configuration), $\Phi$ the integrated flux ($\Phi = \text{POT}\times
\Phi_\mathrm{POT}$, Table~\ref{tab:flux}) and $N_\mathrm{Ar} = 8.71\times10^{29}$ the number of argon
nuclei in the fiducial volume, the same normalisation $N_\mathrm{Ar}\,\Phi/10^{-38}$ as for the
differential results. Every term of Eq.~\eqref{eq:countxsec} is evaluated universe by universe, so the
covariance contains the flux, cross-section, re-interaction and detector variations, the POT and
target terms and the simulation, beam-off and data statistical terms, propagated as for the
differential results. The flux term is $28$--$30\%$, the flux normalisation times $(1+B/S)$ with $B$
the neutrino background (\S\ref{sec:fluxanatomy}); the detector term is $20$--$22\%$ and the
data-statistical term $3$--$5\%$. The differential results carry the same terms bin by bin, with the
regularisation redistributing them between bins. Without additional smearing the one-bin result is
the physical total. It lies above the $A_C$-suppressed $\sigma_\mathrm{int}$ of
Table~\ref{tab:sigint_all}, most visibly in RHC, and agrees with the D'Agostini cross-check
(\suppref{Sec.}{sec:crosschecks}).

A closed-form evaluation of Eq.~\eqref{eq:countxsec} on the central-value prediction checks the
normalisation chain: it reproduces the one-bin extraction of the central-value prediction to four
digits ($1.0315$, $0.9285$ and $0.9723$ inclusive; $0.6269$, $0.5506$ and $0.5831$ proton-tagged) and
provides the $N_\mathrm{sel}$, $N_\mathrm{bkg}$ and $\epsilon$ columns of Table~\ref{tab:cutcount}.
The counts carry the full central-value weight (tune $\times$ PPFX $\times$ normalisation,
\S\ref{sec:mc}); the PPFX weight averages $0.944$ on selected signal, so a count without it would
lie $3$--$6\%$ above the prediction used in the unfolding.

Table~\ref{tab:cutcount} gives the total cross sections of the inclusive selection and of the
proton-tagged subsample. The $\sigma$ column is the one-bin extraction from fake data and fluctuates
around the tune by the Poisson throw; the realised truth of the throw is listed, and the ratio to it
is $0.952$--$0.998$ with $\chi^2/1\ll1$ in all six cases. The uncertainty is that of the one-bin
extraction itself. The ratio RHC/FHC of the tune is $0.900$ ($0.878$ proton-tagged); the extracted

this is also the proton-tagged total to quote; the integrals of the proton-tagged differential
results (\ptref{Table}{tab:wtki_fhc}--\ref{P-tab:wtki_comb}) are $A_C$-suppressed and depend on the
observable.

\section{Limitations and requirements for unblinding}
\label{sec:limitations}
% =============================================================================

\paragraph{Blind signal region.}
The signal region has not been examined in beam-on data, and every result is extracted from fake
data. The results therefore demonstrate the extraction and its uncertainties, not the cross section.
The background model enters strongly at $58$--$59\%$ purity, with a proton reconstructed as the pion
as the largest neutrino background, and closure tests cannot probe it because the same simulation
provides the fake data and the background estimate. It has been tested at reconstruction level in
the four control regions (\S\ref{sec:cr_outcome}), which require no correction; the offset of FHC
Run~1 relative to the other run periods is not yet explained.

\paragraph{Pion momentum.}
Charged pions interact before they stop, so the reconstructed momentum saturates near $0.34\GeVc$
and carries little information above about $0.4\GeVc$. Golden-pion tagging, multiple Coulomb
scattering, calorimetry, multivariate regression, recovery of the daughter energy and the
proton-tagged selection were all studied, and a truth-level golden selection performs no better than
the trained classifier, which places the limit in the physics of pion interactions rather than in
the estimator. For this reason $p_\pi$ is reported in two regions.

\paragraph{Model dependence.}
The closure tests use fake data from the model of the response. The test with reweighted
interaction models bounds the model bias at $0.93\sigma$ (\S\ref{sec:altmodel}), but a test with an
independent generator and its own detector simulation has not been made. The model dependence of
the efficiency matters most for the one-bin total, a single efficiency averaged over the phase space,
and for the open-ended $p_\pi>0.205$~GeV/$c$ bin, inside which the efficiency varies strongly with
the true momentum.

\paragraph{Ensemble offset.}
The ensembles place the mean result $0.4$--$1.1\%$ below the reference, at $0.5$--$1.4$ standard
deviations of the mean ($0.8\%$ at $2.0$ for the RHC total), with the same sign in all six
extractions (\S\ref{par:ensemble}). No correction or uncertainty is assigned; the offset is well
inside the statistical uncertainty of a single measurement.

\paragraph{Muon momentum scale.}
The MCS momentum-scale term (\S\ref{sec:mcs}) is provided as a separate covariance for $p_\mu$ and is
not yet part of the covariance computed by the framework. Its size, $\pm5\%$, rests on an assumption
that a data-driven comparison of MCS and range momenta on contained muons should confirm.

\paragraph{Two totals.}
The Wiener-SVD differential result estimates $A_C x_{\rm true}$, with row sums as low as $0.05$, so
the sum of a differential result is not the physical total cross section. The physical total is the
one-bin extraction.

\paragraph{Proton-tagged results.} The limitations specific to the proton-tagged subsample (the
proton mis-assignment, which no control region yet constrains, the transverse-kinematic
prescription and the binning of $W_\mathrm{had}$) are discussed in the companion
note~\cite{pt_note}.

\subsection{Requirements before the signal region is opened}
\label{sec:unblinding}

The prerequisites are listed as a checklist in \suppref{Sec.}{sec:request}. The main ones are:
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep2pt
  \item written confirmation from the NuMI flux contact that the integrated flux constants of
    Table~\ref{tab:flux} are PPFX-corrected central values (\S\ref{sec:flux_status});
  \item an understanding of the $13\%$ offset of FHC Run~1 relative to the other run periods in the
    control regions: its exposure bookkeeping, the completeness of its beam-on sample and the
    normalisation of its overlay (\S\ref{sec:cr_outcome});
  \item a closure test with a validated independent-generator overlay (\S\ref{sec:altmodel});
  \item the data-driven check of the $\pm5\%$ MCS scale and the inclusion of the term in the
    framework covariance (\S\ref{sec:mcs});
  \item the beam-on POT and gate counts of the unblinded files, checked against the run lists of
    those files, in place of the fake-data values; the Run-1 beam-off sample remains the Run-2
    substitute (\S\ref{sec:mc});
  \item a final reproduction of the result set from empty caches.
\end{enumerate}
The control regions were compared with beam-on data under the frozen procedure of
\suppref{Sec.}{sec:cr_protocol}, and any correction to the background model follows that procedure.
No switch is needed for the beam-off treatment at unblinding: the framework recognises fake data by
the presence of simulated truth, and it refuses a beam-on file of the signal region unless an
explicit unblinding switch is set. Five automated checks protect the result set against regressions:
the normalisation manifest, the assertion coupling the systematics configuration to the file lists,
the staleness check, the table-consistency check and the blinding guard
(\suppref{Sec.}{sec:pipeline}).

\section{Summary}
\label{sec:summary}
% =============================================================================

We have developed a measurement of $\nu_\mu+\bar\nu_\mu$ charged-current single charged-pion
production on argon with the full \uboone{} NuMI exposure: forward horn current
($7.766\times10^{20}$~POT), reverse horn current ($1.1082\times10^{21}$~POT) and their combination
($1.8848\times10^{21}$~POT). The signal region is blind, and all results are extracted from fake data
thrown from the central-value simulation; no statement about a generator is yet a statement about
nature.

\paragraph{Phase space and selection.}
The signal is a $\nu_\mu$ or $\bar\nu_\mu$ charged-current interaction with its vertex in the fiducial
volume and exactly one muon and one charged pion, no neutral pion or kaon and any number of nucleons,
with $p_\mu>0.15\GeVc$, $p_\pi>0.175\GeVc$ and $\theta_{\mu\pi}<2.6$~rad. The selection reaches
$17.5\%$ efficiency at $58$--$59\%$ purity. Angles are measured about the neutrino direction of each
event, $28.6^\circ$ from the detector axis.

\paragraph{Results.}
In each configuration the total flux-averaged cross section and six differential distributions are
measured: $d\sigma/dp_\mu$, $d\sigma/d\cos\theta_\mu$ and $d\sigma/d\theta_\mu$,
$d\sigma/d\cos\theta_\pi$, $d\sigma/d\theta_{\mu\pi}$, and $p_\pi$ in two regions. The total
cross section carries a $38\%$ uncertainty, with flux $28$--$30\%$ and detector $20$--$22\%$; the
flux term is the $17\%$ flux normalisation multiplied by $1+B/S$ through the background subtraction.
The differential results carry $30$--$46\%$ bin-averaged uncertainty without the data statistics
($67\%$ for FHC $p_\pi$). Each differential result is provided with its additional-smearing matrix
$A_C$, which must be applied to a prediction before comparison, and with its covariance by source.

\paragraph{Background.}
The four control regions, disjoint from the signal region, have been compared with beam-on data. The
global test passes ($\chi^2=8.1/8$), no class-specific correction of the background is supported,
and the data lie $12$--$20\%$ above the prediction, within the flux and cross-section uncertainties,
in every run period except FHC Run~1, whose offset of $13\%$ relative to the others is under
investigation.

\paragraph{Validation.}
Closure recovers the input in every extraction, with a mean ratio of $1.011$ over the eighteen
inclusive extractions. Ensembles of $100$ pseudo-experiments in six extractions give pull widths of
$0.92$--$1.07$ and correct coverage in all three configurations. Fake data from reweighted
interaction models bias no bin by more than $0.93\sigma$. The identity $UR=A_C$ holds to
$4.4\times10^{-6}$ (\suppref{App.}{sec:identity}), and the $A_C$ row sums show that the difference
between the integrals of the differential results is the additional smearing. The result set is
rebuilt from the processed ntuples with empty caches in a single pass; an independent rebuild into a
separate tree reproduced every bin of the fifty-one extractions to $10^{-9}$
(\suppref{Sec.}{sec:pipeline}), and the generator curves are reproduced from the provided $A_C$ to
$1.2\times10^{-10}$.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
The saturation of the range-based pion momentum near $0.34\GeVc$ limits $p_\pi$ to two regions and is
the main physics limitation of the selection. Before the signal region is opened, the flux
normalisation must be confirmed, the FHC Run-1 offset understood and a closure test with an
independent generator made (\S\ref{sec:unblinding}).

\section{Distributions not reported}
\label{app:withdrawn}

Two distributions were extracted and examined but are not reported, because their responses do not
support them. Their files remain in the result directory and are not to be quoted.

\subsection{The differential \texorpdfstring{$W_{\pi p}$}{W pi p} shape}

The pion--proton invariant mass would show the $\Delta(1232)$ directly and motivated the
proton-tagged subsample. At the present exposure its shape cannot be measured with any binning
tried. The six-bin scheme that resolves the resonance region has a highest bin with a $2\%$
diagonal, so nearly every event reconstructed there comes from another true bin. Two bins restore
the response ($s_{\min}/s_1=0.26$ FHC, $0.15$ RHC) but leave no shape. Closure against the
$A_C$-smeared truth does not help: it tests whether the response was inverted correctly and cannot
restore information the response does not contain. The proton-tagged sample supports the integrated
cross section and the calorimetric hadronic mass $W_\mathrm{had}$, whose response is adequate at six
bins.

\subsection{The five-bin \texorpdfstring{$d\sigma/dp_\pi$}{d sigma / d p pi}}

Charged pions interact before they stop, so the reconstructed momentum saturates near $0.34$~GeV/$c$
and the migration matrix loses its diagonal above that value: four of five bins fall below the
$0.68$ criterion, the highest reaching $6\%$. The pion momentum is therefore reported in two
regions, one near threshold and one broad inclusive region. The limitation is the estimator, since
the range is a valid momentum estimate only for a pion that stops, and no rebinning of a saturated
variable recovers the information.

\section{The \uboone{} detector}
\label{sec:detector}

\subsection{Operating Principle}
\label{sec:lartpc}

A liquid argon time projection chamber records three-dimensional ionisation images
of charged-particle tracks by exploiting two properties of ultra-pure liquid argon:
\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
  \item Ionisation electrons produced by a traversing charged particle are not
    captured by argon atoms and can drift centimetres or metres under an applied
    electric field without significant recombination, provided the purity is
    sufficient (O$_2$-equivalent $\lesssim 100$~ppt).
  \item Argon scintillates at 127~nm (vacuum ultraviolet), producing $\sim$24\,000
    photons per MeV of deposited energy, enabling nanosecond-scale timing.
\end{enumerate}
An applied electric field drifts the ionisation electrons to a set of sense-wire
planes at the anode, which record three two-dimensional projections of each track.
The third coordinate (along the drift direction) is reconstructed from the known
drift velocity and the timing of the signals relative to the beam-gate start,
combining the two-dimensional wire images into a full 3D reconstruction.

\uboone{} operated from October 2015 to July 2021 at the Fermilab Accelerator
Laboratory, collecting data in both the Booster Neutrino Beam (BNB) and, as a
parasitic experiment, the NuMI beam~\cite{numi_beam}.

\subsection{Active Volume and Electric Field}
\label{sec:tpc}

The \uboone{} active volume has rectangular geometry:
$256~\mathrm{cm}$ (drift, $x$) $\times$ $233~\mathrm{cm}$ (height, $y$) $\times$
$1037~\mathrm{cm}$ (beam, $z$), containing $\approx 87$~tonnes of liquid argon at
$\approx 87$~K.  The cathode is a stainless-steel plane at $x = 256$~cm held at
$-70$~kV; the anode wire planes are at $x = 0$.  The resulting 273~V/cm drift
field gives an electron drift velocity of $\approx 1.11$~mm/$\mu$s, so the
maximum drift time across the full gap is approximately 2.3~ms.

Liquid argon purity is maintained by continuous recirculation through oxygen and
water absorbers, keeping the electron lifetime well above 5~ms (corresponding to
O$_2$-equivalent contamination below $\sim$100~ppt).  At this purity level,
charge attenuation over the full 2.56~m drift is less than 10\%.

\subsection{Wire Readout and Signal Formation}
\label{sec:wires}

Three wire planes at the anode sample the drifting charge:
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
  \item \textbf{U plane} (first induction): 2400 wires at $+60^\circ$ from the
    vertical, 3~mm pitch.
  \item \textbf{V plane} (second induction): 2400 wires at $-60^\circ$ from the
    vertical, 3~mm pitch.
  \item \textbf{Y plane} (collection): 3456 vertical wires, 3~mm pitch.
\end{itemize}
Induction-plane wires are biased to be transparent to the drifting electrons,
producing bipolar (derivative-like) induced signals; collection-plane wires absorb
the charge and produce unipolar signals.  The combination of two stereo induction
views at $\pm60^\circ$ and a vertical collection view provides sufficient 2D
information in each plane to resolve ambiguities during 3D reconstruction.

Front-end electronics (application-specific integrated circuits, ASICs) are
mounted inside the cryostat on the wire planes and cold-multiply each channel at
$\approx 87$~K, reducing thermal noise.  The analogue signals are digitised at
16~MHz inside the cryostat by 12-bit ADCs and transmitted over $\sim$110~m of
cold cables to warm readout electronics.  The data stream is sampled into a
circular buffer; beam triggers and cosmic triggers select 4.8~ms readout windows
(corresponding to $\approx$two full drift windows) for permanent storage.

A dead-wire region at $z \in [675.1, 775.1]$~cm interrupts the collection plane
over a 100~cm span due to a shorted wire bundle.  Tracks crossing this region
have degraded hit-counting and range-based momentum estimation; the fiducial
volume (Section~\ref{sec:fv}) excludes this region.

\subsection{Photon Detection System}
\label{sec:pmt}

Thirty-two 8-inch Hamamatsu R5912-MOD photomultiplier tubes (PMTs) are installed
behind the collection-plane wire frame facing the active volume.  The PMTs detect
the 127~nm argon scintillation light via a tetraphenyl-butadiene (TPB)
wavelength-shifting coating that converts VUV photons to visible ($\sim$430~nm)
light.  The system serves two purposes in this analysis:
\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
  \item \textbf{Beam-gate synchronisation}: the PMT flash time identifies which
    1.6~$\mu$s NuMI spill window produced the neutrino interaction.
  \item \textbf{Flash-matching for cosmic rejection}: the Pandora reconstruction
    associates PFParticle hypotheses with the PMT flash closest in time and
    position, suppressing out-of-time cosmic-ray muons that overlap with
    beam-induced events in the long 2.3~ms drift window.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Calibration and Corrections}
\label{sec:calibration}

Three independent calibration inputs are used:
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
  \item \textbf{UV laser system}~\cite{uboone_laser}: two pulsed laser beams produce
    straight ionisation tracks at precisely known locations.  Deviations of the
    reconstructed track positions from the known trajectories are used to build
    a three-dimensional space-charge distortion map~\cite{sce}, which is applied to all
    reconstructed hits as a position correction.  Distortions reach $\sim$10~cm
    near the anode and cathode edges.
  \item \textbf{Stopping cosmic-ray muons}: the Bragg peak of cosmic muons
    stopping inside the active volume calibrates the absolute energy scale and
    the effective recombination factor as a function of local electric field and
    drift distance.  Through-going muons calibrate relative wire-by-wire gains
    and the electron lifetime.
  \item \textbf{Michel electrons}: $\mu^- \to e^-\bar{\nu}_e\nu_\mu$ decay
    products from stopped muons provide a check of the calorimetric
    reconstruction at $E_e \lesssim 55$~MeV.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Cosmic Ray Environment and Reconstruction}
\label{sec:cosmics}

Operating at the surface with minimal overburden, \uboone{} is exposed to a
cosmic-ray muon rate of $\approx 5$~kHz through the active volume.  Each 4.8~ms
readout window contains on average $\sim$10 cosmic-ray tracks unrelated to the
beam interaction.  The 1.6~$\mu$s NuMI spill itself contains $\lesssim 0.1$
expected cosmics on average, but the drift-window pile-up is the dominant
background at early selection stages (Section~\ref{sec:selection}).

Cosmic rejection proceeds in stages:
\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
  \item Flash-matching (PMT timing): candidate neutrino slices must have a
    consistent optical flash within the beam window.
  \item Topological score: the Pandora-based topological neutrino score
    discriminates between neutrino-like vertex structures and
    through-going/stopping cosmic topologies.
  \item Dedicated BDT scores: the MIP and cosmic
    scores trained on track-level and event-level features further suppress
    residual cosmics that passed the topological cut.
\end{enumerate}
The CRT (Cosmic Ray Tagger) sub-detector~\cite{uboone_crt}, consisting of
scintillator panels surrounding the cryostat, provides an additional cosmic
veto but is not used as a cut in the present analysis: the CRT branches are carried
through the event processing as pass-through quantities only, they are populated only
for the CRT-era data (Runs 3--5) and zero for the pre-CRT Run-1 exposure, and a cut would
require a data/MC tagging systematic; the residual beam-off background is subtracted
instead (\S\ref{sec:mc}).

Reconstruction uses the Pandora~\cite{pandora,uboone_pandora} multi-algorithm pattern recognition
framework, which groups hits into PFParticles organised in a hierarchy with the
neutrino interaction as the top-level particle.  Track candidates are fitted with
a Kalman-filter-based track fitter to obtain momentum from track curvature
(negligible for $<$300~MeV/$c$ muons at 273~V/cm) and from range for
contained tracks.  Log-likelihood ratio particle identification (LLR PID)~\cite{uboone_llr} using
the $dE/dx$ profile along the track is the primary tool for muon/pion/proton
separation (Section~\ref{sec:selection}).
