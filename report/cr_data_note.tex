\noindent\textit{Original abstract (2026-09-09).}
The four inclusive control regions of the $\mathrm{CC}\,1\mu1\pi Xp$ NuMI analysis have
been opened on beam-on data. This note reports what was processed, the yields and
distributions in each region, and the statistics of the frozen comparison procedure
applied to the data. The signal region has \emph{not} been opened: the beam-on files were
processed into a signal-region-stripped skim, verified to contain no event passing the
signal selection.

Two things must be settled before any of these numbers is read as a statement about the
background model. First, \textbf{no Run-2 beam-on file exists}, so Run~2 is excluded from
the data \emph{and} from the prediction throughout; the comparison covers $85.7\%$ of the
FHC and $76.6\%$ of the RHC exposure. Second, and more seriously, the run-by-run
comparison is not uniform: \textbf{FHC Run~1 sits at a data/prediction ratio of
$0.64$--$0.72$ in the high-statistics regions while every other run period of either beam
mode lies between $1.08$ and $1.25$}. Flux and detector uncertainties are common to the
run periods of a mode and cancel in that comparison, so the difference is not a
systematic of the prediction. The natural explanation is the exposure assumed for the
staged FHC Run-1 beam-on file, which no quantity in this comparison verifies. Until that
is resolved the mode-level ratios of \S\ref{sec:yields} should not be interpreted.

With FHC Run~1 set aside, the remaining five run periods sit coherently $10$--$25\%$ above
the prediction in all four regions. That is a common normalisation offset, the one
direction the frozen global test is explicitly insensitive to, and it is within the
$20$--$30\%$ prediction uncertainty. It is nonetheless coherent across regions of very
different composition, and is the second thing to understand.

Applied to the data, the frozen procedure's pass/fail statistic --- the global
normalisation $\chi^2$ over the eight (region, mode) totals --- gives $8.77/8$, $p=0.362$,
and no region is flagged by the per-region pulls. The shape tests, which project the
normalisation out and are therefore blind to all of the above, are a different matter: the
muon-angle distribution fails in the CC$0\pi$ and $\pi^0$ regions in \emph{both} beam
modes, by a wide margin, while the sixteen other tests are unremarkable. The same code on
pseudo-data returned all twenty-two with $p>0.47$. Under the frozen rules that removes both
of those regions from eligibility for a fitted normalisation correction, which requires a
passing shape test.
\end{abstract}

\section{What was opened, and what was not}
\label{sec:scope}
% =============================================================================

The analysis is blind in the signal region. Beam-on data were processed with the
control-region skim: an event is written only if it \emph{fails} the complete
signal-region selection \emph{and} satisfies at least one of the four approved
control-region definitions. The written file carries a marker that the cross-section
extraction refuses, and the sideband tools accept a beam-on file only if it carries that
marker. Each processing job re-read its own output and deleted it unless the marker was
present, no written event carried the signal-region flag, and every written event fell in
at least one control region. All eight files passed.

Because the skim keeps every control-region event and discards only events that are in the
signal region or in no region at all, the control-region distributions below are complete:
nothing that belongs in a control region has been dropped.

\paragraph{The CRT-era trigger flag.} The Run-4 and Run-5 beam-on files carry a
software-trigger flag that is identically zero and no companion pre-CRT flag, exactly the
pattern of the CRT-era beam-off samples. The branch is bound only when the companion
branch is present, so these files keep the fail-open default and are not rejected. Had
that not been the case, Runs~4 and~5 would have processed to zero events silently. This
was verified on the beam-on files directly before processing.

\paragraph{No Run-2 beam-on data.}
The staged files cover run numbers 4952--6998, 14117--17566 and 19660--25759. Nothing
lies in the range 6999--14116, which contains all of Run~2. The exposure the prediction is
normalised to assigns Run~2 $1.268\times10^{20}$~POT in FHC and $2.591\times10^{20}$ in
RHC, so the missing period is $14.3\%$ of the FHC, $23.4\%$ of the RHC and $19.4\%$ of the
combined exposure. Run~2 is therefore dropped from the data and from the prediction ---
the Monte Carlo, the beam-off and the dirt --- everywhere in this note. The beam-off side
of the analysis stands Run~1 in for Run~2 permanently, because beam-off is a
cosmic-only sample matched by run period; that substitution is not available for beam-on
data, which is the measurement.

\paragraph{FHC Run 1 is not a systematic of the prediction.}
The flux uncertainty is a hadron-production model uncertainty common to every run period
of a beam mode, and the detector variations are taken from the same Run-4 samples for all
periods, so both are almost fully correlated between run periods and cancel in a
period-to-period comparison. A prediction systematic can move all FHC periods together;
it cannot put Run~1 at $0.64$ while Runs~4 and~5 sit at $1.17$ and $1.14$. The effect is
also flat across the four regions of Run~1 --- $0.64$, $0.91$, $0.72$, $0.60$ --- which is
the signature of a normalisation applied to the whole period rather than of a
mis-modelled background class, since the four regions have very different compositions.

The quantity that is not verified anywhere in this comparison is the Run-1 FHC beam-on
exposure itself. The prediction is scaled to $3.283\times10^{20}$~POT and the beam-off to
$9\,846\,635$ beam-on gates, both taken from the trigger table; the beam-on ntuples carry
no exposure record of their own, so nothing here cross-checks them against the file that
was actually staged. The staged FHC Run-1 file spans runs 4952--6748 and the RHC Run-1
file begins at run 6748, so Run~1 is split between the horn modes inside the run range,
which is exactly the situation in which a partial file or a mis-assigned split is easy to
miss. The size of the effect is a concrete, falsifiable prediction. Every component of the
Run-1 prediction --- the Monte Carlo through its POT scale, the beam-off through its gate
ratio, the dirt through its exposure --- is proportional to the assumed Run-1 exposure, so
an exposure error rescales the whole prediction. Bringing FHC Run~1 onto the
$1.15$--$1.20$ of the other periods requires the true exposure to be a fraction
$f\simeq0.51$--$0.62$ of the assumed one, that is roughly $1.7$--$2.0\times10^{20}$~POT
in place of $3.283\times10^{20}$, with the three high-statistics regions giving
$f=0.55$ (CC$0\pi$), $0.62$ ($\pi^0$) and $0.51$ (cosmic). The consistency of $f$ across
regions of different composition is itself the evidence that a single multiplicative
exposure factor, and not a background class, is what is wrong. If instead the trigger
table is confirmed against the staged file, the deficit is real and must be treated as a
localised reconstruction failure under the response tree.

\paragraph{The remaining five periods carry a common offset.}
Setting FHC Run~1 aside, the five other periods sit above the prediction by $10$--$25\%$
in the high-statistics regions, in both beam modes. This is a common normalisation
offset. It is within the $20$--$30\%$ prediction uncertainty, and it is the one direction
the global test of \S\ref{sec:protocol} is built to be insensitive to, because the
correlated flux, cross-section and detector terms absorb it. Two features are worth
recording for whatever investigation follows: the offset appears in regions whose
beam-off fraction ranges from $3$--$5\%$ (multi-$\pi$) to $28$--$39\%$ (cosmic), which
argues against a beam-off scaling error; and it appears in both horn modes, which argues against a
mode-specific flux problem.

\section{The frozen comparison procedure applied to the data}
\label{sec:protocol}
% =============================================================================

The frozen procedure defines one pass/fail statistic --- the global normalisation $\chi^2$
over the eight (region, mode) totals with the full pre-fit covariance --- and two
diagnostics, the per-region pulls and the normalisation-marginalised shape tests of the
frozen distributions. All three are computed here with exactly the machinery that was
exercised on pseudo-data before opening, with only the data, the retained run periods and
the removal of the pseudo-data beam-off throw changed. On pseudo-data that machinery
returned a global $\chi^2$ of $0.66/8$ and all twenty-two shape tests with $p>0.47$, so it
is not predisposed to fail.

\paragraph{Normalisation: the set passes.} The global $\chi^2$ is $8.77$ on eight degrees
of freedom, $p=0.362$. No region is flagged by the $|{\rm pull}|>2$ criterion against the
full covariance; the largest is the RHC cosmic region at $1.63$. That is the expected
outcome given \S\ref{sec:perrun}: the offsets are $10$--$25\%$ against a prediction
uncertainty of $19$--$34\%$ per region, and they are strongly correlated between regions
($0.4$--$0.9$), which is exactly the direction the test cannot resolve. Against the
statistical and detector terms alone, which is the diagnostic column, RHC $\pi^0$ reaches
$3.14$ and RHC cosmic $2.21$.

\paragraph{Shape: the muon angle fails, and it is not the normalisation offset.} \textbf{\emph{Superseded
2026-09-16: the muon-angle failures are an artefact of the stale sideband Monte Carlo (\S\ref{sec:stale_mc});
the corrected tests are in Table~\ref{tab:protocol_corrected}.}}
The shape tests project the overall normalisation out, so they are blind to everything
\S\ref{sec:perrun} is about. Six of the twenty-two fail at $p<0.01$, and they are not
scattered: they are the muon momentum and, far more strongly, the muon angle in the two
high-statistics regions, CC$0\pi$ and $\pi^0$, in \emph{both} beam modes. The
$\cos\theta_\mu$ values, $351/4$ and $210/4$ in FHC and $584/4$ and $241/4$ in RHC, are
not marginal. The remaining sixteen tests, including every distribution of the multi-$\pi$
and cosmic regions and the $\pi^0$ shower energy that the region was built to test, are
unremarkable.

Four things bound how far this can be taken.
\begin{itemize}\itemsep3pt
  \item \textbf{It is not an artefact of the test.} The same code on pseudo-data returned
    all twenty-two with $p>0.47$. The failures appear only when real data is substituted.
  \item \textbf{It is not the FHC Run-1 exposure.} A wrong weight on one run period does
    distort a summed distribution, because the periods do not have identical shapes, so
    the FHC numbers are not clean. But the largest failures are in RHC, which contains no
    period suspected of an exposure error.
  \item \textbf{The test is very sensitive by construction.} With $10^4$ events per region
    and a covariance dominated by normalisation-like flux and detector terms, projecting
    the normalisation out leaves a small shape covariance, so a discrepancy of a few per
    cent per bin produces a large $\chi^2$. The size of these numbers should not be read as
    the size of the effect.
  \item \textbf{The binning concentrates the forward peak.} The released
    $\cos\theta_\mu$ edges are $-1$, $0.45$, $0.65$, $0.8$, $0.9$, $1$: the first bin spans
    three quarters of the range, so the test is largely comparing the forward peak with
    everything else. A forward-angle discrepancy is where the \uboone{} BNB
    CC$1\pi^\pm$ measurement also found every tested generator above the data.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Procedural consequence.} \emph{(Superseded 2026-09-16 for the muon angle; the $p_\mu$ failures of
the $\pi^0$ region and of RHC CC$0\pi$ survive the correction, so the conclusion on eligibility for a fitted
correction is unchanged.)}
Under the frozen procedure a fitted normalisation correction is permitted only for a
region whose shape test passes, so that the discrepancy is consistent with a pure
normalisation shift. On these numbers neither CC$0\pi$ nor $\pi^0$ qualifies: both fail
their muon-angle shape test in both modes. Whatever the eventual explanation, a fitted
correction to either region is not available under the rules as frozen, and the response
tree points instead at the added-uncertainty branch or at a model revision with renewed
approval. That conclusion should be revisited once the exposure questions of
\S\ref{sec:perrun} are closed and the tests are repeated.

\paragraph{Removing the statistical part.} A knob-minus-central-value difference also carries the statistical
fluctuation of the finite samples, which a covariance built from the shifts counts as detector uncertainty. The
knob samples are re-simulations of the \emph{same} generated events: matching on the true neutrino energy and
vertex pairs $96.8$--$97.0\%$ of the FHC knob events with central-value events ($94.1\%$ for Recomb2) and
$97.6$--$98.1\%$ in RHC. The statistical noise of the difference therefore comes only from events that migrate
between bins and from the unmatched few per cent. It is estimated with a Poisson bootstrap in which every
generated event receives one Poisson(1) weight shared by its central-value and knob copies (300 replicas):
\begin{gather}
  f_{k,i}=\frac{N^{k}_{i}\,{\rm POT}_{\rm CV}/{\rm POT}_{k}-N^{\rm CV}_{i}}{N^{\rm CV}_{i}},\qquad
  C_k=\mathrm{Cov}_{\rm bootstrap}(f_k),\\
  V_{{\rm det},ij}=\Big[\textstyle\sum_k \big(f_k f_k^{\mathsf T}-C_k\big)\Big]_{{\rm PSD},\,ij}\,\mu_i\,\mu_j,
\end{gather}
since $\mathrm{E}[f f^{\mathsf T}]=\varphi\varphi^{\mathsf T}+C$ for true shifts $\varphi$; the sum is projected
onto the positive semi-definite cone by eigenvalue clipping. On the CC$0\pi$ total the bootstrap noise is $0.3\%$
against $0.9\%$ for independent samples. On region totals the subtraction is a small correction; on
finely binned distributions it is not: in the $(\cos\theta_\mu\times p_\mu)$ bins of \S\ref{sec:jointfit} the
median quadrature shift is $8.3\%$ (FHC), of which $7.3\%$ is statistical, leaving $3.9\%$
(Fig.~\ref{fig:detvar_matched}). The resulting detector term is $1.9\%$ on the FHC CC$0\pi$ total ($20.2\%$
unscaled), $0.8\%$ on $\pi^0$ ($20.1\%$), $6.5\%$ on cosmic ($21.7\%$) and $8.3\%$ in the signal region ($21.0\%$);
in RHC $2.1$, $1.8$, $7.0$ and $8.5\%$ (unscaled $8.4$, $7.2$, $30.9$, $15.4\%$).

\section{Joint FHC+RHC background fit}
\label{sec:jointfit}
% =============================================================================

\paragraph{Status.} This section develops and validates the fit proposed in \texttt{BACKGROUND\_FIT\_PLAN.md} and
applies it, \emph{post-inspection}, to the control regions opened in this note. It is not a response under the
frozen procedure, it does not touch the signal region, and none of its outputs is a release input; using any of
them would be a documented amendment requiring renewed approval. Code: \texttt{xsec\_analyzer/bkgfit/} and
\texttt{macros/bkgfit\_templates.C}; results \texttt{logs/bkgfit/*.json}.

\subsection{Model}
\label{sec:fit_model}
\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
  \item \textbf{Templates} per run period (FHC Runs 1, 2, 4, 5; RHC Runs 1, 2, 3, 4; Run~2 Monte Carlo only, for
    full-exposure projections) from \texttt{sb\_pi0/}: the neutrino Monte Carlo split into five truth classes
    (signal; $\nu_\mu$CC $0\pi$; CC$+$NC with a $\pi^0$; $\nu_\mu$CC $\geq2\pi^\pm$ without $\pi^0$; other) and by
    $\nu/\bar\nu$, with its central value, $\sum w^2$ and all 2\,230 systematic universes (PPFX 600, GENIE
    multisim 600, re-interaction 1\,000, the nine GENIE unisims and the two SCC pairs); the beam-off per run period
    with the gate ratios of the frozen procedure; the dirt; the beam-on skim.
  \item \textbf{Bins}: CC$0\pi$ and $\pi^0$ in reconstructed $\cos\theta_\mu$ (edges $-1,0,0.45,0.65,0.8,0.9,0.95,1$)
    $\times$ $p_\mu$ ($0.15,0.35,0.75,\infty$\GeVc), 21 each, and the cosmic total (``2D''); or the three totals
    (``totals''). Multi-$\pi$ overlaps $\pi^0$ and is left out. The binning requires $p_\mu\ge0.15$\GeVc, which
    removes about $2\%$ of the region events in data and prediction alike.
  \item \textbf{Parameters}: normalisations of truth classes, applied in every universe so that their correlation
    with the flux and cross-section terms is kept; optionally per beam mode, and an exposure factor per run period.
  \item \textbf{Covariance}: the universe families with the extraction's conventions (multisims averaged, unisims
    summed), the event-matched noise-subtracted detector term of \S\ref{sec:detvar_pot} (fully correlated between
    run periods and between the two modes, noise within a mode), POT $2\%$ and targets $1\%$, Monte Carlo, beam-off
    and dirt statistics, and the data term in the Pearson form (prediction variance).
  \item \textbf{Test statistic}: $\chi^2(\theta)=r^{\mathsf T}V^{-1}r$ minimised with Minuit2 (MIGRAD, HESSE, MINOS).
    Two choices are forced by validation. The systematic covariance is held at the nominal prediction: evaluated
    at $\theta$, a poor fit is ``improved'' by inflating it and the parameters run to their limits. The data
    variance is held there too: evaluated at $\theta$ it grows with the prediction and biased the 2D toys upward by
    one standard deviation. It is the Pearson form, the prediction itself: the combined Neyman--Pearson form depends
    on the data and leaves a residual $+0.02$ to $+0.03\sigma$ pull bias that only $10^5$-toy ensembles resolve
    (\noteref{Sec.}{sec:validation}). An iterated variant re-evaluates both at the result.
\end{itemize}

\section{Far sidebands: control regions with less signal in them}
\label{sec:farsb}

Every control region contains some signal. That is what makes a background constraint bite its own
tail: the fit lowers a background component, and the signal region loses the same events a second
time through the response. The four regions carry $5$--$22\%$ signal at data exposure, the
multi-$\pi$ region worst. A \emph{far} sideband adds one cut that pushes the region away from the
signal topology and pays for it in statistics. Three numbers decide whether the trade is worth
taking, and Table~\ref{tab:farsb} gives all three: the signal fraction, the events kept, and the
background mixture --- because a far sideband that constrains a \emph{different} mixture is not a
purer version of the near one, it is a different region, and it constrains the signal region less
well however clean it is. Nothing here needs approval: these are the control regions already opened,
with an extra cut that can only reduce their signal content.

Four things follow.

\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
  \item \textbf{The CC0$\pi$ region has a cheap far definition.} Requiring the muon to be the only
    non-proton-like primary track takes the signal fraction from $8.6\%$ to $2.2\%$, a factor of
    four, while keeping $58\%$ of the events and \emph{raising} the CC0$\pi$ content of the
    background from $72\%$ to $85\%$. It is purer in both senses at once, which is unusual and
    makes it the one far definition worth adopting outright. Demanding exactly one proton as well
    gains little ($1.7\%$) for another third of the statistics.
  \item \textbf{The $\pi^0$ region improves more slowly.} Two showers halve the statistics to reach
    $6.4\%$ signal and three showers cut them to a fifth for $4.3\%$. The $\pi^0$ content does rise
    to $75\%$, so the far region is a better-targeted $\pi^0$ constraint, but the signal fraction
    falls more slowly than the events do --- the residual signal is CC1$\pi^\pm$ with a genuine
    extra shower, which no shower-counting cut removes.
  \item \textbf{The multi-$\pi$ region is the contaminated one and cannot be fixed by cuts.} At
    $22.1\%$ signal it is the region a constraint should trust least, and the only lever available
    is the track count, which halves it to $12.3\%$ at $42\%$ of the statistics --- still four times
    the signal contamination of the far CC0$\pi$ region, with $101$ data events. The $\pi^0$
    multiplicity cut is not available here: \texttt{pion\_number\_reco} saturates at two, so
    ``$\geq3$ pions'' selects nothing, and $\geq3$ non-proton tracks is already implied by the
    region's own definition.
  \item \textbf{The cosmic region changes character rather than purity.} Signal falls $5.4\to3.3\%$,
    but the beam-off and dirt share of the background rises from $33\%$ to $44\%$. That is the point
    of the region, so this is a feature; it does mean the far version constrains the cosmic and dirt
    normalisation specifically rather than the neutrino background.
\end{itemize}

The most useful number in the table is one that does \emph{not} move. The data-to-prediction ratio
is flat under the far cuts --- CC0$\pi$ $1.12\to1.13\to1.13$, multi-$\pi$ $1.12\to1.09$, $\pi^0$
$1.20\to1.23$ (the three-shower version, $1.35$ on $4\%$ signal, moves with the shower content, not the
signal) --- while the signal fraction falls by up to a factor of four. Whatever produces the
$12$--$20\%$ excess of data over prediction in the neutrino-dominated regions, it is therefore not
signal contamination and not anything that scales with it. The cosmic region is the exception that
locates it: as the far cuts raise its beam-off share from $35\%$ to $45\%$, its ratio falls from
$1.21$ to $1.0$, so the excess sits in the neutrino simulation, not in the beam-off. (An earlier
version of this table quoted an $8$--$16\%$ \emph{deficit}: the prediction then included the FHC and RHC
Run-2 periods, which have simulation but no beam-on file, and the FHC Run-1 exposure was wrong. Both
are corrected here.) That is a real constraint on the explanation, and it is available without
unblinding anything.

\section{Selection change of 2026-10-02: the Bragg-pion requirement}
\label{sec:braggfix}

The pion identification of the single-pion selection no longer requires a Bragg-pion score of at least $0.08$. The
beam-on, beam-off and dirt ntuples of FHC Run~1 and RHC Runs~1 and~3b do not carry the score, so the requirement had
applied to the simulation and to only part of the data. An event in which a candidate failed only this requirement now
counts that candidate as a pion and leaves CC$0\pi$, for the signal region or, with a second pion, the multi-$\pi$

Run~1 and RHC Runs~1 and~3 the beam-on selection does not change, since the requirement never applied there, and only
the prediction moves; in the Run-4 and Run-5 data about $85$ predicted events of the new signal region, $6$--$7\%$ of
it, were in the opened control regions before the change. Tables~\ref{tab:yields_braggfix}, \ref{tab:protocol_braggfix},

\section{What this establishes, and what it does not}
\label{sec:conclusions}
% =============================================================================

\begin{itemize}\itemsep3pt
  \item The processing chain runs on real beam-on data of both productions, pre-CRT and
    CRT-era, without error, and the blinding mechanism behaves as designed: no
    signal-region event was written, and the outputs are refused by the extraction.
  \item The control-region distributions are complete and are shown for the first time
    against data.
  \item \textbf{No conclusion about the background model should be drawn yet.} Two
    bookkeeping issues sit in front of any such conclusion: the missing Run-2 exposure,
    which is excluded consistently here but leaves $19\%$ of the analysis unexamined, and
    the FHC Run-1 anomaly, which has the size and the flatness of an exposure error.
  \item \emph{(Superseded 2026-09-16 and 2026-09-19: with corrected inputs the global test failed because of the
    wrong FHC Run-1 exposure and passes on the corrected one, $8.11/8$; the muon-angle failure was an artefact of
    the stale sideband Monte Carlo; see \S\ref{sec:protocol_corrected} and the status list below.)} The frozen normalisation test passes. The muon-angle shape test does not, in the
    two high-statistics regions and in both beam modes; the failure survives the removal of
    the normalisation, is absent from the same code run on pseudo-data, and is largest in
    RHC, which carries no suspected exposure error. It is the one result here that the
    bookkeeping issues do not obviously explain, and it is the thing to understand next
    after them. Its size should not be read from the $\chi^2$: the shape covariance is
    small once the normalisation is projected out, so a few per cent per bin goes a long
    way, and the released binning puts three quarters of the $\cos\theta_\mu$ range in one
    bin.
  \item The frozen procedure's own response tree treats a single-period excursion as a
    reconstruction or exposure issue to be investigated, not as a model correction, and
    forbids excluding a period without independent evidence of a defect. That is the path
    FHC Run~1 should follow.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Required next.}
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=3pt]
  \item Confirm the exposure and gate count of the staged FHC Run-1 beam-on file against
    the trigger table, and confirm that the FHC/RHC split of Run~1 at run 6748 is the one
    the exposure table assumes.
  \item Establish whether Run-2 beam-on data exist upstream. If they do, stage and process
    them; if they do not, Run~2 must be removed from the exposure and the whole
    normalisation regenerated.
  \item Repeat this comparison once both are settled, and only then read the
    normalisation offset of \S\ref{sec:perrun} as a statement about the background.
  \item \emph{(Done 2026-09-16: the failure was the stale sideband Monte Carlo, \S\ref{sec:stale_mc}; the open
    shape question is now the muon momentum.)} Investigate the muon-angle shape failure independently of the exposure work: it is
    present in RHC alone, so it can be studied on the RHC periods while the FHC Run-1
    question is open. A finer $\cos\theta_\mu$ binning of the control regions, which is
    not the released signal-region binning and is free to be chosen, would show whether
    the discrepancy is confined to the forward peak.
  \item The prerequisites for opening the signal region are unchanged and none of them is
    met by this note: the alternative-generator closure, the flux central-value
    confirmation, the MCS momentum bound, the clean-cache reproduction, and the
    inverted-PID regions for the misidentification transfers.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Status after 2026-09-16.}
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=3pt]
  \item The muon-angle shape failure is withdrawn: it compared a detector-frame prediction with beam-frame data
    (\S\ref{sec:stale_mc}). The $p_\mu$ failures of the $\pi^0$ region and of RHC CC$0\pi$ remain and are the open
    shape question; they point at the muon momentum (\S\ref{sec:jointfit}).
  \item With the detector term corrected (\S\ref{sec:detvar_pot}) the frozen global test failed with FHC Run~1 and
    passed without it; the FHC Run-1 exposure check resolved it (non-open-trigger sample, $2.192\times10^{20}$~POT)
    and on the corrected exposure the test passes with all periods, $8.11/8$ ($p=0.42$). What remains is the
    $13\pm2\%$ offset of FHC Run~1 relative to the other five periods (Table~\ref{tab:perperiod_corrected}),
    which is now the gating item.
  \item The sideband transfer-factor uncertainties are unaffected by the POT defect; the control-region
    systematics table of the analysis note (detector column) and the frozen-procedure numbers quoted there are, and
    must be regenerated with \texttt{sb\_protocol\_data.C} defaults.
  \item The joint fit shows that no normalisation correction is warranted and that a normalisation fit propagated
    under the frozen rules cannot reduce the measurement's uncertainty; a conditional constraint could ($35.8\to11.3\%$
    on the one-bin total, Asimov), entirely through the flux and detector correlations. Adopting it would change what
    the control regions are for and needs a collaboration decision.
  \item (2026-10-01) The dirt was seven to twenty times too small throughout this note (\S\ref{sec:dirtfix}). With
    it corrected the frozen test passes at $6.03/8$ and no correction is warranted; the FHC Run-1 residual
    ($0.862\pm0.021$) remains the gating item; the cosmic far sidebands prefer less dirt than predicted, and the
    analysis assigns no dirt normalisation uncertainty.
\end{enumerate}
