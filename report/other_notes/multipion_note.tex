\begin{titlepage}
  \centering
  \vspace*{2cm}
  {\LARGE\bfseries Measurement of $\nu_\mu+\bar\nu_\mu$ Charged-Current Multi-Pion Production
  on Argon with the MicroBooNE Detector in the NuMI Beam\par}
  \vspace{0.8cm}
  {\large Technical Note: the Two-Charged-Pion Final State
  (\texttt{CC1mu2piBDT})\par}
  \vspace{0.5cm}
  {\normalsize Draft 0.9, 2026-10-18\par}
  \vspace{1.5cm}
  % NOTE: author list to be confirmed. Contributors to the multi-pion effort:
  {\large Tusharadri Mahmud, Linhui Gu, Ishanee Pophale, Jarek Nowak\par}
  \vspace{0.5cm}
  {\normalsize\textit{Lancaster University}\par}
  \vspace{2cm}
  \begin{abstract}
    We describe the extension of the \uboone{} NuMI $\nu_\mu+\bar\nu_\mu$ charged-current
    single-charged-pion analysis to final states with two charged pions and any number of
    nucleons, CC$2\pi^\pm X p$. The selection follows the strategy of the BNB CC$2\pi^\pm Np$
    analysis~\cite{majeed}: a four-class particle classifier (muon, pion, proton, other) on every
    track, the assignment of the tracks to one muon and $N$ pions by the largest summed
    log-probability, and an event classifier on the assigned probabilities, trained in two folds by event number so
    that no simulated event is scored by a model that saw it. On the full NuMI exposure
    ($7.766\times10^{20}$~POT in forward and $1.1082\times10^{21}$~POT in reverse horn current) it
    selects $15\%$ of the signal at $48\%$ purity in the combined configuration, against $18\%$ at
    $26\%$ for the selection of the first draft of this note. Without a constraint the uncertainty of the
    one-bin total is dominated by the flux, amplified by the subtraction of a background as large as the
    signal, and by the detector variations. Four control samples defined from the classifier outputs
    condition the covariance of the extraction framework, partly through the signal they contain: in the
    combined configuration the one-bin
    total carries $16.5\%$ with the constraint ($19.0\%$ with the MCS-calibration and soft-pion efficiency terms) and $50\%$
    without, with data statistics then the largest term; treatments that reduce the transfer of the signal model
    from the sidebands give $20$ to $24\%$. Ensembles of pseudo-experiments return the expected truth within
    $1\%$, with nominal statistical coverage without the constraint and a conservative statistical term with it,
    and ensemble means for two reweighted interaction models lie within $0.11\sigma$ of their own truth. In the opened
    control regions of the single-pion analysis, calibrating the MCS momentum of exiting tracks as in the
    single-pion analysis brings the classifier inputs much closer to the beam-on data; with the systematic
    covariance of the prediction the class probabilities, the MCS momentum and the event score are
    statistically compatible with the data, while the LLR score remains incompatible at its proton end, where the long
    protons sit ($p=0.016$), and follows the recombination variation of the detector term at $1.4$ times its size, which moves no
    result by more than $0.12$ of its uncertainty beyond that term. For differential measurements, the
    muon momentum and angle, the angles of the two pions, their opening angles and the proton multiplicity pass the
    $0.50$ migration criterion with 2 to 8 bins and $8$ to $23\%$ data statistics per bin; the pion momenta,
    limited by the saturation of the range momentum, give 2 or 3 bins. Extracted through the framework with the
    sideband constraint, the seven variables are compatible with their regularisation-smeared truths in the nominal
    pseudo-data realisation, with $15$ to $58\%$ uncertainty per bin and $15$ to $18\%$ on their integrals in the
    combined configuration. Three or more pions are not measurable at this exposure and serve as a control
    sample. The signal region is blind, and every result is extracted from pseudo-data.
  \end{abstract}
\end{titlepage}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Charged-current (CC) production of multiple charged pions on nuclei sits at the transition between
the resonant (RES) and deep-inelastic (DIS) regimes of neutrino--nucleus scattering and is strongly
shaped by the intranuclear hadronic cascade. A $\nu_\mu$ interaction that produces several pions at
the primary vertex may have some of them absorbed, charge-exchanged or produced in re-scattering
before they leave the nucleus, so the observed charged-pion multiplicity is a direct probe of
final-state interactions (FSI). Multi-pion final states are also a background to exclusive
single-pion and pion-less measurements, so a measurement of their rate on argon is useful both as a
cross-section result and as a constraint on the interaction model used across the \uboone{}
programme.

This note documents the extension of the NuMI $\nu_\mu+\bar\nu_\mu$ CC$1\pi^\pm$
analysis~\cite{note1} to two charged pions. The first draft of this note (2026-08-13) defined the
selections \texttt{CC1mu2pi} and \texttt{CC1mu3pi} as subselections of the single-pion selection
\texttt{CC1mu1piXp} with a looser pion identification~\cite{mahmud,gu}, on the Run-1 forward-horn
overlay only. Since then the analysis has been rebuilt on the strategy of the BNB CC$2\pi^\pm Np$
analysis~\cite{majeed} and on the current single-pion release: a baseline on the full exposure
(Section~\ref{sec:baseline}), a particle classifier (Section~\ref{sec:pid}), the track assignment and
an event classifier (Section~\ref{sec:event}), a study of the detector term
(Section~\ref{sec:detrobust}), control samples and a background constraint
(Section~\ref{sec:constraint}), the extraction of the one-bin total through the framework
(Section~\ref{sec:framework}), and the comparison of its inputs with beam-on data in the opened control
regions, with the calibration of the MCS momentum and the selection variants (Section~\ref{sec:crdata}), the two-fold
event classifier that removes an overtraining of the first one (Section~\ref{sec:twofold}), and the variables of the
differential measurements (Section~\ref{sec:variables}). The
samples, normalisation, systematic universes and Wiener-SVD
unfolding~\cite{wsvd} are those of the single-pion analysis.

The decisions that fix the scope are:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item D1: the signal is inclusive in nucleons, CC$2\pi^\pm Xp$, with a subsample with at least one
        proton for comparison with the BNB analysis.
  \item D2: a per-pion momentum threshold of $0.10\GeVc$ for the signal, totals and angles;
        pion-momentum bins only above $0.175\GeVc$, where the range estimator is unbiased.
  \item D3: the two-pion signal region is blind. Part of it lies in the multi-$\pi$ control region of
        the single-pion analysis, which has been compared with beam-on data.
  \item D4: NuGraph inputs are not used; NuMI samples processed with NuGraph are not available.
  \item D5: an independent-generator closure with a NuMI NuWro overlay, conditional on the validation of that sample.
  \item D6: the truth requirement $\theta(\mu,\text{leading}~\pi)<2.6$~rad has a reconstructed
        counterpart on the longer assigned pion, the track length standing in for the momentum.
  \item D7: no exclusivity cut against the blind single-pion signal region; the overlapping events
        are opened only after the single-pion signal region.
  \item D8: the measurements stop at two pions. Events with three or more pion candidates are a
        control sample of the two-pion background.
\end{itemize}

\section{Signal definition}
\label{sec:signal}

The signal is a $\nu_\mu$ or $\bar\nu_\mu$ charged-current interaction with its vertex in the
fiducial volume of the single-pion analysis, one muon with $p_\mu>0.15\GeVc$, exactly two charged pions
leaving the nucleus, no neutral pion, kaon or heavier meson, and any number of nucleons. The pion charge is not
measured. Both pions must have $p_\pi>0.10\GeVc$; the multiplicity is counted at any momentum, so an event with
a third charged pion below $0.10\GeVc$ is not signal (such events amount to $6.2\%$ of the signal on the Run-4
forward-horn overlay). The opening angle between the muon and the leading pion, the one of highest true
momentum, is below $2.6$~rad, as in the single-pion definition. The subsample with
at least one proton above $0.3\GeVc$ is $81\%$ of the signal.

\paragraph{Pion-momentum threshold.} In a two- or three-pion event the sub-leading pions are softer
than the leading one, and the softest pion sets how often the full topology can be reconstructed. On
the Run-1 forward-horn overlay the mean leading (softest) true pion momentum is $0.52$ ($0.27$)$\GeVc$
for two pions and $0.57$ ($0.21$)$\GeVc$ for three. The efficiency of the first-draft selection,
evaluated on the signal whose softest pion lies above a threshold $p_\pi^{\min}$, is given in
Table~\ref{tab:mp_thr}.

Raising the threshold changes the efficiency little: with every pion above $0.175\GeVc$ about four in
five events are still lost, so the efficiency is limited by the reconstruction of several tracks from
one vertex, not by the soft pions. The threshold of $0.10\GeVc$, the pion tracking turn-on, removes the
unreconstructable tail ($10\%$ of the two-pion signal) and keeps $90\%$ of it; $0.175\GeVc$ would keep
$65\%$ of the two-pion signal in the combined configuration. Pion-momentum bins start at $0.175\GeVc$
(D2).

\section{Samples, exposure and normalisation}
\label{sec:samples}

The samples and normalisation are those of the single-pion release: fourteen $\nu_\mu$ overlay files
(forward horn current Runs 1, 2, 4, 5; reverse horn current Runs 1, 2, 3, 4a, 4b, 4c), seven beam-off
and seven dirt samples, each run normalised to its recorded exposure, $7.766\times10^{20}$~POT in
forward horn current (FHC), $1.1082\times10^{21}$~POT in reverse horn current (RHC) and
$1.8848\times10^{21}$~POT combined. The detector variations are the Run-4 FHC and RHC samples, a
central-value sample and eight variations each (light yield down and Rayleigh scattering,
recombination, space charge and four wire-response variations). The beam-on data are not used: the
pseudo-data are Poisson throws, run by run, of the overlays and the dirt at the data exposure, to
which the framework adds the beam-off prediction. Generator predictions with the two-pion signal
definition have not yet been produced.

\section{Baseline on the full exposure}
\label{sec:baseline}

\texttt{CC1mu2pi} and \texttt{CC1mu3pi} inherit the \texttt{CC1mu1piXp} selection (slice, fiducial
volume, topology and muon candidate) with a looser pion identification: vertex distance up to
$9.5\cm$, the pion BDT of the first draft in place of the single-pion identification, up to two
uncontained pion candidates, no shower veto and no single-pion opening-angle cut. Following D6 they
now require $\theta(\mu,\text{longest pion candidate})<2.6$~rad. The selections store every counted
candidate (containment, length, range and MCS momenta, direction, identification scores and the
backtracked truth), the true pions, the proton and kaon counts and a truth topology class. On a
30\,000-event slice every branch of the single-pion selection is identical before and after these
changes. Table~\ref{tab:baseline} gives the baseline at the full exposure, beam-off and dirt included.

\section{Particle classifier}
\label{sec:pid}

A four-class classifier (muon, pion, proton, other) is trained with XGBoost~\cite{xgboost} on the tracks of the
pion-candidate pool of the selection (generation 2, track score $\geq0.3$), dumped from all fourteen
overlays ($3.1$~million tracks). Tracks are labelled by their backtracked particle; tracks without a
truth match, mostly cosmic rays of the overlay, are the class ``other''. Training uses run periods 1,
2 and 4 of both horn modes; the held-out periods are RHC Run~3 and FHC Run~5. The beam-on, beam-off and
dirt ntuples of the older production (Runs 1--3) lack twenty of the track branches every overlay
carries (the Bragg-pion likelihoods, truncated $dE/dx$, deflection, end spacepoints, hit and
descendant counts), so the deployed models use the 43 inputs every sample carries. The model is
evaluated in the selection through ROOT's \texttt{RBDT}~\cite{tmva}, which reproduces XGBoost exactly.
Figure~\ref{fig:pid} shows the pion ROC curves on the whole pool of the held-out runs and the pion probability of
the adopted classifier by true class.

The gain is the same in FHC and RHC and for $\pi^+$ and $\pi^-$. With the unmatched tracks as the
fourth class, at the pion efficiency of the first-draft BDT ($91.8\%$) the purity of the counted pion
candidates rises from $27.6\%$ to $43.3\%$ and the fraction of protons passing falls from $13.4\%$ to
$4.1\%$ (63 inputs). With the 43 inputs every sample carries, the pion ROC area on the full pool,
unmatched tracks included, is $0.938$ ($0.946$ with all inputs, $0.868$ for the first-draft BDT). The
pion efficiency does not depend on the true pion momentum more than that of the first-draft BDT and is
higher at threshold. Muon against pion is the hardest separation ($0.895$; about a third of the true
muons in the pool still pass).

Used as the pion identification of the single-pion selection at the same pion efficiency, the
43-input classifier raises the purity of the counted tracks from $52.6\%$ to $64.8\%$ and, at event level on the
held-out runs, the selection purity from $58.0\%$ to $64.7\%$ for $5\%$ of the signal. This is a study
for a later revision of the single-pion analysis.

\paragraph{The LLR-and-geometry classifier.} Section~\ref{sec:detrobust} shows that the detector
variations act on the calorimetric inputs. A second model drops the Bragg likelihoods, $\chi^2$
scores, PIDA and calorimetric energies, and keeps the LLR score (summed and per plane), length, track
score, vertex distance, start and end containment, range and MCS momenta, the proton-hypothesis energy,
the daughter counts and the per-plane hit flags. Its pion ROC area is $0.892$ against $0.926$ for the
deployed model on the same tracks, and at $80\%$ pion efficiency it passes $9.0\%$ of the protons
against $3.9\%$. It is the particle classifier of \texttt{CC1mu2piBDT}.

\section{Track assignment and event classifier}
\label{sec:event}

Sections~\ref{sec:event} to~\ref{sec:crdata} develop the selection with a single event classifier, which
Section~\ref{sec:twofold} replaces by the two-fold one of the final selection.

\paragraph{Assignment.} In events with one neutrino slice and the vertex in the fiducial volume, the
tracks of the pion pool with track score $\geq0.5$, the muon candidate included, carry the four class
probabilities. For the hypothesis ``one muon, $N$ pions, the other tracks protons or other'' the
log-likelihood is
\begin{equation}
  L_N = \log P_\mu(\mu) + \sum_{\text{pions}}\log P_\pi + \sum_{\text{rest}}\log\left(1-P_\mu-P_\pi\right),
\end{equation}
where $1-P_\mu-P_\pi=P_p+P_\mathrm{other}$ and every probability is floored at $10^{-6}$ before the
logarithm; every choice of the muon and the $N$ pions among at most eight tracks is enumerated, and the
best and the runner-up are kept for $N=1,2,3$. In the selected signal of the held-out periods the
assigned muon is a true muon in $89\%$ of events and each assigned pion a true charged pion in $88\%$;
all three are in $76\%$. In the preselected signal, however, only $30\%$ of the events have the muon
and both pions as tracks, so in most signal events no assignment can be fully right.

\paragraph{Event classifier.} An XGBoost classifier separates the two-pion signal from everything else
on 33 features: the probabilities of the assigned tracks, $L_2-L_1$, $L_2-L_3$, the margin over the
runner-up, the class sums and the counts above $0.5$ over all tracks, the track and shower counts, the
topological score and CosmicIP. Its domain is one slice, the vertex in the fiducial volume, the software
trigger, at least three tracks and $\theta(\mu,\text{longest assigned pion})<2.6$~rad (D6). It is trained
on run periods 1, 2 and 4 of both horn modes ($206\,152$ events, $13\,558$ signal) with the nominal
mixture of overlay, dirt and beam-off and the central-value weights, and tested on FHC Run~5 and RHC
Run~3 ($104\,814$ events, $7\,253$ signal), which the particle classifier did not see either. These periods also
choose the working point below, so they are validation periods rather than an untouched test sample. The
ROC area on them is $0.887$; adding the lengths, vertex distances, containment and opening
angles of the assigned tracks raises it to $0.898$, a gain that disappears in the total uncertainty, so
these kinematic features are not used. The largest gains come from the summed pion probability, $L_2$,
the number of tracks with $P_\pi>0.5$, the topological score and the number with $P_\mu>0.5$.
Figure~\ref{fig:evt} shows the score of the LLR-and-geometry selection by true class.

$0.95$, and with all terms the minimum is at $0.92$ (with the two-fold classifier of Section~\ref{sec:twofold},
$0.90$), flat between $0.90$ and $0.94$ within the
statistics of the detector-variation samples (Figure~\ref{fig:wp}).

\section{The detector term}
\label{sec:detrobust}

At the working point of the deployed classifier the detector term, $33\%$, is the largest. A paired
bootstrap over the physical events, with one Poisson weight per event shared by the central-value
sample and the eight variations, attributes $7.7\%$ of it to the statistics of the variation samples.
It comes from the recombination ($+22\%$) and wire-response variations (angle $yz$ $+18\%$, $x$
$+12\%$, $yz$ $+8\%$); the light-yield, space-charge and angle-$xz$ variations are consistent with
zero. At track level these variations move the $\chi^2$ scores, PIDA, the Bragg likelihoods and the
calorimetric energies by up to half of their spread for protons, the LLR score by at most $0.04$ and the
geometric inputs not at all. At event level they lower the selected signal by about $8\%$ and the
background by $10$--$18\%$, and the detector term splits into an efficiency part ($14.3\%$), which no
background constraint can remove, and a background part ($17.8\%$).

the efficiency correction amplify the change of the predicted events by a factor of two to four. The
recombination variation leads in both analyses.

\section{Control samples and the background constraint}
\label{sec:constraint}

The flux term scales the signal and the background together, and the background is as large as the
signal. Control samples that share the background composition constrain it through the conditional
covariance of the extraction framework (\texttt{ConstrainedCalculator}): the covariance of the
predicted counts in the signal region and the sidebands, built from every source, is conditioned on the
sidebands, and the background estimate moves with the sideband data. Four sidebands are defined from
the classifier outputs, exclusive and in this order inside the two-pion domain
(Table~\ref{tab:cuts}): three-pion-like (at
least four tracks and three pions more likely than two; the control sample of D8), proton-like (an
assigned pion with $P_p>0.5$), event score between $0.5$ and the working point, and event score below
$0.5$. The sidebands leave out the events of the blind single-pion signal region, so they can be opened
without it (Table~\ref{tab:regions}, Figure~\ref{fig:regions}).

constrain the background only, the signal variations of the sidebands raise the constrained total from

of the constraint gives $19.0\%$, and both together $21.4\%$. The score band moves the extraction under
universe~545 from $1.012$ to $0.952$; every variant closes within $0.3\sigma$ for both models. The nominal
treatment keeps the signal covariance between the regions because the same flux and interaction parameters act
coherently on one CC$2\pi$ process in all of them; the decorrelated variants are conservative stress tests, not
equally plausible covariance models. The two alternative models hardly change the ratio of score-band to
signal-region signal ($1.684$ nominal, $1.660$ under universe~545, $1.681$ under the $\Delta\to N\pi$
variation), so they do not test this transfer; a stress model outside the covariance does
(Section~\ref{sec:crdata}).

\section{Extraction through the framework}
\label{sec:framework}

\texttt{CC1mu2piBDT} implements the selection in the framework: the track pool, the class
probabilities of the LLR-and-geometry classifier, the assignment, the 33 features, the event score
(\texttt{RBDT}) and the region; the selected events are those of the signal region. On a
30\,000-event slice the features equal those of the Python study exactly, the scores to $6\times10^{-8}$
and the regions in every event, and \texttt{CC1mu1piXp} is bitwise unchanged. The 28 release samples
and the 18 detector-variation samples were processed with it. The bin configuration has the signal
region as an ordinary bin and the four sidebands as sideband bins in the signal block, so that their
signal is predicted. The keyword \texttt{Calculator Constrained} in the extraction configuration selects
\texttt{ConstrainedCalculator}; the extractor propagates the ordinary block of each covariance and,
with the constraint, the part that remains after the conditioning, so that the terms by source add up
to the constrained total.

\subsection{Detector-variation samples}
\label{sec:detvar}

The first framework extraction gave a larger detector term than the study outside it, most in RHC
($28\%$ against $17\%$). Per POT, the Run-4 detector-variation central-value samples predict $1.27$
(FHC) and $1.51$ (RHC) times the event rate of the nominal Run-4 overlays in the fiducial volume, the
ratio rising from $1.1$ below $0.5$~GeV to $1.8$--$3$ above $5$~GeV. The samples were simulated with the
earlier NuMI flux, the nominal overlays with the current one (Geant~v4.10.4): weighted event by event
with the ratio of the two fluxes in true energy and angle to the beamline, they predict $0.94$ times
the nominal rate, flat in energy; the origin of this flat offset is not identified, and the fractional treatment
below cancels it. The framework built the detector covariance from the absolute
difference between each variation and the variation central value, and so carried that ratio. The
covariance type \texttt{DVfrac} applies the fractional change of each variation relative to the
variation central value to the nominal prediction; it is used here and, since version~1.8, in the
single-pion release.

Because the earlier flux overweights high energies, where the two-pion signal sits, the variation
samples are in addition weighted to the current flux: the selection framework applies an optional
per-event weight branch, carried only by reweighted copies of the 18 variation samples. On the
fractional detector change of the selected two-pion count the weight raises the FHC term from $17.6\%$
to $20.6\%$ and leaves RHC unchanged ($9.3\%$ to $9.1\%$); for the single-pion selection it changes the
term by at most $0.3\%$ absolute. Table~\ref{tab:detvar} shows the three treatments.

\section{Beam-on data in the control regions}
\label{sec:crdata}

The inputs and the output of the two-pion selection were compared with beam-on data in the four control
regions of the single-pion analysis, which are open. The skim keeps only events in those regions and
outside the signal regions of both selections, so the two-pion signal region is absent from data and
prediction alike. The prediction is the sum of the overlays, beam-off data and dirt; the shape tests
remove the normalisation and are made per run period and summed. In the two-pion domain the data exceed
the prediction by $10$--$24\%$ (three-pion-like $1.18$, proton-like $1.10$, score $0.5$--$0.92$ $1.20$,
score below $0.5$ $1.24$), as outside it ($1.12$); the largest excess is in the $\pi^0$ control region.

\paragraph{MCS momentum.} The MCS momentum of the pool tracks is the most mis-modelled input: data over
prediction rises to $1.14$ at $0.7$--$0.9\GeVc$ and falls to $0.39$ at $1.8$--$1.9\GeVc$ (shape
$\chi^2=2234/19$). This is the effect found for exiting muons in the single-pion analysis~\cite{note1}, an
angular noise of reconstructed tracks on the scale of the MCS segments that the simulation lacks,
calibrated there from version~1.9 by a quantile map of the MCS momentum in cells of the drift-direction
component and the length of the track. The same map is applied here to the classifier inputs of every
simulated track whose end leaves the containment volume (\texttt{CC1MU1PIXP\_MP\_MCS\_MAP}; without it
every output is unchanged, bit for bit). With it the shape $\chi^2$ of the MCS momentum falls to
$71.5/19$ (data over prediction $0.87$--$1.04$, Figure~\ref{fig:crmcs}), that of $P(\mu)$ from $1100$ to $293$,
of $P(\pi)$ from $256$ to $56$ (19 degrees of freedom) and of the summed $P(\pi)$ of the event from $92$ to $52$
(15), in every period.

  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.62\textwidth]{multipion/cr_mcs}
  \caption{MCS momentum of the pool tracks in the opened control regions, all periods: beam-on data against the prediction (overlays, beam-off, dirt) before and after the MCS calibration, normalised to the data.}

\end{figure}

\paragraph{LLR of long protons.} Among pool tracks longer than $40\cm$, those with an LLR score below
$-0.9$ (an LLR sum below $-630$) are $11$ times more frequent in the data than predicted ($592$ against
$52$); between $-0.9$ and $-0.8$ the data hold $1.33$ times the prediction and between $-0.8$ and $-0.5$
$0.84$. They are long contained protons: both ends contained ($97\%$), starting at the vertex, $40$ to
$100\cm$ long, true protons in $72\%$ of the predicted ones, in every period and both productions and on
all three planes. With the same number of hits they are more proton-like per hit in the data: for
contained tracks longer than $40\cm$ with an LLR score below $-0.5$ the median LLR sum is $-436$ to $-486$
in the Run-4 data and $-339$ in the simulation. Of the detector variations the recombination variation
reproduces it (median $-445$; share beyond $-0.9$ of $0.15$, against $0.16$--$0.23$ in the data and
$0.015$ in the simulation), and the wire-response variations move it the other way. The effect is
therefore inside the detector term, of which the recombination variation is the largest part
(Section~\ref{sec:detrobust}, Figure~\ref{fig:crllr}). The particle classifier calls these tracks protons in either case: contained tracks
longer than $40\cm$ with an LLR score below $-0.5$ have $P_p>0.5$ in $86$ to $92\%$ of cases in every LLR band, in
data and simulation alike within $1$ to $2\%$.

\paragraph{Event score.} With the calibrated simulation the shape $\chi^2$ of the event score is
$140/22$ ($160/22$ before). The bin below the cut holds $0.83$ of the predicted share, and the
recombination variation gives $0.86$ in both modes. The lowest bin ($0.78$) holds control-region CC$0\pi$
events ($97.5\%$); against the normalisation of that region alone it is at $0.85$, the rest coming from the
larger excess of the $\pi^0$ region in the common normalisation, and the recombination variation gives
$0.93$--$0.94$.

\paragraph{With the systematic covariance.} The shape tests above carry the data and simulation statistics only.
With the covariance of the prediction (flux, cross-section and reinteraction universes, the eight detector
variations taken fractionally per horn mode with the statistics of the variation samples inside the detector term,
simulation statistics), built from the ingredients of the extraction, the prediction is scaled to the data total,
which removes one degree of freedom: empty bins are removed, the last remaining bin is dropped and $\chi^2$ is
solved by least squares over the others. The event score gives $\chi^2=6.5/11$, the summed $P(\pi)$ $2.0/7$, the track $P(\mu)$ and
$P(\pi)$ $4.2/9$ each and the MCS momentum $6.2/8$, against $98.5$, $23.6$, $48.1$, $40.4$ and $65.2$ with statistics
alone in the same, coarser bins (Figure~\ref{fig:crscore}). The LLR score of the pool tracks remains incompatible
with the data, $20.8/9$ ($p=0.014$), at its proton end, where the long protons sit; the detector term alone brings it
from $398$ to $36$. All tested observables are thus compatible with the prediction within the systematic covariance
except the LLR score.

\paragraph{Extraction with the calibrated simulation.} The overlays, the dirt and the detector-variation
samples were processed again with the calibrated classifier inputs, and the pseudo-data thrown with the
same seeds, so that they hold the same events. Table~\ref{tab:mcscal} gives the totals. The calibration
changes the expected content of the signal region by less than $1\%$ (signal $-0.9\%$ in FHC and $-0.5\%$
in RHC, all events $-0.7\%$ and $0.0\%$) and the constrained combined total from $15.7\%$ to $15.2\%$. In the
combined configuration this measures the flux-averaged CC$2\pi^\pm$ cross section to $15\%$ from about $380$
selected signal events, the precision of a count of $380\pm57$, of which the data statistics contribute $8.7\%$. The
classification moves about $5\%$ of the signal-region events in or out, and their pseudo-data counts are
Poisson draws made without regard to it; this moves the closure to $1.05$--$1.09$, by $+0.9$ (FHC) and
$+2.3$ (RHC) times the Poisson spread of the migrating events. The calibration uncertainty is evaluated
as in the single-pion analysis: pseudo-data with the classifier inputs calibrated by each of the seven
alternative maps, extracted against the response calibrated with the nominal map, in four fully
correlated terms: $2.2\%$ of the constrained combined total, $2.3$--$5.5\%$ in the other extractions and at
most $0.24$ of their total uncertainty. Each alternative map moves only $4$ to $19$ of the pseudo-data
events in or out of the signal region, and every shift lies within $1.7$ times the Poisson noise of
those events, so the term is an upper estimate. The calibrated selection is used from here on.

\paragraph{Ensembles.} Pseudo-experiments thrown independently from the nominal calibrated simulation (the eight

extracted through the framework (Figure~\ref{fig:ens}). Over 300 of them the mean extracted total exceeds the
expected truth by $+0.7$ to $+1.3\%$, with a standard error of $0.4$ to $0.7\%$, in FHC, RHC and combined, with
and without the constraint. Without the constraint the spread equals the quoted data-statistics term (pull width
$1.01$ to $1.02$; $67$ to $72\%$ of the members inside the $68\%$ interval). With it the spread is $0.83$ to $0.87$
of the quoted term ($75$ to $79\%$ inside, Figure~\ref{fig:enscov}), so the quoted constrained term is conservative; the beam-off, which the
framework adds to the pseudo-data without fluctuation while the quoted term counts it, accounts for little of this
(without it the constrained data-statistics term of Section~\ref{sec:constraint} is $8.6\%$ instead of $8.8\%$).
A first ensemble of 100 members with consecutive seeds had a spread of $0.85$ of the quoted term also without the
constraint; the extraction is linear in the signal-region count and its quoted term is the Poisson error of that
count, and with hashed seeds the difference disappears. Fifty pseudo-experiments each from GENIE multisim
universe~545 and from the $\Delta\to N\pi$ angular variation, extracted with the nominal response, return their
own truth within $0.17\sigma$ of the total uncertainty (the largest: FHC with the constraint, $0.962\pm0.014$ under
the $\Delta\to N\pi$ variation). A stress model outside the covariance, the score-band signal raised by $20\%$
against the signal region, gives $0.910\pm0.010$ of its truth in the combined configuration with the constraint
($-0.54\sigma$) and $0.932\pm0.009$ without ($-0.14\sigma$): the signal carried by the sidebands moves the
constrained result by about half of its total uncertainty for such a change. A second stress model, the background
with three or more charged pions raised by $30\%$ and the single-pion background lowered by $20\%$, gives
$1.006\pm0.011$ with the constraint and $1.021\pm0.010$ without (within $0.18\sigma$ in every configuration). Two
signal-shape stress models weight the signal only. The leading-pion weight $1+0.5\sin(p_\pi^{\rm lead}/\mathrm{GeV})$
raises the signal by $25\%$ almost uniformly over the regions (efficiency $+1\%$): it gives $1.012\pm0.008$ without
the constraint and $0.968\pm0.009$ with it ($-0.24\sigma$), since the conditioning reads part of the signal excess in
the sidebands as background. The softest-pion weight $1+0.5\cos(\pi\min(p_\pi^{\rm soft},0.5\GeVc)/0.5\GeVc)$
lowers the efficiency by $8\%$ and raises the score-band signal against the signal region by $7\%$: it gives

spectrum outside the covariance biases the efficiency correction with or without the constraint. The same

result to $0.939\pm0.010$ ($-0.30\sigma$, against $-0.55\sigma$ with the band) and the softest-pion stress to
$0.922\pm0.012$ ($-0.36\sigma$): leaving the band out halves the transfer of the signal model and leaves the
efficiency effect as it is.

\section{Final selection: the two-fold event classifier}
\label{sec:twofold}

\paragraph{Overtraining.} Over all run periods the signal region of the selection above holds $395$ predicted signal
events, against $378$ projected from the validation periods (Table~\ref{tab:regions}): the selection efficiency is
$14.04\pm0.21\%$ in the run periods on which the classifiers were trained and $13.16\pm0.31\%$ in the validation periods
(RHC Run~3 $13.60\pm0.37\%$, FHC Run~5, a separate production, $12.17\pm0.61\%$), a difference of $2.4\sigma$. It is
overtraining of the event classifier, which was trained on a random $80\%$ of the events of the training periods: within
the domain, at a score above $0.92$, its efficiency is $23.61\pm0.36\%$ on those events, $21.35\pm0.71\%$ on the other
$20\%$ of the same periods and $21.18\pm0.48\%$ in the validation periods (purity $55.4$, $51.0$ and $51.4\%$). The
held-out events of the training periods behave as the validation periods, so the particle classifier adds no measurable
effect. A response built from all periods overestimates the efficiency of independent events, and of the data, by about
$6\%$ and underestimates their background by about $4\%$, about $-2\%$ on the unconstrained total; closure and
ensembles cannot see it, since their pseudo-data come from the same events.

\paragraph{Two folds.} The event classifier is therefore trained twice, on the events with even and with odd event
numbers, and every event, data and beam-off included, is scored by the model of the other parity, so that no simulated
event is scored by a model that saw it. The selection reads the event number of the ntuple and holds both models; on a
$30\,000$-event slice its scores equal those of the study to $6\times10^{-8}$. Each fold model is overtrained on its own
events (efficiency at $0.92$ $21.3$ against $19.5\%$, $19.7$ against $18.8\%$), but no event is scored by it. With the
out-of-fold scores the constrained combined total of the study is $17.4$, $20.4$, $16.5$, $19.5$ and $25.5\%$ at $0.85$,
$0.88$, $0.90$, $0.92$ and $0.94$, the scatter between neighbouring thresholds again set by the detector term, and the
working point moves to its minimum, $0.90$. Table~\ref{tab:tf_regions} gives the regions. In the study the
constraint takes the combined total from $50.1\%$ to $16.5\%$; with the sideband signal decoupled it is $19.6\%$, without
the score band $21.2\%$ and with both $23.9\%$, and the two reweighted models close within $0.21\sigma$.

and the differential variables) and the pseudo-data thrown with the same seeds, so that they hold the same events as

covariance with the gain of the conditioning of the whole signal-region prediction, signal included, while the
estimator subtracts the background with the gain of the background alone, which is smaller because the sidebands contain
signal: in the study the two give $8.6\%$ and $7.6\%$ (ratio $0.88$; $0.77$ to $0.99$ for single sidebands), and with

\section{Differential variables}
\label{sec:variables}

The differential measurements use the tracks of the assignment (Section~\ref{sec:event}). Table~\ref{tab:vardef}
defines the variables. As in the single-pion analysis, reconstructed angles are taken about the direction from the
NuMI target to the reconstructed vertex and true angles about the true neutrino direction, and the muon momentum is
the range momentum when the track ends inside the containment volume and the MCS momentum otherwise, calibrated for
simulated tracks with the map of Section~\ref{sec:crdata}. The two pions are ordered by the variable itself (the more
forward and the more backward pion, the higher and the lower momentum), the same way at truth and reconstructed
level, so that the convention is symmetric in the pions and needs no pion identity. The pion momentum is the range
momentum of a contained track, converted from the muon hypothesis at equal kinetic energy over mass, and is defined
only when both assigned pions are contained. The proton multiplicity counts, at reconstructed level, the tracks
outside the assignment with $P_p>0.5$, and at truth level the protons above $0.3\GeVc$ of the subsample of D1.

\paragraph{Extraction through the framework.} The muon variables, the four angles and the proton multiplicity were
extracted through the framework with the two-fold event classifier (Section~\ref{sec:twofold}) in FHC, RHC and combined,
without and with the sideband constraint, with Wiener-SVD unfolding (filter on, second-derivative regularisation, as the
single-pion differential results) on the pseudo-data of the one-bin total (Table~\ref{tab:varres},
Figure~\ref{fig:varres}); the bins are those of Table~\ref{tab:varbins}. In the nominal pseudo-data realisation every
extraction is compatible with its truth smeared by $A_C$, the regularisation matrix of the unfolding, applied to every
prediction compared with an unfolded result: the $\chi^2$ with the total covariance is at most $4.1$ for 7 bins
(combined) and $4.6/7$ in a single horn mode; closure in ensembles per variable is not yet tested. The constraint sets the precision of the differential results: without it the per-bin uncertainty is
$40$ to $185\%$ in the combined configuration, with it $15$ to $58\%$, the edge bins with the lowest efficiency or the
largest migration being the least precise. The bin-averaged uncertainty is $21$ to $39\%$; the integral over the
bins, with the full covariance, carries $15.1$ to $17.8\%$ (data statistics $9.0$ to $9.9\%$), as the one-bin total
($16.5\%$, $8.9\%$), and lies $2$ to $12\%$ below it, the effect of the $A_C$ smearing of the regularised unfolding on
the integral; the one-bin total remains the normalisation result. The soft-pion efficiency term
(Section~\ref{sec:twofold}, $4$ to $12\%$ per bin, $8.3\%$ on the integral) is not included in Table~\ref{tab:varres}. For $N_p$ a plain inversion (Wiener filter off) gives
the same integral uncertainty ($17.9\%$) and a larger one in the 0-proton bin ($86\%$ against $58\%$).

\section{Three or more pions}
\label{sec:threepi}

In the two-pion domain, events with at least four tracks in which three pions are more likely than two
number $234$ at full exposure (combined, with the deployed particle classifier of the Phase-2 study; the
three-pion-like sideband of the adopted selection holds $294$, Table~\ref{tab:tf_regions}), of which $88$ have three or more charged pions, $41$ a neutral
pion and $35$ are two-pion signal. A three-pion event classifier built the same way reaches a ROC area of
$0.855$, but with all terms every three-pion selection carries a total uncertainty above $100\%$
(\texttt{CC1mu3pi} $245\%$, with the particle classifier $117\%$, the event classifier at best
$105\%$). Four and five pions are out of reach: combined, $3.1$ selected four-pion signal events at
$4.8\%$ purity and $0.1$ five-pion events, and the probability that a true pion multiplicity is
reconstructed in its own bin is $0.84$, $0.48$ and $0.33$ for one, two and three or more pions. The
three-pion-like events are therefore the control sample of Section~\ref{sec:constraint} (D8).

\section{Status and next steps}
\label{sec:status}

\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item The two-pion one-bin total is extracted through the framework in FHC, RHC and combined, with
        and without the sideband constraint, with the MCS-calibrated classifier inputs and the two-fold event
        classifier (Table~\ref{tab:tf_total}).
  \item The inputs of the LLR-and-geometry classifier and the event score are compared with beam-on data
        in the opened control regions (Section~\ref{sec:crdata}): the MCS momentum is calibrated, and the
        LLR of long protons follows the recombination variation of the detector term.
  \item The selection was compared with event-classifier features from the reconstructed showers and with
        the calorimetric particle classifier on the constrained total (Section~\ref{sec:crdata}); no
        variant improves it beyond the statistics of the detector-variation samples, and the selection is
        kept.
  \item Validation through the framework: ensembles of pseudo-experiments, alternative-model and stress-model
        ensembles (Sections~\ref{sec:crdata} and~\ref{sec:twofold}).
  \item The constrained data-statistics term is the framework's, conservative against the ensembles; the LLR
        score of the pool tracks calls for the recombination variation at $1.4$ times its size, which moves no
        result by more than $0.12$ of its uncertainty beyond the detector term (Section~\ref{sec:twofold}).
  \item The softest-pion spectrum is not covered by the interaction-model covariance: a soft-pion efficiency term,
        the envelope of the standalone generators ($8.3\%$ combined), is added; the migration across the $0.10\GeVc$
        threshold is below $1\%$ (Section~\ref{sec:twofold}).
  \item Produce generator predictions using the two-pion signal definition.
  \item The overtraining of the single event classifier (about $6\%$ on the signal efficiency) is removed by training it
        in two folds by event-number parity, with the working point at $0.90$ (Section~\ref{sec:twofold}).
  \item The differential variables of Section~\ref{sec:variables} are extracted through the framework with the
        sideband constraint and are compatible with their truth in the nominal pseudo-data realisation. Open for
        them: ensembles and alternative-model closure per
        variable, generator predictions, and the pion momenta; the invariant masses remain study products unless
        they pass the $0.50$ criterion.
  \item Perform an independent-generator closure if a NuMI NuWro overlay can be validated (D5).
  \item Apply and validate the same sideband-constraint method in the single-pion analysis using pseudo-data.
\end{itemize}
