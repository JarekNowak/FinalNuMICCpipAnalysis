\begin{abstract}
Working note for the \uboone\ search for \Szero\ production in $\numubar$
charged-current interactions in the NuMI beam, $\Szero\to\Lam\gamma$, $\Lam\to p\pi^-$.
The results are for the reverse-horn-current (RHC) exposure of $1.15\times10^{21}$~POT;
the forward-horn-current (FHC) exposure, whose wrong-sign $\numubar$ adds almost as much
signal again, is to be included once its signal samples are complete. Two selections are
maintained, with one signal definition: $\Lam\to p\pi^-$ and the proton, pion and photon
above this detector's reconstruction thresholds (KE $60$, $30$ and $30$~MeV), 16.1 GENIE or 4.0
NuWro events in the fiducial volume. The PeLEE chain runs on standard PeLEE ntuples and ends in
a cross-validated gradient BDT: $S=0.57$ NuWro or $2.53$ GENIE on $B=128$. The \Lam-style chain
follows the \uboone\ \Lam\ CCQE analysis on HyperonProduction ntuples, fitting the \Lam\ decay
vertex, testing the connectedness of the raw wire activity and requiring a proton-like proton
candidate: $S=0.118$ NuWro or $0.475$ GENIE on $B=2.5$. Neither can observe \Szero\ at the
predicted rate; the deliverable is an upper limit. The best median expected 90\%~CL limit is
$8.7\times$ the GENIE and $35\times$ the NuWro rate, about 140 defined-signal events, from the
\Lam-style chain, which gives the better limit at every background systematic. The two generators differ
by a factor of four in the predicted rate, almost entirely because NuWro includes hyperon
final-state interactions. GENIE's $\Delta S=1$ quasi-elastic cross section was found to be
wrong in its hyperon-mass terms; every GENIE sample is corrected by event weights, and the
GENIE signal samples are being regenerated with a fixed GENIE. The \Lam-style chain's open
items, data and dirt in its ntuple format, its beam-off estimate and its systematics, are the
next pieces of work.
\end{abstract}

\section{Status}
\label{sec:status}
% =============================================================================

The analysis sets a limit on \Szero\ production in $\numubar$ charged-current
interactions,
\begin{equation}
  \numubar + \mathrm{Ar} \to \mu^{+} + \Szero + X, \qquad
  \Szero \to \Lam\gamma, \qquad \Lam \to p\,\pi^{-},
\end{equation}
so the reconstructed final state is a muon, a proton, a pion and a photon of about
$75$~MeV. All yields are scaled to the RHC data exposure, $1.1526\times10^{21}$~POT.

Two selections exist, on two ntuple formats, and they are fully independent
(Table~\ref{tab:chains}). The PeLEE chain (\texttt{Sigma/pelee/SigmaSelection.C},
Sec.~\ref{sec:pelee}) is the analysis: it is the only one that can see data, EXT and
dirt. The \Lam-style chain (\texttt{SigmaLambdaSel.C}, \texttt{IslandProcess.C},
Sec.~\ref{sec:lambdastyle}) needs the raw wire activity, which only the
HyperonProduction (``hypana'') format stores; that format exists for MC and EXT but not
for data or dirt.

\paragraph{The main facts.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{The result will be a limit.} The best median discovery significance at
        the predicted rate is about $0.3\sigma$ (GENIE) and $0.07\sigma$ (NuWro). The PeLEE
        ntuples carry the multisim weights the background covariance needs
        (\texttt{weightsGenie}, \texttt{weightsPPFX}, \texttt{weightsReint}).
  \item \textbf{The two signal models differ by a factor of four in rate} (49.3 against
        12.6 in-FV \Szero), almost all of it hyperon final-state interactions, which
        NuWro models and GENIE does not (Sec.~\ref{sec:models}). Their production rates
        agree to about $10\%$ once GENIE's cross section is corrected. Every result is
        quoted under both.
  \item \textbf{GENIE's \Szero\ cross section was $1.85\times$ too high} and its \Lam\
        $0.8\times$ too low (Sec.~\ref{sec:pais}, App.~\ref{app:pais}). All GENIE samples
        carry a correcting weight; the GENIE signal samples are being regenerated with a
        fixed GENIE (Sec.~\ref{sec:plan}).
  \item \textbf{One signal definition for both chains} requires the visible decay,
        $\Lam\to p\pi^-$ with the decay products above threshold (Sec.~\ref{sec:sigdef}).
        With it the \Lam-style chain, which reconstructs the $p\pi^-$ pair, gives the better
        limit at every background systematic: at $f=0$ by $2\%$ (GENIE) and $12\%$ (NuWro) over
        the PeLEE BDT retrained for this definition, at $f\geq10\%$ by $1.4\times$. Its limit is
        Poisson-limited and almost flat in $f$.
  \item \textbf{FHC adds almost as much signal as RHC} ($0.90\times$ per POT, 47
        expected GENIE \Szero\ in $1.10\times10^{21}$ FHC POT against 54 in RHC); its
        background per POT will be higher (Sec.~\ref{sec:fhc}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Signal, samples and signal models}
\label{sec:signal}
% =============================================================================

\subsection{Signal definition and fiducial volume}
\label{sec:sigdef}
The signal is the same for both selections, both generators and every sample:
\begin{itemize}
  \item $\numubar$ charged current with exactly one \Szero\ and no other meson or strange
        particle at the primary vertex;
  \item the true vertex in the fiducial volume, $3.00 < x < 253.35$, $-112.53 < y < 114.47$,
        $3.1 < z < 1033.0$~cm, excluding $675.1 < z < 775.1$~cm (looser than the CC$1\mu1\pi$
        box: the signal is rare enough that acceptance is worth more than vertex quality);
  \item $\Szero\to\Lam\gamma$, $\Lam\to p\pi^-$;
  \item muon momentum above $0.15$~GeV/$c$, proton kinetic energy above $60$~MeV, $\pi^-$
        kinetic energy above $30$~MeV, photon energy above $30$~MeV.
\end{itemize}
Both selections also require the reconstructed vertex in the same volume. The proton, pion
and photon thresholds are where this detector's reconstruction turns on: the momentum at which
the efficiency to reconstruct the particle as the right type (track for $p$ and $\pi^-$, shower
for the photon) reaches half its plateau, measured on the signal samples and identical for
both generators (App.~\ref{app:thresh}). Below them the selections have almost no
efficiency: the thresholds remove a quarter of the $\Lam\to p\pi^-$ signal each (proton,
pion) but only $1$--$4\%$ of what the \Lam-style chain selects. About $61\%$ of in-FV \Szero\
events have $\Lam\to p\pi^-$ and half of those pass the thresholds, so the defined signal is
$32\%$ of all in-FV \Szero: 16.1 GENIE and 4.0 NuWro events in the RHC exposure.

The PeLEE truth record stops at the primary vertex, so the decay mode and the decay-product
momenta come from the HyperonProduction truth of the same productions, matched per event
(\texttt{ThreshStudy.C} $\to$ \texttt{sigdef\_*.tsv}). \Szero\ events outside the definition
(chiefly $\Lam\to n\pi^0$ and soft decay products) are background, and since they are part of
the \Szero\ production being tested, each model takes them from its own sample: GENIE events in
the GENIE-model limits, NuWro events in the NuWro-model limits. The overlay's own \Szero\
events, whose decay the PeLEE record cannot see, stay out of the background.

\subsection{The two signal models}
\label{sec:models}

NuWro and GENIE predict very different \Szero\ yields (Table~\ref{tab:models}). The
difference has two parts: the production cross section, where GENIE was wrong and is
corrected, and hyperon final-state interactions, a genuine model difference.

\paragraph{Hyperon final-state interactions.} NuWro transports the hyperon through the
nucleus; GENIE's INTRANUKE does not re-interact hyperons, so for GENIE produced equals
surviving. In the NuWro sample only $25.3\%$ of $\numubar$ CC events still carry a
\Szero, $27.9\%$ carry a \Lam\ ($\Szero N\to\Lam N$), $10\%$ a $\Sigma^\pm$, and $37.3\%$
no strange particle at all (hyperons below the binding threshold are dropped; the
build used, \texttt{nuwro\_old} with \texttt{hyp\_Eb}~$=30$~MeV, keeps $24\%$ of the
\Szero\ it produces). The corrected GENIE and NuWro agree on production to $0.96$; the
remaining factor $4.4$ in surviving \Szero\ is FSI. The \uboone\ \Lam\ paper
(arXiv:2212.07888) notes the same asymmetry: FSI \emph{adds} \Lam, so there NuWro sits
above GENIE. A NuWro control run with ordinary CCQE instead of the hyperon channel
reproduces GENIE's CCQE rate to $17\%$, so NuWro's flux and target normalisation is sound
(App.~\ref{app:rate}). The measured \Lam\ cross section, $2.0^{+2.2}_{-1.7}\times10^{-40}$
cm$^2$/Ar, brackets both generators. The factor of about two in \Szero\ reduction quoted
for NuWro in the second-class-current study is not reproduced by this build; that is
open.

\subsection{GENIE's hyperon cross section and the correcting weights}
\label{sec:pais}

With NuWro's cascade switched off, the generators agree on \Lam\ production
(NuWro/GENIE $1.07$) but not on \Szero\ or $\Sigma^-$ ($0.59$ each), with the same energy
dependence in all three (Fig.~\ref{fig:pais}, centre). Both use the same Cabibbo SU(3)
form factors. An independent calculation from explicit Dirac traces, validated on
Llewellyn Smith CCQE, gives \Szero/\Lam\ $=0.19$ on a free proton, in agreement with NuWro
($0.21$ on argon), against GENIE's $0.45$. GENIE's \texttt{PaisQELLambdaPXSec} reproduces
the trace when the final baryon has the nucleon mass and diverges as the hyperon mass is
raised: its hyperon-mass terms are wrong. Relative to the trace, GENIE's \Lam\ is
$\times0.80$ and its \Szero\ and $\Sigma^-$ $\times1.85$. The derivation and the checks
are in App.~\ref{app:pais}.

\begin{figure}[h]\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{hyperon_xsec.pdf}
\caption{Left: $\numubar p\to\mu^+Y$ on a free proton, GENIE's Pais splines (dashed)
and the full trace with the same form factors (solid). Centre: NuWro/GENIE in-FV rate
with the NuWro cascade off, flux-weighted, against true $E_\nu$; open squares are GENIE
\Szero\ with the cross-section weight. Right: \Szero\ polarisation in its rest frame
against $E_\nu$ at three values of $u=(Q^2-Q^2_{\min})/(Q^2_{\max}-Q^2_{\min})$.}
\label{fig:pais}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Cross-section weight.} $w(E,u)=[d\sigma/dQ^2]_{\mathrm{trace}}/[d\sigma/dQ^2]_
{\mathrm{Pais}}$ on a free nucleon, with $E$ the $\numubar$ energy in the struck-nucleon
frame and $u=(Q^2-Q^2_{\min})/(Q^2_{\max}-Q^2_{\min})$ (\texttt{HypXsecWeight.h},
\texttt{hyp\_xsec\_weights.root}). For a two-body reaction $E$ and $Q^2$ fix the final
state up to a flat azimuth, so the weight corrects the kinematics as well as the rate;
GENIE's phase space is populated everywhere except a corner holding $\leq0.3\%$ of the
corrected cross section (weight capped at 5). It costs no statistics. On the generator
files:
\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{lrrrr}
\toprule
in-FV per data POT & GENIE & GENIE weighted & NuWro (no cascade) & NuWro/GENIE weighted \\
\midrule
\Lam\      & 209.5 & 263.0 & 223.7 & 0.85 \\
\Szero\    & 81.7  & 54.4  & 47.9  & 0.88 \\
$\Sigma^-$ & 194.9 & 129.3 & 115.0 & 0.89 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The common $0.85$--$0.89$ is a nuclear and flux difference. \texttt{SigmaSelection.C}
applies the weight to every hyperon-QE event of every GENIE sample (overlay, dirt,
detector variations, the QE sample), the struck nucleon taken at rest ($1\%$ effect); the
hypana chains apply it by event identifier. NuWro is not weighted.

\paragraph{\Szero\ polarisation.} The same traces give the \Szero\ polarisation,
$P_L\approx P_T\approx-0.4$ to $-0.5$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:pais}, right). Both generator chains
hand Geant4 an unpolarised \Szero, which decays isotropically. \texttt{PolWeights.C}
weights each signal event by $w=1-\alpha(\vec P_\Sigma\cdot\hat n)(\hat n\cdot\hat q)$
($\hat n$ the \Lam\ direction in the \Szero\ frame, $\hat q$ the nucleon direction in the
\Lam\ frame, $\alpha=0.747$), with the decay directions from the hypana ntuple of the same
production. $\langle w\rangle=0.997$, rms $0.15$; the selected yields move by $0.2$--$1.4\%$.

\paragraph{What the weights cannot correct.} The hyperon's nuclear potential and binding
near threshold, which shift its kinetic energy, and hyperon FSI, which changes the
topology; GENIE has neither. The polarisation weight is needed even with a fixed GENIE.

\subsection{FHC}
\label{sec:fhc}
The wrong-sign $\numubar$ in FHC gives $0.90\times$ the RHC \Szero\ yield per POT
(GENIE, corrected: 49.2 against 54.4 per $1.1526\times10^{21}$~POT) with a similar
spectrum ($\langle E_\nu\rangle$ 1.56 against 1.48~GeV). With the $1.1048\times10^{21}$~POT
of all FHC runs, including Run~5, that is 47 expected in-FV \Szero, $87\%$ of RHC. The
selection does not use the muon charge, so FHC's $\nu_\mu$-dominated background per POT
will be larger. The PeLEE FHC overlay (Runs 1, 2, 4c, 4d, 5) and beam-off samples exist;
the FHC GENIE signal sample is being regenerated (Sec.~\ref{sec:plan}). FHC enters as a
second bin of a combined limit.

\section{The PeLEE selection}
\label{sec:pelee}
% =============================================================================

\subsection{Candidates and cut chain}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Muon}: longest track with $\texttt{trk\_score}>0.8$, start within
        $20$~cm of the vertex, length $>10$~cm, LLR PID $>0.7$.
  \item \textbf{Proton and pion}: each non-muon track is scored for both roles and the
        best ordered pair is taken (\texttt{PidAssign.h}); pion-candidate purity
        $42.5\%$ against a ceiling of $51.4\%$ (the fraction of selected signal with a
        reconstructed true $\pi^-$). The candidates are formed when the tracks exist but
        are not required: requiring them removes more signal than background. Events
        without a pair carry sentinels and the BDT input \texttt{HasPair}.
  \item \textbf{Photon}: the non-track PFPs closest to the vertex; the closest is the
        \Szero\ decay photon, $70$~MeV on average in signal against $74.5$ expected.
\end{itemize}
Stages: \texttt{AllEvents}, \texttt{FiducialV}, \texttt{CosmicVeto}
($\texttt{topological\_score}>0.20$), \texttt{NShowers} ($0<n_{\mathrm{shr}}<4$),
\texttt{MuonCandidate}, \texttt{NTracksCut} ($n_{\mathrm{trk}}<5$), \texttt{BDTG}.

\subsection{BDT}
\label{sec:bdt}
Gradient BDT (600 trees, depth 3) over 57 inputs: candidate PID and geometry, \Lam\ and
\Szero\ masses, \Lam\ and photon vertex pointing, \Lam\ decay distance, a $\pi^0$
di-photon mass, the Moli\`ere angle and the second-shower-veto block of the single-photon
analyses (App.~\ref{app:purity}). Two-fold cross-validation by event parity: each fold
is applied only to events it did not train on, so every BDT number is out of sample.
Each fold trains on $95\%$ of its events; the signal class is the NuWro and GENIE QE
signal of the definition of Sec.~\ref{sec:sigdef} (GENIE scaled to the NuWro summed weight);
the background class holds the overlay, dirt, EXT, NuWro's non-signal hyperons and the
\Szero\ of both generators outside the definition, so the BDT learns to prefer a real $p\pi^-$
pair; every input is mapped into its bulk range (\texttt{BDTClamp.h}), because the ntuples
carry $\pm$FLT\_MAX, $10^5$ and INT\_MAX sentinels that defeat TMVA's evenly spaced cut grid.
Retrained for the definition, it halves the out-of-definition \Szero\ it accepts and lowers
the background by a third at the previous cut. The development measurements are in
App.~\ref{app:bdt}.

\subsection{Detector variations}

\section{The \Lam-style selection}
\label{sec:lambdastyle}
% =============================================================================

The \uboone\ \Lam\ CCQE analysis (Thorpe \& Nowak, Hyperon Technote v2) selects
$\numubar$ CC \Lam\ with $91\%$ purity, where the earlier hypana BDT chain for \Szero\
reached $2.8\%$ at the same efficiency. This chain follows it:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Every ordered track pair is scored}; the pair selector's only
        topological input is the separation of the two track starts, which does not
        penalise a \Lam\ that flew. Pair precuts are a preference, not a veto.
  \item \textbf{The decay vertex is fitted} (V fit), giving a decay length and the
        pointing angle $\alpha$ between the pair momentum and the vertex-to-decay
        direction.
  \item \textbf{The island test} (Fig.~\ref{fig:island}): on each plane a wire $\times$
        time grid of the raw activity is clustered from seeds at the muon, proton and
        pion starts, and the proton and pion clusters must merge while the muon stays
        separate. Connectivity $2$ wires $\times$ $20$ ticks passes $80\%$ of signal with
        $2$--$3$~cm \Lam\ flight. It needs the raw wire windows, hence hypana.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Selection optimisation}
\label{sec:lsopt}
The figure of merit is the expected limit; at $B\approx4.6$ it is set by Poisson
counting, so signal efficiency matters as much as rejection. The background rests on 23
raw events, so every candidate cut is checked at the photon-gap stage (549 raw) and by
parity cross-validation (\texttt{IslandOptimise.py}).
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Proton-candidate PID ($\texttt{llrP}<0$): adopted.} It keeps $91.5\%$
        of signal for both generators and removes about half the background. The expected
        limit improves by $12$--$13\%$ on the median and on the mean over outcomes, and on
        both parity halves; its threshold is the natural midpoint, not tuned.
  \item \textbf{After it, the real \Lam\ is $38\%$ of the background.} Its pair is genuine,
        so only the photon separates it. A maximum photon distance removes it but loses
        the $16\%$ of signal photons that convert beyond $55$~cm, and does not improve the
        limit. Shower shape (hits per plane, length, opening angle, Moli\`ere angle,
        $dE/dx$) does not separate the Michel and $\delta$-ray electrons from the soft
        \Szero\ photon. Photon pointing to the vertex rejects the real \Lam\ but the
        $45$~MeV photon is too poorly reconstructed for a useful cut. The \Szero\ mass
        window costs more signal than it removes. \textbf{A muon-track photon veto} (reject the event if
        its photon starts within $5$~cm of the muon axis and more than $20$~cm along it, up
        to $10$~cm past its end: $\delta$ rays and Michel electrons) removes 2 of the 4
        real-\Lam\ events for $6\%$ of signal, but leaves the mean expected limit unchanged
        ($9.32$ against $9.38$ GENIE); it is not used. Without the $20$~cm the veto would
        catch $23\%$ of true \Szero\ photons, those converting near the vertex in the
        muon's direction (App.~\ref{app:ls}).
  \item \textbf{Loosening any single cut} (mass window, pointing, decay length, photon gap,
        island planes, PID threshold) recovers signal only with proportionally more
        background and does not improve the limit. A full scan finds $20\%$ on the full
        sample but about $6\%$ out of sample; the background statistics do not support a
        finer optimisation.
  \item \textbf{Events without a reconstructed shower} (the soft \Szero\ photon missed, $20\%$
        of the signal at that stage) were taken through the chain as a second bin: $S=0.213$
        GENIE on $B=5.64$ (EXT $1.53\pm0.88$ measured with the hypana EXT sample), purity
        $3.7\%$. Merged with the main bin they worsen the limit; as a separate bin they would
        gain $7$--$8\%$, which is not worth a second category. They are not used
        (App.~\ref{app:ls}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Expected sensitivity}
\label{sec:sensitivity}
% =============================================================================

\subsection{Method}
\texttt{Sensitivity.C} gives the expected 90\%~CL upper limit on $\mu$, the \Szero\ rate
in units of a generator's prediction, for both chains under both generators. Each chain
is a single-bin counting experiment, $n\sim\mathrm{Pois}(\mu S e + b)$:
\begin{itemize}
  \item $b$ is lognormal with mean $B$ and fractional width $\sqrt{r_{\mathrm{MC}}^2+f^2}$,
        $r_{\mathrm{MC}}$ the MC statistics of $B$ and $f$ the background systematic,
        which is scanned;
  \item $e$ is lognormal with mean 1 and width $\sqrt{g^2+r_S^2}$, $g=20\%$ a placeholder
        for flux, efficiency and POT;
  \item Bayesian limit, flat prior in $\mu\geq0$, $b$ and $e$ marginalised (hybrid CL$_s$
        is $4\%$ tighter; both reproduce the PDG table for known $b$);
  \item expected limit at the median $n$ of $\mathrm{Pois}(B)$, band from the $16\%$ and
        $84\%$ quantiles; $Z_A$ from Cowan et al.\ (arXiv:1007.1727).
\end{itemize}
$\muUL=n_{\mathrm{UL}}(B,\sigma_b)/S$: the generator only rescales the limit, and the cut
that minimises it does not depend on the signal normalisation.

        budget and re-measure the detector variations there.
  \item \textbf{Fixed GENIE signal samples.} \texttt{genie::HypQELTracePXSec}
        (App.~\ref{app:hypfix}) is validated; RHC (3050 chunks) and then FHC (1200 chunks)
        are being regenerated, at the lowest batch priority, on all nodes. They then go
        through the overlay chain to PeLEE and hypana ntuples. With them the
        cross-section weight is dropped for the signal samples; the polarisation weight
        stays, and the overlay and dirt keep the cross-section weight. The GENIE problem
        should be reported to the GENIE authors: it moves GENIE's \Lam\ prediction by
        $\times1.26$, the model the \uboone\ \Lam\ measurement compared with.
  \item \textbf{FHC} (Sec.~\ref{sec:fhc}): the FHC background through the PeLEE selection,
        the FHC signal efficiency from the regenerated sample, possibly a separate FHC cut,

  \item \textbf{\Lam\ cross-check.} Redo the comparison with the published \Lam\ CCQE result
        on the QE sample with the corrected GENIE (App.~\ref{app:ls}). Re-measure the PeLEE
        detector variations at the current cut and training.
  \item \textbf{Signal model.} Hyperon FSI is the factor four between the generators, and
        the NuWro build reproduces neither the published FSI reduction nor, so far, a
        reason for the difference; the limit is quoted under both models.
  \item \textbf{Closure and unblinding.} A Poisson-throw fake-data closure test
        (App.~\ref{app:misc}) with the GENIE sample as the alternative model; then the
        observed limit from the RHC beam-on data (\texttt{custom\_pelee\_ntuples}), and the
        absolute cross-section limit from the integrated $\numubar$ flux and the argon
        target count.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Polarisation.} The spin projector $(1+\gamma_5\,s\!\!\!/)/2$ on the hyperon,

corner). The M1 decay passes the polarisation on as $\vec P_\Lam=-(\vec P_\Sigma\cdot\hat
n)\hat n$. The rebuilt decay directions give $75.4\pm10$~MeV/$c$ for the \Lam\ in the
\Szero\ frame (74.5 expected) and $108\pm26$~MeV/$c$ for the nucleon in the \Lam\ frame
(100.6).

\section{What limits the PeLEE purity}
\label{app:purity}
Measured on earlier versions of the chain; the fractions carry over.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{The surviving background is $\pi^0$ events}: resonant $52\%$, DIS $24\%$,
        QE $14\%$, MEC $7\%$, cosmic $3\%$. A $\pi^0$ supplies the fake photon and its
        hadrons the fake proton and pion.
  \item \textbf{The candidate assignment is the weak link}: in background the muon
        candidate is right $81\%$ of the time, the proton $58\%$, the pion $31\%$, the
        photon $29\%$.
  \item \textbf{The signal photon is soft}: $E_\gamma=(m_{\Szero}^2-m_\Lam^2)/2m_{\Szero}=
        74.5$~MeV, against $140$~MeV for background photons. Soft-photon cuts help at
        preselection but not on top of the BDT, which already uses the energy; a $\pi^0$
        mass veto rejects only $9\%$ because it needs two showers; the Moli\`ere angle adds
        $1\%$.
  \item \textbf{Methods from the single-photon analyses}: the second-shower-veto variables
        are BDT inputs; one BDT per background class and a \texttt{HasPair} split were
        tried and do not help; the conversion distance is the \Lam-style photon gap; the
        $\pi^0$ normalisation (GENIE over-predicts NC$\pi^0$ by $25\%$) belongs in the
        covariance and a sideband constraint.
\end{itemize}

\section{Reconstruction study on HyperonProduction ntuples}
\label{app:reco}
The PeLEE truth record never contains the $\Szero\to\Lam\gamma\to p\pi^-\gamma$ chain
(zero of 17\,721 signal events have a backtracked $\pi^-$). Hypana writes the full chain
and per-PFP truth; \texttt{HypInput.h} maps it onto the PeLEE names. Three format traps:
no implicit neutrino-slice requirement (an explicit
\texttt{flash\_match\_nu\_slice\_ID}~$\geq0$ stage brings the chains to $6$--$8\%$
agreement), \texttt{trk\_energy\_proton} is the proton momentum (with the conversion the
\Lam\ mass width falls from $23.5$ to $7.8$~MeV), and \texttt{pfp\_true\_origin} is the
analyser's own enum (1 neutrino primary, 2 \Lam\ daughter, 3 other, 5 \Szero\ daughter,
$-1$ unmatched).

With both the reconstructed and true vertex in the FV and $\Lam\to p\pi^-$ in truth
(3704 signal events), the four particles are reconstructed as: $\mu^+$ $91.8\%$, $p$
$60.9\%$, $\pi^-$ $47.3\%$, $\gamma$ $62.4\%$; all four together $18.1\%$, the ceiling for
any selection requiring all four. Track/shower: the pion is a track $54\%$ of the time,
and the photon a track $15\%$, which feeds the photon contamination of the pion slot.
The \Lam\ flight from the primary vertex is not measurable (median true flight $1.85$~cm
against $5.5$~cm vertex resolution, $r=-0.014$), which is why the \Lam-style chain fits
the decay vertex from the two tracks instead.

\paragraph{Fake data by Poisson throw.} The PeLEE ``data'' slot holds the GENIE QE sample
scaled by POT: a generator test of the efficiency, not a closure test. The CC$1\mu1\pi$
Poisson-throw scheme (\texttt{throw\_perrun\_w.C}: $n\sim\mathrm{Pois}(w_{\mathrm{cv}}

closure test; injecting the GENIE signal into a NuWro-based throw gives the model test.

provides the limit.
