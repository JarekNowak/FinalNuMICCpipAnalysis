\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:pt_intro}
% =============================================================================

The inclusive analysis~\cite{incl_note} measures charged-current single-charged-pion production
with any number of nucleons in the final state. Requiring in addition a reconstructed proton gives
access to the whole visible hadronic system and to two classes of observable that the inclusive
measurement cannot provide: the hadronic invariant mass, which probes the $\Delta(1232)$ and
higher resonances, and the transverse kinematic imbalance (TKI) between the muon and the hadronic
system, which is sensitive to Fermi motion and to final-state interactions.

The proton requirement changes more than the sample size. The signal definition acquires a proton
threshold and a leading-proton convention. The reconstruction acquires a proton candidate that is
the leading true proton in $82\%$ of selected signal events. Two observables, $p_n$ and the
hadronic-mass proxy, rely on a single-nucleon residual-mass prescription written for quasi-elastic
kinematics and not yet validated for pion production. The detector uncertainty, evaluated on the
smaller sample, reaches $75\%$ in the sparsest extractions. The inclusive measurement carries none of
these, and the two analyses are therefore documented and approved separately.

Everything that the two analyses share is described in the inclusive note and not repeated here:
the beam, exposure and flux (\noteref{Sec.}{sec:flux}), the samples and event weights
(\noteref{Sec.}{sec:mc}), the inclusive selection (\noteref{Sec.}{sec:selection}), the extraction
(\noteref{Sec.}{sec:unfolding}) and the uncertainty model (\noteref{Sec.}{sec:systematics}). The
optimisation studies and diagnostics behind the choices made here are in the technical supplement.

\section{Signal definition and selection}
\label{sec:pt_selection}
\label{sec:selection_1p}
\label{sec:wtki_cutflow}
% =============================================================================

\paragraph{Signal.} The signal is the inclusive signal of \noteref{Sec.}{sec:signal} with at least
one true proton above $0.3\GeVc$; the muon and pion phase space is unchanged. The truth-level
proton observables use the leading proton, the one of highest momentum.

\paragraph{Selection.} The ten inclusive cuts of \noteref{Sec.}{sec:cutdefs} are applied unchanged,
followed by an eleventh cut that identifies the proton:
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{lcl}
\toprule
Requirement & Threshold & Description \\
\midrule
Candidate set & --- & track-like primaries other than the muon and pion candidates \\
Proton likeness & LLR PID $<0.05$ & the most proton-like of the set is the candidate \\
Containment & end point in FV & range-based momentum is meaningful only if contained \\
Momentum & $p_p\ge0.3\GeVc$ & tracking threshold; below it protons are not reconstructed reliably \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\noindent The proton momentum is computed from the proton-hypothesis kinetic energy of the candidate
track, which is obtained from its range, as $p_p=\sqrt{T_p^2+2m_pT_p}$. On truth-matched FHC
simulation it underestimates the true momentum by $9\%$ on average, with a resolution of $21\%$;
both are part of the response matrix.

\paragraph{Performance.} At full exposure the selection has an efficiency of $11.5\%$ and a purity of
$52\%$ in FHC ($50\%$ in RHC, $51\%$ combined), against $17.5\%$ and $59\%$ for the inclusive
selection. Relative to the inclusive final cut evaluated under the proton-tagged signal definition,
the proton requirement rejects $67\%$ of the neutrino background for a $32\%$ loss of signal,
raises the purity from $34\%$ to $52\%$ and improves $S/\sqrt{B}$ from $17.1$ to $20.5$ (FHC). It
also rejects cosmic rays: a contained proton above $0.3\GeVc$ reduces the beam-off background by a
factor $3.3$--$3.6$ at the final cut and by up to six in the CC$0\pi$ control region
(\suppref{Sec.}{sec:sidebands_1p}). The cut-flow by stage and category is
\suppref{Table}{tab:cutflow_1p} (stacks in \suppref{Fig.}{fig:cutflow_1p}); it uses the per-run
exposure scaling of the inclusive cut-flow (\noteref{Table}{tab:cutflow}), and its final row gives
the counts of Table~\ref{tab:pt_total}.

\subsection{What the proton candidate is}
\label{sec:prmatch}

On selected signal events the proton candidate is the leading true proton in $81.7\%$ of cases,
some true proton in $87.4\%$, and not a proton in $12.6\%$ ($10\,105$ simulated events). The wrong
candidates are mainly charged pions or other tracks taken for the proton.

The effect of a wrong candidate was measured for the two angular observables. A correct candidate
reconstructs the angle precisely, with an interquartile range of the residual of $0.073$~rad in
$\theta_p$ and $0.124$~rad in $\theta_{\pi p}$. A wrong candidate gives an angle unrelated to the
true one: the interquartile range is $1.0$--$1.4$~rad, and three quarters of those events have
residuals beyond $0.3$~rad. The core resolution averaged over all events is only a quarter to a
third wider than that of correct candidates, but the migrations between bins come from the tails:
residuals beyond $0.3$~rad occur in $18.9\%$ of $\theta_p$ events, and $74\%$ of them are wrong
candidates ($25.9\%$ and $51\%$ for $\theta_{\pi p}$). The candidate purity is therefore the main
source of bin-to-bin migration in $\theta_p$, whose smallest migration diagonal is $0.69$ in FHC and
$0.68$ in RHC at the adopted edges.

The same wrong-candidate rate applies to $W_{\pi p}$, $W_\mathrm{had}$ and the TKI observables,
which use the same candidate; the attribution of the migrations was measured for the angles only.
The multi-$\pi$ control region of the inclusive analysis receives otherwise-signal events whose proton fails the proton-likeness requirement, since the
pion count uses the same PID cut. It constrains the proton$\to$pion direction, not the
pion$\to$proton direction that produces the $12.6\%$, and it is the region with the largest signal
content. A control region that inverts the proton PID at exactly one pion candidate would separate
the two directions (\suppref{Sec.}{sec:cr_1p_scope}).

\section{Observables and binning}
\label{sec:pt_observables}
% =============================================================================

\paragraph{Definitions.} All observables are built from the three candidate tracks with
reconstructed quantities only: the range or multiple-scattering momentum of the muon, the range
momentum of the pion, the range kinetic energy of the proton and the track directions. Angles and
transverse quantities are taken about the neutrino direction of \noteref{Sec.}{sec:beamdir}, the
same convention as the inclusive analysis and the generator predictions.
\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
  \item The proton momentum $p_p$ is that of the candidate; at truth level it is the momentum of the
    leading proton.
  \item The $\pi p$ invariant mass, $W_{\pi p}=\big[(E_\pi+E_p)^2-(\vec p_\pi+\vec p_p)^2\big]^{1/2}$,
    is measured directly and needs no neutrino direction.
  \item The calorimetric hadronic-mass proxy,
    $W_\mathrm{had}=\big[m_p^2+2m_p(E_\mathrm{cal}-E_\mu)-Q^2\big]^{1/2}$, is built from the
    reconstructed calorimetric energy $E_\mathrm{cal}$ and $Q^2$.
  \item The TKI observables are the transverse-momentum imbalance $\delta p_T$, its angles
    $\delta\alpha_T$ and $\delta\phi_T$~\cite{lu_tki}, and the inferred nucleon-momentum proxy
    $p_n$~\cite{furmanski_pn}, computed from the muon against the summed $(\pi+p)$ system with the
    STV tool of the framework after rotating both into the frame in which the neutrino defines the
    $z$ axis.
  \item The proton polar angle and the pion--proton opening angle,
    \[
      \theta_p=\arccos\!\big(\hat p_p\cdot\hat\nu\big), \qquad
      \theta_{\pi p}=\arccos\!\big(\hat p_\pi\cdot\hat p_p\big),
    \]
    of which $\theta_{\pi p}$ is an angle between two reconstructed tracks and does not depend on
    the neutrino direction.
\end{itemize}
The five muon and pion observables of the inclusive analysis ($p_\mu$, $p_\pi$, $\cos\theta_\mu$,
$\cos\theta_\pi$, $\theta_{\mu\pi}$) are measured again on the proton-tagged phase space. The
formulae and the conventions of the TKI tool are given in \suppref{Sec.}{sec:wtki_reco}.

The TKI observables and $W_\mathrm{had}$ are built from the summed hadronic system and are
insensitive to how its momentum is shared between the pion and the proton; $\theta_{\pi p}$ measures
that sharing. $\theta_p$ is the proton direction itself, of which the other observables use only
derived quantities.

\paragraph{Binning.} Every binning meets the $0.50$ migration criterion of the inclusive analysis
with at least $50$ selected FHC signal events per bin (\suppref{Sec.}{sec:binreview};
Table~\ref{tab:pt_binning}): $p_p$ in three bins split at $0.42$ and $0.60\GeVc$ with an open upper
bin, $W_\mathrm{had}$ in two regions split at $1.18$~GeV/$c^2$, $\delta\phi_T$ in three bins,
$\delta p_T$, $\delta\alpha_T$ and $p_n$ in two, and $\theta_p$ and $\theta_{\pi p}$ in four each,
at edges that maximise the smallest migration diagonal. The muon and pion observables use the
inclusive edges, except $p_\pi$, which has two regions because the three inclusive regions fail the
criterion in this sample, and $\cos\theta_\pi$, which has four bins. The RHC and combined samples use
the FHC binnings.

\subsection{The differential \texorpdfstring{$W_{\pi p}$}{W pi p} shape is not reported}
\label{sec:pt_wpip_withdrawn}

The pion--proton invariant mass would show the $\Delta(1232)$ directly and motivated the
proton-tagged subsample. At the present exposure its shape cannot be measured with any binning
tried. The six-bin scheme that resolves the resonance region has a highest bin with a $2\%$
diagonal, so nearly every event reconstructed there comes from another true bin. Two bins restore
the response but leave no shape: the best split, at $1.20$~GeV/$c^2$, passes the $0.50$ criterion by
$0.003$, and its second Wiener filter factor is $0.29$ in FHC and $0.27$ in RHC. A closure test
against the $A_C$-smeared truth cannot change this, since it tests whether the response was inverted
correctly and cannot restore information the response does not contain. The six-bin extractions
remain in the result directory, flagged in \texttt{index\_extractions.tsv}, and their closure and
generator curves are shown as diagnostics in \suppref{Sec.}{sec:w_binning}; they are not results.
The sample supports the integrated cross section and $W_\mathrm{had}$ in two regions.

\section{Extraction and uncertainties}
\label{sec:pt_extraction}
% =============================================================================

Each observable is extracted as in the inclusive analysis: the selected spectrum is scaled per run
to the recorded exposure, the neutrino, beam-off and dirt backgrounds are subtracted, and the result
is unfolded with the Wiener-SVD method~\cite{wienervsd} and the smearceptance matrix of the proton-tagged signal
(\noteref{Sec.}{sec:unfolding}). Every result is provided with its additional-smearing matrix $A_C$,
through which a prediction must be passed before comparison, and with its covariance by source
(\noteref{Sec.}{sec:ac_rowsums}). The combined configuration is an independent extraction with its
own response and $A_C$, not an average of FHC and RHC. The two families of processed files store the
simulated exposure under different conventions, and the run-by-run normalisation is correct under
both (\suppref{Sec.}{sec:wtki_reco}).

The covariance contains the flux, cross-section, re-interaction, detector, POT, target-count and
statistical terms of the inclusive analysis. The detector term ranges from $9.9\%$ to $74.5\%$
(median $30.9\%$) against $16.6$--$34.2\%$ for the inclusive results. It is of the same size in most
extractions, as expected for the same detector, and largest in the sparsest ones (RHC $p_n$ and the
six-bin $W_{\pi p}$, both near $74\%$), where the unfolding amplifies the variations. The flux term
of the one-bin total is $35.5\%$ in FHC. As in the inclusive analysis it is the flux normalisation
multiplied by $1+B/S$ through the background subtraction (\noteref{Sec.}{sec:fluxanatomy}):
$17.7\%\times(1+298/331)=33.6\%$, the difference from the inclusive factor reflecting the purity of
this sample.

\subsection{Muon momentum scale}
\label{sec:pt_mcs}

The muon momentum scale is varied as in the inclusive analysis (\noteref{Sec.}{sec:mcs}): in the fake
data alone, every muon whose momentum is measured by multiple Coulomb scattering is scaled by
$1\pm0.05$, while the simulation, the response and the selection stay nominal, and each variation is
unfolded with the nominal response. The half-difference of the two results is taken as a fully
correlated term. In this sample the muon momentum also enters $W_\mathrm{had}$, $\delta p_T$,
$\delta\alpha_T$ and $p_n$, which are therefore recomputed from the scaled muon for every event with a
proton candidate (\texttt{macros/mcs\_scale\_fakedata\_1p.C}); with a scale of one the recomputed
values reproduce the stored ones exactly. $\delta\phi_T$ depends only on the muon direction, and
$W_{\pi p}$, $p_p$, $p_\pi$ and the angles do not use the muon momentum, so their term is zero.

Table~\ref{tab:pt_mcs} gives the result. The largest term is in the open highest $p_\mu$ bin,
$24\%$ of the bin value and $0.62\sigma$ of its uncertainty in FHC; the lower $p_\mu$ bins move by
$8$--$15\%$, as in the inclusive analysis. For the derived observables the term is at most
$0.37\sigma$: it is largest in the lower $W_\mathrm{had}$ region ($16$--$34\%$, $0.23$--$0.37\sigma$),
in the upper $p_n$ bin ($18$--$24\%$) and in the lower $\delta\alpha_T$ bin ($11$--$13\%$), and it is
below $0.15\sigma$ for $\delta p_T$. For the two-bin $\delta p_T$ the downward scale moves as many
selected events across the edge at $0.3\GeVc$ in one direction as in the other, so its result equals
the nominal one and the half-difference is set by the upward scale alone. Adding the term raises the
per-bin total uncertainty by at most $18\%$ of itself for $p_\mu$ (FHC) and by at most $7\%$ for the
derived observables. The term is provided as \texttt{cov\_MCSscale.txt} and
\texttt{cov\_total\_plusMCS.txt} in the release directory of each extraction
(\texttt{data\_release/mcs\_scale\_1p\_2026-09-27.tsv}) and is not yet included in the covariance
computed by the framework.

\subsection{Effects not included in the covariance}

The beamline-geometry flux term, the uncertainty of the beam-off gate ratios and the use of the Run-1
beam-off sample for Run~2 are bounded in the inclusive note (\noteref{Sec.}{sec:notincov}) and are
not included in the covariance. The beam-off background of the proton-tagged sample is three to four
times smaller than that of the inclusive sample, so the last two are smaller still.

\subsection{Total cross section}
\label{sec:pt_total}

The total cross section is extracted as a single bin, as in the inclusive analysis
(\noteref{Sec.}{sec:total_xsec}): nothing is regularised and $A_C=1$, so the one-bin result is the
physical total in the measured phase space and does not depend on an observable. It is the
proposed secondary result (Table~\ref{tab:pt_total}). A closed-form evaluation on the central-value
prediction reproduces the one-bin extraction of that prediction to four digits ($0.6269$, $0.5506$
and $0.5831$ for FHC, RHC and combined). The extracted RHC/FHC ratio is $0.873$, against $0.878$ for
the tune.

The integrated cross sections of the differential results are $A_C$-suppressed and depend on the
observable, because each $A_C$ changes the normalisation by a different factor. They range from
$0.36$ to $0.57$ in the combined configuration, below the one-bin total of $0.564$, and are not

combined result lies between the FHC and RHC results for ten of the twelve extractions; for

is well inside the $30\%$ uncertainties.

The generator predictions are computed at truth level from the event-level final state of each
generator (GENIE~\cite{genie,genie3} and GiBUU~\cite{gibuu} natively, NuWro~\cite{nuwro} and

binnings the generators differ almost entirely in normalisation: their shapes and that of the tune
agree with the unfolded points within the uncertainties in every panel, so the present exposure does
not discriminate between their treatments of Fermi motion and final-state interactions. A comparison
in which data and prediction used different axes would show shape differences of a factor of
several, so the frame is stated with every transverse quantity.

\subsection{Proton angles \texorpdfstring{$\theta_p$}{theta p} and
  \texorpdfstring{$\theta_{\pi p}$}{theta pi p}}
\label{sec:wtki_ang}

The two proton angles are proposed as secondary scope. $\theta_p$ uses the stored proton direction;
$\theta_{\pi p}$ is written by a reprocessing of the proton-tagged tree that leaves every other branch
unchanged. Both are binned in four bins and unfold well, with closure $\chi^2$ of $3.6/4$, $0.09/4$
and $1.5/4$ for $\theta_p$ and $0.29/4$, $0.57/4$ and $0.24/4$ for $\theta_{\pi p}$ in FHC, RHC and
combined (Fig.~\ref{fig:newobs_xsec}). $\theta_{\pi p}$ retains the most shape: the trace of $A_C$ is
$2.1$--$2.2$, with three components measured at a signal-to-noise ratio above one in FHC and RHC, at
the price of a last bin whose total uncertainty reaches $94\%$ (FHC) and $141\%$ (RHC). $\theta_p$ has
a trace of $2.1$ in FHC and RHC ($2.3$ combined) and a largest per-bin uncertainty of $41\%$ (FHC)
and $84\%$ (RHC); its smallest diagonal passes the $0.68$ criterion by $0.002$ in RHC. Both
distributions are strongly forward peaked, and the generators differ mainly in normalisation, so
these observables discriminate between models through their integrals. The binning study, the
generator predictions and the two-dimensional programme are documented in a separate note.

\subsection{Two-dimensional cross sections}
\label{sec:2d}

Eight two-dimensional pairs were screened and extracted in the three configurations. A
two-dimensional binning is a flat list of analysis bins ordered slice by slice, so the extraction
uses the same machinery, and the integrated normalisation of the two-dimensional chain agrees with
the one-bin total to a median of $1.5\%$ once each is compared with its own $A_C$-smeared truth.
Two pairs are usable, both in the combined configuration only: $\theta_p\times\delta p_T$
($s_{\min}/s_1=0.611$) and $\cos\theta_\pi\times\cos\theta_\mu$ ($0.564$) (Table~\ref{tab:2dextract}).
Combining the beam modes roughly doubles their conditioning ($0.43\to0.61$ and $0.25\to0.56$), through
the larger sample and the shared flux rather than through any separation of $\nu$ and $\bar\nu$. The
pairs built on $W_{\pi p}$ are not usable, as the $39\%$ leakage between its outer bins implies.

The pair $\theta_{\pi p}\times W_{\pi p}$ conditions at $0.537$, as well as the two usable pairs, yet
its extraction carries no usable information: a $78\%$ data-statistical term, per-bin uncertainties up
to $276\%$ and closure ratios down to $-0.80$. Conditioning bounds how much of the response can be
inverted and says nothing about how much data survives in the inverted directions, so the two must be
read together. Conversely, $\cos\theta_\mu\times p_\mu$ screened best of the eight on the migration
diagonal ($0.70/0.68$) and is rank-deficient in FHC ($0.002$), while $\theta_p\times\delta p_T$
screened worst of the proton-tagged pairs and conditions best of all. The migration diagonal is a
necessary condition, not a sufficient one; the one-dimensional binning review measures what the
regularised response retains by the Wiener filter factors of $A_C$ (\suppref{Sec.}{sec:binreview}).

\section{Validation}
\label{sec:pt_validation}
% =============================================================================

The validation follows that of the inclusive analysis (\noteref{Sec.}{sec:validation}): closure under
the nominal model, statistical coverage with ensembles, and robustness to a different interaction
model.

\subsection{Closure under the nominal model}
\label{sec:pt_closure}

The fake data are unfolded and compared with the $A_C$-smeared truth of the throw
(Tables~\ref{tab:wtki_fhc}--\ref{tab:wtki_comb}). In FHC the $\chi^2$ is well below the number of bins
for every observable, and the integral ratios are $0.93$--$1.18$. These ratios compare the unfolded
result with the $A_C$-smeared truth, so the $A_C$ suppression cancels and the departures from unity
reflect the statistical realisation of the throw and any residual bias; the largest are in
$\cos\theta_\mu$ ($1.18$) and $\delta\alpha_T$ ($0.93$). In RHC and combined the closure gives
$\chi^2/\mathrm{ndf}\lesssim1$ for every observable except RHC $\cos\theta_\pi$ ($6.0/4$). As in the inclusive
analysis, the systematic terms are common to the fake data and the reference, so the closure
$p$-values cluster near unity and the statistical coverage is tested with ensembles.

\subsection{Statistical coverage}
\label{sec:pt_ensemble}

Three extractions were repeated on $100$ statistically independent fake-data sets each, with the
procedure of the inclusive ensembles (\suppref{Sec.}{sec:supp_ensemble}). Every member is a run-by-run
Poisson throw of the central-value prediction carried through the whole chain: the universes are
rebuilt from the throw and the result is unfolded. Each unfolded bin is compared with the fixed
central-value truth smeared by that member's own $A_C$,
\[
  p_i \;=\; \frac{\hat{x}_i - \left(A_C\,x_{\rm CV}\right)_i}{\sigma_i}.
\]
The three extractions span the family: $W_{\rm had}$ in two regions in FHC, the two-bin $\delta p_T$ in
FHC, and the two-bin $p_n$ in the combined configuration, which also exercises the combination of the
beam modes. For all $1600$ input files the number of events read by the universe builder agrees with
the entries on disk, so no member read an incomplete file.

\input{tables/ensemble_1p}

Against the statistical covariance the pull widths are $0.94$--$1.08$, each known to about $\pm7\%$,
and the $68\%$ and $95\%$ coverages are $62$--$72\%$ and $92$--$97\%$. The widest case is the two-bin
$\delta p_T$ (width $1.08$, $68\%$ coverage $62\%$), within two standard deviations of nominal; its two
bins are correlated, so its $200$ pulls are fewer than $200$ independent tests. Bin by bin, every mean
is within $0.16$ of zero and every width is $0.92$--$1.09$. The statistical uncertainties of the
proton-tagged results are therefore correctly sized.

Against the full covariance the widths are $0.26$--$0.40$. The systematic part of the uncertainty is
two to three times the statistical part, as in the inclusive analysis, and the ensembles do not test
it, since every member is thrown from the same central-value model; that is the role of the
alternative-model test below. The ensemble-mean integrals agree with the reference within $0.7\%$, at
most $0.8$ standard errors of the mean. The other proton-tagged observables were not run individually.

\subsection{Robustness to a different interaction model}
\label{sec:pt_altmodel}

The fake data are thrown from a reweighted interaction model and unfolded with the nominal response
(\noteref{Sec.}{sec:altmodel}). The two variations of the inclusive analysis are used, GENIE multisim
universe~545, a coherent variation of all interaction parameters, and the $\Delta\to N\pi$ decay-angle
unisim, with the same universes and seeds; the weight applies to signal and background alike.
$W_\mathrm{had}$, the four TKI observables, $\cos\theta_\pi$ and $p_p$ are extracted in FHC. The truth
of the fake data is that of the alternative model, so the closure measures the bias from the model
mismatch, quoted in units of the per-bin uncertainty (Table~\ref{tab:pt_altmodel}).

the varied model makes to the composition of the proton-tagged sample and to the proton-tagging
efficiency, which the nominal response does not follow. The inclusive analysis shows a bias of this
size in one sparse bin only (\noteref{Sec.}{sec:altmodel}). The uncertainty covers the shift at about
one standard deviation, so the extraction does not fail, but the small-imbalance bins of the TKI
observables are model-sensitive at the level of their own uncertainty. A measurement in them needs
either this model dependence in the covariance or a demonstration with a validated independent
generator that the shift does not occur.

\section{Limitations and approval prerequisites}
\label{sec:pt_prereq}
% =============================================================================

The one-bin total is proposed as a secondary result on the same footing as the inclusive results,
with the unblinding requirements of \noteref{Sec.}{sec:unblinding}. Before any proton-tagged
differential result can be proposed, the following are required:
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep2pt
  \item an inverted-proton-PID control region, compared with beam-on data, which separates the
    proton$\to$pion and pion$\to$proton misidentification directions (\S\ref{sec:prmatch});
  \item a validation of $p_n$ and of $W_{\rm had}$ for pion production, whose single-nucleon
    prescriptions were written for quasi-elastic kinematics;
  \item a treatment of the model sensitivity of the low-imbalance $\delta p_T$ and $p_n$ bins, either in
    the covariance or excluded by an independent generator (\S\ref{sec:pt_altmodel});
  \item a bound on the effect of wrong proton candidates, which supply $74\%$ of the large migrations
    in $\theta_p$ (\S\ref{sec:prmatch});
  \item for the two-dimensional results, an assessment of whether their effective rank and statistical
    support make them measurements, and a decision on results available only in the combined
    configuration (\S\ref{sec:2d}).
\end{enumerate}
The statistical coverage of the family is demonstrated by the ensembles of \S\ref{sec:pt_ensemble},
and its sensitivity to the interaction model is measured by the test of \S\ref{sec:pt_altmodel}.
